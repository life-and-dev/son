# What Does It Mean to Believe in Jesus?

“Believe in Jesus” can sound simple until someone asks what to believe, whom to trust, and what faith should change. Scripture gives a fuller answer than a slogan. Jesus announced God’s kingdom and called people to repent. After his death and resurrection, his apostles announced what God had done through him and called people to respond. *Faith trusts the Father’s saving work through his Son; it does not depend on producing a particular feeling or understanding everything at once.*

## What believing means

The Greek verb *pisteuō* can mean believe, have faith, or trust; the related noun *pistis* can mean faith, belief, trust, confidence, or fidelity. Their meanings depend on context ([*pisteuō*](https://biblehub.com/greek/4100.htm); [*pistis*](https://biblehub.com/greek/4102.htm)). English “faith” and “trust” need not be opposed. Nor does every belief amount to saving trust.

Biblical faith has a **person** to trust, **content** to receive, **reliance** on what God has done and promised, and a **response** shaped by that trust. It is not mental force, faith in one’s own faith, or a quota of certainty. Trust does not require complete comprehension or mathematical proof.

Abraham “**believed** the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness” (Genesis 15:6). Paul says Abraham was “fully convinced that **God was able to do what he had promised**” (Romans 4:21). Paul then applies this account to those who believe in God, who raised Jesus: Jesus “was delivered up for **our trespasses** and raised for **our justification**” (Romans 4:23–25). *The reliance is on God and his promise, not on the strength of an inner feeling.*

When a father cried, “I **believe**; help my **unbelief**!” Jesus did not demand flawless confidence before helping him (Mark 9:24). Doubt may call for honest prayer and renewed attention to the gospel, not faith in one’s ability to feel certain.

## Jesus’ gospel of the kingdom

Jesus came proclaiming “the gospel of God” and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; **repent and believe in the gospel**” (Mark 1:14–15). When asked what work God required, he answered that people should believe in the one God had sent (John 6:28–29). His invitation was not mere agreement with a statement: he warned that saying “Lord, Lord” without doing his Father’s will was insufficient, and compared those who heard and did his words to a house built on rock (Matthew 7:21, 24–27). For the meaning of being known by Jesus, see [Known by Jesus](known-by-jesus.draft.md).

Jesus identified himself to the Samaritan woman as the expected Messiah (John 4:25–26). Many Samaritans first believed because of her testimony; after hearing Jesus themselves, they called him “the **Savior of the world**” (John 4:39–42). John states his Gospel’s purpose: “that you may believe that **Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God**, and that by believing you may have **life in his name**” (John 20:31).

Jesus also predicted his death and resurrection, while his disciples did not yet understand what he meant (Mark 8:31–33; 9:31–32). *His first hearers were not required to know every future event before it happened.* After his resurrection, he commissioned disciples to baptise and teach others to observe what he had commanded (Matthew 28:18–20). The later apostolic preaching explains the fulfilment of his kingdom announcement, rather than introducing a competing gospel.

## What the apostles preached after the resurrection

At Pentecost, Peter called Jesus “a **man attested to you by God**,” proclaimed that God raised him, and concluded that “God has made him both **Lord and Christ**” (Acts 2:22–24, 36). He called his hearers to repent and be baptised for forgiveness. About three thousand received his word and were baptised that day; Acts also says the Lord added those being saved to their number (Acts 2:38–41, 47).

Peter later preached that the God of the fathers glorified his servant Jesus, whom God raised. He identified Jesus as the appointed Christ, recalled Moses’ promised prophet, and urged his hearers to repent and turn back (Acts 3:13–26). Acts 4:4 reports that the number of **men** who believed came to about five thousand; it does not describe five thousand additional converts. Nor is this the feeding of about five thousand men in Matthew 14:13–21 and John 6:1–15; Matthew also mentions women and children. Attendance at that meal is not a count of people saved.

To Cornelius’ household, Peter proclaimed that God anointed Jesus, that Jesus was killed and raised by God, and that witnesses saw him alive. God appointed him judge; “everyone who **believes in him** receives **forgiveness of sins** through his name” (Acts 10:34–43). *The message concerns a real person, God’s action through him, and what that action means for sinners.*

Paul likewise reminds the Corinthians of the gospel they received and in which they stand, urging them to hold fast to it. Christ “died for **our sins**” according to the Scriptures, was buried, “was **raised on the third day**” according to the Scriptures, and appeared to witnesses (1 Corinthians 15:1–8). If Christ has not been raised, Paul says, faith is futile and believers remain in their sins (1 Corinthians 15:17). He calls the risen Christ the firstfruits of those who will be raised (1 Corinthians 15:20–23). *Believing the gospel means more than believing Jesus once existed: his death for sins and God’s raising him matter.*

Paul described his preaching as “**repentance toward God and of faith in our Lord Jesus Christ**” (Acts 20:21). In Rome he taught about God’s kingdom and Jesus from Moses and the Prophets (Acts 28:23, 30–31). The Thessalonians turned from idols to serve the living and true God and awaited his Son, whom God raised from the dead (1 Thessalonians 1:9–10). *Paul did not replace Jesus’ kingdom message; he preached its fulfilment in the risen Christ.*

## What faith trusts

Faith receives particular good news, not an undefined promise that everything will feel better:

- **Who Jesus is:** God’s Son, the Christ and the Lord whom God appointed (John 3:16–17; 20:31; Acts 2:36).
- **What his death means:** he gave his life as a ransom; his blood is poured out for forgiveness; Christ died for our sins (Mark 10:45; Matthew 26:28; 1 Corinthians 15:3). For more on his death and the covenant, see [The Cross](the-cross.draft.md).
- **What God did next:** God raised Jesus. His resurrection is essential to the announced forgiveness and the hope of future life (Romans 4:24–25; 1 Corinthians 15:17, 20–23).
- **What God offers through him:** forgiveness, peace with God, access to grace, and life in the Son’s name (Acts 10:43; Romans 5:1–2; John 20:31).

*This is a call to rely on God’s saving work, not a perfect-knowledge examination or a demand that every convert settle every later theological dispute before coming to Christ.*

## How to respond

Hear the gospel: Paul asks how anyone can believe without hearing and says faith comes through hearing the word of Christ (Romans 10:14–17). Turn toward the Father from sin and false allegiances, trusting his grace through Jesus rather than trying to earn acceptance. Confess Jesus as Lord and believe that God raised him from the dead (Romans 10:9). Be baptised and learn to follow what Jesus taught (Acts 2:38–41; Matthew 28:18–20).

When a jailer asked Paul and Silas what he must do to be saved, they answered, “**Believe in the Lord Jesus**” (Acts 16:30–31). They then spoke the word of the Lord to everyone in his house, and the household was baptised (Acts 16:32–34). *The household’s response is not automatic salvation through one person’s faith.* Neither does the account establish a guaranteed ritual sequence or settle every question about baptismal practice.

Salvation is “by **grace** ... through **faith**,” God’s gift, “not a result of works”; believers are nevertheless created in Christ Jesus “for **good works**” (Ephesians 2:8–10). Paul describes “faith **working through love**” (Galatians 5:6). James challenges a claim to faith that gives no help to a brother or sister in need: even demons believe that God is one, and faith without works is dead (James 2:14–19, 26). *Obedience and love are responses to grace, not its purchase price.*

That response includes shared service, not only private conviction. Paul describes members of one body using differing gifts for others (Romans 12:4–8), and the church growing as each part works properly (Ephesians 4:11–16). See [Fellowship](fellowship.draft.md) for practical shared life.

## Must I confess Jesus as Almighty God?

Romans 10:9 names the confession that “**Jesus is Lord**” and belief that “**God raised him from the dead**.” It does not explicitly add a requirement to confess, “Jesus is Almighty God.” Peter likewise distinguishes God, who raised and appointed Jesus, from Jesus, God’s Son and Christ (Acts 2:22–24, 36).

Romans 10:12–13 deserves careful attention: Paul applies Joel 2:32’s promise about calling on the name of the **LORD** to calling on the **Lord** in his discussion of Jesus. This is a serious textual basis for Trinitarian readings, not a verse to brush aside. *The limited point here is about the stated condition of confession: neither Romans 10:9 nor Peter’s invitation explicitly makes the phrase “Almighty God” a test for coming to Jesus.* This observation does not settle every question about Jesus’ identity, claim complete knowledge of every convert’s beliefs, or guarantee salvation to anyone who uses a particular label. For questions of honour and identity, see [Worship](worship.draft.md).

## Conclusion

Believe the gospel by trusting the Father’s saving work through his Son: receive the news that Jesus the Christ died for sins and that God raised him, turn toward God for forgiveness and life, and follow the risen Lord. Faith rests on him, not on perfect feelings or a claim to know everything.
