# The First and Last

Some people connect the titles "First and Last", "Alpha and Omega" or the Hebrew particle אֵת (often called Aleph-Tav) with Jesus to proof that he is the [eternal](eternal.md) LORD God.

Sharing a title does not by itself establish identity. That conclusion requires showing that the title uniquely identifies the eternal LORD God and applies to Jesus in that same sense.

## First and Last in Context

To understand if the argument holds, we need to establish the ordinary meanings of "first and last".

### Hebrew Meanings

[Brown-Driver-Briggs on &#34;first&#34; (רִאשׁוֹן)](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7223.htm), transliterated riʾšôn, gives senses including former, first and chief. [Its entry for &#34;last&#34; (אַחֲרוֹן)](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/314.htm), ʾaḥărôn, describes what comes after or behind, including what is latter or last. Neither adjective alone means Almighty, uncreated or without a beginning.

As a merism, that names two extremes to include everything between them, [ThoughtCo](https://www.thoughtco.com/merism-rhetoric-term-1691307) describes it as *“a pair of contrasting words or phrases”* expressing *“totality or completeness”*. The context supplies the range. The words do not always mean the whole universe or all eternity.

For example:

> Who has performed and done this, calling the generations from the beginning?
>
> I, the LORD, the **first**, and with the **last**; **I am He**.
>
> — Isaiah 41:4 (ESV)

Isaiah's context establishes claims about the LORD Who called *all* "the generations from the beginning".

> Thus says the LORD, **the King of Israel** and his **Redeemer**, **the LORD of hosts**:
>
> “I am the **first** and I am the **last**: besides **Me** there is no god.
>
> — Isaiah 44:6 (ESV)

Isaiah explicitly denies other gods. Here the "first" and "last" is used to highlight that the LORD ([YHWH](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/name#yhwh)) God is "the King of Israel" and "the LORD of hosts" (Isaiah 48:6)

"First and last" is also another royal title like "the King of Israel" and "the LORD of hosts". In this context, these royal titles are significant because it highlights that there are no other king, no other redeemer, no other LORD of hosts and certainly no other god. In other words, the LORD (YHWH) is the only or full God with all these roles.

> “Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called!
>
> **I am He**; I am the **first**, and I am the **last**. My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand spread out the heavens; when I call to them, they stand forth together.
>
> — Isaiah 48:12-13 (ESV)

In this context "first and last" refers to the entire creation which the LORD ([YHWH](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/name#yhwh)) God "laid" with His hand.

The merism, "first and last", used in Isaiah expresses the total or completeness of the LORD God calling, rule and works.

> [!NOTE]
> The LORD God (YHWH) uses singular predicates about himself.
>
> "First" and "Last" is not 2 separate persons. Neither did God replace an earlier god.
>
> It also challenges the argument that [YHWH](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/name#yhwh) is [the Trinitarian 3 person god](../trinity.md), because plural pronouns are required for multiple persons.

These above listed examples from Isaiah do not turn the phrase "first and last" into a fixed dictionary definition of deity wherever they appear.

### Royal Comparisons

The Hebrew הָרִאשֹׁנִים וְהָאַחֲרֹנִים (hāriʾšōnîm wəhāʾaḥărōnîm) in Chronicles means “the first and the last” or “the former and the latter”. Both adjectives are plural and describe the recorded acts, deeds or ways, not the singular king.

[BDB’s Hebrew lexicon](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7223.htm) explains the expression in 1 Chronicles 29:29 as *“the doings of David, the former and the latter”*. The phrase contains no separate Hebrew words for “from” and “to”. The ESV’s “from first to last” expresses its temporal sweep in natural English, while **KJV and NKJV preserve “first and last”** more directly. Neither rendering makes these words a personal title of the king. The expression encompasses earlier and later deeds without specifying exact days or necessarily restricting the records to events during the king’s reign.

These comparisons show that the words “first and last” **do not inherently imply beginningless existence or divinity**.

The following nine accounts describe a king's acts, deeds or ways as **first and last**:

| King        | Scripture          | KJV Excerpt                                                      |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| David       | 1 Chronicles 29:29 | *“the acts of David the king, **first and last**”*               |
| Solomon     | 2 Chronicles 9:29  | *“the rest of the acts of Solomon, **first and last**”*          |
| Rehoboam    | 2 Chronicles 12:15 | *“the acts of Rehoboam, **first and last**”*                     |
| Asa         | 2 Chronicles 16:11 | *“the acts of Asa, **first and last**”*                          |
| Jehoshaphat | 2 Chronicles 20:34 | *“the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, **first and last**”*      |
| Amaziah     | 2 Chronicles 25:26 | *“the rest of the acts of Amaziah, **first and last**”*          |
| Uzziah      | 2 Chronicles 26:22 | *“the rest of the acts of Uzziah, **first and last**”*           |
| Ahaz        | 2 Chronicles 28:26 | *“the rest of his acts and of all his ways, **first and last**”* |
| Josiah      | 2 Chronicles 35:27 | *“his deeds, **first and last**”*                                |

These comparisons show ordinary temporal endpoints covering the full recorded span of a reign. They do **not imply that any king was beginningless or divine**. They therefore rebut an argument based on the words' supposed "divine" intrinsic meaning.

### Greek Meanings and Human Uses

[Thayer&#39;s entry for &#34;first&#34; (πρῶτος)](https://biblehub.com/greek/4413.htm), prōtos, includes first in time, place or rank. [Its entry for &#34;last&#34; (ἔσχατος)](https://biblehub.com/greek/2078.htm), eschatos, includes last, extreme or lowest, according to context. Neither entry defines the word as Almighty God.

| Scripture     | Human Context                                                           | Function of First and Last                                                     |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mark 9:35     | A person who wishes to be first must be last of all and servant of all. | The same prospective human occupies contrasting positions of rank and service. |
| Matthew 19:30 | Many who are first will be last, and the last first.                    | The words describe a reversal of position.                                     |
| Matthew 20:8  | The vineyard workers receive their wages from the last to the first.    | The words distinguish the last-hired and first-hired workers.                  |

These passages show ordinary uses of the Greek vocabulary. They **do not give the people divine titles**.

## Alpha and Omega

A stronger form of the identity argument observes that **Alpha and Omega appears in descriptions of both God the Father and Jesus**.

### Alphabetic Meaning and Speakers

[Alpha](https://biblehub.com/greek/1.htm) is the first letter of the Greek alphabet, and [Omega](https://biblehub.com/greek/5598.htm) is the last.

Like the [above mentioned merism](#hebrew-meanings), they do not make Almighty the literal definition. The argument concerns the biblical context, not an invented parallel.

> “I am the Alpha and the Omega,” says the Lord God, “Who is and Who was and Who is to come, the Almighty.” — Revelation 1:8 (ESV)

The context identifies God, rather than the alphabet itself defining Almighty.

> And **He who was seated on the throne** said, “Behold, I am making all things new.”
> Also He said, “Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.”
> And He said to me, “It is done! I am **the Alpha and the Omega**, **the beginning and the end**. To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment. **The one who conquers** will have this heritage, and **I will be his God and he will be My son**. — Revelation 21:5-7 (ESV)

"The Alpha and Omega" is "He who was seated on the throne" which is the Lord God (Revelation 4). John distinguish "He who was seated on the throne" from "the one who conquers" and explicitly state "I will be his God and he will be My son".

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Rather than supporting that argument that "the Son is the Almighty God", it instead refute it by showing that [the Son has a God](../human/has-a-god.md). To say "the Almighty God" has a greater God is a contradiction.

However, the passage in dispute read as follow:

> And he said to me, “These words are trustworthy and true. And the Lord, the God of the spirits of the prophets, **has sent His angel** to show His servants what must soon take place.”
> “And behold, **I am coming soon**. Blessed is the one who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book.
> I, John, am the one who heard and saw these things. And when I heard and saw them, I fell down to worship at the feet of the angel who showed them to me, but he said to me, “You must not do that! **I am a fellow servant** with you and your brothers the prophets, and with those who keep the words of this book. Worship God.”
> And he said to me, “Do not seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is near. Let the evildoer still do evil, and the filthy still be filthy, and the righteous still do right, and the holy still be holy.”
> “Behold, **I am coming soon**, bringing my recompense with me, to repay each one for what he has done. I am **the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end**.”
> Blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and that they may enter the city by the gates. Outside are the dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood.
> “**I, Jesus**, have sent my angel to testify to you about these things for the churches. I am the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star.”
>
> — Revelation 22:6-16 (ESV)

This passage is unfortunately ambiguous. It could be read as follows:

The speaker changed *without telling us*. An angel was speaking, and somewhere along the way the speaker changed to Jesus, who identifies himself as "the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last."

or

"The angel" (meaning messenger) who was "[sent by God](../human/serve-god.md)," who is "a [fellow servant](../human/serve-god.md)," and who is "coming soon" is Jesus. Nowhere in this passage does John indicate that the speaker changed. This is in line with:

> The revelation of Jesus Christ, **which God gave him** to show to His servants the things that must soon take place. — Revelation 1:1 (ESV)

The final angel that Jesus sent was specifically to the churches (Revelation 2).

## Aleph-Tav

Some symbolic readings connect Aleph-Tav with [Alpha and Omega](#alpha-and-omega), [the Word](../name/word.md), [the crucifixion](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ/crucifixion) or a spiritual marker. These proposals depend on the first and last letters of [the Hebrew alphabet](https://word.ofgod.info/symbolism/hebrew-letters), aleph and tav, which together spell את. The usual pronunciation of the direct-object marker is "et".

### Aleph-Tav as Alpha and Omega

One proposal treats the [Alpha and Omega](#alpha-and-omega) as a Greek translation of the Hebrew particle et because aleph and tav are the first and last Hebrew letters. An alphabetic comparison is possible, but **a comparison is not a translation** of the particle's meaning.

The Hebrew adjectives for first and last are רִאשׁוֹן and אַחֲרוֹן. Et is instead a [grammatical marker](#aleph-tav-a-direct-object-marker) or, in other contexts, the preposition with. Changing the alphabet does not change either function into a divine title. The Greek title's use in Revelation 1:8, 21:6-7 and 22:12-13 must be understood from [its own context](#alpha-and-omega).

### Aleph-Tav: a Direct Object Marker

The claim that et is an untranslatable mystical code overlooks its grammatical function. [Brown-Driver-Briggs identifies אֵת, Strong&#39;s 853](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/853.htm), as a *“mark of the accusative”*. In plain terms, it marks a direct object: the person or thing affected by a verb, usually when that object is definite or identified.

| Feature             | Grammatical Explanation                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hebrew form         | אֵת, often joined to the next word by a maqqef, a short linking stroke.                                    |
| Transliteration     | Et, also represented as eth in older reference works.                                                     |
| Strong's number     | 853.                                                                                                      |
| Function            | It marks a direct object rather than naming that object.                                                  |
| English translation | English normally conveys the relationship through sentence order, without a separate word for the marker. |

Et is a word with a grammatical role, even when English has no separate word corresponding to it. It is not an indefinite article like English a, and it does not identify an indirect object. [Language writer Elon Gilad](https://www.elongilad.com/en/etymology/%D7%90%D7%AA) describes modern et as a *“marker placed before a definite direct object”*. It is therefore not restricted to ancient manuscripts or absent from modern Hebrew.

#### The Same-Spelled Preposition

The same spelling also represents [אֵת, Strong&#39;s 854](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/854.htm), meaning against, among, before, along, beside, besides, has. These are homographs: words with the same written form but different functions.

A symbolic argument cannot combine their occurrences as if they were one hidden title.

The context determines which grammatical function is present. That distinction does not make the text unreliable. It prevents an argument from assigning one invented meaning to different words.

#### Aleph-Tav in Ordinary Contexts

The claim that אֵת means “Alpha and Omega” must account for its use in sentences about ordinary objects and people.

> “dipped **the robe** in the blood.” — Genesis 37:31 (ESV)

The Hebrew includes **אֶת־הַכֻּתֹּנֶת**. Here, אֶת introduces the robe that Joseph’s brothers dipped in blood.

> “David put **his hand** in his bag” — 1 Samuel 17:49 (ESV)

The Hebrew includes **אֶת־יָדוֹ**. Here, אֶת introduces David’s hand, an ordinary human body part.

> “When an ox gores **a man or a woman** to death” — Exodus 21:28 (ESV)

The Hebrew includes **אֶת־אִישׁ אוֹ אֶת־אִשָּׁה**. The same marker appears before both the man and the woman whom the ox gores.

In these passages, אֵת functions as a direct-object marker, **not as a name for Almighty God**. Its presence does not introduce a divine person into these events.

These examples establish that אֵת is **not inherently a divine title**. Consequently, its appearance elsewhere cannot establish the title “Alpha and Omega” merely because its spelling contains Hebrew’s first and last letters. Any proposed additional symbolism requires separate textual evidence.

### Aleph-Tav as the Word of God

Another proposal identifies Aleph-Tav with [the Word of God](../name/word.md). It reads the particle in Genesis 1:1 as a separate Word created before the heavens and the earth. Others treat the first and last letters as representing every letter between them, and therefore a complete or perfect word. These are symbolic interpretations, not the particle's grammatical meaning.

The Hebrew text begins:

> *בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים **אֵ֥ת** הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם **וְאֵ֥ת** הָאָֽרֶץ׃* — Genesis 1:1.

The following is an explanatory gloss, not a verbatim Bible translation:

> *(In the beginning) (created) (God) (**et**, direct-object marker) (the heavens) (and **et**, direct-object marker) (the earth).*

The verse has a marker before both the heavens and the earth. It presents them as the objects of created, not as objects created after a separate being called Et. Neither marker supplies the word then or creates an additional step in the sequence. **God did not create an "et"**.

The proposed connection with John 1 must be examined without inserting a new object into Genesis 1:1:

> *“In the beginning was the **Word**, and the **Word** was with God, and the **Word** was God. The same was in the beginning with God.”* — John 1:1-2 (KJV).

[The Greek noun λόγος](https://biblehub.com/greek/3056.htm), logos, means word or speech in relevant uses. It is not the Hebrew direct-object marker et. The presence of et in Genesis does not settle the interpretation of logos in John. Its normal grammatical function already explains why it appears before many different objects.

### Aleph-Tav as the Crucifixion

A pictogram reading connects an ancient cross-shaped form of tav with the [Christian cross](https://church.ofgod.info/terms/christian), and then identifies Aleph-Tav with Jesus' crucifixion. Some use that proposed identification to support [Trinitarian claims](../trinity.md) about Jesus' divine nature or [existence without a beginning](eternal.md). Even within that argument, sharing divine nature is different from being the same person as God the Father.

A resemblance between a letter shape and a cross is not the ordinary definition of a word containing that letter. The grammatical particle does not acquire the meaning crucifixion simply because tav forms part of its spelling. A symbolic application would need evidence beyond the shape of the letters.

### Aleph-Tav as a Spiritual Marker

Some symbolic readings combine proposed letter meanings into an interpretation such as *a strong or powerful leading mark, sign, signal or monument*. That describes a proposed letter-picture reading, not an established lexical definition of et. Suggested meanings include completeness, a blood covenant, God's presence or God's approval.

A search in TheWord [Bible software](https://word.ofgod.info/downloads) found 5,672 instances in the Hebrew Interpolated Study Bible. That count has not been independently verified and depends on the edition and search query.

| Proposed Universal Code | Consistent Test                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Completeness.           | Each selected occurrence needs contextual evidence for completeness; the alphabet's endpoints alone do not supply it. |
| A blood covenant.       | Each selected occurrence needs evidence of a blood covenant, rather than simply a definite object.                    |
| God's presence.         | The marker's presence does not prove a special divine appearance, and its absence does not prove God's absence.       |
| God's approval.         | A marked object can be something seen, feared, judged or opposed; the verb and context determine the relationship.    |

A universal spiritual code must explain the relevant occurrences consistently, including examples that do not support it. A limited symbolic application needs its own contextual grounds and cannot be expanded into a universal rule without evidence. Neither test requires every same-spelled word to have an identical sense. Normal differences in grammar do not make Scripture unreliable.

#### Approval and Absence

The approval proposal is especially difficult to sustain as a universal rule. A direct object can name what God the Father observes or judges without describing what he approves. The following are explanatory descriptions of the marked expressions.

| Scripture       | Marked Expression or Context                          | Why Approval Does Not Follow                                               |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Genesis 9:22    | His father's nakedness.                               | The object of seeing does not express divine approval.                     |
| Genesis 9:24    | What Noah's younger son had done to him.              | The particle does not approve the son's action.                            |
| Genesis 11:5    | The city and the tower.                               | Their being seen does not imply approval of the project.                   |
| Exodus 3:7,9    | The affliction, cry and oppression of God's people.   | Observing suffering is not approving the oppression.                       |
| Judges 1:4,5,21 | Canaanites, Perizzites and Jebusites.                 | A group named as an object is not thereby approved.                        |
| Malachi 2:2     | The people's blessings, which God threatens to curse. | Marking the object of a curse does not make the particle an approval sign. |

Genesis 9:23 does not place et before the names Shem and Japheth, but it marks the garment and their father's nakedness within the account. The changed sentence structure does not establish the withdrawal of God's approval from Noah, Shem, Japheth or Ham. Likewise, an unmarked name does not prove God's absence. The narrative's statements and actions must establish approval or disapproval, not the marker's presence or absence alone.

## Scriptures Containing the Aleph-Tav

Some proposals identify what follows et as Jesus, what he created or a sign of his promised ancestry. The following categories retain those tests while distinguishing direct objects, same-spelled prepositions and naming expressions. The table descriptions explain the contexts; they are not verbatim Bible quotations.

### Animals

One proposed identity reading treats what follows et as a disclosure of Jesus in the Old Testament.
Applied without a contextual limit, that reading also encounters animals.

| Scripture         | Marked Object                                               |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Genesis 1:25      | The livestock according to their kinds.                     |
| Genesis 1:25      | Everything that creeps on the ground according to its kind. |
| Genesis 8:1       | The wild animals and livestock remembered alongside Noah.   |
| Genesis 8:7       | The raven sent out by Noah.                                 |
| Genesis 8:8,10,12 | The dove sent out by Noah.                                  |

These are objects of the relevant verbs, not definitions of et or identifications of Jesus as an animal.

An alternative proposal treats them as things created through the Word, but that wider claim must also account for the other kinds of objects.

### Creation

A creation reading treats et as identifying things created by or through the Word.
Many marked objects are indeed created things, but their presence as direct objects does not establish that extra meaning.

| Scripture                            | Marked Object                                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Genesis 1:1                          | The heavens and the earth, each with its own marker.                     |
| Genesis 1:28, 6:12, 9:1; Malachi 4:6 | The earth, in contexts of filling, seeing or striking it.                |
| Genesis 1:4                          | The light.                                                               |
| Genesis 1:7                          | The firmament.                                                           |
| Genesis 1:16                         | The two great lights.                                                    |
| Genesis 1:21                         | The great sea creatures, living creatures and winged birds.              |
| Genesis 7:4,23                       | Every living thing to be removed from the ground.                        |
| Genesis 8:1                          | The animals remembered with Noah.                                        |
| Genesis 1:22                         | The waters in the seas.                                                  |
| Genesis 1:25                         | The livestock and everything creeping on the ground.                     |
| Genesis 1:27, 2:7,8,15               | Man, in the accounts of creation, formation and placement.               |
| Genesis 1:29                         | Seed-bearing plants and fruit trees.                                     |
| Genesis 1:30, 3:18                   | Green plants or plants of the field, in their respective food contexts.  |
| Genesis 1:31                         | Everything God had made.                                                 |
| Genesis 9:3                          | Everything given for food, not the earlier comparison with green plants. |

The decisive question is what function the particle has in each sentence, not whether a listed object happens to be created.

#### Objects Beyond Creation

The creation proposal becomes too broad when it treats every marked object as something created by the supposed Aleph-Tav.
A verb can instead describe fearing God, dishonouring his name, bringing an offering or receiving a commandment.

| Scripture      | Grammatical Object                                   | Significance                                                                             |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exodus 1:17,21 | God, whom the midwives feared.                       | This is a genuine direct-object use, but God the Father is not a created object.         |
| Malachi 1:6    | God's name, in the question about despising it.      | The object concerns an attitude towards God's name, not an act of creation.              |
| Malachi 1:13   | The lame and sick animals, and the offering brought. | These objects belong to a dispute about unacceptable offerings.                          |
| Malachi 2:4    | This commandment.                                    | The marked object is the command sent; the covenant with Levi uses the preposition with. |
| Malachi 2:9    | The priests addressed as you.                        | The object concerns their public humiliation, not a hidden creation statement.           |

The [approval examples](#approval-and-absence) also include nakedness, oppression, hostile groups and the city and tower.

### Women

A lineage proposal explains marked women as ancestors of Jesus, with Eve in Genesis 4:1 as an example. However, the grammatical marker also appears before other women, and its presence does not establish their place in a messianic genealogy.

| Scripture      | Marked Woman or Relationship | Contextual Limit                                                                                  |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Genesis 2:24   | A man's mother.              | This is a general statement about family relationships, not a named messianic ancestor.           |
| Genesis 4:17   | Cain's wife.                 | The account belongs to Cain's branch, not the line through Seth.                                  |
| Genesis 6:2    | The daughters of mankind.    | Their beauty is stated; their individual ancestry and morality are not established by the marker. |
| Exodus 2:1     | A daughter of Levi.          | The context concerns Moses' parents, not a claim that et marks Jesus' ancestry.                   |
| Exodus 2:8     | The child's mother.          | The woman is Moses' mother, called to nurse him.                                                  |
| Judges 1:12,13 | Caleb's daughter Achsah.     | The account concerns a marriage promise; the marker does not establish messianic ancestry.        |

The point is not that every woman listed has been proved unrelated to Jesus. The point is that et supplies no genealogical proof. Family relationships must be established from the narrative and genealogies themselves.

Two entries sometimes placed in a women's list belong elsewhere. Exodus 2:9 marks the child and the woman's wages, not a woman. Exodus 2:20 marks the man whom the daughters left behind, meaning Moses, not Zipporah. These examples reinforce the grammatical explanation rather than a female-lineage code.

### Promise of Christ

Another proposal treats et as the promise of the coming Christ or as a marker of his ancestors. The distinction matters: marking an ancestor would not mean that the ancestor is personally the Messiah. Several examples do occur in the ancestral line associated with Jesus.

| Scripture              | Marked Person |
| ---------------------- | ------------- |
| Genesis 4:1            | Eve           |
| Genesis 5:4            | Seth          |
| Genesis 5:6,7          | Enosh         |
| Genesis 5:9,10         | Cainan        |
| Genesis 5:12           | Mahalalel     |
| Genesis 5:15,16        | Jared         |
| Genesis 5:18,19        | Enoch         |
| Genesis 5:21,22        | Methuselah    |
| Genesis 5:25,26        | Lamech        |
| Genesis 5:30, 7:9, 9:1 | Noah          |

These valid ancestral examples do not make every occurrence genealogical evidence. A universal lineage code must also account for separate family branches, groups and objects.

#### Death and Personal Identity

A stronger identity claim would make each marked person the Messiah blood-line, however in the following examples died out before the Messiah was born.

| Scripture      | Marked Person or Object                     | What the Example Tests                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Genesis 4:2    | Abel and the description Cain's brother.    | Abel died childless.                                |
| Genesis 4:11   | The ground's mouth and the brother's blood. | Referring to the blood of Abel.                     |
| Exodus 2:12,14 | The Egyptian whom Moses killed.             | Jesus is not a descendant of the murdered Egyptian. |
| Judges 1:10    | Sheshai, Ahiman, Talmai                     | Defeated enemies of the Israelites.                 |

#### Other Branches and Objects

A universal ancestry code also encounters Cain's separate branch and expressions that are not individual ancestors at all.
These must be distinguished from the valid examples of Seth and Enosh.

| Scripture          | Marked Person or Object        | Classification                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Genesis 4:1        | Cain.                          | A separate branch from Seth's line.                                                    |
| Genesis 4:17       | Cain's wife.                   | The same separate branch.                                                              |
| Genesis 4:18       | Irad.                          | Cain's line.                                                                           |
| Genesis 4:18       | Mehujael.                      | Cain's line.                                                                           |
| Genesis 4:18       | Methushael.                    | Cain's line.                                                                           |
| Genesis 4:18       | Lamech.                        | Cain's line, not the Lamech of Genesis 5:25,26.                                        |
| Genesis 4:22       | Tubal-cain.                    | Cain's line.                                                                           |
| Genesis 5:32, 6:10 | Ham.                           | A son of Noah in a different ancestral branch; the curse in Genesis 9:25 names Canaan. |
| Genesis 10:8       | Nimrod.                        | A descendant of Cush, in Ham's branch.                                                 |
| Genesis 10:11      | Nineveh.                       | A city, not a person in a messianic genealogy.                                         |
| Exodus 3:20        | Egypt.                         | The object of striking.                                                                |
| Exodus 3:22        | Egypt.                         | The object of plundering.                                                              |
| Judges 1:4,5       | The Canaanites and Perizzites. | Peoples named as objects, not individual messianic ancestors.                          |

The marker therefore does not consistently select only Jesus' ancestors. Its grammatical function explains both the ancestral examples and the examples outside that line without inventing a different spiritual code for each difficulty. A limited symbolic reading may be proposed separately, but the particle alone cannot establish it.

## Conclusion

1. **First and Last does not inherently mean Almighty or beginningless.** The [Hebrew terms and their completeness imagery](#hebrew-meanings) depend on context. [Royal records](#royal-comparisons) apply them to deeds, while [Greek human uses](#greek-meanings-and-human-uses) concern time or rank; neither makes ordinary people bearers of divine titles.
2. **Alpha and Omega requires contextual interpretation.** The alphabet's endpoints do not define Almighty God; Revelation identifies speakers through surrounding statements. Even granting Jesus the title in Revelation 22:13, [shared wording alone](#alphabetic-meaning-and-speakers) does not establish that he shares God the Father's identity or uncreated nature.
3. **Aleph-Tav is not intrinsically a divine name.** In the passages examined, [et marks a direct object rather than naming it](#aleph-tav-a-direct-object-marker). Its [ordinary uses](#aleph-tav-in-ordinary-contexts) do not introduce a divine person.
4. **Alphabetic resemblance is not translation.** The [Aleph-Tav comparison with Alpha and Omega](#aleph-tav-as-alpha-and-omega) does not change et's grammatical meaning. In [Genesis 1:1](#aleph-tav-as-the-word-of-god), it marks the heavens and earth, not a separately created Et, and it is not the Greek word logos.
5. **Et does not encode a universal spiritual classification.** It can precede [God the Father as the object of fear](#objects-beyond-creation), as well as [people outside the messianic lineage and ordinary objects](#other-branches-and-objects). Its [presence or absence](#approval-and-absence) does not itself establish divine approval or presence.

These [contextual](#alphabetic-meaning-and-speakers) and [grammatical distinctions](#aleph-tav-a-direct-object-marker) support a limited conclusion: **First and Last, Alpha and Omega, and Aleph-Tav are not sufficient proof by themselves that Jesus is Almighty God.**
