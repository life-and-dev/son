# Did Jesus Exist Before His Birth?

Did Jesus live before his birth, or did God the Father’s promise exist before he did? The question concerns whether Jesus was conscious before his human birth, not simply his importance or authority.

Several passages appear to describe an earlier heavenly life. Others present a promised human descendant whose coming was planned beforehand. A fair judgement must consider both patterns without confusing prior existence with deity. Scripture summaries below are paraphrases.

Conscious pre-existence means a conscious Jesus living before his human birth. Evidence this are:

- John 1:1-18 places the Word with God at creation before becoming flesh. This supports [continuity of a personal subject](#john-11-18-the-word-becoming-flesh).
- [John 3:13](#john-313-descended-from-heaven), 6:38-62, 16:28 connect descent from heaven with departure to God the Father. This suggests [a heavenly origin and return](#john-638-62-come-down-from-heaven).
- John 8:58 places Jesus before Abraham in language that naturally suggests [conscious temporal priority addressed in a different article](claims/i-am.md).
- John 17:5,24 describes glory and love before the world. This supports [an earlier relationship with God the Father](#john-175-glory-before-the-world).
- Various other scriptures implying [Jesus is the Creator addressed in a different article](creator.md).

Existence in foreknowledge means a future Messiah already known and appointed by God the Father, without an earlier conscious life. Foreordination means appointment beforehand. Evidence this are:

- Matthew 1:1,18-20 and Romans 1:3-4 locate Jesus within human descent and birth. This directly supports [a genuinely human Messiah](#birth-and-human-sonship).
- Romans 5:12-19 and 1 Corinthians 15:21,45-49 present Jesus as Adam’s human counterpart, with [heavenly life understood through resurrection](#resurrection-exaltation-and-dependence).
- John 17:3, 20:17 and 1 Corinthians 15:24-28 distinguish Jesus from his God. This supports [continuing dependence rather than independent deity](#resurrection-exaltation-and-dependence).
- Ephesians 1:4; 2 Timothy 1:9; 1 Peter 1:20 describe prior choice, grace and foreknowledge. They establish [purpose before personal manifestation](#foreknowledge-without-a-prior-human-life).

Does [the Bible](https://word.ofgod.info/downloads) contradict itself?

## Examining Conscious Pre-Existence

Some Unitarians, who identify God as one personal being, accept Jesus’ prior conscious existence. Even if the pre-existance of Jesus could be proven, it would still not proof [modalism](../../modalism.md), [partialism](../../partialism.md), [subordinationism](../../subordinationism.md) or [trinitarianism](../../trinity.md). For example Mormons and Jehova Witnesses also belief in the pre-existance of Jesus, yet they do not associate with all these mentioned groups. Debating whether Jesus eternally pre-existed or was created at some point in time is a different topic not addressed by this article.

Let's examamine the evidence for concious pre-existance:

### John 1:1-18 (The Word Becoming Flesh)

Many Christians define ["the Word" in John 1 as Jesus](../../name/word.md) to proof his pre-existance and participates in creation before he becames flesh. The sequence can describe one conscious subject throughout.

The problem with that approach is that:

- It requires a redefinition of the word "Word". Nowhere else, not even in the book of John does "Word" mean Jesus.
- It requires an explanation of multiple paradoxes it create in John 1. For example:
  - In John 1:1 the same "Word" was simultanously with God and was God Himself.
  - God was never seen, yet Jesus was seen (John 1:18).
- It requires an explanation of how God could be His own Son and be born (John 1:18) as a human without being an alien or avatar. No human pre-existed or can give birth to himself.
- It requires an explanation of why John would use riddles to testify that Jesus is God instead of just plainly say so. John said that this is supposed to be a "witness" (John 1:34) not a clever riddle.
- The divinity of Jesus conflicts with his conclusion at then end of John's book: "these have been written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing you may have life in His name." Neither "Christ" nor "the Son" means God. This is not a figure of speech, because it requires "that believing you may have life", implying this is a salvation issue.

If we keep [the original meaning of "word"](../../name/word.md), as it is used in the rest of John and the Bible, which means "utterance, promise or plan", then the Word is God the Father’s effective utterance, promise and plan, fulfilled in Jesus. Genesis 1; Psalm 33:6,9; Isaiah 55:11 provide the background: God the Father creates and accomplishes His purposes through speech. Describing that speech personally need not make it a second conscious being.

On this reading, the Word becoming flesh means that God kept His promise. In other words, what was said before, became reality (flesh). Peter explained to unbelievers:

> “Men of Israel, listen to these words: Jesus the Nazarene, **a man** attested to you by God with miracles and wonders and signs which God did through Him in your midst, just as you yourselves know— this Man, delivered over **by the predetermined plan and foreknowledge of God**, you nailed to a cross by the hands of lawless men and put Him to death. — Acts 2:22-23 (LSB)

### John 3:13 (Descended from Heaven)

Short version:

> No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of Man. — John 3:13 (ESV)

Longer version:

> No one has ascended to heaven but He who came down from heaven, that is, the Son of Man *who is in heaven*. — John 3:13 (NKJV)

This is a textual variation. The longer reading of John 3:13 includes “who is in heaven,” but several early Greek manuscripts, including P66 and P75, omit this clause, as documented in the [NET Bible’s textual note](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203%3A13&version=NET). The clause also appears in the [Latin text of Novatian’s On the Trinity, chapter 13](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/novatian.html), a work [dated to the third century](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/anf05/anf05.vi.ii.html) on which Jerome based his Vulgate which was used to translate the KJV and NKJV translations.

While it is true that the disciples witnessed Jesus been taken up in heaven (Acts 1:9), it significantly changes the meaning of the first occurrence of "heaven" in the same sentence.

If the longer reading is correct, then it means the first occurrence of "heaven" should have the same meaning because John did not qualify that they have different meanings. Therefore, if the last instace of "heaven" means a physical location, then the first instance should also mean a physical location. Since there is no record that Jesus had been in heaven at that point in his life, the logical explanation is that he *must have pre-existed in a literal "heavenly realm"*.

However, if the shorter reading is correct, then "coming down from heaven" is most likely an [idiom which means "send by God"](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/heaven#idiomatic-usage) and do not support the pre-existance claim. Jesus said multiple times directly that he was [send by God](../human/serve-god.md), but nowhere in Scripture does Jesus say directly that he pre-existed or that he literally came down from heaven.

The context is Nicodemus, a Pharisee, asking questions to Jesus about the Kingdom of God. From the conversation we can see that Nicodemus did not understand the concept of being "born again in the Spirit". Jesus replied that if Nicodemus does not even accept their plain (earthly) testimonies, it would be useless to explain of how spiritual (heavenly) things work. Then **the narrator**, not Jesus, adds a comment "No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven" which qualifies Jesus as someone who would have been able to answer Nicodemus. Jesus did not avoid the answer because he was uncertain.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Some bible translations put John 3:13 in red text to imply that this was part of Jesus' dialogue. However, here the narrator refer to "the son of man" as another person.

Then the narrator explains what he meant by "ascended into heaven" as an example by referring to "Moses who lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (John 3:14). What does that mean?

> whoever believes in him may have eternal life — John 3:14 (ESV)

Believing that Jesus testimonies are true, means to "lift up" the "Son of Man" to receive salvation, just as the Israelites were saved when Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness.

### John 6:38-62 (Come Down From Heaven)

In John 6:38-62 it seems like Jesus speaks of his own descent, not merely a message sent to him.

> For I have **come down from heaven**, not to do my own will but the will of **Him Who sent me**. — John 6:38 (ESV)

[As mentioned before](#john-313-descended-from-heaven), "come down from heaven" is an idiom which means "send by God". The context support this view:

> Jesus said to them, “**I am the bread** of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst. But I said to you that you have seen me and yet do not believe. All that **the Father gives** me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out. For **I have come down from heaven**, not to do my own will but the will of **Him Who sent me**. And this is the will of **Him Who sent me**, that I should lose nothing of all that **He has given me**, but raise it up on the last day. — John 6:35-39 (ESV)

Here Jesus was addressing his audience using idioms or figures of speach. Nobody though that Jesus was a literal bread. He makes it clear that the authority he has was given by the Father and with this authority. Then he explains that "come down from heaven" means to be "sent by Him" (the Father) for our benefit.

### John 17:5 (Glory Before the World)

This verse has been used to prove that Jesus is God because of the phrase, “that I had with you before the world was.” There is no question that Jesus “existed” before the world began.

> “that **I had with you** before the world existed” — John 17:5 (ESV)

God had planned to glorify His Son, and now that the time of Jesus’ death was drawing near, Jesus prayed that God would bring His plan to fruition.

Both Christ and those called to be in [the Body of Christ](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ/body), **existed in God’s foreknowledge before being conciously alive**. Christ was [the “logos,” the “plan”](../name/word.md) of God from the beginning, and [that plan became flesh](../name/word.md) only when he was conceived.

It is [Trinitarian](../trinity.md) bias that causes people to read an actual physical existence into this verse rather than a figurative existence in the mind of God. When 2 Timothy 1:9 says that each Christian was given grace “before the beginning of time”, no one tries to prove that we were actually alive with God back then. Everyone acknowledges that we existed in God’s foreknowledge. The same is true of Jesus Christ. His glory was “with the Father” before the world began, and in John 17:5 he prayed that it will come into manifestation.

The immediate context shows that Jesus was speaking of being in God’s foreknowledge. Just two verses earlier, in John 17:3, Jesus said that the Father was **“the only true God.”** **Jesus could not have prayed that while at the same being God too**.

Furthermore, Jesus spoke again about things in God’s foreknowledge in John 17:22 when he said that he had given the glory from God to his disciples. But that had not happened yet either. Both the glory of Jesus and the glory that his disciples would have was in the foreknowledge of God, and Jesus prayed about it in his prayer.

The proper interpretation of John 17 is simple and biblical. Jesus knew he was the promised Messiah and Son of God, and God had spoken of his glory many centuries earlier. Now, on the eve of his arrest, he prayed to his Father, the “only true God,” and asked for God’s plan to glorify His Son to come to pass.

## The Human Messiah in God’s Purpose

The greatest challenge with a concious pre-existing Jesus, is that it means he had to be an alien. No human conciously pre-existed before his birth. This would put Jesus in the category an avatar or shape-shifter.

Conscious-pre-existence interpretations can read the promises and birth accounts as describing the Son’s genuinely human life, while foreknowledge concerns someone already living. Deity interpretations must explain dependence within that human life and the Son’s relationship to God the Father. A created, subordinate Son can affirm dependence without claiming Almighty deity. The response below therefore distinguishes a case against Almighty deity from a judgement about personal origin; neither humanity nor foreknowledge alone decides the latter.

### Birth and Human Sonship

Matthew 1:1 locates Jesus within Abraham’s and David’s family; Matthew 1:18-20 describes conception through the Holy Spirit. The origin narrative presents a human life brought into being through divine action. Romans 1:3-4 connects Davidic descent with resurrection designation as Son of God in power.

Luke 1:35 corroborates this by linking the child’s designation as Son of God with divine action in conception.

Romans 8:3 and Galatians 4:4 describe God the Father sending his Son in human flesh, born of woman and under the law.

Romans 5:12-19 sets Jesus beside Adam as a human representative whose obedience answers disobedience. 1 Timothy 2:5 explicitly identifies the mediator as **the man** Christ Jesus; Hebrews 2:14-17 corroborates shared flesh, blood and human solidarity. This is genuine human life, not a nonhuman disguise. Believers in prior conscious existence can also affirm it, so humanity alone cannot disprove their view.

### Resurrection, Exaltation and Dependence

Acts 2:22,30-36 presents a divinely attested man, David’s heir, who dies, is raised and is made Lord and Messiah. Acts 10:38 describes God the Father empowering Jesus with the Holy Spirit; Acts 17:31 identifies the appointed judge as a man whose resurrection guarantees his role. The movement from human life to exalted authority is explicit.

1 Corinthians 15:21,45-49 presents the last Adam and the second man from heaven within an argument about resurrection bodies. The natural-before-spiritual sequence concerns that transformation. It is not a universal rule excluding all earlier heavenly life. Equally, the heavenly man of 1 Corinthians 15:47 can substantially describe the risen Messiah, without supplying a biography of a prior spirit-person.

John 17:3 identifies God the Father as the only true God and Jesus as his sent Messiah. In John 20:17 the risen Jesus still calls him his God. John 5:19,30 describes the Son’s inability to act independently. First Corinthians 11:3 and 15:24-28 continue the pattern of headship and final subjection beyond earthly weakness.

### Foreknowledge Without a Prior Human Life

2 Timothy 1:9 provides a particularly strong comparison. Grace is described as given to believers in Christ before the ages, although those beneficiaries did not need to exist personally then. A gift can be spoken of as already theirs because God the Father has determined it.

Ephesians 1:4 similarly places believers’ choice in Christ before the world. Romans 8:29-30 connects foreknowledge and appointment with glorification spoken of as accomplished. 1 Peter 1:20 distinguishes Christ being foreknown before the world from his later manifestation. Matthew 25:34 describes a kingdom prepared for later heirs. These are demonstrable patterns of prior purpose and subsequent fulfilment.

## Conclusion

Jesus did not live as a conscious person before his human birth. John’s [word](../name/word.md) can be understood as God’s purpose fulfilled in Jesus, while the descent-from-heaven language can describe the mission God sent him to carry out as seen in the sections that discuss [John 1:1-18](#john-11-18-the-word-becoming-flesh), [John 3:13](#john-313-descended-from-heaven) and [John 6:38-62](#john-638-62-come-down-from-heaven). Jesus’ glory before the world can likewise be understood as held in God’s purpose, just as Scripture speaks of grace and believers as [chosen beforehand](#john-175-glory-before-the-world) or [foreknowledge without a prior human life](#foreknowledge-without-a-prior-human-life).

This reading fits the promised descendant who is born as a human, obeys God, and is later raised and exalted by Him ([birth and human sonship](#birth-and-human-sonship); [resurrection, exaltation and dependence](#resurrection-exaltation-and-dependence)). These passages do not make foreknowledge alone a proof that no earlier conscious life was possible. Rather, taken together, they support the conclusion that Jesus’ personal life began with his human birth and that the earlier language speaks of God’s promise, purpose, and Messiah’s later exaltation.
