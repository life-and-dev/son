# 2 Peter 1:1 (Our God and Saviour Jesus Christ)

> Simeon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus standin
>
> To those who have obtained a faith of equal standing with ours by the righteousness of **our *God* and Savior Jesus Christ**
>
> — 2 Peter 1:1 (ESV)

The strongest [Trinitarian](../../trinity.md) reading takes “God and Savior Jesus Christ” as one referent and therefore understands Peter to call Jesus God.

## What the Grammar Establishes

Many interpreters support that reading from the Greek article before “God” and the two singular personal nouns joined by “and.”

Daniel B. Wallace, *Greek Grammar Beyond the Basics*, discusses the construction commonly called Granville Sharp’s rule and its application to these texts. Its strict scope concerns singular, personal, non-proper nouns joined by *kai* under one article.

The Greek construction strongly connects the two titles to Jesus in this verse. 2 Peter 1:11 uses the same article-and-coordination pattern for “our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ”, where both titles plainly refer to Jesus:

> “for in this way there will be richly provided for you an entrance into the eternal kingdom of **our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ**.” — 2 Peter 1:11 (ESV)

Within that scope, the grammar gives a strong single-referent case for “God and Savior” in 2 Peter 1:1.

## Textual Variation

The Codex Sinaiticus, [ℵ(01) reads “Lord”](https://corebible.app/2PE/1?verseNumber=%2228%22&translation=AICNT) [instead of *“God”*](https://greeknewtestament.net/2pe1-1). This is also confirmed by the Aramaic manuscript as seen in the Aramaic Bible in Plain English:

> Shimeon Petraus, a Servant and an Apostle of Yeshua The Messiah to those who, equal in honor with us, were worthy for the faith by the righteousness of **our Lord and our savior Yeshua the Messiah**.

## Meaning of God

Even if "God" is was the original text, we need to acknowledge that [the meaning of "God"](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/god) in Greek and Hebrew is not the same as in English. Israel’s Scriptures sometimes give divine language to authorised representatives without confusing them with the LORD.

> Then the LORD said to Moses, “See, I make you *[as]* God to Pharaoh, and your brother Aaron shall be your prophet. — Exodus 7:1 (NAS95)

Jesus himself quote from the Greek New Testament, the Hebrew Old Testament and explain that human kings may also be called "gods":

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law,
>
>> I said, you are gods?
>
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came⁠ — and Scripture cannot be broken⁠
>
> — John 10:34-35 (ESV)

That precedent does not make every use of “God” figurative, nor does it make Peter’s direct title unimportant. It shows why a title must be read with context rather than treated as an automatic definition of shared identity or a complete doctrine of the Trinity.

## Context

In 2 Peter, Jesus remains “Lord”, “Savior”, and the beloved Son who receives honour from the Father. In the next verse Peter immediately distinguish between God and Jesus:

> May grace and peace be multiplied to you in the knowledge of God **and of** Jesus our Lord. — 2 Peter 1:2 (ESV)

Matthew recorded that Peter did not belief that Christ is God Himself, when Peter identified Jesus:

> Now when Jesus came into the district of Caesarea Philippi,
>
> he asked his disciples, “Who do people say that the Son of Man is?”
> And they said, “Some say John the Baptist, others say Elijah, and others Jeremiah or one of the prophets.”
> He said to them, “But who do you say that I am?”
> Simon Peter replied, “You are **the Christ, the Son of the living God.**”
> And Jesus answered him, “Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah! For flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven.
>
> — Matthew 16:13-17 (ESV)

Peter’s foundational confession identifies Jesus as the Messiah (Christ) and as [the Son **of** God](../../index.md). [“Christ” or Messiah means God’s anointed king.](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ) The wording honours Jesus while distinguishing him from the living God whose son he is.

In Peter's previous book it was written that Peter also acknowledge that Jesus had a God:

> Blessed be **the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ**! According to His great mercy, He has caused us to be born again to a living hope through **the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead**... — 1 Peter 1:3 (ESV)

God resurrected "Jesus Christ from the dead". An immortal God does not die.

Peter does not present “God” and “Jesus” as interchangeable names. He commonly coordinates them while retaining their distinction. For example:

> For when he received honor and glory **from God the Father**, and **the voice was borne to him** by the Majestic Glory, “This is **My beloved son**, with whom I am well pleased,” **we ourselves heard this very voice borne from heaven, for we were with him on the holy mountain**. — 2 Peter 1:17-18 (ESV)

The apostles testify that they heard a different voice from God the Father, while being with Jesus on the holy mountain. The voice itself identified "My beloved son" as someone else.

## Closing

It is expected that the introduction and closing of a book should complement each other instead of contradicting. 2 Peter closes with the words:

> But grow in the grace and knowledge of **our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ**. To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity. Amen. — 2 Peter 3:18 (ESV)

This too favours the "Lord" [textual variant](#textual-variation).
