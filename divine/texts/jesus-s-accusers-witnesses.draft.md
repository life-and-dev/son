## John 5:18

After Jesus calls God his own Father, John explains why some opponents sought to kill him: they understood him as “making himself equal with God” (John 5:18). This is the narrator’s explanation, not merely hostile speech, so it is serious evidence for a high-Christology reading. Jesus’ answer says that the Son does what he sees the Father doing and receives life, judgement, and authority from the Father (John 5:19-27).

The incarnation and two-natures reading can understand that dependence within the Son’s incarnate mission while retaining shared divine identity. The Unitarian reply observes that the passage itself repeatedly presents the Father as giver and the Son as recipient. *The text establishes an unparalleled unity of work and honour. It does not expressly identify Jesus as the Father or define the one God as more than the Father.*

### John 10 and Psalm 82

Some opponents say to Jesus, “you, being a man, make yourself God” (John 10:33), after his statement that he and the Father are one (John 10:30). Their words are hostile accusation, but the narrative neither makes them a neutral doctrinal definition nor permits readers simply to discard them. Jesus answers by citing Psalm 82:6 and then states his own claim: “I am the Son of God” (John 10:34-36).

> “I said, ‘You are **gods**, sons of the Most High, all of you.’”  
> Psalm 82:6 (ESV)

The psalm’s precise referent is debated. Jesus’ immediate argument is clear enough: people who receive God’s word can bear elevated language without becoming the one God. He describes himself as the one whom the Father consecrated and sent, then returns to mutual indwelling language in John 10:37-38. The Trinitarian reading sees that unity, unique sonship, and works as a coherent implicit deity claim. *The Unitarian reply sees authorised agency and unique mission, while admitting that Psalm 82 does not by itself explain away John’s stronger claims about Jesus.*

## The Trial Charge and Israel’s Scriptures

At the trial, the high priest asks Jesus whether he is “the Christ, the Son of God” (Matthew 26:63; Mark 14:61). Matthew 26:64 records Jesus saying that his hearers will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven. Mark 14:62 records the same two motifs after Jesus answers, “I am.” The seated-at-the-right-hand language evokes Psalm 110:1, and the cloud-coming Son of Man language evokes Daniel 7:13-14.

> “The **LORD** says to my **Lord**: ‘Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool.’”  
> Psalm 110:1 (ESV)

> “And to him was **given dominion** and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should **serve him**.”  
> Daniel 7:14 (ESV)

The council treats Jesus’ answer as blasphemy. This is not an ordinary political claim. The strongest Trinitarian reading argues that the Danielic cloud-coming language and Psalm 110’s enthronement evoke divine prerogative, while incarnation and two natures account for distinction from the Father.

The Unitarian reply follows the direction of the texts alluded to. Daniel’s Son of Man receives dominion from the Ancient of Days; Psalm 110’s lord sits at the LORD’s right hand. *Jesus claims the Messiah’s supreme, future authority, but the cited Scriptures distinguish the recipient of authority from God who grants it.* The trial accounts do not record Jesus saying that he is God in flesh. Luke’s confirming account preserves the council’s questions about whether he is the Messiah and Son of God (Luke 22:67-71).

## Conclusion

John’s narrator supplies a serious [charge of equality with God](#john-518), while Jesus’ reply to the hostile charge requires [Psalm 82 and his mission](#john-10-and-psalm-82) to be read together. The trial adds an exalted [Son of Man claim](#the-trial-charge-and-israels-scriptures). *These texts support a real Father-Son distinction, but a Unitarian conclusion must answer their cumulative high-Christology force rather than dismiss accusers’ words wholesale.* See also [John’s Witness](john-s-witness.draft.md#the-word-and-john-118).
