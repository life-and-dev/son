# Jude 1:5 (Jesus Saved People Out Of Egypt)

> ... that ***Jesus***, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt... — Jude 1:5 (ESV), also NTL & BLB

This is because the Codex Vaticanus (B), Codex Alexandrinus (A) and Codex Sinaiticus render "Jesus".

However, other manuscripts like Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus (C) and Majority Byzantine render "the LORD" and the Minor Latin "God". Therefore, some bible translations render:

> ... that **the Lord**, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt... — Jude 1:5 (KJV), also NIV

The reason why the latter textual variant is more likely correct, is because [every other passage just says "the LORD"](https://youtu.be/inKyhLUpbk4?si=-LX0caWFThT5jwRi).

> And on that very day **the LORD** brought the people of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their hosts. — Exodus 12:51 (ESV)

> “I am **the LORD your God**, Who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. — Exodus 20:2 (ESV)

> For I am **the LORD** Who brought you up out of the land of Egypt to be your God. You shall therefore be holy, for I am holy.” — Leviticus 11:45 (ESV)

If Jude used the Old Testament as his source, he would have used the name "the LORD".

In the book "A Dictionary of Eartly Christian Beliefs" by David W. Bercot, it mention on page 384 that Clement of Alexandria was aware of Jude:

> Jude, who wrote the catholic epistle... — (c. 195, E), 2.573"

And Origin was also aware of Jude:

> Jude wrote a letter... — (c. 245, E) 9.424

This proof they had access to Jude.

People like Origin and Justin Martyr theorized that Jesus was the Angel of the LORD:

> ... He who is called God and appeared to the patriarchs is distinct from God the Father, and is called both Angel and Lord, because he brings messages to men from the Father." — Dialogue with Trypho, ch. 59

If their version of Jude really rendered "Jesus saved people out of Egypt", they would have most certainly cited Jude 1:5, yet they did not. Which shows their version of Jude did not support their view.
