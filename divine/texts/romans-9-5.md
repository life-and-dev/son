# Romans 9:5

> To them belong the patriarchs, and from their race, according to the flesh, is **the Christ, who is God** over all, blessed forever. Amen. — Romans 9:5 (ESV)

This scripture is used by [Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) to proof that *Christ is ~~who~~ God over all*. The Greek phrase "ὁ ὢν ἐπὶ πάντων θεὸς" translates to “who is God over all”. The relative participle can naturally continue the description of Christ. This [Christological](../../dual-nature.md) reading is grammatically possible and is supported by many modern English Bible translations like the NIV, NASB, CSB, NET.

Ancient Greek manuscripts did not provide the modern punctuation that decides whether this is a description of Christ or an independent doxology to God. Paul’s usual doxological patterns and his distinction between God and Christ make a break after “Christ” a live reading. The referent of “God over all” is genuinely disputed. For example compare it with literal translations like:

> whose are the fathers, and of whom is the Christ, according to the flesh, who is over all, **God blessed to the ages**. Amen — Romans 9:5 (YLT)

> To them belong the Fathers, and from them, according to the flesh, is the Christ. **(God, who is over all, be blessed forever. Amen.)** — Romans 9:5 (REV)

Other English Bible translations that support the alternative rendering:

> To them belong the patriarchs, and from them, according to His natural descent, came the Christ (the Messiah, the Anointed), He who is exalted and supreme over all, **God blessed forever**. Amen. — Romans 9:5 (AMP)

> whose are the fathers, and from whom is the Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, **God blessed forever**. Amen. — Romans 9:5 (ASB, NASB77, NASB95)

> whose are the fathers, and of whom is Christ as concerning the flesh, who is over all, **God blessed for ever**. Amen. — Romans 9:5 (ERV)

> Whose are the fathers, and from whom according to the flesh, Christ came, who is over all, **God blessed for ever**. Amen. — Romans 9:5 (Webster)

> they are descended from the famous Hebrew ancestors; and Christ, **as [a human being](../../human.md), belongs to their race. May God, who rules over all, be praised forever!** Amen. — Romans 9:5 (Good News)

> They have those famous ancestors, who were also the ancestors of the Christ. I pray that **God, who rules over all**, will be praised forever! Amen. — Romans 9:5 (CEV)

The second reading that Christ and God are different persons are more likely. In the context of the book of Romans, Paul opens his book with the proclamation:

> Paul, **a servant of Christ Jesus**, called to be an apostle, **set apart for the gospel of God**, which He promised beforehand through His prophets in the holy Scriptures, concerning **His son, who was descended from David according to the flesh** and was **declared** to be [the Son of God](../../index.md) in power according to the Spirit of holiness by **his resurrection from the dead**, Jesus Christ our Lord... — Romans 1:1-4 (ESV)

Paul distincts between being "a servant of Christ Jesus" and being "set apart for the gospel of God". He also shows that "His son" was the "descended from David according ot the flesh" referring to his [humanity](../../human.md). The greater God had to "declare" him. To be "resurrected", it means this human son had to die first. God is immortal.

Also notice how Paul made clear [distinctions](../../human/distinct.md) between God and Christ in the previous chapter of the disputed text:

> What then shall we say to these things?  
> If God is for us, who can be against us?  
> **He who did not spare His own son but gave him up** for us all, how will **He not also with him** graciously give us all things?  
> Who shall bring any charge against God’s elect?
>
> It is **God who justifies**. Who is to condemn? Christ **Jesus is the one who died**⁠ — more than that, who was raised⁠ — who is **at the right hand of God**, who indeed is **interceding** for us.
>
> — Romans 8:31-34 (ESV)

Paul even closes his book with the words:

> to **the only wise God** be glory forevermore **through** Jesus Christ! Amen. — Romans 16:27 (ESV)

Paul did not belief that Jesus was a *second* god, nor that Jesus was the God, because God worked "through" Jesus Christ, which also shows distinction.
