# Titus 2:13 (“our great God... Jesus”)

Titus is often quoted to proof that Jesus is “our great God” in the sense of being Almighty God himself:

> “Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of **the glory** of our great **God and Savior Jesus Christ**.” — Titus 2:13 (ESV)

The [NET Bible note on Titus 2:13](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Titus&chapter=2&verse=13) applies the Granville Sharp rule and takes “our great God **and** Savior” as referring to the one person. Because the two singular personal titles share one article and are joined by “and,” the single-referent reading has substantial grammatical force.

Even if Titus here calls Jesus “God and Savior,” that usage must be read within the letter and the wider scriptural category of representation. **Titus repeatedly distinguishes “God our Savior” from “Christ Jesus our Savior”** (Titus 1:3–4; 3:4–6). The **same saving title** applies to God as source and to Jesus as the one through whom God saves.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Original Greek manuscripts **did not contain English punctuations**.

The meaning of the same text could significantly change, depending on where the translater choose to put punctuations. For example:

> “Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of: our great God, and Savior Jesus Christ.” — Titus 2:13

This version identifies two distinct persons, namely:

1. the Father ("the great God") and
2. the Son (the "Saviour Jesus Christ").

Pauline theology generally **distinguishes between the Son and the Father. Paul also uses the same "Saviour" title for both** throughout the Pastoral Epistles.

This is inline with other Pauline letters:

> In their case the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelievers, to keep them from seeing the light of the gospel of **the glory of Christ**, who is the image of God. For what we proclaim is not ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord, with ourselves as your servants for Jesus’ sake.
>
> For God, who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of **the glory of God** in the face of Jesus Christ.
>
> — 2 Corinthians 4:4-6 (ESV)

Both God and Jesus are [glorified](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/glory):

> But he, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw **the glory of God, and Jesus** standing at the right hand of God. — Acts 7:55 (ESV)

[Glory is not exclusive to God alone (John 17:5).](../preexistance.md#john-175-glory-before-the-world) For example Jesus also prayed to his God:

> **The glory that you have given me** I have **given to them**, that they may be one even as we are one... — John 17:22 (ESV)
