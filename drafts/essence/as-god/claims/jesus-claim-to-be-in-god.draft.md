# Jesus Claims to Be in God

## Context and Response

In John 14:10-11, Jesus says that he is in the Father and the Father is in him. He explains this by saying that his words are not his own and that the Father dwelling in him does his works.

The strongest Trinitarian reading does not identify Jesus as the Father. It holds that the Father and Son are distinct persons who share one divine essence. On this reading, their mutual indwelling reveals a unity proper to divine persons, not merely agreement in purpose.

The immediate topic is Jesus’ words and works: he speaks what the Father gives him, and the Father works through him (John 14:10,24). A Unitarian reading takes this as the Father’s active presence in his sent Son. This reading is supported by Jesus’ related statement that the Father “has not left me alone, for I always do the things that are pleasing to him” (John 8:29). Mutual indwelling language is significant, but it does not itself state that distinct persons share one essence.
