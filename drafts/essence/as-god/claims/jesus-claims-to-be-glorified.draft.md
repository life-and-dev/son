# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

## Immediate Passage Context

John 17 is Jesus’ prayer to the Father immediately before his arrest. Jesus first asks the Father to glorify the Son so that the Son may glorify the Father (John 17:1). He grounds that request in authority the Father **gave** him and in the work the Father **gave** him to finish (John 17:2-4). John 17:5 therefore remains a request from the Son to the Father: “glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed.”

The prayer repeatedly preserves this distinction. Jesus calls the Father “the only true God” and identifies himself as the one whom that God sent (John 17:3). Later he says that the Father gave him the words, people, name, authority, and glory entrusted to him (John 17:2, 6-8, 22). The immediate context is not a declaration that Father and Son are the same being; it is the Son’s appeal to the Father who sent, authorized, and glorifies him.

## Strongest Fair Trinitarian Interpretation

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes John 17:5 as a claim of conscious personal pre-existence. Jesus speaks of glory he “had” with the Father before creation, not simply of a future reward. Read with John 1:1-3 and John 17:24, this naturally supports the view that the Son existed alongside the Father before the world and participated in divine glory. The language is unusually high and should not be reduced to an ordinary human claim.

## Unitarian Reply

Pre-existence, even if John 17:5 is read that way, does not by itself establish shared essence. In this same prayer, glory and authority are bestowed: the Father gives Jesus authority and glory, and Jesus gives glory to his followers (John 17:2, 22-23). Believers do not become God by receiving glory; the gift can express God’s purpose, favor, and representation without making recipients equal in essence to God.

John also presents Jesus’ glorification as bound to faithful obedience and the cross: the Father glorifies Jesus (John 12:23, 28), and God highly exalts him after his obedience unto death (Philippians 2:8-9). Thus John 17:5 can affirm an exalted, pre-worldly glory while still distinguishing the one God—the Father—from his sent and glorified Messiah. The verse is weighty evidence for a high view of Christ; it is not, on its own, a proof that Jesus shares the Father’s essence.
