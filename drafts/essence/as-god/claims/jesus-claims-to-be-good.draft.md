# Jesus Claims to Be Good

## Context and Response

In Mark 10:17-22, a man addresses Jesus as “Good Teacher” while asking about eternal life. Jesus responds, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone,” then directs him to God’s commandments and exposes his attachment to wealth.

The strongest Trinitarian reading hears Jesus inviting the man to recognise the implication of his own greeting: if Jesus is truly good, he must be God. The saying does not treat goodness lightly.

Jesus’ explicit words reserve ultimate goodness for God alone; they do not identify Jesus as that one God. His sinless obedience and faithful revelation of God show the goodness of God’s human Messiah (Hebrews 4:15; Acts 2:22). The scene distinguishes the God whose commands stand behind Jesus’ instruction from Jesus, the teacher who obeys and represents Him.
