## John 10:30 in Context

In John 10:27-30, Jesus says that no one can snatch his sheep from his hand or the Father’s hand, then says, “I and the Father are one.” The immediate subject is their united preservation of the sheep.

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes the opponents’ charge that Jesus makes himself God (John 10:33) as evidence that “one” means one divine essence. The charge shows that Jesus’ words sounded exceptionally high to his hearers.

Jesus answers by citing Psalm 82:6 and by calling himself the one whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world (John 10:34-36). He appeals to his works as the Father’s works in him, not to an assertion that he is the Father or shares His essence. In the same Gospel, Jesus prays that believers may be one as he and the Father are one (John 17:11,21-23). The Son’s unity with the Father is uniquely perfect in mission, will, and protecting power, but the passage does not require an identity of essence.
