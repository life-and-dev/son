# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of God

Jesus speaks of himself as God's Son and accepts the title from others. The question is what the title means in its biblical setting.

## Biblical background

Psalm 2:7 calls the Davidic king God's “son”: “You are my son; today I have begotten you.” It is royal, covenant language for God's anointed ruler. Second Samuel 7:14 likewise promises concerning David's royal descendant, “I will be his father, and he will be my son.” Neither passage identifies the king as God; both distinguish God from his chosen king.

Daniel 7:13 presents “one like a son of man” coming with the clouds of heaven to the Ancient of Days and receiving dominion. Jesus repeatedly uses **Son of Man** for himself, connecting his mission, suffering, vindication, and future rule with this figure. The figure receives authority from God, so the vision distinguishes him from God, the Ancient of Days.

## Jesus' claims

Jesus calls God “my Father” and speaks of the unique relationship between Father and Son. In John 10:36, he explains his claim as being “God's Son,” the one whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world. At his trial, he combines the Messiah/Son of God question with Daniel 7's Son of Man imagery (Mark 14:61–62). These claims place him above ordinary prophets: he is God's appointed Messiah, Son, and eschatological ruler.

## Trinitarian and Unitarian readings

The strongest Trinitarian reading sees Jesus' unique Sonship, his intimate knowledge of the Father, and his use of Daniel 7 as signs of an eternal divine identity. On this reading, **Son of God** is more than a royal title: it expresses the Son's unique sharing in the divine life, while **Son of Man** identifies the incarnate Son with humanity and Daniel's heavenly ruler.

The Unitarian answer is that the titles themselves do not establish that conclusion. Psalm 2 and Second Samuel 7 supply a clear royal-Messianic meaning for **Son of God**, and Daniel 7 portrays the **Son of Man** as God's exalted representative who receives authority from God. Jesus' claims can therefore be read as claims to be God's uniquely authorized human Messiah, without making him God himself.
