# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

## Context and Response

Jesus’ title “Son of Man” recalls Daniel 7:13-14, where one like a son of man receives everlasting dominion, glory, and a kingdom. Jesus applies the title to suffering, future vindication, and authority, including his appearance before the Sanhedrin (Mark 8:31; 14:62).

Most scholarly readings identify the Ancient of Days in Daniel 7 with Israel’s God, while the human-like figure is distinct from and receives dominion from the Ancient of Days. Jewish expectations in the Second Temple period were diverse; Daniel’s figure was not interpreted in one uniform way. Jesus’ use of the title may therefore evoke both Daniel’s royal vision and his human messianic mission.

Trinitarians rightly note that coming with clouds and receiving universal service are exalted claims associated with God’s rule. The same vision distinguishes the recipient from the giver. The Unitarian reading follows that distinction: God raises and exalts Jesus, and authority is given to him (Acts 2:32-36; Matthew 28:18). The Son of Man is the human Messiah whom God vindicates and appoints to rule; delegated dominion does not by itself identify him as YHVH in essence.
