# Jesus Claims to Carry Our Yoke

## Context and Response

In Matthew 11:28-30, Jesus invites the weary to take his yoke and promises rest. Immediately before this invitation, he says that the Father “has handed all things over” to him and that the Son reveals the Father (Matthew 11:27).

The strongest Trinitarian reading connects Jesus’ promised rest with YHVH’s promise of rest in Jeremiah 6:16. Jesus speaks with an authority that belongs to God’s saving work.

Matthew frames that authority as received and revelatory. Jesus does not say that he is YHVH; he calls hearers into his teaching as the Father’s authorised Son. A yoke is a teacher’s way of life, and Jesus’ yoke is gentle because he faithfully makes the Father known. He mediates God’s rest as Messiah and representative, which does not require shared essence.
