# Jesus Said “I Am”

## Context and Response

John 8:58 comes after Jesus says that Abraham rejoiced to see his day, and the crowd asks how he could have seen Abraham while not yet fifty years old (John 8:52-57). Jesus answers, “Before Abraham was, I am.”

The strongest Trinitarian reading hears an allusion to God’s self-identification in Exodus 3:14 and treats the hostile response as confirmation of a divine claim. The wording and scene make that reading understandable.

Yet John does not say that Jesus is the voice at the bush or that *egō eimi* means “I am YHWH.” The expression can be ordinary self-identification, as in John 9:9. The immediate contrast may assert Jesus’ pre-eminence in God’s purpose before Abraham rather than identify him as God. John elsewhere keeps the distinction clear: Jesus is the one sent by the Father, “the only true God” (John 17:3). Even a claim of pre-existence would not by itself establish shared essence.
