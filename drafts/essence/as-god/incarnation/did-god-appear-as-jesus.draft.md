# Did God Appear as Jesus?

Scripture calls Jesus the image of invisible God and God’s appointed Saviour. These texts must be read with their context and with the distinction they make between God and his Messiah.

## Colossians 1:15

> “He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.” — Colossians 1:15 (ESV)

An image makes another known without being identical to the one imaged. Paul also calls man God’s image and glory (1 Corinthians 11:7). Christ uniquely reveals the invisible God.

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes the image as the eternal Son made visible in human flesh. Yet Paul says that Christ is the image *of* God and that God’s fullness was pleased to dwell in him (Colossians 1:19). The language distinguishes God from his Messiah. A two-natures appeal supplies an invisible divine nature alongside visible humanity, but the passage does not make that distinction. It presents God’s perfect human representative.

# Titus 2:13

> “Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ.” — Titus 2:13 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes “our great God and Savior Jesus Christ” as one title for Jesus. The Greek construction is often argued to favour that reading, so the verse deserves more than a grammatical dismissal.

The immediate wording, however, is “the appearing of the glory” of God. Elsewhere Paul distinguishes “God our Savior” from “Christ Jesus our Savior” and greets readers from “God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior” (Titus 1:3-4). Jesus’ appearing can therefore be the public display of the glory of the one God through his appointed Messiah. Titus 2:11-14 does not explain an incarnation or say that YHVH became a man. A two-natures appeal supplies that doctrine from elsewhere; the passage itself can coherently retain Paul’s distinction between God and the Lord Jesus Christ.
