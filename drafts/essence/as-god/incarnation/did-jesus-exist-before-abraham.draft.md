# Did Jesus Exist Before Abraham?

John 8:58 contrasts Jesus with Abraham and is often used to argue for personal pre-existence. Its setting centres on Abraham, God’s promise, and the Messiah’s appointed day.

## John 8:58

> “Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am.” — John 8:58 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes Jesus’ words as a claim to personal existence before Abraham and an allusion to Exodus 3:14. The contrast between Abraham’s coming into being and Jesus’ “I am” gives that reading its force. On this view, the phrase and the attempt to stone Jesus in John 8:59 identify him with YHVH, rather than only with the promised Messiah.

The immediate exchange begins with Jesus’ claim that Abraham rejoiced to see his day (John 8:52-56). His listeners ask how he could have seen Abraham when he is not yet fifty years old. The Trinitarian argument therefore reads John 8:58 as Jesus’ direct answer: he existed before Abraham and speaks with divine identity.

The biblical-Unitarian reply accepts that the wording is stronger than a simple claim that Abraham anticipated a future Messiah. It holds, however, that the passage does not expressly say that Jesus is YHVH. Jesus had already distinguished himself from God: “my Father … is the one of whom you say, ‘He is our God’” (John 8:54). Abraham saw the Messiah’s day through God’s promise that all nations would be blessed through his offspring (Genesis 12:3; 22:18). Jesus may therefore speak from the certainty and priority of God’s purpose for the Messiah, as Scripture also places believers and grace in God’s purpose before their historical existence (Ephesians 1:4; 2 Timothy 1:9).

The Greek *egō eimi* is the first-person expression formed with [*eimi*, “to be”](https://biblehub.com/greek/1510.htm). It does not by itself reproduce Exodus 3:14, where the [Septuagint reads *egō eimi ho ōn*](https://biblehub.com/sep/exodus/3.htm) (“I am the One who is”). John also uses *egō eimi* in non-divine statements, including Jesus’ self-identification to the Samaritan woman (John 4:26) and the healed man’s identification of himself (John 9:9). The phrase alone, therefore, cannot settle the question.

Within this context, the attempted stoning shows that Jesus’ words were received as an extraordinary claim. It does not state why the hearers understood them that way, nor does it override Jesus’ distinction between himself and the one God in John 8:54. The passage supports the Messiah’s priority in God’s purpose and his God-given superiority to Abraham, but it does not require a two-natures distinction or identify Jesus as YHVH.
