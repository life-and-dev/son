# Did Jesus Have Divine Knowledge as a Child?

Jesus’ temple visit reveals unusual understanding, while Luke also records his growth in wisdom. Both parts of the account matter when considering his humanity and God-given wisdom.

## Growth in Wisdom

> “And Jesus increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man.” — Luke 2:52 (ESV)

Luke 2:49–52 presents one continuous scene. Jesus tells Mary and Joseph that he must be in his Father’s house. They do not understand what he means. He then returns with them to Nazareth, remains submissive to them, and increases in wisdom, stature, and favour with God and people.

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes Jesus’ words in Luke 2:49 as more than awareness of a prophetic calling. His distinctive reference to God as “my Father”, his certainty about where he must be, and the extraordinary understanding already displayed among the teachers are understood as evidence that the eternal Son knew his unique divine relationship even during childhood. On this view, Luke 2:52 describes growth in his human experience, while his divine nature remained omniscient.

The biblical-Unitarian reply should address the whole sequence rather than isolate Luke 2:52. Luke does present Jesus as uniquely conscious of the Father’s purpose and unusually wise. Yet he does not identify Jesus as YHVH or say that he possessed unlimited knowledge. Mary and Joseph’s failure to understand in Luke 2:50 concerns the meaning of his saying, not evidence that he was secretly omniscient. Luke follows this with Jesus’ submission to his parents and his increase in wisdom. These details naturally describe genuine human development.

Jesus’ later testimony gives the source of this wisdom: the Father taught him and gave him what to say (John 8:28; 12:49). God had likewise given extraordinary wisdom to a human servant when Solomon asked for it (1 Kings 3:9-12). A two-natures proposal can reconcile omniscience with growth by assigning them to different natures, but Luke does not make that explanation. In context, Luke 2:49–52 portrays God’s human Messiah as aware of his Father’s calling, obedient to his parents, and growing in God-given wisdom.
