# Did Nebuchadnezzar See a Pre-Incarnate Jesus?

Daniel’s furnace account includes a fourth figure. The king’s words and Daniel’s own explanation identify the kind of heavenly help described.

## Daniel’s Fourth Figure

> “The appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods.” — Daniel 3:25 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading identifies the fourth figure as the pre-incarnate Son. The figure’s presence in the fire and the servants’ rescue make this conclusion understandable as an interpretation of divine deliverance.

Nebuchadnezzar’s words, however, are the observation of a Babylonian king. His phrase “a son of the gods” is not Daniel’s identification of the figure. When he interprets the rescue, he says that God “sent his angel and delivered his servants” (Daniel 3:28). The chapter gives no name for that angel, no title of Messiah, and no connection to Jesus.

The direct biblical-Unitarian reply follows Daniel’s own explanation: YHVH protected his servants through an angelic messenger. God can act through an angel without making the angel God or the Messiah. Two natures are irrelevant here, since Daniel 3 concerns neither incarnation nor Jesus’ human and divine natures. The account cannot establish Jesus’ personal existence before birth.
