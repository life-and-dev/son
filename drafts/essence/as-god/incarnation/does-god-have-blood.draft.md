# Does God Have Blood?

Acts 20:28 links God’s church with the blood by which it was obtained. Its textual and grammatical questions must be considered alongside the apostolic distinction between God and Jesus.

## Acts 20:28

> “The church of God, which he obtained with his own blood.” — Acts 20:28 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes “his own blood” to mean that the one called God shed blood in the incarnation. A related textual question remains: manuscripts read either “church of God” or “church of the Lord.” Neither reading identifies the Father as the one who died.

Where “church of God” is retained, the phrase can be read as “the blood of his own,” meaning God’s own Son. That sense matches Paul’s wording that God “did not spare his own Son” (Romans 8:32). Acts repeatedly distinguishes God from Jesus: God raised Jesus, made him Lord and Messiah, and worked through him (Acts 2:22,24,36; 3:13-15).

The contextual biblical-Unitarian reading is therefore that YHVH obtained the congregation through the sacrificial blood of his human Messiah. Jesus gave his life, while God raised him from the dead (John 10:18; Galatians 1:1). The two-natures explanation can say that a divine person bled only humanly, but Acts 20:28 itself does not describe two natures or say that YHVH has blood.
