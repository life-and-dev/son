# How Can Jesus Be the Son of God?

The New Testament calls Jesus God’s unique Son and also presents him as Mary’s human descendant. The conception accounts give the immediate explanation for this title.

## Conception and Sonship

> “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy—the Son of God.” — Luke 1:35 (ESV)

Luke gives a stated reason for the title “Son of God”: the Holy Spirit comes upon Mary and the power of the Most High overshadows her. Matthew gives the same cause, saying that Jesus was conceived from the Holy Spirit (Matthew 1:18,20). The Spirit is God’s Spirit and power at work. Neither account describes sexual generation.

Jesus is consequently God’s Son while also being Mary’s human son and David’s promised descendant (Luke 1:31-33; Romans 1:3). His sonship includes miraculous conception, holiness, appointment, and mission. The title does not make YHVH a biological father or make Jesus other than human.

The strongest Trinitarian reading understands the child as the incarnation of an eternally begotten Son. The direct biblical-Unitarian reply is that Luke explains the title from God’s creative action in Jesus’ conception, not from an eternal divine birth. A two-natures account adds an eternal divine Son alongside the child born to Mary; Luke identifies the one born as God’s human Messiah.

# If Jesus was God's biological son

God is Spirit, not a human being (John 4:24; Numbers 23:19). The virgin conception does not describe sexual generation. Matthew and Luke explicitly attribute it to God’s Holy Spirit and power (Matthew 1:18,20; Luke 1:35).

The strongest Trinitarian reading avoids biological language by distinguishing eternal generation from the incarnation. That theological distinction is more careful than treating God as a physical father. Yet “only begotten” is not proof of eternal generation. The Greek [*monogenēs*](https://biblehub.com/greek/3439.htm), used of Jesus in John, commonly marks uniqueness or an only one. Isaac is called Abraham’s *monogenēs* even though Abraham had another son, because Isaac was the unique son of promise (Hebrews 11:17; Genesis 21:12).

John presents Jesus as the Father’s unique and beloved Son, sent so that the world may be saved through him (John 3:16-17). It does not explain the title by an eternal divine birth. The birth narratives and John’s mission language fit together: God uniquely brought forth, authorised, and sent his human Messiah.
