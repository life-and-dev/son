## Is Jesus God’s Glory?

Scripture calls Jesus the radiance of God’s glory and distinguishes him from the God who glorifies and exalts him.

### Radiance of God’s Glory

> “He is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature.” — Hebrews 1:3 (ESV)

Trinitarians reasonably take Hebrews 1:3 as strong evidence that the Son shares God’s eternal divine nature. The verse presents Jesus as the perfect disclosure of God’s glory. It also says that he sat at the right hand of the Majesty on high, distinguishing him from God whose glory he displays.

Isaiah 42:8 says that the LORD will not give his glory to another. In context, the LORD speaks of his unique sovereignty and rejects idols, while also introducing his chosen servant, on whom he puts his Spirit (Isaiah 42:1,8). A Trinitarian may reason that Jesus’ sharing in divine glory requires shared deity. The Unitarian reading notes that God glorifies his obedient servant and Messiah without surrendering his unique identity (John 17:1-5; Philippians 2:9-11). Hebrews’ imagery therefore establishes Jesus as God’s exact and exalted representative; it does not explicitly identify him as the God who says “I am the LORD.”
