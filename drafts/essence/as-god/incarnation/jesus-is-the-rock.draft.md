## Is Jesus the Rock?

> “That Rock was Christ.” — 1 Corinthians 10:4 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes Paul’s statement that the Rock “followed” Israel and “was Christ” as an identification of the pre-incarnate Son with YHVH’s wilderness presence. Since the Rock gave Israel water, the reading sees the passage as evidence that Jesus personally acted before his human birth (Exodus 17:6; Numbers 20:11; 1 Corinthians 10:4).

Paul, however, calls both the food and drink “spiritual” and says Israel drank from a “spiritual Rock” (1 Corinthians 10:3–4). His point is a warning to the Corinthian church: despite Israel’s shared benefits, God was displeased with many of them (1 Corinthians 10:5–12). In that typological argument, “the Rock was Christ” identifies Christ as the spiritual source or fulfilment to which the provision pointed; it does not say that Jesus was physically present at Exodus or that he was YHVH.

YHVH alone is repeatedly called Israel’s Rock and saving refuge (Deuteronomy 32:4; Psalm 18:2). Jesus, by contrast, is the rejected stone whom God made the cornerstone (Psalm 118:22; Acts 4:10–11). Shared rock imagery therefore marks agency and appointment, not identity: God supplied Israel, and God made the crucified Jesus Lord and Christ (Acts 2:36). Paul can speak of Christ in God’s saving purpose before his appearance without making him a pre-human divine person; he was foreknown before the world’s foundation and revealed in these last times (First Peter 1:20). A two-natures appeal adds that personal pre-existence, but it is not required by Paul’s wilderness typology.
