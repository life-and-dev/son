## Was Jesus in the Garden of Eden?

> “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring.” — Genesis 3:15 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading identifies the LORD God who walks in the garden with the pre-incarnate Son, often joining that claim to the Son’s role in creation and to later statements about Christ’s pre-existence (Genesis 3:8; John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:15–17). On that reading, the Son personally spoke with Adam and Eve before becoming human.

Genesis itself gives no name or distinction within YHVH God in Eden. The speaker creates, judges, and promises that the woman’s future offspring will crush the serpent; the promised seed is not said to be present in the garden (Genesis 3:14–15, 22–24). Reading Jesus back into the scene therefore goes beyond its immediate context. Jesus’ later words identify the Father as “the only true God” and himself as the one sent by that God (John 17:3).

The New Testament identifies Jesus as David’s descendant according to the flesh and as the man through whom God brings life (Romans 1:3; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45). He is the promised offspring in God’s foreknown plan: known before the world’s foundation, then revealed in the last times (First Peter 1:20). Such foreordination or prophetic ideal existence is not the same as personal presence in Eden. Jesus begins his human life through conception by God’s Holy Spirit and power (Luke 1:35); God later grants the risen Messiah authority and subjects all things to him (Matthew 28:18; 1 Corinthians 15:27–28). A two-natures appeal adds personal pre-existence to Genesis, but Genesis does not distinguish two natures. YHVH—the Father alone—is God; Jesus is the human Messiah God promised.
