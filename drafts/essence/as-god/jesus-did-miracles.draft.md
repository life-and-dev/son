Some make the [wrong conclusion](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#wrong-conclusion) that if Jesus could do miracles he has to be [God](/god.md).

The strongest Trinitarian reading says that Jesus' authority over sickness, demons, nature, and death reveals God acting in person. His works are extraordinary. The immediate and canonical Unitarian answer is that Jesus identifies their source: "the Father who dwells in me does his works" (John 14:10), and Peter calls him "a man attested to you by God with mighty works and wonders and signs that God did through him" (Acts 2:22). Miracles show God's approval and the Messiah's delegated authority; they do not by themselves settle Jesus' identity as God.

Jesus acted on behalf of the Father (John 14:24, 31). He prayed before feeding the crowds and raising Lazarus, expressly thanking the Father who heard him (Matthew 14:19; John 11:41-42). After his resurrection, he still says, "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God" (John 20:17).

> “... and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father. Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it. -- John 14:13-14 (ESV)

Believers can also do works in Jesus' authority, because God works through his appointed Son and through those who trust him.

> If you love me, **you will keep my commandments**. -- John 14:15 (ESV)

If Jesus' miracles were meant to prove that he is God, it seems inconsistent that:
1. His miracles appear relatively small in scale (often healing individuals in private settings), compared to the great miracles God performed in the Old Testament, such as the global flood in Noah's time (Genesis 7), destroying Sodom and Gomorrah with fire from heaven (Genesis 19:24), sending the ten plagues upon Egypt (Exodus 7-12), parting the Red Sea (Exodus 14), providing manna from heaven for the entire nation of Israel for forty years (Exodus 16:35), stopping the sun in the sky for Joshua (Joshua 10:13), and consuming Elijah's water-soaked altar with fire from heaven (1 Kings 18:38).
2. It was still a revelation to his disciples to discover that Jesus was the Christ in Matthew 16:20, even after he had already performed multiple miracles in their presence.
3. Jesus prayed to God before miracles (Matthew 14:19; Matthew 15:36; Luke 9:16; Mark 6:41; John 6:11; John 11:41-42).

Jesus is greater than the prophets because God gave him all authority and raised him from death (Matthew 28:18; Acts 2:24, 36). That exalted role remains agency: the one God is the Father, and Jesus is the Christ whom he commissioned (John 17:3; Acts 3:13).
