YHVH, the Father, is called "the Holy One of Israel" (2 Kings 19:22; Isaiah 43:3). His Spirit is therefore naturally "the Holy Spirit"—God's own Spirit and power at work (Psalm 51:11; Isaiah 63:10-13; Luke 1:35; Acts 1:8).

The strongest Trinitarian reading says that calling Jesus the "Holy One" places him within the divine identity: the angel calls him holy at his birth, Peter applies Psalm 16 to him, and Hebrews calls him holy (Luke 1:35; Acts 2:27, 13:35; Hebrews 7:26). The title is truly fitting for God's sinless Son, but Scripture does not make holiness an exclusive name for God. It describes what God sets apart for his purpose.

Human priests were called holy ones:

> Do this: take censers, Korah and all his company; put fire in them and put incense on them before the LORD tomorrow, and **the man** whom the LORD chooses **shall be the holy one**. -- Numbers 16:6-7 (ESV)

and

> When men in the camp were jealous of Moses and **Aaron the holy one of the LORD**, the earth opened and swallowed up Dathan, and covered the company of Abiram. -- Psalm 106:16-17 (ESV)

Believers are likewise called holy in the New Testament:

> Therefore, if anyone cleanses himself from what is dishonorable, he will be a vessel for honorable use, **set apart as holy**, useful to the master of the house, ready for every good work. -- 2 Timothy 2:21 (ESV)

and

> As obedient children, do not be conformed to the passions of your former ignorance, but as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct, since it is written, ***“You shall be holy, for I am holy.”*** -- 1 Peter 1:14-16 (ESV)

The immediate and canonical Unitarian answer is that Jesus' holiness has a unique place in God's saving purpose. He was conceived by God's Holy Spirit, remained without sin, and was consecrated as high priest and Messiah (Luke 1:35; Hebrews 4:15; Hebrews 7:26). Yet the same texts distinguish him from God: God sanctified and sent him (John 10:36), and Jesus calls the Father "the only true God" (John 17:3). Holiness identifies Jesus as God's faithful human Messiah, not as YHVH himself.
