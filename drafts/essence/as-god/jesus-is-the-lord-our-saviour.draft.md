# Bad translations

## Titus 2:13

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes “our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ” as one person and treats Titus 2:13 as a direct identification of Jesus as God. That grammatical reading is possible and should not be dismissed by calling translations biased.

The sentence remains compatible with a distinction between God and His Messiah when read with Titus’s immediate and canonical language. Paul calls “God our Saviour” and “Christ Jesus our Saviour” in the same letter (Titus 1:3-4; 2:10; 3:4-6). God is Saviour as the source of salvation; Jesus is Saviour as the one through whom God brings it.

Titus 3:4-6 makes the order explicit: “the goodness and loving kindness of God our Saviour appeared,” and God “poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Saviour.” Elsewhere Paul speaks of “the appearing of the glory of our great God and Saviour” without requiring that Jesus be the same person (compare 1 Timothy 1:1; 1 Timothy 2:3-5). Titus 2:13 therefore cannot override Paul’s repeated confession of one God, the Father, and one human mediator, Christ Jesus (1 Corinthians 8:6; 1 Timothy 2:5).

### Contextual Distinction

Even if Titus 2:13 grammatically calls Jesus “God and Saviour,” one debated construction cannot overturn the letter’s explicit distinction. The Father is “God our Saviour,” and salvation is poured out through Jesus Christ our Saviour (Titus 3:4-6). “Saviour” describes both source and appointed means. This honours Jesus’ saving office while keeping the one God as the Father, as Titus and Paul elsewhere do (Titus 1:3-4; 1 Corinthians 8:6).
