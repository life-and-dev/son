## Romans 14:11 context

> For it is written, “As I live, says **[the LORD](/god/father/name.md#yhwh)**, every knee shall bow to Me, and every tongue shall **confess to God**.” -- Romans 14:11 (ESV)

This scripture is often quoted [out of context](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#context) to "proof" that Jesus is God. However, [in this context Paul to does refer to Jesus, but instead to YHWH as God](/god/son/as-god/similarities/act.md#jesus-judge-as-the-father).

## Philippians 2:9-11

The strongest Trinitarian reading is that Philippians 2:10-11 applies Isaiah 45:23, where every knee bows to YHVH, directly to Jesus. Paul does place universal honour before Jesus: every tongue will confess that “Jesus Christ is Lord.”

Paul also gives the reason and the limit of that honour. “Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name” (Philippians 2:9). Jesus did not seize this status. God gave it after his obedience to death. The confession ends “to the glory of God the Father” (Philippians 2:11).

Isaiah’s confession remains a confession to the one God, YHVH. In Philippians, God fulfils His purpose by appointing the crucified Messiah as Lord. This is delegated royal authority, not a second God. The same distinction appears in 1 Corinthians 15:27-28: all things are subjected to Christ, yet the Father who subjected them is excepted, and the Son finally subjects himself to God.

### Contextual Distinction

The strongest case is that Paul applies YHVH’s exclusive language in Isaiah 45 to Jesus. Paul’s own wording explains how: God highly exalts Jesus and gives him the name, while every confession of Jesus as Lord ends “to the glory of God the Father” (Philippians 2:9-11). The honour is universal and proper, but it is bestowed and directed to the Father. “Lord” therefore marks Jesus as God’s supreme Messiah, not as the God who exalted him.

# The LORD is my Righteousness

> “Behold, the days are coming,” says the LORD,<br/>
> "That I will raise to David a branch of righteousness; A king shall reign and prosper, and execute judgment and righteousness in the earth. In his days Judah will be saved, and Israel will dwell safely;<br/>
> Now this is his name by which he will be called: **THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS**."
>
> -- Jeremiah 23:5-6 (NKJV)

Some argue that this prophecy refers to Jesus, and therefore he is considered "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS". Therefore, they make a [bad correlation](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#bad-correlation) between "the LORD" which refers throughout the Old Testament to the Almighty God and "the Lord" which often refers to Jesus in the New Testament to proof that Jesus is the Almighty God. However, this same name is given to Jerusalem which no one consider to be God:

> In those days Judah will be saved, and **Jerusalem** will dwell safely.
> And this is the name by which she will be called: **THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS**.
> 
> -- Jeremiah 33:16 (NKJV)

In the ancient cultures names often have a meaning associated with them. Jeremiah did **not** say that "the branch" or "the king" *will be* "The LORD our righteousness", but instead he said that he will be ***called*** "The LORD our righteousness".

# Jesus got all authority

> Jesus came and said to them, “**All authority in heaven and on earth has been [given](https://biblehub.com/greek/1325.htm) to me.**” -- Matthew 28:18 (ESV)

and

> **All that the Father has is mine**; therefore I said that He will take what is mine and declare it to you. -- John 16:15 (ESV)

and

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, “Father, the hour has come; [glorify Your Son](/god/son/essence/as-god/claims/glory.md) that the Son may glorify You, since **You have [given](https://biblehub.com/greek/1325.htm) him authority over all flesh**, [to give eternal life to all whom You have given him](/god/son/essence/as-god/similarities/act.md#jesus-judge-as-the-father). -- John 17:1-2 (ESV)

Some would reason that because God gave everything to Jesus, that it proof that Jesus is our "new" God.

[Out of context](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#context), it seems like Jesus says that God handed over all his authority to him so that he could become the "new" God.

The fact that Jesus authority was given by a greater God and did not come from himself, proofs that [Jesus is not the Almighty God](/god/son/essence/not-god.md).

According to NAS Exhaustive Concordance, the Greek word didómi which has been translated to "given" in the ESV could also mean: granted, allow or permitted.

Nothing in the context suggest any kind of handover or giving up such that the old God, no longer have any authority because it was "given" to the new God. [Neither is Jesus competing with another God](/god/son/essence/as-god/similarities.md#philippians-2-6).

Neither did [God the Father](/god/father.md) retire, nor did Jesus took over the Father's role. Instead, [Jesus is God the Father's servant or steward](/god/son/essence/not-god/serve-god.md) (Isaiah 42:1; Acts 3:13 (ESV); John 4:34, 5:19, 6:38, 10:37, 14:24, 14:31) [who has been given all authority](/god/son/essence/as-god/exalted.md) so that he may glorify God the Father (Philippians 2:10). This implies God the Father is still in control and [Jesus still need to submit to his God](/god/son/essence/not-god/serve-god.md).

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, **[the Son can do nothing of his own accord](/god/son/essence/of-man/limitations.md), but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise.** For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that He Himself is doing. **And greater works than these will He show him**, so that you may marvel.” -- John 5:19-20 (ESV)

Nebuchadnezzar received the following revelation:

> And at the end of the time I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me; and I blessed the **Most High** and praised and honored Him who lives forever:
>
> For **His dominion is an everlasting dominion**, and **His kingdom is from generation to generation**.
> All the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing;
> He does according to His will in the army of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth.
> No one can restrain His hand or say to Him, “What have You done?”
>
> At the same time my reason returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom, my honor and splendor returned to me. My counselors and nobles resorted to me, I was restored to my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added to me. Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and extol and honor the King of heaven, all of whose works are truth, and His ways justice. And those who walk in pride He is able to put down.
>
> -- Daniel 4:34-37

Daniel would not have written down Nebuchadnezzar's revelation if he did not agree. The Most High God reigns **forever**. There is no hand over.

Paul teaches:

> So let no one boast in men. For **all things are yours**, whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or the world or life or death or the present or the future —
>
> **all are yours, and you are Christ’s, and Christ is God’s**. -- 1 Corinthians 3:23 (ESV)

Paul has never suggested that we too are gods, because "all things" are ours, just like they are Christ's.

According to Paul, when [Christ had submitted himself under God's authority](/god/son/essence/not-god/serve-god.md), he had received all that is God's. If [we submit ourselves under Christ's authority](/kingdom/body.md), **we too** will have authority over all that Christ has authority.

# King of Kings

> ... which he will display at the proper time — he who is the blessed and only Sovereign, **the King of kings and Lord of lords** -- 1 Timothy 6:15 (ESV)

> They will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is **Lord of lords and King of kings**, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful. -- Revelation 17:14 (ESV)

> On his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, **King of kings and Lord of lords**. -- Revelation 19:16 (ESV)

Some [assume](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#assumption) that only God can be in higher authority than the king, therefore they come to the [wrong conclusion](/bible/interpretations/rebuttals.md#wrong-conclusion) that Jesus should be God.

However, this reasoning is flawed because:
* Both Nebuchadnezzar (Daniel 2:37) and Artaxerxes (Ezra 7:12) also held this titles and neither one is considered to be [the Almighty God](/god.md) nor a member of [the Godhead](/bible/concepts/godhead.md).
* Nothing prevent God to delegate His authority ([name](/bible/concepts/name.md)) to another "King of kings" or "Lord or lords" to acts as an agent of God by ruling over mankind on behalf of Himself, especially if this delegate is His Own begotten Son in whom He is "well pleased" (Mark 1:11), namely Jesus [the Christ](/bible/concepts/christ.md).

Even Jesus himself said that he does not possess the highest authority:

> And he saith unto them, Ye shall drink indeed of my cup, and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with: but to sit on my right hand, and on my left, **is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared of my Father**. -- Matthew 20:23 (KJV)
