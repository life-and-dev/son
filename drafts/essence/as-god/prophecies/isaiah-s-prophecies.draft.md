### Mighty God

Unlike "God **Al**mighty" usually written as "Elohim", in this case "[God](/bible/concepts/god)" is written as just "’êl", which could also mean ["god-like", "mighty one", "mighty man", "man of rank", "mighty hero", "angel", "powerful one"](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/410.htm)

> Although English makes a clear distinction between “God” and “god,” the Hebrew language, which has only capital letters, cannot. A better translation for the English reader would be “mighty hero,” or “divine hero.” Both Martin Luther and James Moffatt translated the phrase as “divine hero” in their Bibles.

#### Contextual Distinction

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes the traditional titles of Isaiah 9:6 as direct names of the child and treats “Mighty God” as decisive evidence that the Messiah shares God’s identity. Isaiah 9:6–7 presents the child as the ruler through whom God restores righteous Davidic rule after the oppression and darkness described in Isaiah 9:1–5. His government increases without end as he reigns on David’s throne and over David’s kingdom (Isaiah 9:7; Luke 1:32-33).

A Unitarian reading recognises the force of this interpretation but understands the titles within royal and prophetic language. They can proclaim God’s character and work through His appointed king without identifying the king as YHVH Himself. The Davidic throne, kingdom, justice, and righteousness in verse 7 identify the child’s promised Messianic office. Even if each title is applied directly to the child, a title alone does not settle the question of identity; the canonical Messiah remains God’s anointed servant (Isaiah 42:1; Acts 2:36).

### Everlasting-Father

Isaiah 9:6 is the only verse in the entire [bible](/bible) where Jesus is possibly called "Father" according to the modern English [bible translations](/bible/translations). Because there are no other confirmations, it makes the translation questionable.

There are also no cross-references to this verse. This would have been the ideal verse to quote to proof that Jesus is God, yet neither Jesus nor any of his disciples ever quoted this verse.

The challenge is that the word translated as "Everlasting-Father" is translated from "abi-ad" and the intended meaning is not clear. There is another person in the bible whose name is also "Abi".

> "Abi *(translated as "Abijah")* daughter of Zechariah" -- 2 Kings 18:2

It is unlikely that Jesus would be named after the daughter is Zechariah who cannot be a father, and certainly not [God the Father](/god/father).

The name "Abi" means "Yahweh is my father". Combine it with ["ad"](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/5703.htm) which is ofter translated as "everlasting", it could mean "Yahweh is his everlasting Father" or the Son of God.

The next title is "Prince of Peace". This fits the Gospels where Yahweh Himself confirmed that Jesus is His Son (Matthew 3:16-17; Luke 3:21; John 1:51).

Another possible explanation is that "abi-ad" does not mean "everlasting" but "age" so that the text should have been translated "father of the *[coming]* age":

> In the culture of the Bible, anyone who began anything or was very important to something was called its “father.” For example, because Jabal was the first one to live in a tent and raise livestock, the Bible says, “he was the father of those who live in tents and raise livestock” (Genesis 4:20). Furthermore, because Jubal was the first inventor of musical instruments, he is called, “the father of all who play the harp and flute” (Genesis 4:21). Scripture is not using “father” in the sense of literal father or ancestor in these verses, because both these men were descendants of Cain, and all their descendants died in the Flood. “Father” was being used in the cultural understanding of either one who was the first to do something or someone who was important in some way. Because the Messiah will be the one to establish the age to come, raise the dead into it, and rule over it, he is called “the father of the coming age.”

This too fits Jesus' description as the "[firstborn](/god/son/firstborn)".

### The throne of David

Isaiah 9:7 continues the child’s description:

> Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on **the throne of David** and over his kingdom, to establish it and to uphold it with justice and with righteousness from this time forth and forevermore. -- Isaiah 9:7 (ESV)

The verse describes restoration through David’s promised royal line. The ruler establishes and upholds David’s kingdom with justice and righteousness. Trinitarians may understand this as the incarnate Son ruling as David’s descendant. Unitarians understand the same Davidic rule as the office of God’s human Messiah, through whom God fulfils His promise.

# Preparing the way of the LORD

> A voice cries: “In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD; make straight in the desert a highway for our God. -- Isaiah 40:3 (ESV)

Which was quoted by John the Baptist (Matthew 3:1-3; Mark 1:2-3; Luke 3:4-6)

[The confusion comes when John was making 2 distinct statements](/god/son/essence/as-god/prophecies/john-the-baptist):
1. repent and bear good fruit (Matthew 3:7-10; Mark 1:4-5; Luke 3:7-14) as warning of God the Father's wrath
2. when people confused him with Christ, he explained the difference (Luke 3:15-16)

When scholars mix up these 2 statements it is easy to come to false conclusions about the divinity of Jesus.

# The Teacher

It was prophesied:

> All your sons will be taught of [Yahweh](/god/father/name#yhwh); And the peace of your sons will be great. -- Isaiah 54:13 (LSB)

Then Jesus came and quote Isaiah:

> It is written in the Prophets, ‘And they will all be taught by ***God***.’ 
> 
> Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me. 
> 
> -- John 6:45 (ESV)

The fact that Jesus claim to have fulfilled this prophecy which states the Israel will be taught by the LORD, seems to imply that Jesus is God. However, Isaiah did not say that God is the only teacher and it ignores Jesus previous words:

> Jesus answered them, “Do not grumble among yourselves.  **No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him**. And I will raise him up on the last day. -- John 6:43-44 (ESV)

Jesus was God's delegated teacher (John 8:28; 12:49-50).
