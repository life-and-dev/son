# Second coming

The strongest Trinitarian reading of the Son of Man’s return is that Jesus comes with divine glory, angels, judgment, and the title “King of kings and Lord of lords”; therefore he must be God (Matthew 16:27; Revelation 19:11-16).

Jesus says that he comes “in the glory of his Father” (Matthew 16:27). His glory is received and represents the Father. Daniel’s vision supplies the same pattern: “one like a son of man” receives dominion from the Ancient of Days (Daniel 7:13-14). Receiving an everlasting kingdom is extraordinary royal authority, but it is not identity with the One who gives it.

Revelation likewise distinguishes “God the Almighty” from the rider who executes God’s wrath (Revelation 19:15). The Lamb is honoured beside God, while God remains the one who sits on the throne (Revelation 5:13; Revelation 19:6). Jesus returns as God’s appointed Messiah and judge. His authority, honour, and victory belong to him by the Father’s grant.

## Contextual Distinction

The strongest reading sees Jesus’ coming with angels and judgment as divine activity. Daniel gives the controlling context: the Son of Man comes before the Ancient of Days and receives dominion from him (Daniel 7:13-14). Jesus also says the Father has given all judgment to the Son because he is the Son of Man (John 5:22,27). His future glory, honour, and judicial rule are real, but all are received from God and therefore do not identify the Messiah as YHVH.
