# Second message: John is not the Christ

People confused John with the prophesied [Christ](/god/son/christ). That is why Luke wrote:

> As the people were in expectation, and all were questioning in their hearts concerning John, **whether he might be the Christ**, John answered them all, saying,
>
> “I baptize you with water, but he who is mightier than I is coming, the strap of whose sandals I am not worthy to untie. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.” -- Luke 3:15-16 (ESV)

John made it clear how he is different from the Christ.  

John never said that Jesus is God, instead he only said that Jesus is "mightier" and more "worthy" because Jesus can baptize with ["the Holy Spirit"](/god/spirit) and ["fire"](/bible/concepts/trials).

These are two different points that John made which should not be confused with each other.

# Jesus prophetic fulfillment

The strongest Trinitarian reading joins Isaiah 40:3 and Malachi 3:1 to John the Baptist’s preparation for Jesus. Since these texts speak of preparing YHVH’s way, John’s ministry can appear to prove that Jesus is YHVH.

The Gospels identify John as the forerunner of the Messiah, but they also keep the Messiah’s relation to God clear. John calls Jesus “the Lamb of God” and says, “God … gave me this command” concerning the one on whom the Spirit rests (John 1:29,33-34). Jesus is the one sent and anointed by God, not the God who sends him.

Malachi’s context allows agency rather than identity. YHVH sends His messenger, and “the Lord whom you seek” comes to His temple (Malachi 3:1). A royal agent can come in the name and authority of the one who sends him. The New Testament uses this pattern when it says that God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself (2 Corinthians 5:19). John prepares Israel for God’s saving visitation through His Messiah.

## Contextual Distinction

The strongest case treats the Gospel’s use of Isaiah 40:3 as a direct identification of Jesus with YHVH. The same Gospel presents John as sent by God to prepare for the one on whom God places His Spirit (John 1:6,33). In Scripture, preparing a ruler’s way can prepare for the ruler’s saving arrival through his authorised representative. John’s witness identifies Jesus as God’s Messiah and Lamb, while retaining God as the sender and Jesus as the one sent (John 3:34-35).
