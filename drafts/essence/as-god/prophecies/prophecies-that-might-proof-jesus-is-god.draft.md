The strongest relevant Trinitarian reading treats prophecy as a central argument because several passages use exalted language, divine titles, or YHVH texts in connection with Jesus. These connections should be examined rather than dismissed. The immediate and canonical Unitarian question is whether a prophecy identifies the Messiah as YHVH himself, or whether YHVH fulfills his promises through his chosen human king and servant.

The prophets consistently distinguish YHVH from his anointed Messiah. God promises David a descendant, calls that descendant his servant, and gives him rule over the nations (2 Samuel 7:12-16; Psalm 2:2, 6-8; Isaiah 42:1). The New Testament identifies Jesus as that promised Christ, "a man attested ... by God" (Acts 2:22, 36). Honor, divine agency, and prophetic fulfillment therefore do not erase the distinction between the one God, the Father, and his Son.
