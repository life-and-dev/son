> And Jesus answered him, “It is written, “‘You shall [worship](/god/worship.md) the Lord your God, and **Him only shall you serve**.’” -- Luke 4:8 (ESV); Matthew 4:9-10

And yet, Jesus said:

> “**If anyone serves me**, he must follow me; and where I am, there will my servant be also. **If anyone serves me**, the Father will honor him.” -- John 12:26 (ESV)

And Paul wrote:

> Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. **You are serving the Lord Christ.** -- Colossians 3:23-24 (ESV)

The strongest Trinitarian reading treats service to Jesus as proof that Jesus is the Lord God whom Jesus says must be served alone. In Colossians, Paul tells slaves to obey their earthly masters, yet to do their work wholeheartedly for a higher master: they will receive the inheritance “from the Lord,” because they are serving “the Lord Christ” (Colossians 3:22-24). Thus the text gives Christ serious, ultimate authority over Christian conduct, reward, and accountability—not merely casual assistance.

Yet Colossians 3:24 does not say that Christ alone must be served, nor does it identify him as “the only God.” Its point is that a believer's ordinary labor for a human master is rendered under Christ's lordship. The word there is *douleuō* (serve as a slave), not *latreuō*; John 12:26 uses *diakoneō*. Different Greek words can overlap in English, but the distinction alone does not settle the question; see [serve](/bible/concepts/serve.md). Jesus himself directs exclusive worship and *latreia* (sacred service) to “the Lord your God” (Matthew 4:10; Luke 4:8).

The surrounding letter keeps God and Christ distinct: God is “the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Colossians 1:3), raised Christ (Colossians 2:12), and is thanked through Christ (Colossians 3:17); Christ is seated at God's right hand (Colossians 3:1). Serving Christ therefore need not compete with serving God. The Father honors the person who serves Jesus (John 12:26), because Jesus is God's appointed Messiah. God exalted Jesus and gave him lordship after his obedience, “to the glory of God the Father” (Philippians 2:9-11). Christian service honors God's Son under the one God, the Father, rather than identifying the Son as that God.
