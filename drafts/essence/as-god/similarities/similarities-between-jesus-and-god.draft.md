# Jesus' equality with God

Some translators add titles in their bibles, like *"Jesus Is Equal with God"*, based on their understanding and could imply meaning to scriptures that the original author did not intend. They do this to separate a section as it is common practise in modern articles like the one you are reading at the moment. Unfortunately, it clashes with Jesus own words:

> My Father, who has given them to me, **is greater** than all -- John 10:29 (NKJV)

and

> Jesus answered him: ... "You heard me say to you, ‘I am going away, and I will come to you.’ If you loved me, you would have rejoiced, because I am going to the Father, for **the Father is greater than I**." -- John 14:28 (ESV)

The prophet Isaiah wrote:

> I am [the LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name), and **there is no other, besides Me there is no God**;  
> I equip you, though you do not know me, that people may know, from the rising of the sun and from the west, that **there is none besides Me**;  
> I am [the LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name), and **there is no other**.
>
> ...
> 
> For thus says the LORD, Who created the heavens (He is God!), Who formed the earth and made it (He established it; He did not create it empty, He formed it to be inhabited!):
>
> "I am [the LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name), and **there is no other**."
> 
> ...
> 
> To whom will you **liken Me and make Me equal, and compare me, that we may be alike?**
> 
> ...
> 
> Remember this and stand firm, recall it to mind, you transgressors, remember the former things of old; for   
> I am God, and **there is no other**;  
> I am God, and **there is none like me**,  
>  declaring the end from the beginning and from ancient times things not yet done. 
> 
> --Isaiah 45:5-6,18;46:5,8-10

Many scholars will look at this prophecy and reason that Isaiah was referring to the [Godhead](/bible/concepts/godhead) and there is only 1 Godhead, but [inside the Godhead we have an equal Father and Son](/bible/doctrines/trinitarian). However, Isaiah believed that [YHWH](/god/father#name) (translated as "the LORD") Who was speaking is [the Father](/god/father):

> For **you are our Father**, though Abraham does not know us, and Israel does not acknowledge us; you, O [LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name), are **our Father**. -- Isaiah 63:16 (ESV)

> O [LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name), you are our Father. -- Isaiah 64:8 (ESV)

Jeremiah also believed that [YHWH](/god/father#name) is the Father:

> “I said, 'How I would **set you among My sons**, and give you a pleasant land, a heritage most beautiful of all nations.'
> 
> And I thought you would call Me, **'My Father'**, and would not turn from following Me.  
> 
> Surely, as a treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous to Me, O house of Israel", declares [the LORD *(YHWH)*](/god/father#name).
> 
> -- Jeremiah 3:19-20 (ESV)

Malachi wrote that God is only 1 Father:

> Have we not all **one Father**? Has not **one God** created us? -- Malachi 2:10 (ESV)

Therefore, Jesus addresses the Father:

> In that hour Jesus rejoiced in the Spirit and said, “I thank You, Father, **Lord** of heaven and earth -- Luke 10:21

If Jesus was "equal" with the Father, then why does he address his Father as "Lord"?

## Philippians 2:6

The strongest Trinitarian reading sees “the form of God” and “equality with God” as proof that Christ possessed full deity before becoming human (Philippians 2:6-8). The wording is profound and the passage should not be reduced to a mere slogan about humility.

Its immediate argument is ethical. Paul tells believers to have Christ’s mindset: refusing selfish ambition, serving others, and obeying God (Philippians 2:3-8). Christ does not exploit equality with God. He takes the form of a servant and is obedient “to the point of death.” The text therefore distinguishes the obedient Christ from God, who responds by exalting him.

The climax confirms this order: “Therefore God has highly exalted him” and universal confession that Jesus is Lord is “to the glory of God the Father” (Philippians 2:9-11). The passage gives Jesus unmatched honour because of God’s gift and vindication. It does not call Jesus God, nor does it say that the Father ceased to be the one God.

### Contextual Distinction

The strongest reading of Philippians 2:6 takes “form of God” as full pre-human deity. The passage does not explicitly say this, and its stated movement is from Christ’s self-emptying obedience to God’s exaltation of him (Philippians 2:6-9). Whatever “form of God” conveys, Christ is the one who obeys and God is the one who exalts. The final confession gives glory to God the Father, preserving the one God and His obedient Messiah (Philippians 2:11; 1 Corinthians 8:6).
