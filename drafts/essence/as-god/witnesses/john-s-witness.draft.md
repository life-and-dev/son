## The Father and the Sent Son

John’s Gospel gives Jesus its highest language while repeatedly distinguishing him from the Father. It identifies Jesus as the Messiah and Son of God, the one sent by the Father (John 1:1-18; John 20:31). Any Unitarian reading must therefore account fairly for John’s Word language, Thomas’ confession, and Jesus’ unity with the Father.

### The Trinitarian Reading

John 1:1 calls the Word *theos*, and John 1:14 says that the Word became flesh. With Thomas’ “my Lord and my God” in John 20:28, Trinitarians read this as the incarnation of the eternal God the Son. This reading takes John’s elevated language, divine titles, and worship of the Son with full seriousness.

### The Unitarian Reply

John explicitly identifies the Father as “the only true God” and Jesus as the Messiah whom He sent (John 17:3). After his resurrection, Jesus calls the Father “my God” (John 20:17), says the Father is greater than he is (John 14:28), and receives works, words, authority, and judgement from the Father (John 5:19,30; John 12:49).

A Unitarian reading understands the Word as God’s self-expression, purpose, and wisdom embodied in His human Messiah. John 1:1 then describes the Word’s divine origin and character without identifying Jesus as the Father or requiring a later tri-personal definition of God. Jesus’ unique authority and honour arise from the Father’s sending and gift. John’s stated purpose is faith that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God (John 20:31).
