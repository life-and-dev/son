## A Teacher Sent from God

Nicodemus tells Jesus, “We know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him” (John 3:2). His statement is not a complete confession, but it identifies a central Johannine pattern: God is with Jesus and works through His sent representative.

### The Trinitarian Reading

John 3 continues with the Son’s descent from heaven and God’s gift of His only Son (John 3:13,16). Trinitarians reasonably see this wider context as more than an ordinary prophet’s mission. Jesus’ heavenly origin and unique sonship are central to their reading.

### The Unitarian Reply

Even in this context, God is the giver and sender, while Jesus is the Son given for the world. John later shows Nicodemus seeking fair hearing for Jesus and helping to bury him (John 7:50-51; John 19:39-40). Neither action changes his first category: Jesus is a teacher from God, and God is with him.

Descent and sending language can express the Messiah’s unique origin and commission. John repeatedly says that the Father sent Jesus and gave him works, words, and authority (John 5:19,30,36; John 12:49). The passages establish unique sonship and divine agency, while preserving the Father as the one God who sends His human Messiah.
