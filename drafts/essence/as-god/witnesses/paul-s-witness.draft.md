## One God, One Lord

Paul writes that “for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist” (1 Corinthians 8:6). The confession gives Jesus a cosmic and unique role, while expressly identifying the one God as the Father.

### The Trinitarian Reading

Many Trinitarians read 1 Corinthians 8:6 as Paul including Jesus within the divine identity by echoing the Shema of Deuteronomy 6:4. The text places Jesus within Christian monotheistic confession and cannot be reduced to an ordinary human office. Its “through whom are all things” language is substantial evidence for that reading.

### The Unitarian Reply

Paul’s wording nonetheless marks a distinction of source and agency. All things are “from” the Father and believers exist “for” Him; all things are “through” Jesus. God may accomplish His purpose through His appointed agent without making the agent identical with the God who is the source. Paul likewise says that God judges through Jesus (Romans 2:16).

The end of Paul’s resurrection argument is decisive for this distinction: the Son is subjected to God, “that God may be all in all” (1 Corinthians 15:24-28). Philippians 2:9-11 also says that God highly exalted Jesus and gave him the supreme name, so that every tongue confesses Jesus Christ as Lord to the glory of God the Father. Exaltation, worship, and universal authority belong to Jesus by God’s grant; the Father remains the one God.
