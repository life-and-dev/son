# Peter's Opening

Second Peter 1:1 speaks of “the righteousness of our God and Saviour Jesus Christ.” The Greek uses one article before “God and Saviour,” a construction that commonly presents the two nouns as one referent. The strongest Trinitarian reading is therefore that Peter directly calls Jesus “our God and Saviour.” Older English translations that separate the nouns should not settle the grammatical question.

A Unitarian reading should concede that this is a serious title for Jesus. It does not, by grammar alone, define Jesus as the one God or establish a Trinitarian doctrine. Biblical representatives can receive *god* language because they bear God’s authority, as Exodus 7:1 and Psalm 82 show. Titles require their context.

Second Peter also distinguishes “God” from “Jesus our Lord” (2 Peter 1:2) and speaks of “the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Peter 1:3). God grants Jesus honour and glory at the transfiguration (2 Peter 1:17). Thus, even if 2 Peter 1:1 applies “God” to Jesus as God’s exalted Saviour and representative, Peter still names the Father as Jesus’ God and source of authority. The title should not cancel this recurring distinction.
