## Psalm 45 and the Royal Title

Psalm 45:6-7 addresses Israel’s king as “God” and immediately says, “God, your God, has anointed you.” Hebrews 1:8-9 applies this royal psalm to Jesus. The quotation gives the Son exceptional honour, while preserving the distinction that the Son has a God who anoints him.

### The Trinitarian Reading

Trinitarians treat Hebrews 1:8 as a direct declaration that the Son is God. The wording is stronger than a mere statement that Jesus resembles God, and the author’s contrast between Son and angels gives the title great weight. This is the strongest reading that a Unitarian account must answer.

### The Unitarian Reply

Psalm 45 is a royal wedding song in which the Davidic king represents God’s rule. Its wording can be understood as elevated royal language for God’s authorised king. Hebrews uses the whole citation, not verse 8 alone. Verse 9 continues, “God, your God, has anointed you,” so the Son remains the recipient of God’s anointing.

The title therefore need not identify Jesus as the one God. It expresses his delegated royal dignity and his superiority to angels, which is Hebrews’ immediate argument. The wider New Testament pattern agrees: God anoints, appoints, and exalts Jesus as Messiah (Acts 10:38; Philippians 2:9).
