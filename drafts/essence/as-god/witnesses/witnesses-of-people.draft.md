## Human Testimony and Jesus’ Identity

People call Jesus teacher, prophet, Messiah, Lord, and Son of God. These titles are important, but narrative context determines their force. “Messiah” means God’s anointed king, and “Son of God” identifies God’s uniquely chosen representative. The titles establish Jesus’ authority without automatically defining him as the one God.

### Honour and Agency

Jesus receives honour because the Father gave him authority to judge and to give life (John 5:22-27). The Father’s purpose is that people honour the Son as they honour the Father (John 5:23). This is the strongest basis for reading honour to Jesus as divine honour, and it cannot be treated as mere courtesy.

Yet the same passage repeatedly says that the Father gave the Son this authority. Jesus acts as the Father’s authorised judge and life-giver. Equal honour within God’s saving arrangement does not itself state equal identity. Worship and honour directed to God’s appointed Messiah can acknowledge the God who sent him rather than replace the Father as the one God.

### Witnesses and the One God

John the Baptist calls Jesus the Lamb of God and God’s Son (John 1:29,34). Martha confesses him as “the Christ, the Son of God” (John 11:27). These witnesses use the Gospel’s own categories for Jesus. They give no direct confession that Jesus is God.

Jesus’ prayers and teaching still name the Father as his God and as the only true God (John 17:1-3; John 20:17). Human testimony should therefore affirm Jesus’ unique authority, saving role, and honour while preserving the Father as the one God and Jesus as His Messiah.
