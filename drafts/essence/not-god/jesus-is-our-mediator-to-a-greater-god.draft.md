## One God and One Mediator

First Timothy 2:5 states: “There is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.” Paul identifies the one God and then identifies the mediator as “the man Christ Jesus.” The following verse grounds that mediation in Jesus’ self-giving ransom (1 Timothy 2:6).

### The Trinitarian Reading

Trinitarians hold that the one mediator is both fully God and fully human. They argue that “man” names the incarnate Son’s role, rather than exhaustively defining his identity, and that only a divine mediator can finally reconcile humanity to God. This reading takes Jesus’ unique saving office seriously.

### The Unitarian Reply

Paul’s wording gives the reason for mediation: Jesus is the man who gave himself. A mediator stands between God and humanity; the office therefore distinguishes Jesus from the God to whom he brings people. God appointed, raised, and exalted Jesus for this saving work (Acts 2:32-36; Philippians 2:8-11). Unique office and delegated authority establish Jesus’ saving agency, not identity with the one God.

After his resurrection Jesus still says, “my God” (John 20:17; Revelation 3:12). The New Testament honours him as Lord and mediator, yet names the Father as the one God (1 Corinthians 8:6).
