# Jesus fasted

Jesus was led by God’s Spirit, fasted forty days, and was hungry (Matthew 4:1-2). He answers temptation by trusting and obeying God’s written word. Luke also says that he returned “in the power of the Spirit” (Luke 4:14).

A Trinitarian reading holds that these experiences belong to Jesus’ genuine human life, not a denial of his divine nature. That reading fairly recognises the reality of his hunger and temptation. The Unitarian reading notes that the narratives themselves explain Jesus’ victory through Spirit-led obedience, not through an unstated second nature.

His temptation establishes him as the faithful human Messiah. It also preserves the text’s direction of worship: Jesus serves God and resists the devil by confessing God alone as worthy of worship.
