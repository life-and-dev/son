## Sanctified and Sent

Jesus answers the charge in John 10 by describing himself as “him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world” (John 10:36). To consecrate is to set apart for God’s purpose. The Father consecrates and sends; Jesus is the one set apart and commissioned.

### The Trinitarian Reading

Trinitarians may understand “consecrated” as the eternal Son’s setting apart for his incarnate mission, not as a denial of his deity. They also read “I and the Father are one” as more than agreement of purpose. That reading gives weight to Jesus’ unique works and unity with the Father in John 10:30.

### The Unitarian Reply

Jesus’ own answer defines his claim as “I am God’s Son,” not “I am God” (John 10:36). He answers the charge through Psalm 82, where those who receive God’s word can be called “gods” without being the one God. The psalm’s precise referent is debated, but its argument shows that a divinely authorised title does not by itself establish identity with God.

Jesus then points to works that the Father gave him to do (John 10:32,36-38). The Father is in Jesus and Jesus is in the Father through God’s working presence and purpose, as John 14:10 explains. This is unity and agency, while the Father remains the one who consecrates, sends, and authorises His Son.
