## Strongest Trinitarian Reading and Reply

A strong Trinitarian reading observes that Jesus teaches with striking authority. In Matthew 5:22,28, he says, “But I say to you,” and in Mark 2:5-12 he forgives sins in a scene where his opponents ask who can forgive sins but God alone. It can therefore understand Jesus setting the standard as the divine Son exercising God's own authority.

The direct reply is that Jesus explicitly receives authority and instruction from the Father. He says that the Son can do nothing from himself in John 5:19, that the Father gave him authority to judge in John 5:27, and that the Father gave him the command he spoke in John 12:49. The standard is still binding: Jesus commands his disciples to love one another as he loved them in John 13:34. Yet these passages support Jesus as the human Messiah appointed by the Father, while the Father alone is God.

The Holy Spirit explains God's continuing power and interaction with disciples. Jesus promises that they will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon them in Acts 1:8, and Luke 1:35 parallels the Holy Spirit with the power of the Most High. A Trinitarian reader may see Father, Son, and Spirit together in Matthew 28:19. This reply accepts that threefold language while concluding that it does not require Jesus to be God or the Spirit to be a separate God.
