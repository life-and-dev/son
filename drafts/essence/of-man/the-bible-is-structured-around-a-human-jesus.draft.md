## Strongest Trinitarian Reading and Reply

A strong Trinitarian reading gives full weight to passages that describe the Word as God and Jesus as sharing glory with the Father, including John 1:1 and John 17:5. It also notes Thomas's confession, “My Lord and my God,” in John 20:28. On that reading, the Bible's structure centres on the incarnate Son, not merely on a human representative.

The direct reply is that these texts must be read with the Bible's repeated distinction between God and Jesus. The apostolic proclamation calls Jesus “a man attested by God” and says that God raised him, in Acts 2:22,24. Paul identifies “one God, the Father” and “one Lord, Jesus Christ” in 1 Corinthians 8:6, and calls Jesus “the man Christ Jesus” in 1 Timothy 2:5. This reading therefore holds that the Father alone is God, while Jesus is the human Messiah through whom God works.

The Holy Spirit belongs in this account as God's power and active interaction. Luke 1:35 parallels the Holy Spirit with “the power of the Most High,” and Acts 1:8 connects receiving the Holy Spirit with receiving power. These facts do not deny the seriousness of Trinitarian texts. They offer a different conclusion: Father, Messiah, and Spirit have distinct biblical roles without requiring that Jesus be God or that the Spirit be a separate God.
