## Biblical Distinctions

### Attributes

| The Father | The Son | The Spirit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the one God (Deuteronomy 6:4; John 17:3; 1 Corinthians 8:6) | Is the human Messiah and one Lord (Acts 2:22,36; 1 Timothy 2:5) | Is God’s Spirit (Matthew 10:20; Acts 2:17) |
| Has life in Himself (John 5:26) | Receives life from Father (John 5:26) | Gives life as God works (John 6:63; Romans 8:11) |
| Is immortal (1 Timothy 1:17) | Died and was raised by God (Acts 2:24; Romans 10:9) | Is God’s life-giving presence (Psalm 104:30) |

### Father, Son, and Spirit in Salvation

God sent His Son from love (John 3:16; 1 John 4:14). Jesus gave himself as a ransom (Mark 10:45; Ephesians 5:2), and God raised him (Acts 2:24). Believers have access to the Father through Christ in one Spirit (Ephesians 2:18). The Spirit is God’s gift, making believers new and helping them pray (Titus 3:5; Romans 8:26).

## Father, Son, and Spirit

The biblical distinction is not that the Father, Son, and Spirit are three names for one person. The Father alone is identified as the one God: Jesus calls the Father “the only true God” (John 17:3), and Paul writes, “for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things” (1 Corinthians 8:6). Jesus is the one Lord and Christ through whom God’s purpose is carried out (Acts 2:36; 1 Corinthians 8:6).

The strongest Trinitarian reading commonly joins John 1:1, John 10:30, and John 20:28 to say that the Son is God in the same sense as the Father. Those texts must be read with Jesus’ own distinctions: the Word is “with God” in John 1:1; Jesus and the Father are one in their works and protection of the sheep (John 10:28-30); and the risen Jesus still says, “my God and your God” (John 20:17). Thomas’s confession honours the Father revealed in his risen Messiah, but it does not cancel Jesus’ stated relationship to God.

The Holy Spirit is God’s own Spirit, power, and active presence. God gives the Spirit (Luke 11:13), anoints Jesus with the Spirit and power (Acts 10:38), and works in believers through it (Ephesians 3:16). Scripture can speak personally of the Spirit because God communicates, guides, and acts by His Spirit; this does not make the Spirit a second God or blur the Father with His Son.
