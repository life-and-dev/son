# The Names and Titles of Jesus Christ

The name of Jesus Christ is recognized globally. Yet the linguistic journey of
this name across different languages and centuries reveals a fascinating
history. How did a Hebrew name from the first century become the familiar
English name used today? Exploring the origins of both his personal name and his
messianic title provides insight into the identity of the one known as the
Savior.

Before the birth of Jesus, he was referred to as "the Son of God." As a human
born into a Jewish family, an angel instructed Joseph to name the child
Yehoshua. The name Yehoshua is transliterated into English as "Jesus." The angel
explained the significance of this name.

> "She will bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his
> people from their sins" — Matthew 1:21 (ESV)
>
## The Origin of the Name Jesus

> The English form Jesus is derived from the New Testament Greek name Ihsouß,
> pronounced "Yesous." According to Strong’s, Yesous (Strong’s #2424) is "of
> Hebrew origin" and can be traced back to Joshua’s Hebrew name, Yehoshua.
>
> Someone who reads the Bible in Hebrew, though, knows that the name Joshua
> sometimes appears in its shortened form, Yeshua (Nehemiah 8:17). It is
> apparent even in English: "Jeshua the son of Nun." (The letter J was
> pronounced like a Y in Old English.) Strong does not tell the reader that the
> Greek Yesous is actually transliterated from this shortened Hebrew form,
> Yeshua, and not directly from the longer form Yehoshua. The process from
> "Yehoshua" to "Jesus" looks like this:
>
> There is no "sh" sound in Greek, which accounts for the middle "s" sound in
> Yesous. The "s" at the end of the Greek name is a grammatical necessity, to
> make the word declinable.
>
> — [Dr. Daniel Botkin](http://www.yashanet.com/library/yeshua_or_yahshua.htm)
>
The evolution of the name Jesus follows a clear linguistic path. This journey
from the original Hebrew to modern English is documented in [historical
linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_(name)).

1. The Hebrew name *Yehoshua*
2. Shortened to the Hebrew name *Yeshua*
3. Transliterated to the Greek name *[Iēsous](https://biblehub.com/greek/ie_sous_2424.htm)*
   (the Greek language required adding the 's' at the end for grammatical
   declension)
4. Transliterated to the Old English *Iesus*
5. Transliterated to the modern English *Jesus*

Some groups prefer to use the form *Yahshua*. This form is central to the
[Sacred Name movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Name_Movement).

> So where did the transliteration Yahshua come from? This form of the name can
> be traced back to the beginnings of the Sacred Name movement, a movement that
> grew out of the Church of God, 7th Day, in the late 1930s. I have in my files
> an article entitled, "A Brief History of the Name Movement in America" by L.D.
> Snow, a Sacred Name believer. According to this article, "John Briggs and Paul
> Penn were the FIRST to pronounce and use the name Yahshua" (emphasis Snow’s).
> This was in 1936 and in 1937, the article states. No information is given
> about how Briggs and Penn came up with this (mis)translation.
>
> — [Dr. Daniel Botkin](http://www.yashanet.com/library/yeshua_or_yahshua.htm)
>

## The Origin of the Title Christ

> Messiah comes from the Hebrew word mashiach and means “anointed one” or
> “chosen one.” The Greek equivalent is the word Christos or, in English,
> Christ. The name “Jesus Christ” is the same as “Jesus the Messiah.” In
> biblical times, anointing someone with oil was a sign that God was
> consecrating or setting apart that person for a particular role. Thus, an
> “anointed one” was someone with a special, God-ordained purpose.
>
> In the Old Testament, people were anointed for the positions of prophet,
> priest, and king. God told Elijah to anoint Elisha to succeed him as Israel’s
> prophet (1 Kings 19:16). Aaron was anointed as the first high priest of Israel
> (Leviticus 8:12). Samuel anointed both Saul and David as kings of Israel (1
> Samuel 10:1; 16:13). All of these men held “anointed” positions. But the Old
> Testament predicted a coming Deliverer, chosen by God to redeem Israel (Isaiah
> 42:1; 61:1–3). This Deliverer the Jews called the Messiah.
>
> — [Got Questions](https://www.gotquestions.org/what-does-Messiah-mean.html)
>
The evolution of the title Christ involves several stages of translation. The
historical background of this
[title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_(title)) confirms its messianic
origin.

1. The Hebrew title *Messiah* (the chosen one to deliver)
2. Transliterated to the Greek title *Christos*
3. Transliterated to the English title *[Christ](../christ/christ.md)*

## Conclusion

The names and titles of Jesus Christ connect his earthly ministry to his eternal
purpose. The [personal name Jesus](#the-names-and-titles-of-jesus-christ)
originates from the Hebrew name *Yehoshua*, meaning "the LORD saves." This
reflects his mission to deliver humanity from sin. His [title
Christ](#the-origin-of-the-title-christ) comes from the Hebrew *Mashiach*,
meaning "Anointed One," which identifies him as the chosen Deliverer predicted
in the Old Testament. While the [pronunciation of his name has
evolved](#the-origin-of-the-name-jesus) through different languages, its core
meaning remains a testament to his role in the salvation of mankind.
