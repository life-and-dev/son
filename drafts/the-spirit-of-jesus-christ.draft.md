# Word of Christ

The “word of Christ” is Christ’s teaching and testimony received by believers. Paul tells the church to let it dwell richly among them through teaching, singing, and thankfulness to God (Colossians 3:16). This is not a claim that Christ personally possesses a believer.

Romans 10:17 connects faith with hearing the word about Christ. The risen Son’s message is made effective by God’s Spirit, but the message, the human Messiah who gave it, and the Father who sent him remain distinct (John 12:49; John 16:13-14).

# Jesus' Spirit

Jesus had a genuinely human spirit. He was troubled in spirit (John 11:33; 13:21), perceived in his spirit (Mark 2:8), and committed his spirit to the Father at death (Luke 23:46). His human spirit is not the Holy Spirit.

Romans 8:9, Galatians 4:6, and Philippians 1:19 speak of “the Spirit of Christ” or “the Spirit of his Son.” A Trinitarian reading treats this as a distinct divine person equal with Father and Son. The passages instead identify the one Spirit as God’s gift: God sends the Spirit of His Son into hearts (Galatians 4:6), and Jesus says the Spirit proceeds from the Father (John 15:26). The Spirit is called Christ’s because it forms Christ’s life and message in believers, while remaining the Father’s Spirit.
