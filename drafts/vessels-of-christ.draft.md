# Vessels of Christ

Believers are called vessels because God works through human lives. Paul describes believers as jars of clay carrying treasure, so that power is seen to belong to God rather than to the people He uses (2 Corinthians 4:7). Christ lives in believers by faith, and God strengthens them through His Spirit (Ephesians 3:16-17).

Trinitarians do not ordinarily claim that Christ and the Holy Spirit are personally identical. They hold that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct persons who share one divine nature. From this view, “Christ in you” and the Spirit’s work can describe the united work of distinct divine persons.

Ephesians 3:14-19, however, distinguishes their roles without stating that they share one divine nature. Paul prays to the Father, who strengthens believers through His Spirit, so that Christ may dwell in their hearts through faith. The wider context identifies “one God and Father of all” (Ephesians 4:4-6). A Unitarian reading therefore understands the Father as the one God, working through His Spirit to form the life and character of His risen Son in human vessels. This reading recognises the distinction of Father, Son, and Spirit while finding no statement here that they share a divine nature.
