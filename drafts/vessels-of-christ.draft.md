# Vessels of Christ

Believers are vessels because God works through ordinary human lives. Paul describes jars of clay so that **“the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us”** (2 Corinthians 4:7). He prays to the Father, who strengthens believers through His Spirit, so that Christ may dwell in their hearts through faith (Ephesians 3:14-17).

Romans 8:9-11 calls the one life-giving presence “the Spirit of God,” “the Spirit of Christ,” and “Christ in you,” then says God will give life through His Spirit. Classical Trinitarians see this interwoven language as the coordinated indwelling of distinct Father, Son, and Spirit who share one divine nature. It does not require them to be personally identical.

A Unitarian reading treats the phrases as complementary descriptions of God’s one Spirit in its Christ-given mission. God’s Spirit marks belonging to Christ, and the risen Christ is present in believers through faith and Spirit. Romans 8:11 keeps God as the one who raised Jesus and who gives life through His Spirit, supporting a positive Father-centred account of indwelling without denying Christ’s living authority.

Second Corinthians 3:17 says, **“the Lord is the Spirit”** (ESV), in an argument about the new-covenant ministry and freedom. Trinitarians often read this as close identification or unity of operation, not collapse of persons. Unitarians may read “Lord” in the immediate ministry context as the risen Christ encountered through the Spirit. Neither reading should ignore Paul’s distinctions elsewhere. See [The Spirit of Jesus Christ](the-spirit-of-jesus-christ.draft.md#jesus-spirit).

## Conclusion

God works in human vessels through His Spirit and forms Christ’s life in believers. The identity question remains linked to [the Spirit of Christ](the-spirit-of-jesus-christ.draft.md#jesus-spirit).
