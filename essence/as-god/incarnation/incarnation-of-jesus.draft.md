# The Incarnation of Jesus Christ

The doctrine of the incarnation posits that the eternal Son of God took on human
nature and became man in the person of Jesus Christ. This concept invites deep
reflection on the nature of God and the relationship between the divine and the
human. While many believers see the incarnation as the ultimate expression of
God's love and redemptive plan, it also raises questions about how a singular
and unchanging Deity interacts with His creation. This article examines the
scriptural basis for the incarnation and the various ways Jesus' presence has
been interpreted throughout history.

## Scriptural Foundation for One God

The Old Testament emphasizes the absolute oneness and unchanging nature of God.
These passages serve as the foundation for both Jewish and Christian monotheism.
>
> “You are my witnesses,” declares the LORD, “and my servant whom I have chosen,
> that you may know and believe me and understand that I am he. Before me no god
> was formed, nor shall there be any after me.” — Isaiah 43:10 (ESV)
>
> For I the LORD do not change. — Malachi 3:6 (ESV)

These scriptures remind readers that any understanding of the incarnation must
be reconciled with the fact that God is eternal and His character remains
consistent. Critics of the incarnation often point to these verses to argue
against the possibility of God taking on a new, human nature.

## Manifestations in the Old Testament

Throughout the Hebrew Scriptures, there are accounts of divine appearances or
figures that many Christians interpret as pre-incarnate manifestations of Jesus
Christ, often referred to as
[Christophanies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophany).

### The Fourth Man in the Furnace

In the book of Daniel, three men are thrown into a fiery furnace for refusing to
worship an idol. The king witnesses a fourth figure protecting them.
>
> He answered and said, “But I see four men unbound, walking in the midst of the
> fire, and they are not hurt; and the appearance of the fourth is like a son of
> the gods.” — Daniel 3:25 (ESV)

While [King Nebuchadnezzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebuchadnezzar_II)
used the term "son of the gods," many Christian traditions identify this figure
as [the Son of God](did-nebuchadnezzar-saw-a-pre-incarnation-of-jesus.md).
However, others argue that the king, being polytheistic, was merely describing a
celestial being or an angel sent by God.

### The Rock in the Wilderness

The apostle Paul provides a retrospective interpretation of the Israelites'
journey through the wilderness, identifying a spiritual source of their
sustenance.
>
> and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual Rock
> that followed them, and the Rock was Christ. — 1 Corinthians 10:4 (ESV)

Trinitarians often use this as evidence of
[Christ's active presence](jesus-is-the-rock.md) in the Old Testament.
Conversely, some Unitarian perspectives suggest that the Rock was Christ is a
metaphor, indicating that the Messiah was the goal or the future hope toward
which the wilderness experiences pointed.

### Presence in the Garden

Some theological views suggest that the pre-incarnate Christ was present at the
very beginning of human history.
>
> And they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of
> the day. — Genesis 3:8 (ESV)

Interpretations vary on whether this
[walking God](was-jesus-in-the-garden-of-eden.md) was a manifestation of the Son
or the Father appearing in a form that humans could perceive.

## Declarations of Pre-Existence

The New Testament records several instances where Jesus or his followers make
claims that suggest his existence prior to his birth in Bethlehem.

### Before Abraham Was

One of the most famous and debated statements occurs during a confrontation
between Jesus and the Jewish leaders.
>
> Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am.” —
> John 8:58 (ESV)

This statement is widely viewed as a claim to divine identity and [eternal
existence](did-jesus-exist-before-abraham.md), echoing the divine name revealed
to Moses. Some alternative interpretations argue that Jesus was referring to his
existence in the mind and plan of God before Abraham.

### Glorified Before the World

In his high priestly prayer, Jesus speaks of a shared glory with the Father that
predates the creation of the world.
>
> And now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had
> with you before the world existed. — John 17:5 (ESV)

This verse is central to the argument for the
[pre-existence of the Son](was-jesus-glorified-before-the-world-existed.md).
Interpretations vary between those who see this as a literal pre-existence and
those who view it as the glory destined for him in God's foreknowledge.

### Coming From Heaven

Jesus frequently spoke of his origin being from above rather than of this world.
>
> For I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him
> who sent me. — John 6:38 (ESV)

This claim of [coming from heaven](did-jesus-already-came-from-heaven.md)
suggests an origin that transcends his physical birth, often interpreted as his
[coming forth from God](did-jesus-came-forth-from-god.md).

### Satan Falling Like Lightning

Jesus also mentioned witnessing celestial events that some interpret as
occurring before his earthly life.
>
> And he said to them, “I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven.” — Luke
> 10:18 (ESV)

This statement is often used to support the idea that Jesus was present during
[Satan's original fall from heaven](jesus-saw-satan-fall-from-heaven.md).
Alternatively, some see this as a prophetic vision of Satan's defeat during the
ministry of the seventy disciples.

## The Nature of the Incarnated Son

The incarnation is not just about pre-existence but also about the unique nature
of Jesus as both divine and human.

### The Begotten Son

A central part of the sonship debate is the concept of being "begotten." Some
view this as an eternal process, while others see it as a specific point in time
or a prophetic declaration.
>
> I will tell of the decree: The LORD said to me, “You are my Son; today I have
> begotten you.” — Psalm 2:7 (ESV)

Interpretations of [Psalm 2](how-can-jesus-be-the-son-of-god.md) range from the
eternal generation of the Son to a prophecy fulfilled at the resurrection.

### Lord of the Sabbath

Jesus claimed authority over one of the most sacred institutions in Judaism,
suggesting a role that surpasses human limitations.
>
> So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath. — Mark 2:28 (ESV)

This claim to be [Lord of the Sabbath](sabbath.md) is seen by many as an
assertion of his divine nature and his role in the original creation. Others
interpret "Son of Man" here as referring to humanity's representative authority
over the Sabbath.

### The Image of God

Jesus is described as the visible representation of the invisible God.
>
> He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. —
> Colossians 1:15 (ESV)

This suggests that in Jesus, humanity can see and know God. However, the term
image is also used for humanity in general, leading to debates about whether
[Jesus as God's image](did-god-appear-as-jesus.md) implies ontological equality
or a unique [manifestation of God's glory](is-jesus-god-s-glory.md). Some even
explore if this means [Jesus was God's body](is-jesus-god-s-body.md).

### Divine Knowledge and Human Growth

The Gospels present a Jesus who possesses profound insight yet also grows in
wisdom.
>
> And Jesus increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man. —
> Luke 2:52 (ESV)

This verse highlights the human experience of Jesus. Theologians often discuss
whether he had
[divine knowledge as a child](did-jesus-had-divine-knowledge-as-a-child.md) or if
he fully experienced human limitations. This even extends to questions about
whether [God has blood](does-god-have-blood.md) through the person of Jesus.

## Conclusion

The incarnation remains a profound mystery that explores the intersection of the
divine and the human. Scriptural evidence points to [foundational
monotheism](#scriptural-foundation-for-one-god) while also presenting
[manifestations in the Old Testament](#manifestations-in-the-old-testament) and
clear [declarations of pre-existence](#declarations-of-pre-existence) by Jesus
himself. Whether understood as a literal pre-existence or a prophetic
fulfillment, the
[nature of the incarnated Son](#the-nature-of-the-incarnated-son) continues to
be a central focus of faith and theological inquiry.
