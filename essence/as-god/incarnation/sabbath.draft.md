# Lord of the Sabbath

When Jesus declared himself to be the "Lord of the Sabbath," he sparked a debate
that continues to this day. This statement is often used to support various
views about his divine nature and existence before his birth. By examining the
context of this claim, one can gain a deeper understanding of the relationship
between humanity, the Sabbath, and the authority Jesus exercised.

## Purpose of the Sabbath

Jesus taught that the Sabbath was intended to serve humanity.

> And he said to them, “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath.” — Mark 2:27-28 (ESV)

The Sabbath was established as a day of rest and restoration for people. It was
not meant to be a burden of legalistic rules. Jesus emphasized that the needs of
people take precedence over rigid ritual observance.

## Dominion and Humanity

The title "Son of Man" often emphasizes the representative role of Jesus as the
ideal human. In the creation account, God gave humanity dominion over the earth.

> Then God said, “Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And let them have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over the livestock and over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.” — Genesis 1:26 (ESV)

The LORD established the environment and its laws for the benefit of those he
created to rule over it. This dominion includes authority over the institutions
created for human benefit.

> what is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him? Yet you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor. You have given him dominion over the works of your hands; you have put all things under his feet — Psalm 8:4-6 (ESV)

As the Son of Man, Jesus exercises this intended human dominion. If the Sabbath
was made for man, then the one who represents humanity most perfectly has
authority over how it is observed. This perspective suggests that his lordship
is an expression of his role as the [representative of humanity](../../../son-of-man/human.md)
rather than an inherent claim to be the Creator.

## Addressing Pre-Existence Claims

Some argue that because the Sabbath was established at creation, Jesus must have
existed then to be its Lord. This view suggests that only the Creator could
claim such authority. However, this assumption overlooks how authority is often
granted. Just as animals and plants were created before humans yet placed under
human dominion, the Sabbath could be created for man and subsequently placed
under the authority of the Son of Man. The sequence of creation does not
necessitate that the one in authority existed at the time of the thing's origin.

Critics might argue that "Lord of the Sabbath" is a title reserved for the LORD
who instituted it in the Ten Commandments. However, the logic Jesus used
connects his lordship directly to the fact that the Sabbath was made for man.
This suggests that his authority is derived from his unique relationship to
humanity and his mission to fulfill the law's true intent of mercy and rest.

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus is the Lord of the Sabbath highlights his authority and the
[purpose of the Sabbath](#purpose-of-the-sabbath) as a gift for humanity. His
[dominion](#dominion-and-humanity) as the Son of Man reflects the original
intent for human rule over creation. While some use this to argue for his
[pre-existence](#addressing-pre-existence-claims), the biblical context suggests
his authority stems from his role as the representative of the people for whom
the Sabbath was made.
