Some make the [wrong conclusion](bible/interpretations/rebuttals#wrong-conclusion) that if Jesus could do miracles he has to be [God](/god).

However, just like [Jesus acting on behalf of the Father](/god/son/essence/as-god/similarities/act) (John 14:24,31), believers should also act according to Jesus' will.

> “... and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father. Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it. -- John 14:13-14 (ESV)

This gives believers the ability to do miracles in Jesus' authority too, just like Jesus was doing miracles.

> If you love me, **you will keep my commandments**. -- John 14:15 (ESV)

If Jesus' miracles were meant to prove that He is God, it seems inconsistent that:
1. His miracles appear relatively small in scale (often healing individuals in private settings), compared to the great miracles God performed in the Old Testament, such as the global flood in Noah's time (Genesis 7), destroying Sodom and Gomorrah with fire from heaven (Genesis 19:24), sending the ten plagues upon Egypt (Exodus 7-12), parting the Red Sea (Exodus 14), providing manna from heaven for the entire nation of Israel for forty years (Exodus 16:35), stopping the sun in the sky for Joshua (Joshua 10:13), and consuming Elijah's water-soaked altar with fire from heaven (1 Kings 18:38).
2. It was still a revelation to his disciples to discover that Jesus was the Christ in Matthew 16:20, even after he had already performed multiple miracles in their presence.
3. Jesus would not have confused his audience by praying to "another" God before he performed miracles (​Matthew 14:19, 15:36; ​Luke 9:16; ​Mark 6:41; ​John 6:11, 11:41-42).
