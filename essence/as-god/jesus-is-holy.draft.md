# Jesus Is Holy

Holiness is the hallmark of divinity, a quality that separates the Creator from
His creation. Yet, throughout the New Testament, the title reserved for God, the
"Holy One," is attributed to Jesus. This raises a significant question about
his identity. Does this shared title imply a shared nature, or does the Bible's
broader usage of the term suggest a different relationship between the Son and
the Father?

## Divine Holiness in Scripture

The Old Testament frequently refers to God as "the Holy One of Israel." This
title emphasizes His absolute purity and separation from sin. It is used
extensively by the prophets to denote the unique status of
[the LORD](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament). The Hebrew word for holy,
*qadosh*, signifies being set apart or sacred.

> For I am the LORD your God, the Holy One of Israel, your Savior. I give Egypt
> as your ransom, Cush and Seba in exchange for you. — Isaiah 43:3 (ESV)

God is called "Holy" in many other passages as well (2 Kings 19:22; Psalm 71:22,
78:41, 89:18; Proverbs 9:10, 30:3; Isaiah 1:4, 5:19, 5:24, 10:20, 12:6, 17:7,
29:19, 29:23, 30:11-15, 31:1, 37:23, 40:25, 41:14-20, 43:3, 43:14-15, 45:11,
47:4, 48:17, 49:7, 54:5, 55:5, 60:9, 60:14; Jeremiah 50:29, 51:5; Ezekiel 39:7;
Hosea 11:9-12; Habakkuk 1:12). Because God is holy, His Spirit is naturally
called "the Holy Spirit." This designation appears in both the Old and New
Testaments to describe God's active presence and power (Psalm 51:11;
Isaiah 63:10-13; Matthew 1:18, 3:11, 12:32; Luke 11:13; Ephesians 1:13, 4:30;
2 Peter 1:21).

## Jesus as the Holy One

In the New Testament, Jesus is explicitly called the "Holy One" or the "Holy One
of God." Some argue that this title proves his divinity or his membership in the
[Godhead](../../godhead.md). This view is supported by several key verses where
the title or description is applied to him (Acts 2:27, 13:35; Hebrews 7:26).

> And the angel answered her, "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the
> power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born
> will be called holy—the Son of God." — Luke 1:35 (ESV)

Even demons recognized this title when they encountered Jesus during his earthly
ministry. They acknowledged his unique status even when his own disciples were
still learning who he was.

> What have you to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us?
> I know who you are—the Holy One of God. — Mark 1:24 (ESV)

Unitarian scholars often point out that being the
[Holy One of God](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/verses/mark-1-24) identifies
Jesus as the Messiah who is set apart by God, rather than being God himself.

## Human Application of Holiness

The argument that "Holy One" exclusively denotes divinity must be weighed
against its application to humans. In the Hebrew Old Testament, specific
individuals were designated as "holy ones." For instance, Aaron and certain men
chosen by God were given this title to indicate their sanctified roles.

> Do this: take censers, Korah and all his company; put fire in them and put
> incense on them before the LORD tomorrow, and the man whom the LORD chooses
> shall be the holy one. — Numbers 16:6-7 (ESV)
>
> When men in the camp were jealous of Moses and Aaron, the holy one of the
> LORD, the earth opened and swallowed up Dathan, and covered the company of
> Abiram. — Psalm 106:16-17 (ESV)

Similarly, the New Testament calls all believers to be holy. The Greek word
*hagios* is used for both Jesus and the followers of Christ who are "set apart"
for God's use. Every Christian is called to reflect the holiness of the One who
called them.

> Therefore, if anyone cleanses himself from what is dishonorable, he will be a
> vessel for honorable use, set apart as holy, useful to the master of the
> house, ready for every good work. — 2 Timothy 2:21 (ESV)
>
> As obedient children, do not be conformed to the passions of your former
> ignorance, but as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your
> conduct, since it is written, "You shall be holy, for I am holy." — 1 Peter
> 1:14-16 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The title "Holy One" identifies both [God](#divine-holiness-in-scripture) and
[Jesus](#jesus-as-the-holy-one) as beings uniquely set apart for divine
purposes. However, the Bible's application of this terminology to
[priests and faithful followers](#human-application-of-holiness) suggests a
functional rather than purely ontological meaning. The shared title highlights a
sacred mission and dedicated status within the divine plan.
