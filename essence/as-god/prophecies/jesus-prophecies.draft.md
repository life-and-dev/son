# The Prophetic Return of Jesus Christ

Few events in biblical prophecy carry as much weight or evoke as much
anticipation as the promised return of Jesus Christ. While many view this event
as the ultimate proof of his nature, a closer look at the descriptions given by
Jesus and the apostles reveals a nuanced picture of shared glory and distinct
authority. How do these ancient visions reconcile the presence of both the
Father and the Son on the final day?

## The Glory of the Father

Some believe that Jesus will return as God to judge the world. However, Jesus
spoke of his return as a coming in the glory of another. He identified himself
as the Son of Man who acts on behalf of the Father.

> For the Son of Man is going to come with his angels in the glory of his
> Father, and then he will repay each person according to what he has done.
> Truly, I say to you, there are some standing here who will not taste death
> until they see the Son of Man coming in his kingdom. — Matthew 16:27-28 (ESV)

This suggests that the glory Jesus displays is not inherently his own as the
source. Instead, he radiates the glory of the Father. The reward he distributes
is based on the authority granted to him by God.

## The Word of God

The apostle John also witnessed a vision of the returning Christ. In this
vision, Jesus is depicted as a warrior judge who carries a specific title and
authority.

> Then I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse! The one sitting on it is
> called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he judges and makes war. His
> eyes are like a flame of fire, and on his head are many diadems, and he has a
> name written that no one knows but himself. He is clothed in a robe dipped in
> blood, and the name by which he is called is [The Word of God](../../../word.md).
> And the armies of heaven, arrayed in fine linen, white and pure, were
> following him on white horses. From his mouth comes a sharp sword with which
> to strike down the nations, and he will rule them with a rod of iron. He will
> tread the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty. On his robe
> and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and Lord of lords.
> — Revelation 19:11-16 (ESV)

Jesus is called the Word of God in this context. This title often signifies that
he is the messenger or the expression of God's will. He executes the wrath of
God the Almighty. This indicates that he is the instrument through which God's
judgment is carried out. His [name](../../../drafts/the-son-s-name.draft.md) or
title as King of kings is given to him by the Father. This follows the pattern
of exaltation described by Paul.

> Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is
> above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in
> heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus
> Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. — Philippians 2:9-11 (ESV)

## The Shared Throne

The final chapters of Revelation describe the ultimate state of the kingdom.
Here, the relationship between God and the Lamb is shown as one of perfect
unity and shared dominion.

> Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal,
> flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb through the middle of the
> street of the city; also, on either side of the river, the tree of life with
> its twelve kinds of fruit, yielding its fruit each month. The leaves of the
> tree were for the healing of the nations. No longer will there be anything
> accursed, but the throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his
> servants will worship him. They will see his face, and his name will be on
> their foreheads. And night will be no more. They will need no light of lamp or
> sun, for the Lord God will be their light, and they will reign forever and
> ever. — Revelation 22:1-5 (ESV)

Both the Son and the Father are present in this vision. The throne is shared by
God and the Lamb. This signifies their joint rule over the new creation.
However, the use of the singular pronoun suggests that the worship is directed
toward the singular Deity on the throne. The Son serves as the visible
expression of the Father's presence.

The Old Testament also speaks of the arrival of God. Zechariah prophesied about
this event.

> Then the LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with him.
> — Zechariah 14:5 (ESV)

Both Jesus and God are linked to the return. This does not prove that Jesus is
God. It shows that they work in perfect harmony. Jesus returns with the
authority and glory of his Father to fulfill the divine plan.

## Summary of the Prophetic Return

The prophetic return of Jesus highlights his unique role in God's kingdom.

- Jesus returns in the glory of his Father as the Son of Man, acting as the
  authorized judge for [the glory of the Father](#the-glory-of-the-father).
- As the Rider on the white horse, he executes the will of God under the title
  of [the Word of God](#the-word-of-god).
- The final state reveals a [shared throne](#the-shared-throne) where the Son
  and Father reign together in perfect unity.
