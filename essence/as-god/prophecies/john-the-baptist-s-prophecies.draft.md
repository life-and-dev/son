# John the Baptist's Prophecies

The appearance of John the Baptist in the Judean wilderness sparked a movement
that changed the course of history. Clad in camel's hair and eating locusts, he
claimed to be the fulfillment of ancient prophecies. Many today view his
mission as the direct preparation for the arrival of God Himself in human form.
But what did the original prophecies actually say, and how did John and Jesus
interpret them?

> A voice cries: “In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD; make straight
> in the desert a highway for our God. — Isaiah 40:3 (ESV)

Which was quoted by John the Baptist:

> In those days John the Baptist came preaching in the wilderness of Judea,
> “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”
>
> For this is he who was spoken of by the prophet Isaiah when he said,
>
> “The voice of one crying in the wilderness: ‘Prepare the way of the Lord;
> make his paths straight.’”
>
> ...
>
> Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan to John, to be baptized by him.
>
> — Matthew 3:1-3, 13 (ESV)

Some assume that John was preparing the way of Jesus which would mean that
Jesus would be "the LORD" and "our God" as prophesied by Isaiah. However, if
one continues reading Isaiah, the next verse states:

> Every valley shall be lifted up, and every mountain and hill be made low; the
> uneven ground shall become level, and the rough places a plain. And the glory
> of the LORD shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together, for the
> mouth of the LORD has spoken. — Isaiah 40:4-5 (ESV)

None of these physical transformations happened when Jesus arrived at John's
baptism. While some interpret the revelation of God's glory in Isaiah 40:5 as
being fulfilled in the person and miracles of Jesus, others point out that the
prophecy speaks of "all flesh" seeing it "together." This global visibility
was not a feature of Jesus' humble earthly ministry. Some theologians argue
that these descriptions refer to spiritual changes in the hearts of people or
to a future day of the LORD. However, the immediate context of John's ministry
suggests a focus on the moral readiness of Israel. John himself later
identified his role when he was questioned by the priests and Levites.

> He said, “I am the voice of one crying out in the wilderness, ‘Make straight
> the way of the Lord,’ as the prophet Isaiah said.” — John 1:23 (ESV)

## First Message: Repent and Bear Good Fruit

"The voice in the wilderness" was crying that people need to repent because of
God's wrath that would come. It was not merely announcing that Jesus would
come to be baptized. Even Matthew made this point clear when he recorded that
John's main concern was that people needed to repent.

> But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his baptism, he
> said to them, “You brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the wrath to
> come? Bear fruit in keeping with repentance. And do not presume to say to
> yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our father,’ for I tell you, God is able
> from these stones to raise up children for Abraham. Even now the axe is laid
> to the root of the trees. Every tree therefore that does not bear good fruit
> is cut down and thrown into the fire.” — Matthew 3:7-10 (ESV)

This was John's main concern with that specific prophecy. This is also evident
in the parallel passages in Mark 1:4-5 and Luke 3:7-14. John's primary focus
was repentance and that the people's lives should bear good fruit.

## Second Message: John Is Not the Christ

People confused John with the prophesied [Christ](../../../christ/christ.md). That is
why Luke wrote:

> As the people were in expectation, and all were questioning in their hearts
> concerning John, whether he might be the Christ, John answered them all,
> saying, “I baptize you with water, but he who is mightier than I is coming,
> the strap of whose sandals I am not worthy to untie. He will baptize you
> with the Holy Spirit and fire.” — Luke 3:15-16 (ESV)

John made it clear how he is different from the Christ. John never said that
Jesus is God. Instead, he only said that Jesus is mightier and more worthy.
This is because Jesus can baptize with [the Holy Spirit](../../../trinity/holy-spirit.md)
and [fire](../../../nature.md#concerns). These are two different points that John
made which should not be confused with each other.

## Jesus' Prophetic Fulfillment

> As they went away, Jesus began to speak to the crowds concerning John: “What
> did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken by the wind? What
> then did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft clothing? Behold, those
> who wear soft clothing are in kings’ houses. What then did you go out to
> see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet. This is he of whom
> it is written, ‘Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who will
> prepare your way before you.’ Truly, I say to you, among those born of women
> there has arisen no one greater than John the Baptist.”
> — Matthew 11:7-11 (ESV)

Jesus quoted Malachi:

> “Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me. And the
> Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple; and the messenger of
> the covenant in whom you delight, behold, he is coming,” says the LORD of
> hosts. — Malachi 3:1 (ESV)

The substitution of YHWH's name with
["the LORD"](../../../nature.md#bible-translations) in English translations
can lead to confusion between the Almighty and the Messiah. This prophecy
describes a messenger who prepares the people for the judgment of the
LORD as mentioned in Malachi 2:17.

Then Malachi also mentions a second "Lord who will come to his temple." This
title in the Hebrew text (*ha-Adon*) is often used of God, but its specific
usage here alongside "the messenger of the covenant" has led to varying
interpretations. Trinitarians often view this as proof that the Messiah is God
coming to His own temple. Conversely, Unitarians argue that since the text
distinguishes between "the LORD of hosts" (who is speaking) and "the Lord whom
you seek" (the one coming), they are distinct beings. In this view, the
Messiah comes to the temple of his God as His representative.

## Conclusion

The prophecies fulfilled by John the Baptist focus on a call to
[repentance and spiritual preparation](../../../nature.md#concerns) for the coming
of God's kingdom. John explicitly
[distinguished himself from the Christ](#second-message-john-is-not-the-christ),
pointing to one mightier than himself who would baptize with the Holy Spirit.
While some interpret these texts as proof of Jesus' divinity, the
[distinction between YHWH and the coming Lord](#jesus-prophetic-fulfillment)
in Malachi suggests a more nuanced relationship between the messenger, the
Messiah, and the Almighty.
