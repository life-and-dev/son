# Biblical Perspective on Worshipping Jesus

Throughout the Gospels, many people fall at the feet of Jesus in acts of profound reverence. While modern translations often describe these moments as "worship," they raise a crucial question about the nature of Jesus' identity. By examining the original biblical languages, we can uncover whether these acts were intended as divine worship or a different form of honor reserved for God's anointed King. This article explores the linguistic and cultural context of worship in the Bible to clarify the status of Jesus in relation to the Almighty.

## The Nativity Story

![The adoration of the shepherds](https://cdn.creazilla.com/illustrations/1956471/the-adoration-of-the-shepherds-by-l-ortolano-giovanni-battista-benvenuti-1512-di-md.jpeg)

Children are often taught with nativity stories that the angels and shepherds worshipped the baby Jesus. This common view is supported by Christmas carols that celebrate the infant as the recipient of angelic worship. However, the biblical account presents a different focus.

> And in the same region there were shepherds out in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night. And an angel of the Lord appeared to them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were filled with great fear. And the angel said to them, "Fear not, for behold, I bring you good news of great joy that will be for all the people. For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Savior, who is Christ the Lord. And this will be a sign for you: you will find a baby wrapped in swaddling cloths and lying in a manger." And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host praising God and saying, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among those with whom he is pleased!" When the angels went away from them into heaven, the shepherds said to one another, "Let us go over to Bethlehem and see this thing that has happened, which the Lord has made known to us." And they went with haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in a manger. And when they saw it, they made known the saying that had been told them concerning this child. And all who heard it wondered at what the shepherds told them. But Mary treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart. And the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, as it had been told them. — Luke 2:8-20 (ESV)

There are several key differences between traditional depictions and the biblical text. The angels praised "God in the highest" rather than the infant Jesus. The shepherds expressed a desire to witness the event rather than to worship the baby. There is no explicit evidence in the text that the shepherds worshipped Jesus as a deity. Their primary purpose was to testify about what they had seen. When the shepherds returned, they glorified and praised God for the fulfillment of the angelic message.

## The Wise Men Honored Jesus

The Gospel of Matthew records the visit of the magi, who are often described as worshipping the young child.

> And going into the house, they saw the child with Mary his mother, and they fell down and worshiped him. Then, opening their treasures, they offered him gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh. — Matthew 2:11 (ESV)

## The Disciples "Worshipped" Jesus

Numerous verses confirm that people bowed before Jesus, and he did not rebuke them for doing so.

> And behold, a leper came to him and knelt before him, saying, "Lord, if you will, you can make me clean." — Matthew 8:2 (ESV)
>
> While he was saying these things to them, behold, a ruler came in and knelt before him, saying, "My daughter has just died, but come and lay your hand on her, and she will live." — Matthew 9:18 (ESV)
>
> And those in the boat worshiped him, saying, "Truly you are the Son of God." — Matthew 14:33 (ESV)
>
> But she came and knelt before him, saying, "Lord, help me." — Matthew 15:25 (ESV)
>
> Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came up to him with her sons, and kneeling before him she asked him for something. — Matthew 20:20 (ESV)
>
> And behold, Jesus met them and said, "Greetings!" And they came up and took hold of his feet and worshiped him. — Matthew 28:9 (ESV)
>
> Now the eleven disciples went to Galilee, to the mountain to which Jesus had directed them. And when they saw him they worshiped him, but some doubted. — Matthew 28:16-17 (ESV)
>
> And when he saw Jesus from afar, he ran and fell down before him. — Mark 5:6 (ESV)
>
> And they worshiped him and returned to Jerusalem with great joy. — Luke 24:52 (ESV)
>
> He said, "Lord, I believe," and he worshiped him. — John 9:38 (ESV)

When Satan tempted Jesus to worship him, Jesus responded by quoting the Law.

> And Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'You shall worship the LORD your God, and him only shall you serve.'" — Matthew 4:10; Luke 4:8 (ESV)

Jesus was referencing the spirit of the Law found in the Old Testament.

> Samuel said to all the house of Israel, "If you are returning to the LORD with all your heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you and direct your heart to the LORD and serve him only, and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines." So the people of Israel put away the Baals and the Ashtaroth, and they served the LORD only. — 1 Samuel 7:3-4 (ESV)

[Out of context](../../nature.md#concerns), it might appear that Jesus was identifying himself as the LORD God. However, the issue was that Satan requested Jesus to serve him as a deity. The context suggests that "worship" in this specific instance meant to serve a god.

## Distinction Between Proskuneo and latreuo

The Greek word translated as "worship" in many modern English Bibles is *proskuneo*.

| Term | [Strong's Concordance](https://biblehub.com/greek/4352.htm) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Original Word | προσκυνέω |
| Part of Speech | Verb |
| Transliteration | proskuneó |
| Phonetic Spelling | (pros-koo-neh'-o) |
| Definition | To do reverence to |

HELPS Word-studies defines *proskuneo* as properly meaning to kiss the ground when prostrating before a superior. The NAS Exhaustive Concordance defines it as doing reverence to. Thayer's Greek Lexicon explains that it can mean to kiss the hand toward one, to fall upon the knees, to do homage, or to worship.

Therefore, *proskuneo* can mean "worship" in a religious sense, but it also describes an act of showing respect to a superior. Satan was not asking Jesus for a simple gesture of respect. He was demanding that Jesus prostrate himself before him as a superior. If Jesus were the Almighty God, such a request would have been meaningless. Jesus had to choose whom he would serve.

> "You shall worship the LORD your God and him only shall you serve." — Matthew 4:10; Luke 4:8 (ESV)

Jesus mentions both "worship" (*proskuneo*) and "serve" (*latreuo*) to clarify the context. The word *latreuo* refers to the specific type of cultic service and devotion reserved exclusively for God. While Jesus receives *proskuneo* many times, he never receives *latreuo* in the New Testament. This distinction is vital. It means that Jesus could rightly receive reverential homage as the exalted Lord, while true cultic worship remains directed to God alone.

### Honoring vs Worshipping

The relationship between honor and worship is often seen in how different translations handle the same concepts. For example, Isaiah's prophecy regarding lip service uses the word "honor" in the Old Testament.

> And the LORD said: "Because this people draw near with their mouth and honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me, and their fear of me is a commandment taught by men." — Isaiah 29:13 (ESV)

When Jesus quotes this passage in the New Testament, the Greek text uses *proskuneo*.

> "And in vain do they worship me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men." — Mark 7:7 (ESV)

This demonstrates that the act of "worshiping" in the sense of *proskuneo* can be a synonym for "honoring" or "fearing" God through outward actions.

### Translation Variations

Modern Bible translations vary in how they render *proskuneo* when it refers to Jesus. Some choose "worship" to emphasize his status, while others use "bow down" or "pay homage" to reflect the cultural act of prostration.

**New International Version:**
> When they saw him, they worshiped him; but some doubted. — Matthew 28:17 (NIV)

**Young's Literal Translation:**
> having seen him, they bowed to him, but some did waver. — Matthew 28:17 (YLT)

These variations highlight that the Greek term does not inherently carry a divine connotation. The meaning depends on the status of the person receiving the honor.

## Ancient Perspectives on Bowing

The act of serving anything other than God was strictly forbidden. Every time "serve" is used in combination with "worship" in the Law, it refers to the worship of a deity.

> You shall fear the LORD your God. You shall serve him and by his name you shall swear. — Deuteronomy 6:13 (ESV)
>
> You shall not make for yourself a carved image, or any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth. You shall not bow down to them or serve them, for I the LORD your God am a jealous God. — Exodus 20:4-5 (ESV)

Many nations were warned of destruction because they served and worshipped idols.

> And if you forget the LORD your God and go after other gods and serve them and worship them, I solemnly warn you today that you shall surely perish. — Deuteronomy 8:19 (ESV)

The Hebrew word for serve is *abad*, which means to work for or serve. The word for worship is *shachah*, meaning to bow down or prostrate oneself. *Shachah* appears over 170 times in the Old Testament and is used for both God and men. For instance, Abraham bowed before his visitors in Genesis 18:2, and Lot bowed before angels in Genesis 19:1. These were acts of respect, not divine worship.

King David was also "worshipped" in the sense of receiving royal homage.

> Then David said to all the assembly, "Bless the LORD your God." And all the assembly blessed the LORD, the God of their fathers, and bowed their heads and paid homage to the LORD and to the king. — 1 Chronicles 29:20 (ESV)

The people did not worship David as a god. They honored him as their king. This is a fundamental distinction. Jesus never requested that anyone build him altars or make sacrifices to him. However, receiving homage as the Messianic Lord is both allowed and encouraged.

## Jesus Taught to Serve God

Throughout his ministry, Jesus consistently directed his followers' focus toward God the Father. He never sought to establish his own cult of worship or to receive sacrifices. Instead, he emphasized that his mission was to do the will of the Father who sent him. By instructing his disciples to worship and serve God alone, Jesus maintained the fundamental distinction between the Creator and His Messiah.

The New Testament provides many examples of people who were honored with *proskuneo*. In a parable, Jesus mentions a servant who "worshipped" a king to implore mercy.

> So the servant fell on his knees, imploring him, "Have patience with me, and I will pay you everything." — Matthew 18:26 (ESV)

Other biblical figures received similar honor:

- David paid homage to King Saul in 1 Samuel 24:8.
- Joab bowed before David in 2 Samuel 14:22.
- Nathan the prophet bowed before the king in 1 Kings 1:23.
- Elisha received honor from the sons of the prophets in 2 Kings 2:15.

The Psalmist also exhorts a royal bride to honor her king.

> Hear, O daughter, and consider, and incline your ear: forget your people and your father's house, and the king will desire your beauty. Since he is your lord, bow to him. — Psalm 45:10-11 (ESV)

Nothing in scripture forbids honoring Jesus through acts of homage and respect.

> For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. — John 5:22-23 (ESV)

God has highly exalted Jesus so that "every knee should bow" to him as Lord.

> Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the [name](../../nature.md#bible-translations) that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. — Philippians 2:9-11 (ESV)

Paul did not suggest that Jesus should be worshipped instead of the Father. Rather, he taught that honoring Jesus brings glory to God the Father.

## God's Angels "Worship" Jesus

The book of Hebrews mentions that angels are commanded to honor the Son.

> And again, when he brings the firstborn into the world, he says, "Let all God's angels worship him." — Hebrews 1:6 (ESV)

Some [ESV](../../nature.md#bible-translations) footnotes cross-reference this to Deuteronomy 32:43 or Psalm 97:7. Both of these Old Testament passages refer to the LORD Yahweh. Moses spoke of the LORD, not Jesus. There is no indication in the song of Moses that Jesus was the intended recipient of angelic worship. Most likely, the author of Hebrews meant that the angels recognize Jesus as their superior because God granted him the authority to lead them.

## All Creation "Worship" Jesus

The book of Revelation depicts a scene where every creature honors both God and the Lamb.

> And I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea, and all that is in them, saying, "To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honor and glory and might forever and ever!" And the four living creatures said, "Amen!" and the elders fell down and worshiped. — Revelation 5:13-14 (ESV)

In this vision, God and the Lamb are distinct. Only one "sat on the throne" in Revelation 4:2. The Lamb is described as standing "in the midst of the throne" and coming to take the scroll from the hand of "Him who sat on the throne." The Lamb "stood" while God "sat." The Lamb was among the elders while God was on the throne. The worship described is [a build up of different events](../prophecies/john-from-patmos-s-revelation.draft.md#worship-of-every-creature) that lead to the ultimate worship of God.

Even when the term "worship" is used in the context of the eternal state, the distinction remains.

> No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship him. — Revelation 22:3 (ESV)

The Greek word for worship here is *latreuo*, signifying cultic service. The use of the singular pronoun "him" (*autō*) refers to the shared throne, suggesting service rendered to the singular Deity on the throne rather than dual worship.

## Trinitarian Perspective on Worship

Trinitarians often point to Jesus accepting *proskuneo* as evidence of his divinity. They argue that since only God should be worshipped, Jesus' acceptance of such acts indicates he is God. However, this view often overlooks the broader linguistic context. In both Hebrew and Greek, the words for bowing down or paying homage were commonly applied to human superiors and kings. The crucial distinction lies in *latreuo*, the cultic service and devotion reserved for the Creator. Jesus never receives *latreuo* in the New Testament, maintaining his distinct role as the Messianic King subordinate to the Father.

The biblical evidence shows a clear distinction between the worship reserved for God and the homage paid to Jesus as His Messiah.
