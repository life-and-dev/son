# The Son Is Not God

The identity of Jesus Christ remains one of the most debated topics in theology.
While many see him as God incarnate, others point to various scriptures that
seem to distinguish him from the Father. This article examines the biblical
evidence used to support the view that the Son is a distinct being from God the
Father.

Multiple scriptures show that [Jesus is a distinct person from God the
Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) and that [Jesus had human
limitations](../../son-of-man/limitations.md).

## Jesus Denies Being God

One of the most frequently cited passages is Jesus' response to the rich young
ruler.

> And he said to him, “Why do you ask me about what is good? There is only one
> who is good. If you would enter life, keep the commandments.” — Matthew 19:17
> (ESV)

Trinitarian theologians often interpret this not as a denial of divinity, but as
a challenge to the questioner. They suggest Jesus was asking the young man to
recognize the divine source of goodness and, by extension, Jesus' own identity
as the Son of God.

And

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of
> his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the
> Father does, that the Son does likewise. ... How can you believe, when you
> receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the
> only God? Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who
> accuses you: Moses, on whom you have set your hope. — John 5:19,44-45 (ESV)

While some see this as a statement of limitation, others argue that Jesus'
claim to do "whatever the Father does" actually implies a shared nature and
power with God. In this view, the Son's inability to act "of his own accord"
reflects a perfect unity of will between the Father and the Son.

And

> Jesus answered, “I do not have a demon, but I honor my Father, and you
> dishonor me. Yet I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and
> he is the judge. — John 8:49-50 (ESV)

And

> Jesus answered, “If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father who
> glorifies me, of whom you say, ‘He is our God.’ — John 8:54 (ESV)

And

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you,
> since you have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to
> all whom you have given him. And this is eternal life, that they may know
> you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. — John 17:1-3
> (ESV)

The phrase "the only true God" in John 17:3 is a focal point of debate. Those
holding a Unitarian view see this as an explicit exclusion of Jesus from the
Godhead. Conversely, many scholars argue that the contrast is between the
Father and false idols, rather than between the Father and the Son. They note
that eternal life is found in knowing both the Father and Jesus Christ
(cf. [1 John 5:20](https://www.biblehub.com/1_john/5-20.htm)).

## Jesus Has a God

Numerous passages record Jesus referring to the Father as his God.

> Jesus said to her, “Do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended to the
> Father; but go to my brothers and say to them, ‘I am ascending to my Father
> and your Father, to my God and your God.’” — John 20:17 (ESV)

Trinitarian theology addresses these statements through the doctrine of the two
natures of Christ, often called the
[hypostatic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypostatic_union). From this
perspective, Jesus speaks as a man in his human nature when referring to the
Father as "my God," while remaining divine in his essential nature.

Jesus prayed to his Father:

> And going a little farther he fell on his face and prayed, saying, “My Father,
> if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will,
> but as you will.”
> ...
> Again, for the second time, he went away and prayed, “My Father, if this
> cannot pass unless I drink it, your will be done.”
> ...
> Do you think that I cannot appeal to my Father, and he will at once send me
> more than twelve legions of angels? — Matthew 26:39,42,53 (ESV)
>
> In these days he went out to the mountain to pray, and all night he continued
> in prayer to God. — Luke 6:12 (ESV)
>
> And Jesus lifted up his eyes and said, “Father, I thank you that you have
> heard me.” — John 11:41 (ESV)
>
> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you —
> John 17:1 (ESV)

Paul also states that [God the Father](../../nature.md#the-father) is Jesus
Christ's [God](../../nature.md).

> Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in
> Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places... — Ephesians 1:3
> (ESV)

And

> For this reason, because I have heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus and your
> love toward all the saints, I do not cease to give thanks for you,
> remembering you in my prayers, that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the
> Father of glory, may give you the Spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the
> knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened... — Ephesians
> 1:15-18 (ESV)

And

> May the God of endurance and encouragement grant you to live in such harmony
> with one another, in accord with Christ Jesus, that together you may with one
> voice glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. — Romans 15:5-6
> (ESV)

And

> Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies
> and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our affliction, so that we may
> be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, with the comfort with
> which we ourselves are comforted by God. — 2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (ESV)

Peter also said:

> Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his
> great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the
> resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, to an inheritance that is
> imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you, who by God's
> power are being guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in
> the last time. — 1 Peter 1:3-5 (ESV)

John opens his revelation with:

> Grace to you and peace from him who is and who was and who is to come, and
> from the seven spirits who are before his throne, and from Jesus Christ the
> faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of kings on earth.
> To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood and made us a
> kingdom, priests to his God and Father, to him be glory and dominion forever
> and ever. Amen. — Revelation 1:4-6 (ESV)

Jesus also reveals:

> When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. But he laid his right hand
> on me, saying, “Fear not, I am the first and the last, and the living one. I
> died, and behold I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of Death and
> Hades. Write therefore the things that you have seen, those that are and
> those that are to take place after this.” — Revelation 1:17-19 (ESV)

This suggests that Jesus is the witness who is revealing what should
“take place after this”. The same witness continues his revelation through the
remainder of chapter 1, 2 and in chapter 3:

> The one who conquers, I will make him a pillar in the temple of my God. Never
> shall he go out of it, and I will write on him the name of my God, and the
> name of the city of my God, the new Jerusalem, which comes down from my God
> out of heaven, and my own new name. — Revelation 3:12 (ESV)

If this “witness” somehow silently switched to John, then it is unclear why
John would be the one who makes “the one who conquers” a “pillar in the temple”
of God. It is also unclear why John would “write the name” of God on “the one
who conquers.” This suggests that it is still Jesus speaking.

## Jesus Was Baptized

The baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist is also used to distinguish him from
God, particularly as John's baptism was one of repentance.

> God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change
> his mind. Has he said, and will he not do it? Or has he spoken, and will he
> not fulfill it? — Numbers 23:19 (ESV)

And

> And Paul said, “John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the
> people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, Jesus.” —
> Acts 19:4 (ESV)

However,

> In those days Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee and was baptized by John in
> the Jordan. — Mark 1:9 (ESV)

When questioned about the need for his baptism, Jesus explained that it was
necessary "to fulfill all righteousness" (Matthew 3:15). Defenders of his
divinity argue that he was not repenting of personal sin, as he was sinless,
but was instead identifying with humanity and setting an example for his
followers.

## Criticism

Some would quote Paul to prove that Jesus was not a "man":

> Paul, an apostle—not from men nor through man, but through Jesus Christ and
> God the Father, who raised him from the dead. — Galatians 1:1 (ESV)

The majority of scholars understand that Paul was not implying that Jesus was
some non-human being, but simply stating that his authority to write these
books came from no man-made institution, but directly from Jesus Christ and God
the Father themselves.

Other translations clarify this point further. For instance, the Amplified
Bible emphasizes the source of Paul's commission.

> Paul, an apostle (not commissioned and sent from men nor through the agency
> of man, but through Jesus Christ—the Messiah—and God the Father, who raised
> Him from the dead)... — Galatians 1:1 (AMP)

## Conclusion

The biblical record provides numerous instances where Jesus is distinguished
from God. This includes moments where [Jesus denies being God](#jesus-denies-being-god)
and acknowledges that [he has a God](#jesus-has-a-god). His
[baptism](#jesus-was-baptized) and the [beliefs of the apostles](#the-apostles-believed-jesus-had-a-god)
further emphasize this distinction. While [certain passages are used to critique
this view](#criticism), the overall narrative presents a Son who is in
relationship with, and often subordinate to, the Father.
