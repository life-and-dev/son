If Jesus was truly God Almighty, at least one of more prophets or apostles would have said so. But instead we read right in the beginning, when God gave [His Word](/bible/concepts/word) to Eve:

> I *(God)* will put enmity between you *(the devil)* and the woman *(Eve)*, and between your offspring and **her offspring**;  
> He *(Jesus)* shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel.”
>
> -- Genesis 3:15 (ESV)

This is the first time Jesus is mentioned in scripture.  God never told Eve that He Himself would personally come and bruise the devil.  He promised that Eve's offspring would do that. [This was also the expectation of many other prophets](/god/son/essence/of-man/prophecies).

When we finally get to the New Testament which specifically focus on Jesus, it starts with:

> **The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David**, the son of Abraham.  
> Abraham was the father of Isaac, and Isaac the father...
>
> So all the generations from Abraham to David were fourteen generations, and from David to the deportation to Babylon fourteen generations, and from the deportation to Babylon to the Christ fourteen generations. **Now the birth of Jesus Christ took place in this way:** When his mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together **she was found to be with child** from the Holy Spirit. And her husband Joseph, being a just man and unwilling to put her to shame, resolved to divorce her quietly. But as he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, “Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take Mary as your wife, for that which is conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will bear **a son**, and you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.”
>
> -- Matthew 1:1-2,17-21 (ESV)

Matthew's introduction state that Jesus is a human Jew. Although Mary's pregnancy was supernatural, nothing here hints that Jesus is God Himself, nor [the Angel of the Lord](/god/son/essence/as-angel).

The next book, Mark starts with: "The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, [the Son **of** God](https://son.ofgod.info)." which states to the layman that Jesus is not God as he was identified as "the Son".

The first time Luke mentions Jesus, he also does not call Jesus God, instead he wrote:

> In the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee named Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David. And the virgin's name was Mary.
>
> And he came to her and said, “Greetings, O favored one, the Lord is with you!”
>
> But she was greatly troubled at the saying, and tried to discern what sort of greeting this might be.
>
> And the angel said to her, “Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God. And behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear **a son**, and you shall call his name Jesus. He will be great and will be called [the Son **of** the Most High](https://son.ofgod.info). And the Lord God will give to him the throne of **his father David**, and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there will be no end.”  
> And Mary said to the angel, “How will this be, since I am a virgin?”  
> And the angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy — [the Son **of** God](https://son.ofgod.info).
>
> -- Luke 1:26-35 (ESV)

The next book, John starts with: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and **the Word was God**." To the [Trinitarian](/bible/doctrines/trinitarian) believer this means "Jesus is God", however [they only assumed that Jesus is the Word](/god/son/essence/as-god/word) because it fits their theology. John never writes "Jesus is God".

The next book, Acts starts with Jesus leaving his disciples behind to return to God. This by itself proof that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct).

The next book, Romans starts with 

> Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, set apart for the gospel of God, which He promised beforehand through His prophets in the holy Scriptures, concerning **His Son, who was descended from David according to the flesh** and was **declared** to be the Son of God" -- Romans 1:1-3 (ESV)

Even Paul introduces 
* [Christ Jesus distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct) as "His Son"
* a descended from David (instead of God)
* **declared** the Son of God
  
Which means God is not his biological Father.

The next apostle who wrote Hebrews starts with:

> Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets, **but in these last days He has spoken to us by his Son** -- Hebrews 1:1-2 (ESV)

This too shows that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct).

The next book James starts with: "James, a servant of God **and** of the Lord Jesus Christ." James too shows that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct).

The next book 1 Peter, opens with a blessing: "Blessed be **the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ!**" This state that [Jesus has a God](/god/son/essence/not-god) and Who is also [His Father](/god/son/essence/of-god/parent).

1 John explain at the beginning that the purpose of the book is: "... so that you too may have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father **and** with [His Son Jesus Christ](/god/son/essence/of-god/parent)." John too shows that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct).

Jude starts with: 

> Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James.
> To those who are called, beloved in God the Father **and** kept for Jesus Christ.
> 
> -- Jude 1:1 (ESV)

Jude too shows that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct).

The next book Revelation starts with: "The revelation of Jesus Christ, **which God gave him** to show to his servants the things that must soon take place."

John of Patmos also shows that [Jesus is distinct from God](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct) as they even interact with each other in the book of Revelation.
