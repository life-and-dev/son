# Godhead According to Trinitarians

Many [Trinitarian](../trinity.md) scholars agree that the Godhead consists of three members. Each member is believed to fulfill a different function within the divine plan. This raises the question of how these members relate to one another. This article explores the various models and theories proposed by scholars to describe the nature of the Godhead.

## Gods

Some scholars argue that each member of the Godhead is a God by himself. Together they form one unified God or [Godhead](../godhead.md).

When the Bible states there is only one God, proponents of this view often refer to the ["Trinity"](../trinity.md). The word "Trinity" itself does not exist in the biblical text. There is no single scripture that explicitly explains the [Trinity doctrine](../trinity.md) in its entirety. The doctrine is built upon various scriptures scattered throughout the Bible. Theologians correlate these texts using complex interpretations to support this doctrinal model. Defenders of the Trinity argue that this synthesis is necessary to comprehend the full biblical witness.

## Incarnations

Another view suggests that God was first the Father. He then [incarnated into the Son](https://eternal.family.net.za/god/son/essence/as-god/incarnation). Finally, He ascended and became the invisible Holy Spirit to be present with humanity.

However, critics point out that the Father and the Son interacted with each other as [distinct individuals](../son-of-man/distinct.md). Furthermore, [the Holy Spirit is not Jesus](https://eternal.family.net.za/questions/what-is-the-holy-spirit) because Jesus received the Holy Spirit while he was a human among humans.

A logical challenge to this incarnation theory is the maintenance of the universe. If the Almighty God entirely limited Himself to a human form, it raises the question of who sustained creation during that time. Additionally, if the supreme God became a limited human and returned to divine supremacy independently without a superior God, it blurs the distinction between divine power and human limitation. Supporters of this view maintain that God's omnipresence allows Him to be incarnate while still sustaining the universe.

## Modes

Some scholars argue that God operates in different modes depending on the situation:

* When someone needs a father, He acts as the Father.
* When someone needs a friend or savior, He acts as the Son.
* When someone needs spiritual power, He acts as the Holy Spirit.

Critics of this modalist view argue that the Father and the Son interacted with each other simultaneously as [distinct individuals](../son-of-man/distinct.md), rather than as sequential modes of a single person. Defenders of modalism suggest that an infinite God can manifest in multiple modes at the same time.

## Mystery

Some believers argue that it is pointless to attempt to understand how the members of the Godhead relate to each other. They suggest we should accept it as a [mystery](https://church.ofgod.info/terms/mystery) that human minds will never be able to solve.

However, classifying the nature of God as an absolute mystery may discourage believers from searching for the truth. Seeking to understand divine matters is a central theme in Scripture. Proponents of the mystery view counter that acknowledging human limitations in understanding the infinite honors God's transcendent nature.

## Parts

Some scholars compare the Godhead to human anatomy. A human person has different parts like a body, soul, and spirit. Even human physical bodies have different parts like hands and feet. In this view, God also has different components like the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

However, in the biblical accounts of Jesus, these components interact with each other as distinct individuals. These parts can even be separated, as seen when Jesus cried out to God on the cross (Matthew 27:46; Mark 15:34). If the members were merely parts of a single organism, this separation would imply that God was literally divided. Defenders of this view argue that the analogy of parts is merely a conceptual tool and should not be stretched to physical limits.

## Roles

Some argue that the same person can hold different roles simultaneously. A single man can be a father, son, husband, friend, colleague, and teacher. Following this logic, God could also fulfill different roles like a Father, Son, and Spirit.

Critics of this role-based theory point out that:

* Jesus' roles interact with each other as [distinct individuals](../son-of-man/distinct.md).
* Some of these distinct roles appear incompatible when applied to the same entity.

For example, claims about Jesus often present seemingly contradictory roles simultaneously. He is considered his own Father and also his own Son. He is seen as our Almighty God and also as a human like one of us. He is positioned as the greatest God and also as a servant of God. He is described as the Creator and also as the firstborn of creation. He is the one being worshipped and also the sacrifice offered to God. He is the one worthy of all prayer and also the mediator that prays to God on our behalf.

Supporters argue that Jesus possesses both a divine and human nature, allowing him to fulfill these contrasting roles.

## Shape-Shifting

Some theories suggest that the Almighty God can appear in any form He wishes. He appeared as a burning bush, a pillar of cloud, and a rock. Therefore, it is no problem for Him to appear simultaneously as a Heavenly Father, a human Son, and a Holy Spirit.

However, the biblical text often records instances where disciples witnessed the Father and the Son interacting with each other simultaneously as [distinct individuals](../son-of-man/distinct.md), rather than as temporary manifestations. Defenders argue that an omnipotent God is not limited by physical laws and can manifest in multiple interactive forms at once.

## Conclusion

Scholars propose multiple theories to understand the Godhead. Some view the members as multiple [Gods](#gods) forming a unified Godhead. Others suggest the Godhead consists of sequential [incarnations](#incarnations) or different [modes](#modes) of a single being. Some theologians conclude the Godhead is simply an unsolvable [mystery](#mystery). Other models compare the members to structural [parts](#parts), various [roles](#roles), or manifestations through [shape-shifting](#shape-shifting). The various theological models attempt to reconcile the concept of the Godhead with established doctrines. An alternative approach to understanding the Godhead is to [study the words of Jesus](/bible/concepts/godhead/according-to-jesus).
