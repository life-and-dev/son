# Serving Jesus

The English Bible often uses the single word "serve" to translate several
distinct Greek terms, each carrying its own unique nuance and theological
weight. Understanding these differences provides clarity on how the New
Testament distinguishes between the religious worship due to God alone and the
various forms of service rendered to Jesus and fellow believers. This
exploration into the original Greek vocabulary reveals a precise framework for
service, ministry, and devotion within the early Christian context.

## Distinguishing Service

There are different Greek words that are all translated to the same English word
"serve" in our modern English bibles:

For example, in the same [ESV translation](nature.md#bible-translations)
Jesus said:

> [The Son of Man](son-of-man/human.md) came not to be ***served*** but to
> ***serve***” — Matthew 20:28 (ESV)

but then Jesus also said:

> “**If anyone *serves* me**, he must follow me; and where I am, there will my
> servant be also. **If anyone *serves* me**, the Father will honor him.” —
> John 12:26 (ESV)

Jesus did not contradict himself. This is a result of
[confusing translations](nature.md#bible-translations).

## Worship Service

The following scriptures use the Greek word "latreuó":

> “Blessed be the Lord God of Israel... **serve** *(latreuó)* Him without fear,
> in holiness and righteousness before Him all our days. —
> Luke 1:68,74-75 (ESV)
>
> There was a prophetess, Anna, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of
> Asher... She did not depart from the temple, **worshiping** *(latreuó)* with
> fasting and prayer night and day. — Luke 2:37 (ESV)
>
> God spake on this wise, That his seed should sojourn in a strange land; and
> that they should bring them into bondage
> *[(douleuó)](#obedient-bondage-service)*, and entreat them evil four hundred
> years. And the nation to whom they shall be in bondage
> *[(douleuó)](#obedient-bondage-service)* will I judge, said God: and after
> that shall they come forth, and **serve** *(latreuó)* me in this place. —
> Acts 7:6-7 (KJV)
>
> Paul replied... "But this I confess to you, that according to the way, which
> they call a sect, I **worship** *(latreuó)* the God of our fathers, believing
> everything laid down by the Law and written in the Prophets. —
> Acts 24:14 (ESV)
>
> Then Paul stretched out his hand and made his defense... now I stand here on
> trial because of my hope in the promise made by God to our fathers, to which
> our twelve tribes hope to attain, as they earnestly **worship** *(latreuó)*
> night and day. — Acts 26:1,6-7 (ESV)
>
> Paul stood up among them and said... this very night there stood before me an
> angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I **worship** *(latreuó)* ... —
> Acts 27:23 (ESV)
>
> God is my witness, whom I **serve** *(latreuó)* with my spirit in the gospel
> of his Son... — Romans 1:9 (ESV)
>
> We are the circumcision, who **worship** *(latreuó)* by the Spirit of God... —
> Philippians 3:3 (ESV)
>
> I thank God whom I **serve** *(latreuó)*, as did my ancestors, with a clear
> conscience, as I remember you constantly in my prayers night and day. —
> 2 Timothy 1:3 (ESV)
>
> Therefore they are before the throne of God, and **serve** *(latreuó)* Him day
> and night in His temple. — Revelation 7:15 (ESV)
>
> No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of the
> Lamb will be in it, and His servants will **worship** *(latreuó)* Him. —
> Revelation 22:3 (ESV)

The term *latreuō* consistently appears in contexts of religious service or
worship directed toward God. It is never used in the New Testament to describe a
form of service rendered to Jesus. This distinction is significant because
[Jesus was worshipped differently](nature.md#worship). The specific type of
worship service denoted by *latreuō* is reserved exclusively for the Creator:

> Jesus said to him, “Be gone, Satan! For it is written, ‘You shall worship the
> Lord your God and Him only shall you **serve** *(latreuó)*.’” —
> Matthew 4:9-10 (ESV)
>
> Jesus answered him, “It is written, “‘You shall [worship](nature.md#worship)
> the Lord your God, and Him only shall you **serve** *(latreuó)*.’” —
> Luke 4:8 (ESV)
>
> Therefore God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts to impurity, to the
> dishonoring of their bodies among themselves, because they exchanged the
> truth about God for a lie and worshiped and **served** *(latreuó)* the
> creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever! —
> Romans 1:25 (ESV)

### Trinitarian Perspectives on Worship

While *latreuō* is typically reserved for God, some Trinitarian scholars point to
specific passages as evidence that Jesus also receives this level of service.
For example, Daniel 7:14 in the Septuagint (LXX) describes the Son of Man being
served (*latreuousin*) by all nations. However, it is important to note that the
Greek Old Testament has different versions. The Old Greek version uses
*latreuousin*, while Theodotion's version uses *douleuousin*. Most modern
translations follow the sense of *douleuousin*, which implies obedient service
rather than exclusive religious worship.

Another point of discussion is Revelation 22:3. The text mentions the throne of
God and of the Lamb and states that "his servants will worship (*latreuō*) him."
Critics may argue that the singular pronoun "him" refers to both God and the
Lamb as a single object of worship. Conversely, others suggest the pronoun
refers specifically to God on the throne. This singular reference may indicate
that service is rendered to the one Deity on the throne, with the Lamb being
present in the shared authority of that throne.

## General Minister Service

> Then he will say to those on his left, "Depart from me, you cursed, into the
> eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. For I was hungry and you
> gave me no food, I was thirsty and you gave me no drink, I was a stranger and
> you did not welcome me, naked and you did not clothe me, sick and in prison
> and you did not visit me."
> >
> Then they also will answer, saying, "Lord, when did we see you hungry or
> thirsty or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison, and did not
> **minister** *(diakoneó)* to you?"
> >
> Then he will answer them, saying, "Truly, I say to you, as you did not do it
> to one of the least of these, you did not do it to me." —
> Matthew 25:41-45 (ESV)

Jesus was not requesting prayer, worship or sacrifice. He was requesting
ministry service.

> “If anyone **serves** *(diakoneó)* me, he must follow me; and where I am, there
> will my servant be also. If anyone **serves** *(diakoneó)* me, the Father will
> honor him.” — John 12:26 (ESV)

Another example where Jesus was "served":

> Then the devil left him, and behold, angels came and were
> **ministering** *(diakoneó)* to him. — Matthew 4:11 (ESV)

The angels did not worship Jesus. They attended to his needs so that he could
survive in the desert.

> Now as they went on their way, Jesus entered a village. And a woman named
> Martha welcomed him into her house.
> >
> But Martha was distracted with much **serving** *(diakoneó)*. And she went up
> to him and said, “Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to
> **serve** *(diakoneó)* alone? Tell her then to help me.” —
> Luke 10:38,40 (ESV)

> Simon's wife's mother lay sick of a fever, and anon they tell him of her. And
> he came and took her by the hand, and lifted her up; and immediately the fever
> left her, and she **ministered** *(diakoneó)* unto them. —
> Mark 1:30-31 (KJV)

> And the twelve summoned the full number of the disciples and said, “It is not
> right that we should give up preaching the word of God to
> **serve** *(diakoneó)* tables.” — Acts 6:2 (ESV)

> So he sent into Macedonia two of them that **ministered** *(diakoneó)* unto
> him, Timotheus and Erastus; but he himself stayed in Asia for a season. —
> Acts 19:22 (KJV)

> At present, however, I am going to Jerusalem **bringing aid** *(diakoneó)* to
> the saints. — Romans 15:25 (ESV)

## Obedient Bondage Service

> these things meant.
> >
> And he said to him, "Your brother has come, and your father has killed the
> fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound."
> >
> But he was angry and refused to go in.
> >
> His father came out and entreated him, but he answered his father, "Look,
> these many years I have **served** *(douleuó)* you, and I never disobeyed your
> command, yet you never gave me a young goat, that I might celebrate with my
> friends."
> >
> — Luke 15:25-29 (ESV)

> They answered him, “We are offspring of Abraham and have never been
> **enslaved** *(douleuó)* to anyone. How is it that you say, ‘You will become

> No one can **serve** *(douleuó)* two masters, for either he will hate the one
> and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other.
> You cannot **serve** *(douleuó)* God and money. — Matthew 6:24 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The New Testament maintains a clear distinction between different types of
service through its choice of Greek vocabulary. Religious worship and sacred
service are denoted by [*latreuó*](#worship-service), a term reserved for God.
In contrast, Jesus and fellow believers are served through
[*diakoneō*](#general-minister-service), which refers to practical ministry and
assistance. Furthermore, [*douleuó*](#obedient-bondage-service) describes the
condition of being in bondage or under the authority of a master. These
linguistic differences ensure that the unique honor due to God is not confused
with the honorable service provided to the Son or to one another.
