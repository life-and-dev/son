# Serving Jesus

Many readers find it confusing when the Bible speaks about serving Jesus and
serving God. Some verses suggest that Jesus came to serve others. Other verses
command believers to serve him. This article explores the specific Greek words
used for "serve" to clarify these different roles. By examining the original
language, one can better understand the unique nature of the service rendered to
God and the service rendered to Jesus.

There are different Greek words that are all translated to the same English word
"serve" in our modern English bibles:

For example, in the same [English Standard Version (ESV)](nature.md#bible-translations)
Jesus said:

> For even the [Son of Man](son-of-man.md) came not to be served but to serve,
> and to give his life as a ransom for many. — Matthew 20:28 (ESV)

but then Jesus also said:

> If anyone serves me, he must follow me; and where I am, there will my servant
> be also. If anyone serves me, the Father will honor him. — John 12:26 (ESV)

Jesus did not contradict himself. This is a result of [confusing translations](nature.md#bible-translations).

## Worship Service

The following scriptures use the Greek word *latreuō*:

> Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has visited and redeemed his
> people... that we, being delivered from the hand of our enemies, might serve
> (*latreuō*) him without fear, in holiness and righteousness before him all
> our days. — Luke 1:68,74-75 (ESV)
>
> And there was a prophetess, Anna, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of
> Asher. She was advanced in years, having lived with her husband seven years
> from when she was a virgin, and then as a widow until she was eighty-four.
> She did not depart from the temple, worshiping (*latreuō*) with fasting and
> prayer night and day. — Luke 2:36-37 (ESV)
>
> And God spoke to this effect, that his offspring would be sojourners in a
> land belonging to others, who would enslave (*douleuō*) them and afflict them
> four hundred years. ‘But the nation that they serve (*douleuō*) I will judge,’
> said God, ‘and after that they shall come out and worship (*latreuō*) me in
> this place.’ — Acts 7:6-7 (ESV)
>
> But this I confess to you, that according to the Way, which they call a sect,
> I worship (*latreuō*) the God of our fathers, believing everything laid down
> by the Law and written in the Prophets. — Acts 24:14 (ESV)
>
> And now I stand here on trial because of my hope in the promise made by God to
> our fathers, to which our twelve tribes hope to attain, as they earnestly
> worship (*latreuō*) night and day. And for this hope I am accused by Jews,
> O king! — Acts 26:6-7 (ESV)
>
> For this very night there stood before me an angel of the God to whom I
> belong and whom I worship (*latreuō*). — Acts 27:23 (ESV)
>
> For God is my witness, whom I serve (*latreuō*) with my spirit in the gospel
> of his Son, that without ceasing I mention you. — Romans 1:9 (ESV)
>
> For we are the circumcision, who worship (*latreuō*) by the Spirit of God and
> glory in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh. — Philippians 3:3
> (ESV)
>
> I thank God whom I serve (*latreuō*), as did my ancestors, with a clear
> conscience, as I remember you constantly in my prayers night and day. — 2
> Timothy 1:3 (ESV)
>
> Therefore they are before the throne of God, and serve (*latreuō*) him day and
> night in his temple; and he who sits on the throne will shelter them with his
> presence. — Revelation 7:15 (ESV)
>
> No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of the
> Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship (*latreuō*) him. —
> Revelation 22:3 (ESV)

The word *latreuō* is always used in connection to worship God and never as a
form of worship service to Jesus. [Jesus was worshipped differently](nature.md#worship),
because this kind of worship service is forbidden to anyone other than God
himself:

> Then Jesus said to him, “Be gone, Satan! For it is written, ‘You shall worship
> the LORD your God and him only shall you serve (*latreuō*).’” — Matthew 4:10
> (ESV)
>
> And Jesus answered him, “It is written, ‘You shall worship the LORD your God,
> and him only shall you serve (*latreuō*).’” — Luke 4:8 (ESV)
>
> because they exchanged the truth about God for a lie and worshiped and served
> (*latreuō*) the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever!
> Amen. — Romans 1:25 (ESV)

### Counter Arguments Regarding Latreuo

Trinitarians often point to Daniel 7:14 and Revelation 22:3 to suggest that Jesus
receives *latreuō*. In the Septuagint (LXX) Old Greek version of
[Daniel 7:14](son-of-man/prophecies.md), it states that all peoples shall serve
(*latreuousin*) the Son of Man. However, Theodotion's version of the same verse
uses *douleuousin* (the word for slave-like service). Most modern English
translations follow the Hebrew/Aramaic text, which uses the word *pelach*.
While *pelach* often refers to religious service, its translation into Greek
was debated even in ancient times.

In Revelation 22:3, the text mentions "the throne of God and of the Lamb" and
states that "his servants will worship him." Some argue that "him" refers to
both God and the Lamb. Others maintain that the singular pronoun "him" refers
specifically to God. This follows the pattern in Revelation where God is the
primary object of *latreuō*.

## General Minister Service

> Then he will say to those on his left, ‘Depart from me, you cursed, into the
> eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. For I was hungry and you
> gave me no food, I was thirsty and you gave me no drink, I was a stranger and
> you did not welcome me, naked and you did not clothe me, sick and in prison
> and you did not visit me.’ Then they also will answer, saying, ‘Lord, when
> did we see you hungry or thirsty or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison,
> and did not minister (*diakoneō*) to you?’ Then he will answer them, saying,
> ‘Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these, you did not
> do it to me.’ — Matthew 25:41-45 (ESV)

Jesus was not requesting prayer, worship or sacrifice. He was requesting
**ministry service**.

> If anyone serves (*diakoneō*) me, he must follow me; and where I am, there
> will my servant be also. If anyone serves (*diakoneō*) me, the Father will
> honor him. — John 12:26 (ESV)

Another example where Jesus was "served":

> Then the devil left him, and behold, angels came and were ministering
> (*diakoneō*) to him. — Matthew 4:11 (ESV)

The angels did not worship Jesus. Instead they attended to his needs so that he
could survive in the desert.

> Now as they went on their way, Jesus entered a village. And a woman named
> Martha welcomed him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, who sat
> at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching. But Martha was distracted
> with much serving (*diakoneō*). And she went up to him and said, “Lord, do
> you not care that my sister has left me to serve (*diakoneō*) alone? Tell her
> then to help me.” — Luke 10:38-40 (ESV)
>
> And when Jesus was come into Peter's house, he saw his wife's mother laid, and
> sick of a fever. And he touched her hand, and the fever left her: and she
> arose, and ministered (*diakoneō*) unto them. — Matthew 8:14-15 (KJV)
>
> And the twelve summoned the full number of the disciples and said, “It is not
> right that we should give up preaching the word of God to serve (*diakoneō*)
> tables.” — Acts 6:2 (ESV)
>
> And having sent into Macedonia two of his helpers, Timothy and Erastus, he
> himself stayed in Asia for a while. — Acts 19:22 (ESV)
>
> At present, however, I am going to Jerusalem bringing aid (*diakoneō*) to the
> saints. — Romans 15:25 (ESV)

## Obedient Bondage Service

> Now his older son was in the field, and as he came and drew near to the house,
> he heard music and dancing. And he called one of the servants and asked what
> these things meant. And he said to him, ‘Your brother has come, and your
> father has killed the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe
> and sound.’ But he was angry and refused to go in. His father came out and
> entreated him, but he answered his father, ‘Look, these many years I have
> served (*douleuō*) you, and I never disobeyed your command, yet you never
> gave me a young goat, that I might celebrate with my friends.’
> — Luke 15:25-29 (ESV)
>
> They answered him, “We are offspring of Abraham and have never been enslaved
> (*douleuō*) to anyone. How is it that you say, ‘You will become free’?”
> — John 8:33 (ESV)
>
> No one can serve (*douleuō*) two masters, for either he will hate the one and
> love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You
> cannot serve (*douleuō*) God and money. — Matthew 6:24 (ESV)

Followers of Jesus are also described as those who serve him with *douleuō*.
This indicates an obedient, slave-like service to him as Lord.

> for he who serves (*douleuō*) Christ in this way is acceptable to God and
> approved by men. — Romans 14:18 (ESV)
>
> Knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward.
> You are serving (*douleuō*) the Lord Christ. — Colossians 3:24 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The [distinction](#serving-jesus) between *latreuō*, *diakoneō*, and *douleuō*
clarifies how Jesus and God are served. Religious [worship service](#worship-service)
is reserved for God alone, while Jesus receives
[ministry service](#general-minister-service) and
[obedient service](#obedient-bondage-service). While some argue that Jesus
receives [worship service](#counter-arguments-regarding-latreuo) in specific
verses, the overall pattern of the New Testament maintains a clear distinction.
Understanding these terms resolves the [apparent contradiction](#serving-jesus)
in how Jesus is served.
