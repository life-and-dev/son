# Serving Jesus

The English word "serve" often obscures the rich variety of meanings found in
the original Greek text of the New Testament. In many modern translations,
distinct concepts such as religious worship, ministerial assistance, and
obedient bondage are all rendered with the same English term. This can lead to
apparent contradictions and misunderstandings regarding the nature of service
rendered to Jesus.

For example, the [English Standard Version (ESV)](nature.md#bible-translations)
records Jesus saying that the [Son of Man](son-of-man.md) came not to be served
but to serve. Yet in other passages, Jesus speaks of the honor bestowed upon
those who serve Him. These statements are not contradictory; rather, they
reflect the use of different Greek verbs that convey specific types of service.
By examining these original words, readers can gain a clearer understanding of
how the early church understood its relationship to both God and Jesus.

## Worship Service

The following scriptures use the Greek word *latreuó*:
>
> “Blessed be the Lord God of Israel... **serve** *(latreuó)* Him without fear,
> in holiness and righteousness before Him all our days. — Luke 1:68,74-75 (ESV)
>
> There was a prophetess, Anna, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of
> Asher... She did not depart from the temple, **worshiping** *(latreuó)* with
> fasting and prayer night and day. — Luke 2:37 (ESV)
>
> God spake on this wise, That his seed should sojourn in a strange land; and
> that they should bring them into bondage
> *[(douleuó)](#obedient-bondage-service)*, and entreat them evil four hundred
> years. And the nation to whom they shall be in bondage
> *[(douleuó)](#obedient-bondage-service)* will I judge, said God: and after
> that shall they come forth, and **serve** *(latreuó)* me in this place. — Acts
> 7:6-7 (KJV)
>
> Paul replied... "But this I confess to you, that according to the way, which
> they call a sect, I **worship** *(latreuó)* the God of our fathers, believing
> everything laid down by the Law and written in the Prophets. — Acts 24:14
> (ESV)
>
> Then Paul stretched out his hand and made his defense... now I stand here on
> trial because of my hope in the promise made by God to our fathers, to which
> our twelve tribes hope to attain, as they earnestly **worship** *(latreuó)*
> night and day. — Acts 26:1,6-7 (ESV)
>
> Paul stood up among them and said... this very night there stood before me an
> angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I **worship** *(latreuó)* ... —
> Acts 27:23 (ESV)
>
> God is my witness, whom I **serve** *(latreuó)* with my spirit in the gospel
> of his Son... — Romans 1:9 (ESV)
>
> We are the circumcision, who **worship** *(latreuó)* by the Spirit of God... —
> Philippians 3:3 (ESV)
>
> I thank God whom I **serve** *(latreuó)*, as did my ancestors, with a clear
> conscience, as I remember you constantly in my prayers night and day. — 2
> Timothy 1:3 (ESV)
>
> Therefore they are before the throne of God, and **serve** *(latreuó)* Him day
> and night in His temple. — Revelation 7:15 (ESV)
>
> No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of the
> Lamb will be in it, and His servants will **worship** *(latreuó)* Him. —
> Revelation 22:3 (ESV)

Some interpreters argue that because the "throne of God and of the Lamb" is
mentioned immediately before "His servants will worship Him" in Revelation 22:3,
the singular pronoun "Him" refers to both God and the Lamb collectively.
However, others point out that "Him" is singular (*autō*), and it naturally
refers to the primary subject, which is God. This distinction is maintained
throughout the book of Revelation, where the Lamb is seen as distinct from the
One who sits on the throne.

The Greek verb *latreuó* is consistently used in the context of religious
worship offered to God. While it appears 21 times in the New Testament, it is
never used to describe a service rendered exclusively to Jesus as a distinct
person. This suggests that the early Christian understanding of *latreuó* was
reserved for the Deity. This is consistent with Jesus' own instruction that only
God should receive this type of service.
>
> Jesus said to him, “Be gone, Satan! For it is written, ‘You shall worship the
> Lord your God and Him only shall you **serve** *(latreuó)*.’” — Matthew 4:9-10
> (ESV)
>
> Jesus answered him, “It is written, “‘You shall [worship](nature.md#worship)
> the Lord your God, and Him only shall you **serve** *(latreuó)*.’” — Luke 4:8
> (ESV)

A notable case is found in the Septuagint (LXX) translation of Daniel 7:14,
where the Son of Man is said to be "served" (*latreuousin*). Trinitarians often
cite this as evidence that Jesus receives divine worship. However, other Greek
versions, such as Theodotion’s, use the word *douleuousin* (from *douleuó*),
which refers to general or political service. The choice of *latreuó* in some
manuscripts may reflect later theological developments rather than the original
intent of the passage, especially considering that Jesus explicitly restricted
*latreuó* to God alone.
>
> Therefore God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts to impurity, to the
> dishonoring of their bodies among themselves, because they exchanged the truth
> about God for a lie and worshiped and **served** *(latreuó)* the creature
> rather than the Creator, Who is blessed forever! — Romans 1:25 (ESV)

## General Minister Service
>
> Then he will say to those on his left, "Depart from me, you cursed, into the
> eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. For I was hungry and you
> gave me no food, I was thirsty and you gave me no drink, I was a stranger and
> you did not welcome me, naked and you did not clothe me, sick and in prison
> and you did not visit me."
> Then they also will answer, saying, "Lord, when did we see you hungry or
> thirsty or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison, and did not **minister**
> *(diakoneó)* to you?"
> Then he will answer them, saying, "Truly, I say to you, as you did not do it
> to one of the least of these, you did not do it to me." — Matthew 25:41-45
> (ESV)

Jesus was not requesting prayer, worship, or sacrifice, but rather ministerial
service. In this context, *diakoneó* refers to practical assistance, such as
providing food, care, or administrative help.
>
> “If anyone **serves** *(diakoneó)* me, he must follow me; and where I am,
> there will my servant be also. If anyone **serves** *(diakoneó)* me, the
> Father will honor him.” — John 12:26 (ESV)

Another example where Jesus was "served" involves the help of angels:
>
> Then the devil left him, and behold, angels came and were **ministering**
> *(diakoneó)* to him *(Jesus)*. — Matthew 4:11 (ESV)

The angels did not offer religious worship to Jesus in this instance. They
attended to his physical needs to sustain him in the wilderness.
>
> Now as they went on their way, Jesus entered a village. And a woman named
> Martha welcomed him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, who sat
> at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching. But Martha was distracted
> with much **serving** *(diakoneó)*. And she went up to him and said, “Lord, do
> you not care that my sister has left me to **serve** *(diakoneó)* alone? Tell
> her then to help me.” — Luke 10:38 (ESV)
>
> Simon's wife's mother lay sick of a fever, and anon they tell him of her. And
> he came and took her by the hand, and lifted her up; and immediately the fever
> left her, and she **ministered** *(diakoneó)* unto them. — Mark 1:30-31 (KJV)
>
> And the twelve summoned the full number of the disciples and said, “It is not
> right that we should give up preaching the word of God to **serve**
> *(diakoneó)* tables.” — Acts 6:2 (ESV)
>
> So he sent into Macedonia two of them that **ministered** *(diakoneó)* unto
> him, Timotheus and Erastus; but he himself stayed in Asia for a season. — Acts
> 19:22 (KJV)
>
> At present, however, I am going to Jerusalem **bringing aid** *(diakoneó)* to
> the saints. — Romans 15:25 (ESV)

## Obedient Bondage Service
>
> Now his older son was in the field, and as he came and drew near to the house,
> he heard music and dancing. And he called one of the servants and asked what
> these things meant.
>
> And he said to him, "Your brother has come, and your father has killed the
> fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound."
>
> But he was angry and refused to go in.
>
> His father came out and entreated him, but he answered his father, "Look,
> these many years I have **served** *(douleuó)* you, and I never disobeyed your
> command, yet you never gave me a young goat, that I might celebrate with my
> friends."
>
> — Luke 15:25-29 (ESV)
>
> They answered him, “We are offspring of Abraham and have never been
> **enslaved** *(douleuó)* to anyone. How is it that you say, ‘You will become
> free’?” — John 8:33 (ESV)

The word *douleuó* denotes service as a slave or a bondservant, characterized by
total obedience and the assignment of personal ownership rights to a master.
>
> No one can **serve** *(douleuó)* two masters, for either he will hate the one
> and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other.
> You cannot **serve** *(douleuó)* God and money. — Matthew 6:24 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The distinction between the Greek words for service clarifies how the early
church understood the roles of God and Jesus. The religious worship of *latreuó*
is reserved for God alone, as demonstrated by [its usage in the New
Testament](#worship-service) and [Jesus' own commands](#worship-service). In
contrast, the service rendered to Jesus is characterized as [ministerial
assistance](#general-minister-service) or [obedient
bondage](#obedient-bondage-service). By recognizing these nuances, readers can
appreciate the specific ways in which believers are called to serve both the
Father and the Son.
