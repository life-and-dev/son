# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

In the Gospel of John, Jesus makes a striking declaration that has sparked
centuries of theological discussion: "I and the Father are one." This claim
often raises questions about the nature of Jesus' identity and His relationship
with God. By examining the context of this statement alongside other biblical
examples of unity, we can gain a clearer perspective on what it means to be
"one" in a scriptural sense.

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30

However, Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When Jesus says two separate
persons are one, he means they are united or "joined together." A group of
people could also be considered "one":

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that **they may
> be one, even as we are one**. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that **they may be one
> even as we are one**, I in them and you in me, that **they may become
> perfectly one**, so that the world may know that you sent me and **loved them
> even as you loved me**. — John 17:23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. Just as the
disciples are distinct individuals yet united as one, so [Jesus and the Father
are distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) yet united as one.

## Unity in Purpose

The original Greek language provides further insight into the nature of this
unity. In John 10:30, the Greek word for "one" is *hen* (ἕν), which is the
neuter form of the word. If Jesus had intended to claim that He and the Father
were the same person, the masculine form *heis* (εἷς) would have been expected.
The use of the neuter *hen* typically signifies a unity of essence, purpose, or
will rather than personal identity.

Scholars often point out that this is the same kind of unity Jesus prays for His
disciples to have in John 17:11, 21-23. In those verses, Jesus uses the same
Greek word *hen* to describe how the disciples should be one, just as He and the
Father are one. This suggests a functional oneness or a perfect agreement in
mission and action. For more detailed analysis, see [Bible Exposition
Commentary](https://versebyversecommentary.com/2017/10/05/john-1030/) and [FAIR
Latter-day
Saints](https://www.fairlatterdaysaints.org/answers/Question:_What_does_John_10:30_have_to_do_with_Trinitarianism%3F).

## Jesus Defends His Claim

When the Jews accused Jesus of blasphemy following His statement, He did not
respond by asserting His ontological equality with God. Instead, He pointed to
the scriptures to show that the term "gods" could be applied to humans to whom
the word of God came:

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world,
> ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?” — John 10:34-36
> (ESV)

Jesus was quoting Psalm 82:6, where God addresses human judges or rulers. By
using this defense, Jesus clarified that His claim to be "one with the Father"
or the "Son of God" was not a claim to be the Almighty God Himself, but rather
an assertion of His divine commission and unique relationship with the Father.

> I said, “You are gods, sons of the Most High, all of you; nevertheless, like
> men you shall die, and fall like any prince.” — Psalm 82:6-7 (ESV)

## Humans Can Also Be One With Each Other

### One Flesh

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh;
> she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and **they shall become one flesh**. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Also, Jesus quoted this:

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your
> hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of
> creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is
> **joined to his wife**, and **the two are united into one**.’ Since **they
> are no longer two but one**, let no one split apart what God has **joined
> together**.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)
>
> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute **becomes one
> body** with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.” — 1
> Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

> But he who is joined to the Lord **becomes one spirit** with him. — 1
> Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend **who is as your own soul**... — Deuteronomy
> 13:6 (ESV)
>
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, **the soul of Jonathan was knit
> to the soul of David**, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took
> him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then
> Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. —
> 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
>
> Now the full number of those who believed were of **one heart and soul**, and
> no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they
> had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The statement "I and the Father are one" is a declaration of [unity in
purpose](#unity-in-purpose) and mission rather than a claim of ontological
identity. Jesus clarified this by [defending His claim](#jesus-defends-his-claim)
using scripture and pointing out that even [humans can also be one With each
other](#humans-can-also-be-one-with-each-other) in various spiritual and
physical ways without becoming the same person.
