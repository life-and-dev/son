# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

When Jesus declared that he and the Father are one, he made one of the most
debated statements in the Bible. Many people believe this is a direct claim to
being the same person or having the same essence as God. However, a closer look
at the Greek text and other biblical examples of "oneness" suggests a different
interpretation. This article explores the various ways the Bible uses this
concept to describe relationships and unity.

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

However, Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When Jesus says two separate
persons are one, he means they are united or joined together. A group of people
could also be considered one.

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be
> one, even as we are one. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one
> even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly
> one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you
> loved me. — John 17:22-23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. Just as the
disciples are distinct individuals yet united as one, so are
[Jesus and the Father distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) yet united as one.

## Functional Unity

The Greek word used for "one" in John 10:30 is *hen*, which is the neuter form.
If Jesus had intended to say that he and the Father were the same person, he
would have used the masculine form, *heis*. The use of the neuter *hen* points to
a unity of purpose, mission, or action. This is often called functional unity.

When the religious leaders accused Jesus of making himself God, he responded by
quoting Psalm 82:6.

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world,
> ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?”
> — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

Jesus pointed out that even human judges were called "gods" because they
represented God. He argued that his claim to be the "Son of God" was consistent
with his role as the one sent by the Father.

## One Flesh

Humans can also be one with each other in various ways. One example is the
union between a husband and wife.

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh;
> she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Also, Jesus quoted this:

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your
> hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of
> creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is joined
> to his wife, and the two are united into one.’ Since they are no longer two
> but one, let no one split apart what God has joined together.”
> — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)
>
> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute becomes one body
> with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.”
> — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

## One Spirit

The Bible also describes a spiritual oneness between a believer and the Lord.

> But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him.
> — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

## One Soul

Oneness can also describe an intense bond of friendship or a community of
believers.

To emphasize the closeness of a friend, the Law describes them as being as your
own soul.

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend who is as your own soul entices you secretly,
> saying, ‘Let us go and serve other gods’... — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)

The relationship between David and Jonathan is another powerful example of this
kind of unity.

> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to
> the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him
> that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan
> made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul.
> — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)

The early church also demonstrated a collective oneness in their shared life and
mission.

> Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no
> one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they had
> everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus and the Father are one describes a
[unity of purpose and mission](#functional-unity) rather than a shared
ontological identity. Just as a husband and wife become [one flesh](#one-flesh),
or believers become [one spirit](#one-spirit) with the Lord, Jesus is one with
the Father in his work and message. This [oneness](#one-soul) is the same kind
of unity that Jesus prayed for his disciples to experience with one another and
with God.
