# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

When Jesus declared that he and the Father were one, it sparked an immediate and intense reaction from those who heard him. This statement has been at the center of theological debates for centuries, with various interpretations about what this oneness actually means. Does it imply an identical nature, a shared identity, or a profound unity of purpose? By examining the context and how the Bible describes oneness in other relationships, the depth of this claim becomes clearer.

## Oneness of Purpose

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

### Linguistic Distinction

The Greek word used for one in this verse is *hen*, which is in the neuter form. This grammatical choice is significant because it typically denotes a unity of essence, purpose, or action rather than a personal identity. If Jesus had intended to claim that he and the Father were the same person, the masculine form *heis* would have been expected. This distinction suggests that Jesus was speaking of a perfect union in will and mission.

### Response to Blasphemy Charges

When the religious leaders accused Jesus of blasphemy for making himself equal to God, Jesus responded by appealing to the scriptures. He pointed out that the law even referred to human judges as gods to whom the word of God came.

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’? If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world, ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?” — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

By quoting Psalm 82:6, Jesus argued that if those who represented God's authority could be called gods, then he was certainly justified in calling himself the Son of God. His defense focused on his consecration and being sent by the Father, emphasizing his divine mission rather than ontological identity.

### Human Expressions of Oneness

Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When Jesus says two separate persons are one, he means they are united or joined together. A group of people could also be considered one.

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are one. — John 17:11 (ESV)

> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you loved me. — John 17:23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. Just as the disciples are distinct individuals yet united as one, [Jesus and the Father are distinct individuals](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) who are united as one.

## Humans Can Also Be One With Each Other

### One Flesh

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Also, Jesus quoted this:

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is joined to his wife, and the two are united into one.’ Since they are no longer two but one, let no one split apart what God has joined together.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)

> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute becomes one body with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.” — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

> But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him. — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

> “If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the wife you embrace or your friend who is as your own soul entices you secretly, saying, ‘Let us go and serve other gods,’ which neither you nor your fathers have known,” — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)

> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)

> Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim of Jesus to be one with the Father describes a profound unity of purpose and mission rather than a numerical identity of personhood. This is supported by the [linguistic use of the neuter form](#linguistic-distinction) for the word one in the original Greek text. Jesus defended his statements by [referring to the delegated authority](#response-to-blasphemy-charges) given to humans in the scriptures. Similar [expressions of oneness](#human-expressions-of-oneness) are found throughout the Bible to describe [spiritual unity](#one-spirit) and [deep personal bonds](#one-soul) between individuals.
