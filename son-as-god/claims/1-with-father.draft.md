# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

The statement that Jesus and the Father are one is often used to support the
idea that they are the same being. However, the Bible provides many examples of
unity that do not imply ontological identity. By exploring these various forms
of oneness, a clearer understanding of the relationship between Jesus and the
Father can be achieved.

## The Unity of the Father and the Son

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

The Greek word for "one" in this verse is the neuter *hen*, which signifies
unity of purpose, action, or will. It is not the masculine *heis*, which would
indicate ontological identity or personhood. This same kind of unity is
described when Jesus prays for his followers:

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be
> one, even as we are one. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one
> even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly
> one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you
> loved me. — John 17:22-23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. Just as the
disciples are distinct individuals, yet united as one, so are Jesus and the
Father distinct, yet united as one. This is explained further in
[Jesus and the Father distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md).

Furthermore, when the Jews accused Jesus of blasphemy for this claim, he
defended himself by pointing to the delegation of authority:

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the
> world, ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?”
> — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

## Humans Can Also Be One With Each Other

The Bible uses the concept of oneness to describe various human relationships
where two or more distinct entities are considered "one."

### One Flesh

The most common example of two distinct persons becoming one is found in
marriage.

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man. Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my
> flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-25 (ESV)

Jesus also quoted this passage to emphasize the permanence of the marital
union:

> But from the beginning of creation, ‘God made them male and female.’
> ‘Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and the two shall become one flesh.’ So they are no longer two but one
> flesh. What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate.
> — Mark 10:6-9 (ESV)

The apostle Paul also uses this imagery when discussing sexual immorality:

> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute becomes one body
> with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.”
> — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

Believers are also said to be united with Christ in a spiritual sense.

> But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him.
> — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

Oneness is also used to describe deep friendship and the unity of the early
church.

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend who is as your own soul...
> — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
>
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to
> the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him
> that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan
> made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul.
> — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
>
> Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no
> one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they
> had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus and the Father are one does not mean they are the same
person or being. Biblical unity often refers to a
[union of purpose and action](#the-unity-of-the-father-and-the-son) rather than
ontological identity. This type of oneness is observed in [marriage](#one-flesh),
[spiritual union](#one-spirit), and [close personal relationships](#one-soul).
When Jesus spoke of being one with the Father, he was describing a unique and
perfect unity that he also [desired for his followers](#the-unity-of-the-father-and-the-son).
