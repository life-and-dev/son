# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

The statement where Jesus claims to be one with the Father is often cited as
evidence for his divinity. This claim appears in the Gospel of John and has been
interpreted in various ways throughout church history. By examining the biblical
context and the original language, one can gain a deeper understanding of what
Jesus intended to communicate about his relationship with God.

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

However, Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When he says two separate persons
are one, he means they are united or joined together. A group of people could
also be considered one:

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be
> one, even as we are one. — John 17:11 (ESV)

> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one
> even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly
> one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you
> loved me. — John 17:23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. Just as the
disciples are distinct individuals, yet united as one, [Jesus and the Father
are distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md), yet united as one.

## Linguistic Distinction

In the original Greek of John 10:30, the word for "one" is the neuter *hen*,
rather than the masculine *heis*. This distinction is significant because *heis*
would imply that Jesus and the Father are the same person (ontological
identity), whereas *hen* signifies a unity of essence, purpose, or action. This
same neuter form is used in John 17:11, 21-23 to describe the unity of
believers, who clearly remain distinct individuals.

## Response to Blasphemy Charges

When the religious leaders accused Jesus of blasphemy for this claim, he
responded by quoting Psalm 82:6:

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world,
> ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?” — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

Jesus pointed out that even humans to whom the word of God came were called
"gods" in the Law. He argued that his claim to be the "Son of God" was even more
appropriate given his consecration and mission.

## Human Expressions of Oneness

Biblical unity is not limited to the relationship between God and Jesus. The
scriptures provide several examples of how humans can be considered "one" with
each other or with the Lord.

### One Flesh

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man.
> >
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh;
> she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
> >
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Also, Jesus quoted this:

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your
> hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of
> creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is
> joined to his wife, and the two are united into one.’ Since they are no
> longer two but one, let no one split apart what God has joined together.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)

> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute becomes one body
> with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.” — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

> But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him. — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend who is as your own soul entices you secretly,
> saying, ‘Let us go and serve other gods,’ which neither you nor your fathers
> have known... — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
> >
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to
> the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him
> that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan
> made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
> >
> Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no
> one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they
> had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus is one with the Father signifies a [unity of purpose and
essence](#linguistic-distinction) rather than an ontological identity as the
same person. This is supported by the [linguistic use of the neuter
*hen*](#linguistic-distinction) and Jesus' own [defense against blasphemy
charges](#response-to-blasphemy-charges). Similar expressions of unity are found
in [human marriage](#one-flesh), [spiritual union with the Lord](#one-spirit),
and [intimate friendships](#one-soul), all of which describe distinct
individuals who are joined together as one.
