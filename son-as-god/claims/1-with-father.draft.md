# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

Unity is a central theme in the Bible, often used to describe deep relationships.
When Jesus stated that he and the Father are one, he used language that has
sparked centuries of theological debate. Understanding the nature of this
oneness requires looking at how the same concept is applied to human
relationships and shared missions throughout the scriptures.

## Unity in Purpose

Jesus stated that he and the Father are one.

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

Jesus did not claim to be the same person as the Father. When he described two
separate persons as one, he referred to them being united or joined together. A
group of people can also be considered one in this manner.

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be
> one, even as we are one. — John 17:11 (ESV)
> >
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one
> even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly
> one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you
> loved me. — John 17:23 (ESV)

Jesus did not ask the Father to turn his disciples into gods. The disciples
remained distinct individuals while being united as one. Similarly, Jesus and
the Father are [distinct individuals](../../son-of-man/distinct.md), yet they
are united in purpose and action.

## Oneness in Human Relationships

### Physical Unity

The Bible describes marriage as two people becoming one flesh.

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man.
> >
> Then the man said, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh;
> she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man."
> >
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed.
> — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Jesus later quoted this passage to emphasize the permanence of the marital bond.

> But Jesus responded, "He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your
> hard hearts. But 'God made them male and female' from the beginning of
> creation. 'This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is joined
> to his wife, and the two are united into one.' Since they are no longer two
> but one, let no one split apart what God has joined together."
> — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)

The apostle Paul also used this language to describe physical union.

> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute becomes one body with
> her? For, as it is written, "The two will become one flesh."
> — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### Spiritual and Emotional Unity

Oneness can also be spiritual or emotional in nature.

> But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him.
> — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

Close friendships and community bonds are sometimes described as being of one
soul or one heart.

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend who is as your own soul...
> — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
> >
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to
> the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him
> that day and would not let him return to his father's house. Then Jonathan
> made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul.
> — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
> >
> Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no
> one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they had
> everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Addressing the Accusation of Blasphemy

Critics often point to the immediate reaction of the Jewish leaders as evidence
that Jesus was claiming deity. They picked up stones to kill him, stating that
he was making himself God.

> The Jews answered him, "It is not for a good work that we are going to stone
> you but for blasphemy, because you, being a man, make yourself God."
> — John 10:33 (ESV)

However, Jesus immediately corrected this misunderstanding. He asked why they
accused him of blasphemy for saying "I am the Son of God" when their own
scriptures used the word "gods" for human rulers to whom the word of God came.

> Jesus answered them, "Is it not written in your Law, 'I said, you are gods'? If
> he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world,
> 'You are blaspheming,' because I said, 'I am the Son of God'?"
> — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

By quoting Psalm 82:6, Jesus clarified that his claim to be "one" with the
Father was a statement of divine commission and shared purpose rather than an
assertion of being the Almighty himself.

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus and the Father are one is a declaration of
[perfect unity in purpose and action](#unity-in-purpose). This type of oneness
is consistent with biblical descriptions of [marital bonds](#physical-unity) and
[spiritual fellowship](#spiritual-and-emotional-unity) among believers. When
confronted with accusations of stoning, Jesus defended his language by
appealing to [scriptural precedents](#addressing-the-accusation-of-blasphemy) for
delegated authority.
