# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

The statement "I and the Father are one" is one of the most discussed verses in
the New Testament. While it has sparked centuries of theological debate, the
concept of "oneness" appears throughout the scriptures in various contexts.
Understanding how this term is applied to relationships is essential for
grasping the depth of Jesus' message to his followers. This concept applies to
both divine and human relationships.

Jesus told his disciples:
>
> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)
>
## The Meaning of Oneness

The Greek word for "one" in this verse is the neuter *hen*. This is distinct
from the masculine *heis*. If Jesus had used the masculine form, it could imply
that he and the Father are the same person. However, the use of the neuter form
indicates a unity of essence, purpose, or mission rather than a numerical
identity.

Many scholars and commentaries, including the [REV
Bible](https://www.revbible.com/John/10/30) and the [NET Bible
notes](https://netbible.org/bible/John+10), suggest that this oneness refers to
a functional unity. This means Jesus and the Father are perfectly united in
their work and will. When the religious leaders accused Jesus of making himself
God, he defended his statement by quoting Psalm 82:6.
>
> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’? If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world, ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?” — John 10:34-36 (ESV)
>
Jesus pointed out that the scriptures used the term "gods" to refer to human
judges because they represented God's authority. His claim to be "one" with the
Father was a claim of divine representation and shared mission, not an
assertion
of being the Father himself.

## Unity in the Prayer of Christ

Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When he says two separate persons are
one, he means they are united. They are joined together in purpose and mission.
A group of people can also be considered one.
>
> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that **they may be one, even as we are one**. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that **they may be one even as we are one**, I in them and you in me, that **they may become perfectly one**, so that the world may know that you sent me and **loved them even as you loved me**. — John 17:22-23 (ESV)
>
Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. The disciples
are distinct individuals who are united as one. In the same way, [Jesus and the
Father are distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md). They are two separate
beings
who are united in one purpose.

## Models of Human Unity

The scriptures provide several examples of how distinct individuals can be
described as one. These models help to explain the type of unity Jesus shared
with the Father.

### One Flesh
>
> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and **they shall become one flesh**. And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed.
>
> — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)
>
Jesus later quoted this passage when discussing marriage.
>
> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is **joined to his wife**, and **the two are united into one**.’ Since **they are no longer two but one**, let no one split apart what God has **joined together**.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)
>
The apostle Paul also used this concept to describe various types of unions.
>
> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute **becomes one body** with her? For, as it is written, “The two will become one flesh.” — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)
>
### One Spirit

Believers are also said to have a spiritual union with the Lord.
>
> But he who is joined to the Lord **becomes one spirit** with him. — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)
>
### One Soul

Unity can also be expressed through a shared soul or heart.
>
> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the wife you embrace or your friend **who is as your own soul**... — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
>
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, **the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David**, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
>
> Now the full number of those who believed were of **one heart and soul**, and no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)
>
## Conclusion

The biblical concept of oneness describes a deep unity of purpose and mission
rather than ontological identity. Jesus' claim to be one with the Father in
[John 10:30](#the-meaning-of-oneness) is supported by his own
defense in [John 10:34-36](#the-meaning-of-oneness) and his prayer
for his disciples in [John 17:22-23](#unity-in-the-prayer-of-christ).
Furthermore, the scriptures use similar language to describe the [union of
marriage](#one-flesh), [spiritual union](#one-spirit), and [deep
friendship](#one-soul), all of which involve distinct individuals becoming "one"
in a functional or relational sense.
