# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

The statement "I and the Father are one" has been at the center of theological
debates for centuries. Does it imply an identity of being? Or does it point
to something else entirely? By examining how the Bible uses the concept of
"oneness" in other contexts, we can gain a clearer perspective on what Jesus
was communicating to his listeners.

## Unity of Purpose

Jesus told His disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

However, Jesus does not claim to be the Father. When Jesus says two separate
persons are one, he means they are united or "joined together." A group of
people could also be considered "one":

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that **they may
> be one, even as we are one**. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that **they may be one
> even as we are one**, I in them and you in me, that **they may become
> perfectly one**, so that the world may know that you sent me and **loved them
> even as you loved me**. — John 17:23 (ESV)

Jesus was not asking the Father to turn his disciples into gods. The disciples
are distinct individuals. They are also united as one. In the same way,
[Jesus and the Father are distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md). Yet they
remain united as one.

The apostle Paul used similar language when describing his ministry alongside
Apollos. He explained that although they had different roles, they shared the
same goal:

> He who plants and he who waters are one, and each will receive his wages
> according to his labor. — 1 Corinthians 3:8 (ESV)

Just as Paul and Apollos are two distinct men with a single purpose, Jesus and
the Father are distinct beings who work in perfect harmony.

## Human Unity

### One Flesh

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept
> took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the
> LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the
> man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh;
> she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his
> wife, and **they shall become one flesh**. And the man and his wife were both
> naked and were not ashamed. — Genesis 2:21-24 (ESV)

Also, Jesus quoted this:

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your
> hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of
> creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is
> **joined to his wife**, and **the two are united into one**.’ Since **they
> are no longer two but one**, let no one split apart what God has **joined
> together**.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)
>
> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute **becomes one
> body** with her? For, as it is written, *“The two will become one flesh.”*
> — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

> But he who is joined to the Lord *(Jesus Christ)* **becomes one spirit**
> with him. — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the
> wife you embrace or your friend **who is as your own soul**...
> — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
>
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, **the soul of Jonathan was
> knit to the soul of David**, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And
> Saul took him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house.
> Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own
> soul. — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
>
> Now the full number of those who believed were of **one heart and soul**,
> and no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but
> they had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The biblical concept of "oneness" often refers to a shared purpose or a deep
spiritual connection rather than a literal identity of being. Jesus and the
Father are one in their [divine purpose](#unity-of-purpose). This type of
unity is also seen in [human relationships](#human-unity), where individuals
remain distinct while being perfectly united in heart, soul, and spirit.
