# Jesus Claims to Be One With the Father

When Jesus declared that he and the Father are one, he made a statement that has sparked centuries of theological debate. Some interpret this as a claim of ontological identity, while others see it as a description of perfect unity in purpose and action. Understanding the biblical context and the original language reveals a profound concept of oneness that extends beyond the person of Jesus to his followers and the very nature of human relationships.

## Unity of Purpose

Jesus told his disciples:

> I and the Father are one. — John 10:30 (ESV)

Jesus did not claim to be the same person as the Father. When he described two separate persons as one, he referred to being united or joined together. A group of people can also be considered one in this manner.

> Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that **they may be one, even as we are one**. — John 17:11 (ESV)
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that **they may be one even as we are one**, I in them and you in me, that **they may become perfectly one**, so that the world may know that you sent me and **loved them even as you loved me**. — John 17:22-23 (ESV)

The request to the Father was not to turn the disciples into gods. The disciples remained distinct individuals while being united as one. Similarly, [Jesus and the Father are distinct](../../son-of-man/distinct.md), yet united as one.

## A Defense Against Ontological Identity

Critics often point to John 10:30 as evidence that Jesus claimed to be God by essence. However, Jesus himself provided a defense against this interpretation just moments later. When accused of blasphemy for "making himself God," he reminded his accusers of the language used in the Law.

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’? If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world, ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?” — John 10:34-36 (ESV)

By quoting Psalm 82:6, Jesus clarified that his claim to be one with the Father or the "Son of God" was consistent with how God delegates authority and titles to humans. The Greek word for "one" in John 10:30 is the neuter *hen*, which signifies unity in purpose or action, much like the unity of the church described in John 17. If Jesus had intended to claim ontological identity or personhood, the masculine *heis* would have been the grammatically appropriate choice.

## Biblical Examples of Oneness

### One Flesh

> So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man.
>
> Then the man said, “This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.”
>
> Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and **they shall become one flesh**. And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed.
>
> — Genesis 2:21-25 (ESV)

Jesus also quoted this principle when discussing marriage.

> But Jesus responded, “He wrote this commandment only as a concession to your hard hearts. But ‘God made them male and female’ from the beginning of creation. ‘This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is **joined to his wife**, and **the two are united into one**.’ Since **they are no longer two but one**, let no one split apart what God has **joined together**.” — Mark 10:5-9 (NLT)
>
> Or do you not know that he who is joined to a prostitute **becomes one body** with her? For, as it is written, *“The two will become one flesh.”* — 1 Corinthians 6:16 (ESV)

### One Spirit

> But he who is joined to the Lord **becomes one spirit** with him. — 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)

### One Soul

> If your brother, the son of your mother, or your son or your daughter or the wife you embrace or your friend **who is as your own soul**... — Deuteronomy 13:6 (ESV)
>
> As soon as he had finished speaking to Saul, **the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David**, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. And Saul took him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house. Then Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul. — 1 Samuel 18:1-3 (ESV)
>
> Now the full number of those who believed were of **one heart and soul**, and no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own, but they had everything in common. — Acts 4:32 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus is one with the Father in John 10:30 describes a [unity of purpose](#unity-of-purpose) and action rather than personal identity. This [biblical oneness](#biblical-examples-of-oneness) is a recurring theme that characterizes the relationship between [believers](#one-spirit), [married couples](#one-flesh), and [close friends](#one-soul). Just as the disciples were invited to be one as Jesus and the Father are one, this unity represents a profound communion of heart and spirit that preserves the distinct identity of each individual.
