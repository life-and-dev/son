# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

The question of who can receive glory often leads to a search for consistency
within the biblical text. In the Old Testament, God declares that His glory
belongs to Him alone and will not be shared with another. Yet, the New Testament
records multiple instances where Jesus is glorified or claims that the hour for
his glorification has arrived. This exploration examines how these statements
coexist and what they reveal about the relationship between the Father and the
Son.

## God's Glory and Idols

The Hebrew scriptures emphasize that God is unique and does not share His
essence or glory with idols.

> I am [the LORD](/god/father/name#yhvh), that is My name;
> And **My [glory](/bible/concepts/glory) I will not give to another**, nor
> My [praise](/god/praise) to carved images.
> — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translation of this verse uses the word *heterō* (a
form of *heteros*) for "another." This term typically refers to another of a
different kind. In this context, it refers to idols or carved images which are
fundamentally different from the living God. Scholarly analysis, such as that
found in the NET Bible notes and the REV Bible commentary, suggests that this
does not preclude God from sharing glory with His Son. The Son is considered to
be of the same kind as the Father rather than a "different kind" like an idol.

Yet Jesus made statements that appear to claim glorification for himself.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
> glorified."
> — John 12:23 (ESV)

Jesus also stated his status in a prayer to the Father.

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and **I am
> [glorified](/bible/concepts/glory)** in them.
> — John 17:10 (ESV)

When viewed [out of context](../../nature.md#concerns), it seems like Jesus
contradicts Isaiah. To solve this contradiction, some argue this proves that
Jesus is [the LORD](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament). This argument is
often supported by the confusion caused by removing the divine name in [bible
translations](../../nature.md#bible-translations). The divine name is replaced
with "the LORD" which is the same title used for Jesus.

In the context of Isaiah 42:8, the issue was that God did not want to share His
glory with foreign idols or carved images. However, as the Father of Jesus, He
has no problem with anyone glorifying His Son. Glorifying the Son automatically
glorifies the Father.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify
> him at once.
> — John 13:32 (ESV)

## The Relationship Between Father and Son

A healthy relationship between a parent and child involves mutual support rather
than competition. A human father is often honored when his son is praised. The
success of the son reflects well on the upbringing provided by the father.

> All may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father.
> — John 5:23 (ESV)

Both the statements in Isaiah and those made by Jesus are consistent when
understood through this relationship. In the prayer recorded in John 17, Jesus
explained this dynamic.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> "Father, the hour has come; **glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You**,
> since [You have given him authority over all flesh](/god/son/essence/as-god/lord#jesus-got-all-authority),
> [to give eternal life to all whom You have given him](/god/son/essence/as-god/similarities/act#jesus-judge-as-the-father)."
> — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

Giving glory to Jesus results in glory being given to God the Father. Jesus
emphasized that seeking his own glory would be meaningless.

> Jesus answered, "**If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father
> Who glorifies me**, of Whom you say, 'He is our God.'"
> — John 8:54 (ESV)

For the Father to glorify the Son, [Jesus must be distinct from God the
Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md).

## Conclusion

The evidence suggests that the glorification of Jesus does not contradict God's
declaration in Isaiah ([evidence](#gods-glory-and-idols)). While God does not
share His glory with idols, He actively glorifies His Son
([evidence](#gods-glory-and-idols)). This process ultimately brings glory back
to the Father ([further reading](#the-relationship-between-father-and-son)).
The distinction between the Father and the Son is essential for this mutual
glorification to occur ([evidence](#the-relationship-between-father-and-son)).
This relationship demonstrates that honoring the Son is a way of honoring the
Father who sent him ([further reading](#the-relationship-between-father-and-son)).
