# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

Does Jesus claiming to be glorified contradict God's statement that He will not
share His glory with another? This question touches on the very nature of the
relationship between God the Father and His Son. Some argue that this proves
Jesus is God Himself. Others see a beautiful harmony where the Father is honored
through the Son. Exploring the context of these statements reveals how both are
true without contradiction.

## The Warning Against Idolatry

The Old Testament contains a strong declaration from God regarding His unique
status. This is often quoted to suggest that no one else can ever receive glory.

> I am [the LORD](../../shema.md), that is My name;
> And **My [glory](../../word.md#glory) I will not give to another**, nor My
> [praise](../../shema.md#praise) to carved images.
> — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

In the context of Isaiah 42, the issue was that God did not want to share His
glory with foreign idols. These "carved images" were inanimate objects that
people worshipped instead of the Creator. The Greek translation of the Old
Testament (Septuagint) uses the word *heteros* for "another." This term refers
to someone of a different kind. This suggests that God will not share His glory
with different kinds of gods, like idols. However, it does not necessarily
exclude sharing glory with His own Son who represents Him.

## Jesus Receives Glory

During his ministry, Jesus spoke openly about being glorified. This was not a
claim of self-exaltation but a fulfillment of God's plan.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
> glorified. — John 12:23 (ESV)

Jesus also stated that he was glorified in his followers. This glory was
directly linked to his relationship with the Father.

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and **I am [glorified](../../word.md#glory)**
> in them. — John 17:10 (ESV)

[Some might argue](../../nature.md#majority) that this contradicts Isaiah. They
might use this as proof that Jesus is [the LORD](../../shema.md). This argument
is often supported by [the confusion caused by removing God's name](../../shema.md).
In many translations, the divine name is replaced with "the LORD," which is also
a title used for Jesus.

## Glorifying the Son Glorifies the Father

As the Father of Jesus, God has no problem with people glorifying His Son. This
is because glorifying the Son automatically glorifies the Father as well. The
two are not in competition.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify
> him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

A human parent is not diminished when their child is honored. Instead, the
parent is also glorified because they raised the child. In the same way,
honoring the Son is a way of honoring the Father who sent him.

> All may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. — John 5:23 (ESV)

In his final prayer, Jesus made this relationship clear. He asked the Father to
glorify him so that he could, in turn, glorify the Father.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> “Father, the hour has come; **glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You**,
> since [You have given him authority over all flesh](../claims.md), [to give
> eternal life to all whom You have given him](../similarities/act.draft.md).
> — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

## Distinction Between Father and Son

Jesus himself taught that it is useless to glorify oneself. True glory must
come from God.

> Jesus answered, “**If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father
> Who glorifies me**, of Whom you say, ‘He is our God.’ — John 8:54 (ESV)

For the Father to glorify the Son, [Jesus has to be distinct from God the
Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md). This relationship shows that the glory
given to Jesus does not take away from God. Instead, it serves to fulfill God's
purposes and bring Him further praise.

## Conclusion

The statements of Isaiah and Jesus are both correct when understood in their
proper context. God will not share His glory with idols or different kinds of
gods. However, He actively glorifies His Son because [honoring the Son is how
people honor the Father](#glorifying-the-son-glorifies-the-father). This
process does not create a contradiction but demonstrates a perfect [distinction
and harmony](#distinction-between-father-and-son) between the two. The glory
of the Son is ultimately for [the warning against idolatry](#the-warning-against-idolatry)
to be fulfilled by pointing all people back to the one true God.
