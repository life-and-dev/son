# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

The Old Testament declares that God will not share His glory with another. Yet
the New Testament frequently speaks of Jesus being glorified. Does this mean
Jesus is the same person as God, or is there a biblical way for God to share His
glory with His Son? This article explores how Jesus' claims to be glorified
align with the monotheistic statements of the Old Testament.

## God and His Glory

The Bible emphasizes the unique glory of God. In the book of Isaiah, God makes a
strong statement about His name and His glory.

> I am the LORD, that is My name; and My glory I will not give to another, nor
> My praise to carved images. — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

This verse is often used to argue that if Jesus receives glory, he must be the
LORD. The argument suggests that since God does not share His glory, any
recipient of divine glory must be God Himself. This perspective is sometimes
reinforced by the common practice of replacing the divine name with "the LORD"
in English translations. This can lead to confusion because Jesus is also
frequently called "Lord" in the New Testament.

## The Context of Shared Glory

When the statement in Isaiah is read in context, the primary focus is on the
rejection of idols. God refuses to share His glory with "carved images" or false
gods. However, the relationship between God and His Son presents a different
dynamic. Jesus frequently spoke of his own glorification.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
> glorified." — John 12:23 (ESV)

Jesus also stated that he is glorified in his followers.

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and I am glorified in them.
> — John 17:10 (ESV)

These statements do not necessarily contradict Isaiah. The Greek translation of
the Old Testament, the Septuagint, uses the word *heteros* for "another" in
Isaiah 42:8. This term often refers to another of a different kind. This
implies that God will not give His glory to idols or beings of a different
nature. It does not strictly forbid sharing glory with His Son, who is the
"image of the invisible God."

## A Reciprocal Relationship

The glorification of Jesus is not a competition with the Father. Instead, it is
a reciprocal relationship where the glory given to the Son ultimately honors
the Father.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify
> him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

Just as a human father is honored when his son is praised for his character or
achievements, God the Father is glorified when people honor His Son. Jesus
explicitly stated that the purpose of his glorification was to bring glory to
the Father.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You.”
> — John 17:1 (ESV)

Jesus received his authority and his glory from the Father. He did not claim
glory for himself independently.

> Jesus answered, “If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father who
> glorifies me, of whom you say, ‘He is our God.’” — John 8:54 (ESV)

For one person to glorify another, they must be distinct individuals. The fact
that the Father glorifies the Son proves that they are separate beings. This
relationship allows all people to honor the Son just as they honor the Father
without violating the principle that God is one.

> ...that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not
> honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. — John 5:23 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The glorification of Jesus does not contradict the declaration that God will not
give His glory to another. While God refuses to share His praise with idols, He
actively glorifies His Son. This shared glory [reflects a reciprocal
relationship](#a-reciprocal-relationship) where honoring the Son brings glory
to the Father. The distinction between the two is maintained because [it is the
Father who bestows glory](#the-context-of-shared-glory) upon the Son.
Ultimately, the [Son's glory serves to point back to the
Father](#a-reciprocal-relationship), fulfilling the purpose for which he was
sent.
