# The Glorification of Jesus and the Glory of God

The concept of glory is central to biblical theology, often associated with the unique majesty of the Creator. In the book of Isaiah, a declaration is made concerning the exclusivity of God's glory. However, the New Testament records multiple instances where Jesus speaks of his own glorification. This article examines these seemingly conflicting statements and explores how the glorification of the Son relates to the honor of the Father.

## The Exclusivity of God's Glory

The prophet Isaiah recorded a statement from God regarding His glory and its exclusivity.

> I am [the LORD](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament), that is My name;
> And **My [glory](../../nature.md#gods-glory) I will not give to another**, nor My [praise](../../nature.md) to carved images. — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

## Jesus and the Claim of Glory

In contrast to the statement in Isaiah, Jesus made several claims regarding his own glorification.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. — John 12:23 (ESV)

Jesus also stated his shared glory with the Father in his prayer.

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and **I am [glorified](../../nature.md#gods-glory)** in them. — John 17:10 (ESV)

## Understanding the Context

When taken [out of context](../../nature.md#concerns), these statements may appear to contradict the declaration in Isaiah. Some interpreters use this perceived contradiction as evidence that Jesus is [the LORD](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament). This argument is often supported by the [confusion caused by removing the divine name](../../nature.md#bible-translations) and replacing it with the title "the LORD". This title is also frequently applied to Jesus in the New Testament.

In the context of Isaiah 42:8, the focus is on the refusal of God to share His glory with foreign idols or carved images. As the Father of Jesus, God permits the glorification of His Son because it serves to glorify the Father.

The Septuagint (LXX) translation of Isaiah 42:8 uses the Greek word *heterō* for "another". This term refers to someone of a different kind, such as the idols mentioned in the same verse. In contrast, when referring to the Holy Spirit as "another" Helper, the New Testament uses *allos*, indicating someone of the same kind. This linguistic distinction suggests that God's refusal to give glory to "another" applies to false deities rather than the Son who shares His nature or purpose.

## Reciprocal Glorification

The relationship between the Father and the Son involves a mutual glorification that does not violate the exclusivity mentioned in Isaiah.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

The glorification of a son can be seen as reflecting honor upon the father. When a son is honored, it often suggests that the father raised him well.

> that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. — John 5:23 (ESV)

In the context of John 17, Jesus prayed for this mutual glorification.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, “Father, the hour has come; **glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You**, since [You have given him authority over all flesh](../../nature.md#jesus-inheritance), [to give eternal life to all whom You have given him](../similarities/act.md#jesus-judge-as-the-father). — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

When glory is given to Jesus, it is ultimately directed toward God the Father.

## The Source of Glory

Jesus emphasized that his glory does not originate from himself, but is granted by the Father.

> Jesus answered, “**If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father Who glorifies me**, of Whom you say, ‘He is our God.’ — John 8:54 (ESV)

The act of being glorified by the Father implies that [Jesus is distinct from God the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md).

## Critiques and Defenses

Critics often argue that because God claims He will not give His glory to another, Jesus must be the same person as the Father to receive that glory. However, the reciprocal nature of their glorification suggests a relationship between two distinct parties rather than a single being. If Jesus were the same person as the Father, the statement that the Father glorifies the Son would be redundant.

Furthermore, the New Testament indicates that Jesus received glory as a result of his obedience and the authority granted to him by the Father. This delegation of glory does not diminish God's own majesty but rather demonstrates His power through His Son.

## Conclusion

The biblical record indicates that the [exclusivity of God's glory](#the-exclusivity-of-gods-glory) is primarily directed against idols. The [glorification of Jesus](#jesus-and-the-claim-of-glory) does not contradict this principle but rather [serves to glorify the Father](#reciprocal-glorification) through their unique relationship. The [distinction between the Father and the Son](#the-source-of-glory) is maintained by the fact that the Son's glory is granted by the Father.
