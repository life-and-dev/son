# Jesus Shares God's Glory

Does God share His glory? At first glance, the Bible seems to give two different
answers. A famous prophecy in Isaiah declares that God will not give His glory
to another, yet Jesus frequently spoke of sharing in the Father's glory.
Exploring this apparent contradiction reveals a profound understanding of the
relationship between the Creator and His Son.

## Isaiah's Declaration

The prophet Isaiah records a powerful statement from God regarding His unique
status and glory.

> I am the LORD, that is My name;
> And My glory I will not give to another,
> Nor My praise to carved images. — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

In this context, the LORD (YHVH) is emphasizing His exclusivity against idols
and "carved images." The Hebrew word for "another" in this passage refers to a
different kind of being. Specifically, it points to foreign gods or inanimate
idols that have no claim to divine honor.

## Jesus's Claims

Despite the absolute tone of Isaiah's prophecy, Jesus often spoke about his own
glory and his expectation to be glorified by the Father.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
> glorified." — John 12:23 (ESV)

Later, in his prayer to the Father, Jesus reaffirms this shared glory:

> All mine are yours, and yours are mine, and I am glorified in them.
> — John 17:10 (ESV)

Without understanding the context, it might seem as though Jesus is
contradicting the words of Isaiah. Some use this apparent contradiction to
argue that Jesus must be the LORD Himself. They suggest that if God shares His
glory with no one, and yet Jesus is glorified, then Jesus and the LORD must be
the same being. This confusion is often compounded when modern translations
replace God's personal name with the title "the LORD," which is also used for
Jesus.

## Sharing Glory

The context of Isaiah 42:8 reveals that God's refusal to share glory is
specifically directed at foreign idols. God does not want His people to
attribute His works to carved images. However, as the Father of the Son, God has
no conflict in glorifying Jesus. When the Son is glorified, it does not take
away from the Father. Instead, it brings further honor to Him.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in himself, and glorify
> him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

A human father is not diminished when his son is honored. Rather, the father is
often glorified through the success and character of his child. In the same
way, the glorification of Jesus is an extension of the Father's own glory.

## Honoring the Son

Jesus taught that the Father's intent is for the Son to receive the same level
of honor as the Father receives.

> that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not
> honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. — John 5:23 (ESV)

In the Greek Septuagint (LXX) translation of Isaiah 42:8, the word used for
"another" is *heteros*. This word means "another of a different kind." This
suggests that while God will not give His glory to a different kind of being
like an idol, He can share it with His Son, who is of the same kind. This
distinction allows for Jesus to be glorified without violating the principle
established in Isaiah.

## Distinguishing the Son

Jesus himself made it clear that he did not seek his own glory independently of
the Father.

> Jesus answered, "If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father who
> glorifies me, of whom you say, 'He is our God.'" — John 8:54 (ESV)

This statement shows that [Jesus is distinct from God the Father][1]. For the
Father to glorify the Son, they must be separate enough for one to bestow honor
upon the other. Jesus prayed for this glorification so that he could, in turn,
bring glory back to the Father.

> Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you,
> since [you have given him authority over all flesh][2], [to give eternal life
> to all whom you have given him][3]. — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The relationship between the Father and the Son is one of mutual glorification
rather than competition for honor.

* Isaiah 42:8 protects God's glory from being attributed to [idols][4].
* Jesus's claims to glory are consistent with his role as the Son who
  [represents the Father][5].
* The Father [bestows glory][6] upon the Son to ultimately [glorify
  Himself][7].

By honoring the Son, believers are fulfilling the Father's own desire and
participating in the divine order of glory.
