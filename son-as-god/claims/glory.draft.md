# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

The question of whether God shares His glory is a central theme in biblical
theology. While the Old Testament declares that God will not give His glory to
another, Jesus often spoke of being glorified. This article explores how these
statements harmonize without contradiction.

## Glory and Idolatry

> I am [the LORD](../../shema.md#yhvh), that is My name;
> And **My [glory](../../nature.md#gods-glory) I will not give to another**,
> nor My [praise](../../nature.md#worship) to carved images.
>
> — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

In the context of Isaiah 42:8, the issue was that God does not want to share His
glory with foreign idols or carved images. The Greek translation of the Old
Testament, the Septuagint, provides further insight into this statement. It uses
the word *heteros* for "another," which refers to someone of a different kind,
such as a false god or an idol.

In contrast, when the New Testament speaks of "another" in a way that implies
equality or shared nature, it often uses the word *allos*. By using *heteros*,
Isaiah 42:8 emphasizes that God will not yield His glory to different,
competing deities. This distinction suggests that God sharing glory with His
Son, who is of the same kind, does not violate His declaration. Reputable
sources like the [REV Bible commentary](https://www.revbible.com/isaiah/42/8)
and [NET Bible notes](https://netbible.org/bible/Isaiah+42) support this nuanced
understanding of the Greek text.

## Shared Glory

Yet Jesus said:

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
> glorified." — John 12:23 (ESV)

And:

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and
> **I am [glorified](../../nature.md#gods-glory)** in them. — John 17:10 (ESV)

Out of context, it seems like Jesus contradicts Isaiah. To solve this
contradiction, some would argue this proves that Jesus is
[the LORD](../../shema.md#yhvh). This argument is supported by the confusion
caused by removing God's name, which is replaced with "the LORD," the same title
used for Jesus.

## Unity in Honor

As the Father of Jesus, God is pleased when others glorify His Son. In biblical
terms, to be glorified means to be honored, praised, or revealed in one's true
splendor. When the Son is glorified, it ultimately brings honor back to the
Father.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and
> glorify him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

A human father is often honored when his son receives praise. This recognition
reflects positively on the father for his role in his son's life. Similarly, the
relationship between God and Jesus allows for a shared honor that does not
compete with God's unique status.

> All may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. — John 5:23 (ESV)

In the context of John 17, Jesus prayed for this mutual glorification.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
> “Father, the hour has come; **glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You**,
> since [You have given him authority over all flesh](../../nature.md#jesus-got-all-authority),
> [to give eternal life to all whom You have given him](../similarities/act.md#jesus-judge-as-the-father)."
> — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

## Source of Glory

Jesus emphasized that seeking one's own glory is not the goal. Instead, true
glory comes from the Father.

> Jesus answered, “**If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father
> Who glorifies me**, of Whom you say, ‘He is our God.’” — John 8:54 (ESV)

Because the Father is the one who bestows glory upon Jesus, the two must be
distinct individuals. [Jesus has to be distinct from God the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md)
to be glorified by Him. One person does not typically speak of being glorified
by themselves in such a manner.

## Conclusion

The apparent contradiction between Isaiah and Jesus is resolved when considering
the context of idolatry versus the relationship between the Father and the Son.
God does not share His glory with idols ([Glory and Idolatry](#glory-and-idolatry)),
but He glorifies His Son because it ultimately brings glory back to Himself
([Unity in Honor](#unity-in-honor)). Jesus receives his glory from the Father
rather than seeking it for himself ([Source of Glory](#source-of-glory)), which
maintains the distinction between them ([Shared Glory](#shared-glory)).
