> I am [the LORD](/ofgod/father/i-am-who-i-am.draft.md), that is My name;<br/>
> And **My [glory](/word/terms/glory.md) I will not give to another**, nor My [praise](/word/terms/praise.draft.md) to carved images.
> 
> -- Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

Yet Jesus said:

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. -- John 12:23 (ESV)

and

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and **I am [glorified](/word/terms/glory.md)** in them. -- John 17:10 (ESV)

Out of context, it seems like Jesus contradicts Isaiah. To solve this "contradiction", some argue that this proves Jesus is [the LORD](/ofgod/father/i-am-who-i-am.draft.md). This argument is strengthened when God's name is replaced with "the LORD", a title also applied to Jesus.

In Isaiah 42:8, God contrasts himself with carved images and refuses praise for idols. The verse does not say that God cannot glorify a human servant. In the immediate context, God calls his servant the one whom he upholds and delights in (Isaiah 42:1). John likewise presents Jesus' glory as received from the Father and directed back to the Father.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify him at once. -- John 13:32 (ESV)

> All may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. -- John 5:22 (ESV)

Therefore, both Isaiah and Jesus are correct. In John 17, Jesus prayed a few verses earlier:

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, “Father, the hour has come; **glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You**, since [You have given him authority over all flesh](/son/drafts/the-authority-of-jesus.draft.md#given-authority-not-rival-authority), [to give eternal life to all whom You have given him](/son/son-as-god/similarities/act.md). -- John 17:1-2 (ESV)

Jesus himself says that it is useless to glorify oneself:

> Jesus answered, “**If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father Who glorifies me**, of Whom you say, ‘He is our God.’ -- John 8:54 (ESV)

Therefore, [Jesus has to be distinct from God the Father](/son/son-of-man/distinct.md) to be glorified by Him.

# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

Jesus speaks of glory with the Father, especially in John 17:5. Trinitarians reasonably read this as evidence that the Son personally shared divine glory before creation. The wording deserves careful attention.

The same prayer distinguishes Jesus from the Father. Jesus asks the Father to glorify him and calls the Father “the only true God” (John 17:1, 3, 5). God can give glory to his chosen servant and Messiah without becoming that servant. Jesus also says that the Father gave him glory for the unity of his followers (John 17:22).

The strongest contextual reading keeps both points. Jesus has a unique, God-given glory and mission. But a glory received from, requested of, and directed back to the Father does not identify Jesus as YHVH.
