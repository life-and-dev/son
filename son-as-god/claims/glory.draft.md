# Jesus Claims to Be Glorified

The concept of glory is central to understanding the relationship between God
and Jesus. In the Old Testament, God makes a definitive statement about His
glory that seems to create a conflict with the later claims of Jesus. This
article explores how Jesus can be glorified without contradicting the exclusive
claims of the Almighty.

## The Context of God's Glory in Isaiah

In the book of Isaiah, God declares His unique status and His refusal to share
the honor due to Him.

> I am the LORD, that is My name; and My glory I will not give to another, nor
My praise to carved images. — Isaiah 42:8 (NKJV)

This statement was originally made in a context where God was distinguishing
Himself from foreign idols. These "carved images" were inanimate objects that
people mistakenly worshipped. God was asserting that the honor belonging to the
Creator should never be directed toward created things.

The Greek translation of the Old Testament provides further clarity. The word
used for "another" in this verse is *heterō*. This term specifically refers to
another of a different kind. In this context, it refers to idols which are
fundamentally different from God. In contrast, the Greek word *allos* refers to
another of the same kind. This linguistic distinction allows for God to share
His glory with someone of His own kind, such as His Son, while strictly
withholding it from idols.

## Jesus Receives Glory From the Father

Despite the declaration in Isaiah, the New Testament frequently speaks of Jesus
being glorified. Jesus himself pointed toward a specific time when this would
occur.

> And Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be
glorified." — John 12:23 (ESV)

Jesus also expressed that he was already glorified through his followers. He
acknowledged that everything he possessed ultimately came from the Father.

> All mine are Yours, and Yours are mine, and I am glorified in them. — John
17:10 (ESV)

When read without considering the broader context, it might seem that Jesus is
claiming a status that Isaiah 42:8 forbids. Some interpret this as proof that
Jesus must be the LORD Himself. This argument often relies on the idea that if
God does not give His glory to another, and Jesus has that glory, then Jesus
cannot be "another."

The apostles later confirmed that it was God who bestowed this glory upon
Jesus. This confirms that the glory was not something Jesus seized for himself,
but something given to him by the Father.

> ...who through him are believers in God, who raised him from the dead and
gave him glory, so that your faith and hope are in God. — 1 Peter 1:21 (ESV)

## Glorifying the Son Glorifies the Father

The apparent contradiction is resolved when observing how the Father and Son
interact. Jesus prayed for glory specifically so that he could reflect it back
to his Father.

> When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said,
“Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify You,
since [You have given him authority](../../nature.md#jesus-inheritance) over all
flesh, to [give eternal life](../similarities/act.md#jesus-judge-as-the-father)
to all whom You have given him.” — John 17:1-2 (ESV)

The Father has no problem with the glorification of His Son. This is because
the honor given to the Son is not in competition with the Father. Instead, it
reflects back to the Father.

> If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in Himself, and glorify
him at once. — John 13:32 (ESV)

A human example helps illustrate this principle. A father is honored when his
son is praised for his character or achievements. The praise given to the son
is a testament to the father who raised him. In the same way, the Father is
glorified when people recognize the authority and work of the Son.

> ...that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does
not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. — John 5:23 (ESV)

Jesus explicitly stated that he did not seek his own glory. He recognized that
true glory must come from the One who sent him. This relationship demonstrates
that [Jesus serves God](../../nature.md#jesus-serves-god) and is distinct from
Him.

> Jesus answered, “If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father
who glorifies me, of whom you say, ‘He is our God.’” — John 8:54 (ESV)

The Father is the source of the glory that Jesus receives. This distinction
shows that Jesus is not a separate god competing for attention. He is the
authorized representative and Son who brings ultimate honor to the Father.
Ultimately, the glory of Jesus is a reflection of the
[glory of God](../../nature.md#gods-glory).

## Conclusion

The declaration that God will not give His glory to another remains true in the
sense that He will not share it with
[idols](#the-context-of-gods-glory-in-isaiah). However, God chose to glorify His
Son as a means of magnifying
[His own glory](#jesus-receives-glory-from-the-father). By honoring the Son,
believers are fulfilling the purpose of the Father and acknowledging the unique
relationship
[between them](#glorifying-the-son-glorifies-the-father).
