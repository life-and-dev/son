# Jesus' Claim to Be in God

When Jesus spoke of being "in" the Father, he used a term that has puzzled many
readers for centuries. Does this imply a literal, physical presence within God,
or is it a description of a unique and powerful relationship? By exploring the
context of these claims, we can better understand how this bond extends not only
to Jesus but also to every believer.

## The Promise of Fellowship in John 14

> I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you. Yet a little while and
> **the world will see me no more, but you will see me**. Because I live, you
> also will live. — John 14:18-19 (ESV)

Jesus promised to return one day to collect his followers so that they can live
with him and the Father. This return ensures that those who belong to God will
never be truly alone.

> In that day you will know that **I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in
> you**. — John 14:20 (ESV)

When the Father, Jesus, and believers live "in" each other, it signifies a close
communion or fellowship. Living in unity with the Father and the Son implies
that a person's character and actions begin to reflect divine qualities.

> Whoever has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me. And he who
> loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and **manifest myself
> to him**.”
>
> Judas (not Iscariot) said to him, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest
> yourself to us, and not to the world?”
>
> Jesus answered him, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father
> will love him, and **we will come to him and make our home with him**.”
>
> — John 14:21-23 (ESV)

## The Analogy of the Vine and Branches

Jesus continued to explain this relationship by using the metaphor of a vine and
its branches. This illustration shows how essential it is to remain connected to
the source of life.

> “I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. Every branch in me that
> does not bear fruit he takes away, and every branch that does bear fruit he
> prunes, that it may bear more fruit. Already you are clean because of the word
> that I have spoken to you. **Abide in me, and I in you**. As the branch cannot
> bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, **neither can you, unless
> you abide in me**. **I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me
> and I in him**, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do
> nothing. If anyone does not abide in me he is thrown away like a branch and
> withers; and the branches are gathered, thrown into the fire, and burned.
> **If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, ask whatever you wish, and it
> will be done for you.** By this my Father is glorified, that you bear much
> fruit and so prove to be my disciples. As the Father has loved me, so have I
> loved you. **Abide in my love**. If you keep my commandments, you will abide
> in my love, just as I have kept my Father’s commandments and abide in his
> love.” — John 15:1-10 (ESV)

When believers "abide in" Jesus or Jesus "lives in" them, it does not mean they
literally become Jesus. In the same way, Jesus being "in" God does not
necessarily mean he is ontologically the same as God. Instead, it describes a
relationship where Jesus submits to the Father's authority and does his will.
Through this connection, people are able to know the Father by observing how
Jesus represented him. This concept of oneness is often compared to the unity
between a husband and wife or the unity of the early church (Acts 4:32).

## Covenant Language and Fellowship

The term "in" carries specific meaning when used in a biblical context. It often
refers to a deep, personal connection rather than a physical location.

> When used in the sense of “in God,” or “in Christ,” the word “in” refers to a
> close communion, a tight fellowship. It was part of the covenant language of
> the day, when people spoke of being either “in” or “cut off from” the
> covenant.
>
> — Morgridge, pp. 116 and 117
> — Racovian Catechism, pp. 142 and 143
> — [Spirit & Truth Fellowship International](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/verses/john-14-11)

## A Prayer for Perfect Unity

Jesus emphasized this concept further during his prayer to the Father. He asked
for his followers to experience the same kind of unity that he shares with God.

> I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through
> their word, that **they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I
> in you, that they also may be in us**, so that the world may believe that you
> have sent me.
>
> The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that **they may be one
> even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly
> one**, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you
> loved me.
>
> Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, **may be with me
> where I am, to see my glory that you have given me** because you loved me
> before the foundation of the world. O righteous Father, even though the world
> does not know you, **I know you**, and these know that you have sent me. **I
> made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known**, that the
> love with which you have loved me **may be in them, and I in them**.
>
> — John 17:20-26 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus is "in" God describes a [perfect unity of purpose and
fellowship](#a-prayer-for-perfect-unity) rather than a literal physical
presence. This relationship is mirrored in the [analogy of the vine and
branches](#the-analogy-of-the-vine-and-branches), illustrating how believers
must remain connected to Christ. Ultimately, this indwelling is rooted in
[covenant language](#covenant-language-and-fellowship) that emphasizes a deep,
personal communion between God, his Son, and his people as [promised in
John 14](#the-promise-of-fellowship-in-john-14).
