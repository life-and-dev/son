# Is Jesus Omnipresent?

The question of whether Jesus is omnipresent has profound implications for
understanding his nature. Many point to his promises of being with his followers
as evidence of divinity, yet other passages suggest physical limitations. This
article examines the context of these statements to clarify the biblical
portrayal of Jesus' presence.

> For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I ***among*** them.
— Matthew 18:20 (ESV)

And

> Behold, **I am with you always, to the end of the age**.
— Matthew 28:20 (ESV)

Some argue that it is physically [impossible for a human to be
omnipresent](../../son-of-man.md). If Jesus intended to communicate that he
would always be present with billions of Christians globally, it would suggest
a divine nature.

However, this perspective may overlook scriptures indicating that [Jesus could
not be present everywhere](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent).
The accounts in John 17 describe him leaving his disciples and promising to
return. [Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often suggest that while his human
body was not omnipresent, he remained spiritually present, similar to [the Holy
Spirit](../../trinity/holy-spirit.md).

## Jesus Meant to Be Spiritually Present

The concept of being spiritually present can be interpreted in several ways.
The Apostle Paul made a similar statement in his letter to the Corinthians.

> **When you are assembled** in the name of the Lord Jesus and **my spirit is
present**, with the power of our Lord Jesus, you are to deliver this man to
Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in the
day of the Lord. — 1 Corinthians 5:4-5 (ESV)

Paul was not considered divine or omnipresent. Therefore, some scholars suggest
that the words of Jesus should not be taken literally as a claim of physical or
magical presence among all Christians at once.

The intent behind these statements may involve shared purpose or delegated
authority.

* This could mean that when an assembly agrees with the Holy Spirit, they act
  in the same spirit as Jesus and Paul. In this sense, they are united as one.
* It might also indicate that Jesus and Paul passed their approval to their
  disciples. This would allow them to make decisions as if their leaders were
  present to authorize the actions.

## Jesus Meant It Literally

Other views reject the idea that these statements are merely spiritual or
idiomatic. They argue there is no explicit evidence that Jesus was speaking
figuratively or referring to a spiritual realm in these instances.

### Matthew 18 20

> For where two or three are gathered together in My name, **I am there
*in the midst* of them.**” — Matthew 18:20 (NKJV)

When viewed in isolation, this verse might suggest that Jesus magically appears
whenever Christians gather. However, the context involves instructions on how
to handle a brother who sins. One approach involves gathering two or three
witnesses to confront the individual in the [authority](../../nature.md) of
Jesus. In such cases, Jesus would be among those witnesses, supporting their
pursuit of reconciliation.

> “If your brother sins against you:
>
> 1. go and tell him his fault, between you and him alone. If he listens to
you, you have gained your brother.
> 2. But if he does not listen, take **one or two** others along with you,
that every charge may be established by the evidence of **two or three
witnesses**.
> 3. If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church.
>
> And if he refuses to listen even to the church, let him be to you as a
Gentile and a tax collector.
>
> Truly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven,
and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Again I say to
you, **if two of you** agree on earth about anything they ask, it will be
done for them by my Father in heaven. For where **two or three are
gathered** in my *name ([authority](../../nature.md))*, there am I
***[among](https://biblehub.com/greek/3319.htm)*** them.”
— Matthew 18:15-20 (ESV)

Some interpreters suggest the topic shifts from church discipline to general
gathering for blessings. This view is often influenced by paragraph breaks and
headings added by modern translators which were not in the original
manuscripts. These additions can sometimes obscure the intended message.

In this context, binding and loosing likely refer to intercession for those who
sin. When earlier steps fail, the final resort is to gather and plead with the
Father in heaven for the sinner. This is the strategy Jesus authorized and
supported.

The discussion continues as Peter asks a related question about forgiveness.

> Then Peter came up and said to him, “Lord, how often will my brother sin
against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?”
> Jesus said to him, “I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven
times. Therefore the kingdom of heaven may be compared to...”
— Matthew 18:21-23 (ESV)

Jesus answered the question and provided a parable that reinforces the original
topic of confronting and forgiving sin.

### Matthew 28 20

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them [in the name
of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit](../../trinity/baptism-formula.md), teaching them to observe all that
I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.
— Matthew 28:19-20 (ESV)

In this passage, Jesus expressed concern for the [eternal
salvation](../../son-of-man/why.md) of all people. The goal of making
disciples was to enable them to eventually live in the presence of Jesus.
Jesus had previously mentioned that he would return to gather his followers.
Although he is not physically present currently, his future return is a
central hope for believers.

A parallel account in the book of Acts provides further clarity.

> So when they had come together, they asked him, “Lord, will you at this
time restore the kingdom to Israel?” He said to them, “It is not for you to
know times or seasons that [the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) has
fixed by [His own authority](../../son-of-man/limitations.md). But you will
receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my
witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the
earth.”
>
> And when he had said these things, as they were looking on, he was lifted
up, and a cloud took him out of their sight. And while they were gazing into
heaven as he went, behold, two men stood by them in white robes, and said,
“Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was
taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go
into heaven.” — Acts 1:6-11 (ESV)

In this account, Jesus referred to the Father as a distinct person with supreme
authority. The disciples did not seem to view Jesus' promise of being with them
as a claim of omnipresence. Instead of questioning why he was leaving after
promising to stay, they watched him ascend. The two men in white robes
confirmed his departure and promised his return in a similar manner.

### Acts 18 9 10

> And the Lord said to Paul one night in a vision, “Do not be afraid, but go
on speaking and do not be silent, for I am ***with*** you, and no one will
attack you to harm you, for **I have many in this city** who are my people.”
— Acts 18:9-10 (ESV)

This scripture does not demonstrate omnipresence in a divine sense. Jesus was
assuring Paul of his protection and the presence of a supportive community in
the city. It was an assurance that Paul was not working alone.

## Conclusion

The evidence suggests that the promises of Jesus to be with his followers are
best understood within their specific contexts. These statements often relate
to [shared spiritual purpose](#jesus-meant-to-be-spiritually-present),
[authorized church discipline](#matthew-18-20), or the [assurance of future
restoration](#matthew-28-20). When compared with the accounts of his
[ascension and promised return](#acts-18-9-10), it appears that the biblical
writers did not view these as claims of omnipresence but as assurances of his
ongoing support and eventual return.
