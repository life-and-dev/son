# Jesus Claims to Be Omnipresent

The concept of omnipresence is the ability to be everywhere at once. This
attribute is traditionally reserved for the Divine. Yet several statements
attributed to Jesus in the New Testament suggest he might share this quality.
If Jesus can be present with any two or three gathered in his name, does this
prove he is God? This article explores the context of these claims and how they
were understood by his earliest followers.

> For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I ***among*** them.
> — Matthew 18:20 (ESV)

And

> Behold, **I am with you always, to the end of the age**.
> — Matthew 28:20 (ESV)

It is physically [impossible for a human to be omnipresent](../../son-of-man/human.md).
If Jesus really meant that he would always be present with billions of
Christians all over the world, it would mean that Jesus has to be divine.

However, this argument ignores the scriptures that state that [Jesus could
not be present everywhere](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent)
and that he was leaving his disciples behind and would be returning (John 17).
[Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) might argue that his [human body, which
cannot be omnipresent](../../son-of-man/limitations.md), would leave and
return. They suggest he would still be spiritually present in the same manner
as [the Holy Spirit](../../trinity/holy-spirit.md).

## Spiritual Presence

Being spiritually present is a vague statement. Even Paul mentioned this
concept.

> **When you are assembled** in the name of the Lord Jesus and **my spirit is
> present**, with the power of our Lord Jesus, you are to deliver this man to
> Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in the
> day of the Lord. — 1 Corinthians 5:4-5 (ESV)

And

> For though I am absent in body, yet **I am with you in spirit**, rejoicing to
> see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ.
> — Colossians 2:5 (ESV)

Paul is not divine, and he was not omnipresent. Therefore, some suggest that
like the words of Paul, the words of Jesus should not be considered literally.
They suggest he would not be physically among or with all Christians at all
times.

The intent behind the statements of Jesus and Paul may be understood in several
ways.

* One interpretation is that both Jesus and Paul meant that when the assembly
  is in agreement with God's Holy Spirit, they act in the same spirit of the
  Lord Jesus or Paul. This [unites them as one](./1-with-father.draft.md), as
  if Jesus or Paul were actually present.
* Another possibility is that both Jesus and Paul passed on their authority to
  their disciples. This enabled the disciples to make decisions as if Jesus or
  Paul were present to give approval.

## Literal Presence

Some reject the argument that the words of Jesus should be considered
spiritually. They argue there is no evidence he was speaking in idioms, nor did
he mention the spiritual realm.

### Matthew 18

> For where two or three are gathered together in My name, **I am there *in the
> midst* of them.**” — Matthew 18:20 (NKJV)

Without context, it may seem as though Jesus magically appears in the midst of
every Christian gathering. The context shows Jesus was addressing how to deal
with a brother who sins. One solution is to gather two or three witnesses to
confront him in the name or authority of Jesus. In such a case, Jesus is among
those witnesses by supporting their actions.

> “If your brother sins against you:
>
> 1. go and tell him his fault, between you and him alone. If he listens to you,
>    you have gained your brother.
> 2. But if he does not listen, take **one or two** others along with you, that
>    every charge may be established by the evidence of **two or three
>    witnesses**.
> 3. If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church.
>
> And if he refuses to listen even to the church, let him be to you as a Gentile
> and a tax collector.
>
> Truly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and
> whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Again I say to you, **if
> two of you** agree on earth about anything they ask, it will be done for them
> by my Father in heaven. For where **two or three are gathered** in my
> *name (authority)*, there am I ***[among](https://biblehub.com/greek/3319.htm)***
> them.” — Matthew 18:15-20 (ESV)

Some argue the topic changes from confronting sin to claiming blessings. This is
often because modern Bible translations separate these verses with paragraph
breaks or unrelated titles. These additions were not part of the original text.

Jesus continued the topic of intercessory prayer. He meant for believers to
intercede for those who sin. Intercession is to plead with the Father in
heaven. When the first three steps fail, the last alternative is to gather and
intercede for the sinner. This is the strategy that Jesus authorized and
supported.

The topic continues in the following verses. Peter asked a question relating to
the same subject.

> Then Peter came up and said to him, “Lord, how often will my brother sin against
> me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?”
> Jesus said to him, “I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven times.
> Therefore the kingdom of heaven may be compared to...”
> — Matthew 18:21-23 (ESV)

Jesus answered the question and explained his answer with a parable. This
parable is still related to the original topic of confronting sin.

### Matthew 28

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of
> the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all
> that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the
> age. — Matthew 28:19-20 (ESV)

In this context, Jesus addressed the salvation of all people. The purpose of
making disciples of all nations was to allow them to live forever in his
presence. [Jesus previously said that he would return to gather his
own](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent) (Matthew 24:23-44).
Although he is currently not physically present, he will be in the future.

Furthermore, a parallel witness in the book of Acts states:

> So when they had come together, they asked him *(Jesus)*, “Lord, will you at
> this time restore the kingdom to Israel?”
> He said to them, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that
> [the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) has fixed by His own authority. But
> you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be
> my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the
> earth.”
>
> And when he had said these things, as they were looking on, he was lifted up,
> and a cloud took him out of their sight. And while they were gazing into
> heaven as he went, behold, two men stood by them in white robes, and said,
>
> “Men of Galilee, why do you stand **looking into heaven**?
> This Jesus, who **was taken up from you** into heaven, **will come in the same
> way** as you saw him go into heaven.” — Acts 1:6-11 (ESV)

In this passage, Jesus referred to [his Father as a distinct
person](../../son-of-man/distinct.md). The disciples did not question Jesus
about a perceived contradiction between his promise to be with them and his
physical departure. This suggests they did not understand his statement as a
claim of literal omnipresence. The appearance of the two men in white robes
confirmed that Jesus had physically departed.

### Acts 18

> And the Lord said to Paul one night in a vision, “Do not be afraid, but go on
> speaking and do not be silent, for I am ***with*** you, and no one will attack
> you to harm you, for **I have many in this city** who are my people.”
> — Acts 18:9-10 (ESV)

This scripture does not prove the omnipresence of Jesus. Jesus did not
physically appear at the trial of Paul. Jesus was assuring Paul that he was not
alone and that he would protect him through his people in the city.

## Conclusion

The claims of Jesus's omnipresence primarily stem from interpretation of
specific promises. These statements may refer to his [delegated authority and
spiritual presence](#spiritual-presence) rather than literal
location. His presence among witnesses specifically relates to [the exercise of
his name and authority](#matthew-18). The physical departure of Jesus from the
world further indicates that his [presence is not literal or
physical](#matthew-28). Additionally, his appearance in visions confirms he is
[not always physically present](#acts-18) in every location.
