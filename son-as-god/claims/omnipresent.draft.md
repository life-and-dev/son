# Jesus Claims to Be Omnipresent

The concept of omnipresence refers to the ability to be present everywhere at
the same time. This attribute is traditionally reserved for God. Many believe
that Jesus claimed this divine trait when he promised to be with his followers
until the end of time. This article explores whether these statements were
intended as claims of divinity or if they had a different, more specific meaning
within their biblical context.

> For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I ***among*** them.
> — Matthew 18:20 (ESV)

and

> Behold, **I am with you always, to the end of the age**.
> — Matthew 28:20 (ESV)

Omnipresence is a term used to describe God's ability to be everywhere at once.
It is physically impossible for a human to be omnipresent. If Jesus really meant
that he would always be present with billions of Christians all over the world,
it would mean that Jesus has to be divine.

However, this argument ignores the scriptures that state that [Jesus could not
be present everywhere](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent).
It also overlooks that he was leaving his disciples behind and would be
returning (John 17). Some would argue that Jesus meant that his human body would
leave and return. They believe he would also be spiritually present like the
Holy Spirit.

Trinitarians often argue that the presence of Jesus is mediated through the
Holy Spirit. They suggest that the Holy Spirit, being the third person of the
Trinity, brings the presence of both the Father and the Son to believers. This
view allows them to maintain that Jesus is both a man in heaven and omnipresent
on earth. However, this interpretation must be balanced with the clear biblical
statements about the physical departure and future return of Jesus.

## Jesus Meant It Literally

Some reject the argument that the words of Jesus should be considered
spiritually. They see no evidence that he was speaking in idioms. He also did
not mention anything about the spiritual realm in these contexts.

### Matthew 18 20

> For where two or three are gathered together in My name, **I am there *in the
> midst* of them.**” — Matthew 18:20 (NKJV)

Out of context, it seems like Jesus magically appears in the midst every time
Christians gather. The context here is that Jesus was addressing how they
should deal with a brother who sins against you. One solution would be to
gather two or three witnesses to confront him in the authority of Jesus. In
such a case, Jesus would be among those witnesses. He would be on their side.

> “If your brother sins against you:
>
> 1. go and tell him his fault, between you and him alone. If he listens to
>    you, you have gained your brother.
> 2. But if he does not listen, take **one or two** others along with you,
>    that every charge may be established by the evidence of **two or three
>    witnesses**.
> 3. If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church.
>
> And if he refuses to listen even to the church, let him be to you as a
> Gentile and a tax collector.
>
> Truly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven,
> and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Again I say to
> you, **if two of you** agree on earth about anything they ask, it will be
> done for them by my Father in heaven. For where **two or three are gathered**
> in my name, there am I ***[among](https://biblehub.com/greek/3319.htm)***
> them.”
>
> — Matthew 18:15-20 (ESV)

Some would argue that the topic has changed from gathering to confront sin to
gathering to claim blessings from God. The reason they say so is that most
modern Bible translations separate this verse from the next verse with a
paragraph break. They often separate it even further by including an unrelated
title between them. This was not part of the original text. This is a common
practice by Bible translators to make their copies more readable. Ironically,
it adds confusion to the intended message.

Jesus did not switch to prosperity preaching. Instead he meant to intercede for
those who sin when using the terms bind and loose. Intercession is to plead
with the Father in heaven. When the first three steps failed, then the last
alternative is to gather to intercede for the sinner. This is the strategy that
Jesus authorized and supports.

The topic is not finished yet. In the next verse Peter asked a valid question
relating to the same topic:

> Then Peter came up and said to him, “Lord, how often will my brother sin
> against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?”
> Jesus said to him, “I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven
> times. Therefore the kingdom of heaven may be compared to...”
>
> — Matthew 18:21-23 (ESV)

Not only did Jesus answer his question, but he also explained his answer with a
parable. This parable is still related to the original topic of how to
confront sin.

### Matthew 28 20

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of
> the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe
> all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end
> of the age. — Matthew 28:19-20 (ESV)

In this context Jesus was concerned about the eternal salvation of people. The
purpose of making disciples of all nations was to make it possible for them to
live forever in the presence of Jesus. Jesus previously said that he would
return to gather his own (Matthew 24:23-44). Although he is currently not
physically present, he will be in the future. This is something to look
forward to (Matthew 19:28-30).

## Jesus Meant to Be Spiritually Present

Being spiritually present is a vague statement. Even Paul said:

> **When you are assembled** in the name of the Lord Jesus and **my spirit is
> present**, with the power of our Lord Jesus, you are to deliver this man to
> Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in
> the day of the Lord. — 1 Corinthians 5:4-5 (ESV)

Paul is not divine. Neither was he omnipresent.

Therefore, some suggest that like Paul, the words of Jesus should not be
considered literally as if he would be physically among or with all Christians
always.

So what was the intent for the statements from Jesus and Paul?

* This could perhaps mean that both Jesus and Paul meant that when the assembly
  are in agreement with the Holy Spirit of God, then they are acting in the
  same spirit of the Lord Jesus and of Paul. It is as if Jesus or Paul was
  present or among them such that they are united as one.
* It could also perhaps mean that both Jesus and Paul passed on their authority
  to their disciples. They were able to make decisions as if Jesus or Paul was
  present to give approval.

Furthermore, a parallel witness in the book of Acts states:

> So when they had come together, they asked him, “Lord, will you at this time
> restore the kingdom to Israel?”
> He said to them, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that
> [the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) has fixed by His own authority.
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you
> will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the
> end of the earth.”
>
> And when he had said these things, as they were looking on, he was lifted
> up, and a cloud took him out of their sight. And while they were gazing into
> heaven as he went, behold, two men stood by them in white robes, and said,
>
> “Men of Galilee, why do you stand **looking into heaven**?
> This Jesus, who **was taken up from you** into heaven, **will come in the
> same way** as you saw him go into heaven.”
>
> — Acts 1:6-11 (ESV)

In this passage, Jesus referred to his Father as a distinct person with more
authority.

What is also surprising is that none of the disciples ever questioned Jesus.
They did not ask him or the angels why he contradicted himself. He said that he
will always be present with them, yet after saying those words, he left them.
Instead of questioning the omnipresence of Jesus, they looked into heaven.
This hints that the disciples did not understand the statement from Jesus as
an omnipresence claim.

It is also confirmed when the two men in white robes confirm that Jesus really
left them. If Jesus was truly omnipresent from that moment, he would have
appeared to them himself. It would not have been necessary for God to send
two men.

### Acts 18 9-10

> And the Lord said to Paul one night in a vision, “Do not be afraid, but go
> on speaking and do not be silent, for I am ***with*** you, and no one will
> attack you to harm you, for **I have many in this city** who are my people.”
> — Acts 18:9-10 (ESV)

This scripture does not prove the omnipresence of Jesus. Jesus did not
magically appear at the trial of Paul. Jesus was simply assuring Paul that he
was not alone. He would protect him with his people in the city.

## Conclusion

The promises of Jesus to be with his followers are often interpreted as claims
of omnipresence. However, the biblical context suggests that these statements
refer to his [authority](#matthew-18-20) and the [future gathering of his
followers](#matthew-28-20). The disciples and later apostles like Paul
understood these words within the framework of [divine approval and
representation](#jesus-meant-to-be-spiritually-present) rather than a literal,
physical omnipresence. Ultimately, the scriptures maintain a distinction
between the [limitations of Jesus as a
man](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent) and the
unlimited nature of God.
