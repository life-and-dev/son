# Jesus Claims To Be Omnipresent

Many Christians believe that Jesus is omnipresent, pointing to his promises to
be with his followers always. This article examines the scriptures often used to
support this view and considers alternative interpretations that align with his
human nature and eventual departure to heaven.

> For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I ***among*** them. — Matthew 18:20 (ESV)

and

> Behold, **I am with you always, to the end of the age**. — Matthew 28:20 (ESV)

It is physically [impossible for a human to be omnipresent](../../son-of-man.md). If Jesus really meant that he would always be present with billions of Christians all over the world, it would mean that Jesus has to be divine.

However, this argument ignores the scriptures that state that [Jesus could not be present everywhere](../../son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-is-not-omnipresent). He was leaving his disciples behind and would be returning (John 17). Trinitarians would argue that Jesus meant that [his human body, which cannot be omnipresent](../../son-of-man.md), would leave and return. They believe that he would also be spiritually present, much like [the Holy Spirit](../../trinity/holy-spirit.md).

## Spiritually Present

Being "spiritually present" is a vague statement. Even Paul said that:

> **When you are assembled** in the name of the Lord Jesus and **my spirit is present**, with the power of our Lord Jesus, you are to deliver this man to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord. — 1 Corinthians 5:4-5 (ESV)

Surely Paul is not divine, and neither was he omnipresent.

Therefore, some suggest that like Paul, Jesus words should not be considered literally as if he would be physically "among" or "with" all Christians always. 

So what was the intent for Jesus and Paul's statements?

* This could mean that both Jesus and Paul intended that when the assembly is in agreement with God's Holy Spirit, they are acting in the same spirit of the Lord Jesus and of Paul. [It is as if Jesus or Paul was present or among them](https://hermeneutics.stackexchange.com/questions/50129/colossians-25-i-am-present-with-you-in-spirit-is-this-literal-or-metaphor). This would mean that [they are united as one](./1-with-father.draft.md).
* It could also mean that both Jesus and Paul passed on their authority to their disciples. They were able to make decisions as if Jesus or Paul was present to give approval.
* It could also perhaps mean that both Jesus and Paul passed on their authority (approval) to their disciples so that they were able to make decisions as if Jesus or Paul was present to give approval.

# Jesus meant it literally

Some reject the argument that Jesus' words should be considered "spiritually" as there is no evidence that he was speaking in idioms. Neither did he mention anything about the spiritual realm.

### Matthew 18:20

> For where two or three are gathered together in My name, **I am there *in the midst* of them.**” — Matthew 18:20 (NKJV)

Out of context, it seems as if Jesus magically appears in the midst every time Christians gather. The context is that Jesus was addressing how to deal with a brother who sins. One solution is to gather two or three witnesses to confront him in the authority of Jesus. In such a case, Jesus would be among those witnesses.

> “If your brother sins against you:
> 1. go and tell him his fault, between you and him alone. If he listens to you, you have  gained your brother. 
> 2. But if he does not listen, take **one or two** others along with you, that every charge may be established by the evidence of **two or three witnesses**. 
> 3. If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church. 
>
> And if he refuses to listen even to the church, let him be to you as a Gentile and a tax collector. 
>
> Truly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Again I say to you, **if two of you** agree on earth about anything they ask, it will be done for them by my Father in heaven. For where **two or three are gathered** in my *name ([authority](/bible/concepts/name))*, there am I ***[among](https://biblehub.com/greek/3319.htm)*** them.”
>
> — Matthew 18:15-20 (ESV)

Some argue that the topic changed from gathering to confront sin to gathering to claim blessings from God. They say so because most modern Bible translations separate this verse from the next verse with a paragraph break. They often separate it even further by including an unrelated title. This was not part of
the original text. This is a common practice by Bible translators to make their copies more readable. Ironically, it adds confusion to the intended message.

Jesus did not switch to prosperity preaching. Instead, he intended for "bind" and "loose" to mean intercession for those who sin. Intercession is to plead with the Father in heaven. When the first three steps fail, the last alternative is to gather to intercede for the sinner. This is the strategy that Jesus authorized and supported.

The topic is not finished yet. In the next verse, Peter asked a question relating to the same topic:

> Then Peter came up and said to him, “Lord, how often will my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?”   
> Jesus said to him, “I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven times. Therefore the kingdom of heaven may be compared to...”
>
> — Matthew 18:21-23 (ESV)

Not only did Jesus answer his question, but he also explained his answer with a parable which is still related to the original topic "how to confront sin".

### Matthew 28:20

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them [in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit](/eternal/saved/baptize/formula), teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age. -- Matthew 28:19-20 (ESV)

In this context Jesus was concerned about the [eternal salvation](/eternal/saved) of people. The purpose of making "disciples of all nations" was to make it possible for them to live forever in the presence of Jesus. [Jesus previously said that he would return to gather his own](/life/end-times) (Matthew 24:23-44). Although he is currently not physically present yet, he will be in the future and this is something to look forward to (Matthew 19:28-30).

Furthermore, a parallel witness in the book of Acts states:

> So when they had come together, they asked him *(Jesus)*, “Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?”  
> He said to them, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that [the Father](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) has fixed by [His own authority](../../son-of-man/has-a-god.md). But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.” 
> 
> And when he had said these things, as they were looking on, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight. And while they were gazing into heaven as he went, behold, two men stood by them in white robes, and said, 
> 
> “Men of Galilee, why do you stand **looking into heaven**?   
> This Jesus, who **was taken up from you** into heaven, **will come in the same way** as you saw him go into heaven.” 
> 
> — Acts 1:6-11 (ESV)

In this passage, Jesus referred to [his Father as a distinct person](/god/son/essence/not-god/distinct) with [more authority](/god/son/essence/not-god/serve-god).

What is also surprising is that none of the disciples ever questioned Jesus. They did not ask
him or the angels why he contradicted himself when he said that he would always be present with them, yet after saying those words, he left them.

Instead of questioning the omnipresence of Jesus, they were looking into heaven. This hints that the disciples did not understand the statement of Jesus as an omnipresence claim. The appearance of the two men in white robes also confirms that Jesus really left them. If Jesus was truly omnipresent from that moment, he
would have appeared to them himself. It would not have been necessary for God to send two men.

### Acts 18:9-10

> And the Lord said to Paul one night in a vision, “Do not be afraid, but go on speaking and do not be silent, for I am ***with*** you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for **I have many in this city** who are my people.” — Acts 18:9-10 (ESV)

This scripture does not prove that Jesus is omnipresent. Jesus did not magically appear at the trial of Paul. Jesus was simply assuring Paul that he was not alone. He would protect him through his people in the city.

## Conclusion

The claim that Jesus is omnipresent often relies on a literal interpretation of his promises to be with his followers. However, when examined in context, these statements may refer to a [spiritual presence](#spiritually-present) or his [literal presence](#literally-present) in specific authoritative or future
contexts. The biblical evidence suggests that Jesus and his disciples understood his departure as a real event, rather than a transition to an omnipresent state.
