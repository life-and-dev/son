## The Strongest Deity Reading and the Contextual Answer

The titles [Son of God](../../sons-of-god.md) and [Son of Man](../../son-of-man.md) require contextual reading. The New Testament uses “Son of God” for Jesus and also uses related sonship language for Israel and believers (Exodus 4:22; Romans 8:14-17; 2 Corinthians 6:18). Jesus repeatedly calls himself the Son of Man, including Matthew 8:20; 9:6; 12:8; 16:13; 20:28; 24:30; 26:24, 45; Mark 2:10,28; Luke 5:24; John 5:27; Acts 7:56; and Revelation 1:13.

The Hebrew Scriptures use sonship language for Israel, kings, and other figures, but they do not supply the New Testament’s full presentation of Jesus as “the Son of God.” Daniel 3:25 records Nebuchadnezzar’s Aramaic words, often translated “a son of the gods,” and is not a direct messianic prophecy. Daniel 7:13-14 provides important contextual background for Jesus’ distinctive use of “Son of Man.”

Luke 1:35 describes Jesus’ miraculous conception through the Holy Spirit and says that the child will be called God’s Son. It does not describe Jesus’ creation and neither establishes nor rules out views about pre-existence.

Trinitarians rightly note John 5:18-23: Jesus calls God his own Father, works as the Father works, and is to receive honour as the Father is honoured. The same speech explains that the Father shows the Son what to do and gives him life and authority to judge (John 5:19-27,30). The Unitarian reading takes Jesus as God’s uniquely appointed Messiah and representative. The titles and honour show exceptional status; they do not by themselves settle whether Jesus is YHVH or a distinct divine person.
