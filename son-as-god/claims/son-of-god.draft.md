# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of God

The title "Son of God" is one of the most prominent identifiers for Jesus in the
New Testament. While modern readers often associate this phrase with a claim of
divinity, its meaning in the first century was rooted in a complex blend of
Jewish messianic expectations and Greco-Roman cultural concepts. Understanding
how Jesus and his contemporaries used this title requires an exploration of
historical context and biblical usage.

Jesus is identified as the "Son of God" in the Gospels, for example:

> The beginning of the gospel of **Jesus Christ, the Son of God**. — Mark 1:1
> (ESV)
> And I have seen and have borne witness that this (Jesus) is **the Son of
> God**. — John 1:34 (ESV)
> Now Jesus did many other signs in the presence of the disciples, which are
> not written in this book; but these are written so that you may believe that
> **Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God**, and that by believing you may have
> life in his name. — John 20:31 (ESV)

## Pagan Understanding

Some argue that because Jesus is called "the Son of God," he must be divine in
the same way as God. This reasoning often points to pagan cultures during the
time of Jesus. Many Greeks and Romans believed that the children of their gods
were also gods. When an audience from these cultures heard someone referred to
as a "son of God," they might understand it as a claim to divinity. The Greeks
were familiar with demi-gods who appeared human but were considered divine.
The Roman Empire even maintained that the Caesar was a god. Each son of a
previous caesar-god was seen as the next divine ruler.

However, the author of the book of Hebrews wrote to a non-pagan Hebrew audience.
This author used the phrase "the Son of God" several times (Hebrews 4:14, 6:6,
7:3, 10:29). For a Hebrew believer, [the term "Son of God"](#new-testament) did
not necessarily signify a leader claiming to be ontologically divine.

## Same Essence

Some people incorrectly assume that a person can only be called a "son of God"
if he is made of the same essence as God.

The title "Son of God" relates to a person's relationship with their Father,
who is God. It does not necessarily describe a person's essence. Therefore, the
Bible describes many ["sons of God"](../../sons-of-god.md).

For example, if a family adopts a child, they do not need to change the child's
genetic makeup. The relationship of "son" is established through adoption
rather than biological essence.

[The term "Son of God"](#new-testament) is also [applied to people other than
Jesus](../../sons-of-god.md). This is seen in Matthew 5:9; Galatians 4:4-7;
Romans 8:14,16-17; Ephesians 1:5; 2 Corinthians 6:18. These references
highlight a relationship with God the Father rather than ontological essence.

Jesus was not the first "son" who called God "my Father":

> “I said, 'How I would **set you among My sons**, and give you a pleasant
> land, a heritage most beautiful of all nations.' And I thought you would call
> Me, **'My Father'**, and would not turn from following Me. Surely, as a
> treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous to Me, O
> house of Israel", declares [the LORD
> (YHVH)](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament). — Jeremiah 3:19-20 (ESV)
>
> **Have we not all one Father?** Has not one God created us? — Malachi 2:10
> (ESV)

## The Son of God and Man

Numerous scriptures refer to Jesus as the "Son of Man." These include: Matthew
8:20, 9:6, 10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27-28, 17:9,12,22, 18:11, 19:28,
20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13,31, 26:2,24,45,64; Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38,
9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62; Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44,56,58,
11:30, 12:8,10,40, 17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7;
John 1:51, 3:13-14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31; Acts 7:56;
Revelation 1:13, 14:14.

Many [Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) point to these references as proof that
[Jesus](../../son-of-man.md) was simultaneously 100% [God](../../nature.md) and
100% [human](../../son-of-man/human.md).

However, this conclusion requires a deeper examination of the historical and
biblical meanings of the two concepts: "The Son of God" and "the son of man."

### Old Testament

In the Hebrew Old Testament:

* "The Son of God" is never mentioned, except by a [bad translation of Daniel
  3:25](../../sons-of-god.md#daniel-325) in certain [Bible
  translations](../../nature.md#bible-translations).
* "The son of man" is not used as a unique title for a single individual.
  Instead, this phrase describes humans generally. It is often used to
  highlight that God is not human. Examples include: Numbers 23:19; Job 25:6,
  35:8; Psalm 8:4, 80:17, 144:3, 146:3; Isaiah 56:2; Jeremiah 49:18,33, 50:40;
  Ezekiel 2:1,3,6,8, 3:1,3-4,10,25; Daniel 8:17.

### New Testament

However, in the Greek New Testament:

* [The Son of God](../../sons-of-god.md) refers to someone who is considered
  God's son. This title does not inherently imply divine essence or equality
  with God.
  * It can refer to [beings created directly by God, such as Adam or the
    angels](../../sons-of-god.md). In the case of Jesus, he was conceived in
    the womb of the virgin Mary by [God's Spirit](../../trinity/holy-spirit.md)
    without a human father.
  * It can also refer to those adopted by God. When God adopted the nation of
    Israel or Christian believers as ["sons of God"](../../sons-of-god.md), he
    did not transform them into divine beings.
* Only in the Greek New Testament does ["the son of man"](../../son-of-man/human.md)
  refer to Jesus as a specific title. Unlike [the Hebrew meaning of "son of
  man"](#old-testament), this title is unique in its application to the
  Messiah. It is often argued that the title serves a theological purpose
  rather than merely proving his humanity, as his physical presence as a human
  was evident to all who saw him.
  * It could be [the prophesied seed of Adam](../../son-of-man/human.md).
  * It is ultimately used in the context of the prophesied [Christ who would
    save mankind](../../son-of-man.md).

Therefore, Jesus does not have to be divine to be both "the Son of God" and "the
son of man."

## Conclusion

The titles attributed to Jesus provide insight into his role and relationship
with God rather than his biological or ontological essence. The historical
[pagan understanding](#pagan-understanding) of "son of God" as a claim to
divinity differs significantly from the [Hebrew perspective](#old-testament)
found in scripture. Biblical evidence suggests that the title "Son of God"
refers to a [relational or adopted status](#same-essence) rather than a shared
divine essence. Furthermore, the distinct usage of
[“Son of Man”](#the-son-of-god-and-man) in the New Testament highlights Jesus'
unique role as the prophesied Christ without requiring him to be ontologically
divine.
