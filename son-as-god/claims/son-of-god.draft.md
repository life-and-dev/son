# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of God

The title "Son of God" appears throughout the New Testament, yet its meaning
often depends on the cultural and theological lens of the reader. While some
view it as a direct claim to divinity, others see it as a messianic title rooted
in Jewish tradition. Exploring the historical and biblical context of this title
provides clarity on how the early followers of Jesus understood his identity and
relationship with the Father.

Jesus is identified as the "Son of God" in the Gospels, for example:

> The beginning of the gospel of **Jesus Christ, the Son of God**. — Mark 1:1
> (ESV)
>
> And I have seen and have borne witness that this is **the Son of God**. —
> John 1:34 (ESV)
>
> Now Jesus did many other signs in the presence of the disciples, which are not
> written in this book; but these are written so that you may believe that
> **Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God**, and that by believing you may have
> life in his name. — John 20:30-31 (ESV)

## Pagan Understanding

Some would argue the fact that Jesus is called "the Son of God", means he had to
be divine like God. The reasoning is that the pagan cultures in Jesus life-time
believed that their gods' children were also gods. Therefore, if someone would
refer to Jesus as "the son of God", the audience would understand that he is
divine. The Greeks were used to demi-gods who looked like humans, but who were
actually supposed to be gods. The Roman empire even believed that the Caesar was
a god and that each son of the previous caesar-god was the next god.

However, the author of the book of Hebrews who had supposedly written to the
non-pagan Hebrew nation, also used the phrase "the son of God"
(Hebrews 4:14, 6:6, 7:3, 10:29) multiple times. To the Hebrew believer,
[the term "son of God"](#new-testament) does not mean yet leader claiming to be
divine.

## Same Essence

Some people incorrectly assume that a person can only be called a "son of God"
if he is made of the same essence as God.

The title "son of God" has nothing to do with a person's essence, but with his
relationship with his Father who is God. Therefore, God has many
["sons of God"](../../sons-of-god.md).

For example if a white European family adopts a black African child, they do not
have to change his essence (DNA, ethnicity or skin colour) to be able to adopt
him as a "son" in their family.

[The term "son of God"](#new-testament) is also
[applied people other than Jesus](../../sons-of-god.md)
(Matthew 5:9; Romans 8:14,16-17; 1 Corinthians 6:18; Ephesians 1:5;
Galatians 4:4-7) which never refers to their essence, but instead highlights
their relationship with their Father God.

Jesus was not the first "son" to address God as "Father":

> “I said, ‘How I would **set you among My sons**, and give you a pleasant land,
> a heritage most beautiful of all nations.’
>
> And I thought you would call Me, **‘My Father’**, and would not turn from
> following Me.
>
> Surely, as a treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous
> to Me, O house of Israel,” declares
> [the LORD *(YHVH)*](../../shema.md#god-in-the-old-testament).
>
> — Jeremiah 3:19-20 (ESV)
>
> **Have we not all one Father?** Has not one God created us? —
> Malachi 2:10 (ESV)

## Dual Titles

Numerous scriptures refer to Jesus as the "son of man" (Matthew 8:20, 9:6, 10:23,
11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27,28, 17:9,12,22, 18:11, 19:28, 20:18,28,
24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13,31, 26:2,24,45,64; Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31,
10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62; Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44,56,58, 11:30,
12:8,10,40, 17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7;
John 1:51, 3:13,14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31; Acts 7:56;
Revelation 1:13, 14:14). [Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often argue that these
titles prove [Jesus](../../son-of-man.md) was simultaneously 100%
[God](../../nature.md) and 100% [human](../../son-of-man/human.md).

However, such a conclusion requires a precise understanding of the distinct
meanings behind the concepts of "the Son of God" and "the son of man."

### Old Testament

In the Hebrew Old Testament:

* "The Son of God" is never mentioned, except by a
  [bad translations of Daniel 3:25](../../sons-of-god.md#daniel-325) of certain
  [bible translations](../../nature.md#bible-translations).
* "The son of man" is not used as a title for anyone. This phrase is used to
  describe humans and is usually used in the context of highlighting that God is
  not a human for example: Numbers 23:19; Job 25:6; 35:8; Psalm 8:4; 80:17;
  144:3; 146:3; Isaiah 56:2; Jeremiah 49:18,33; 50:40; Ezekiel 2:1,3,6,8;
  3:1,3,4,10,25; Daniel 8:17; etc.

### New Testament

However, in the Greek New Testament:

* [The Son of God](../../sons-of-god.md) refers to someone who is considered
  God's son. It has nothing to do with his essence, divinity or power.
  * It could be
    [someone made directly by God like Adam or the angelic beings](../../sons-of-god.md).
    In Jesus' case he was "conceived" (made) directly in the womb of the
    **virgin** Mary by [God's Spirit](../../trinity/holy-spirit.md) without a
    human father's seed.
  * It could also be someone that is adopted by God. When God adopted Israel or
    the Christian believers as ["sons of God"]](../../sons-of-god.md), He did not
    turn them into demi-gods.
* Only in the Greek New Testament does ["the son of man"](../../son-of-man/human.md)
  refer to Jesus, with the exception of
  [Hebrews 2:6 which quotes Psalm 8:4-6](../../son-of-man/human.md). Unlike the
  [Hebrew meaning of "son of man"](#old-testament), this title is used uniquely.
  Given his physical presence, it might seem unnecessary for Jesus to prove his
  humanity since he was clearly observable as a man.
  * It could be [the prophesied seed of Man (Adam)](../../son-of-man.md).
  * Ultimately it always used in the context of the prophesied
    [Christ who would save mankind](../../son-of-man.md).

Therefore, Jesus does not have to be divine to be both "the Son of God"
(a child of God) and "the son of man" (the prophesied Christ).

## Common Objections

Critics often point to specific interactions where Jesus' claim to be the
"Son of God" was interpreted as a claim to equality with God.

### Equality with God

In John 5:18, the Jews sought to kill Jesus because he "was even calling God his
own Father, making himself equal with God." However, the context of Jesus'
response in the very next verse clarifies his position:

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, **the Son can do nothing of
> his own accord**, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the
> Father does, that the Son does likewise.” — John 5:19 (ESV)

Jesus emphasizes his total dependence on the Father rather than an inherent,
independent equality. His authority is delegated, a concept familiar in Jewish
law where an agent (*shaliah*) is as the one who sent him but is not
ontologically identical to the sender.

### I and the Father Are One

Another common reference is John 10:30, where Jesus states,
"I and the Father are one." When the Jews accused him of blasphemy for
"making yourself God" (John 10:33), Jesus defended his title by quoting
Psalm 82:6:

> Jesus answered them, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
> If he called them gods to whom the word of God came—and Scripture cannot be
> broken—do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world,
> ‘You are blaspheming,’ because I said, **‘I am the Son of God’**?” —
> John 10:34-36 (ESV)

Jesus explains that if human judges could be called "gods" due to their divinely
appointed roles, then his title as "Son of God" is even more appropriate given
his consecration by the Father. He identifies "one" as a unity of purpose and
work, similar to how he prayed for his disciples to be "one" even as he and the
Father are one (John 17:21-23).

## Conclusion

The title "Son of God" is a significant biblical designation that highlights a
unique relationship and [delegated authority](#equality-with-god) rather than a
claim to [divine essence](#same-essence). By examining the
[pagan](#pagan-understanding) versus [Jewish](#old-testament) contexts, it
becomes evident that the early followers of Jesus understood his sonship in
terms of his [messianic role](#new-testament) and
[unity of purpose](#i-and-the-father-are-one) with the Father. Ultimately,
Jesus' identity as both the "Son of God" and the ["son of man"](#dual-titles)
affirms his status as the prophesied Christ without requiring him to be
ontologically identical to the Almighty God.
