# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

## The Strongest Trinitarian Reading

Trinitarians usually do not identify the Son of Man with the Ancient of Days. They understand the vision to distinguish the Son from the Father while showing the Son's divine status. The one like a son of man comes with the clouds of heaven, receives universal service and an everlasting kingdom, and is invoked by Jesus before the Sanhedrin (Daniel 7:13–14; Mark 14:62). On this reading, these are more than honours given to a merely human ruler.

## The Contextual Answer

Daniel saw:

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like **a son of man**, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

The mainstream interpretation identifies the Ancient of Days with Israel's God. Interpretations of the human-like figure vary: many scholars understand it as a symbol for the holy people who receive the kingdom in Daniel 7, while other Jewish and Christian readings understand it as an individual heavenly or messianic figure. In every reading, the figure is distinct from the Ancient of Days and receives dominion from him.

Jesus uses “Son of Man” for his authority, suffering, death, resurrection, and future coming (Matthew 9:6; 12:8; 20:28; 24:30; Mark 8:31; 14:62; John 5:27). His use can therefore recall Daniel's royal vision while also identifying his human messianic mission.

Second Temple Jewish expectations were diverse. Many Jews expected a human Davidic Messiah, but Jewish texts do not present one uniform expectation or one settled interpretation of Daniel's figure. The Unitarian reading takes the distinction in the vision seriously: God appoints and exalts Jesus as the human Messiah and gives him dominion. The title establishes Jesus' exceptional authority; it does not by itself identify him as YHVH.
