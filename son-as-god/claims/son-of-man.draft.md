# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

The title "Son of Man" was the most frequent way Jesus referred to himself. It appears over eighty times in the Gospels. While it might sound like a simple statement of humanity, its roots in the Hebrew Scriptures suggest a much deeper significance. This title connects Jesus to ancient messianic prophecies and reveals his identity in relation to God.

## The Vision of Daniel

In the book of Daniel, the prophet describes a celestial vision of a figure receiving an eternal kingdom.

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

Throughout the New Testament, Jesus repeatedly identified himself with this figure. His references appear in numerous passages, including:

* Matthew 8:20, 9:6, 10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27-28, 17:9,12,22, 19:28, 20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:31, 26:2,24,45,64
* Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62
* Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44,58, 11:30, 12:8,10,40, 17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7
* John 1:51, 3:13-14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31
* Acts 7:56
* Revelation 1:13, 14:14

Some versions include additional "Son of Man" references in Matthew 18:11, 25:13, and Luke 9:56. These variations depend on the specific manuscripts used by the translators.

## Common Usage of the Title

In the Old Testament, the phrase "son of man" was commonly used to refer to human beings in general. This usage emphasized the mortality and frailty of humans in contrast to the eternal nature of God.

> What is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him? — Psalm 8:4 (ESV)

> God is not a man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind. — Numbers 23:19 (ESV)

The prophet Ezekiel was addressed as "son of man" ninety-three times. This title served as a reminder of his human status while he received divine revelations.

> And he said to me, “Son of man, stand on your feet, and I will speak with you.” — Ezekiel 2:1 (ESV)

## Trinitarian Interpretations

[Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often use [Daniel's prophecy](../../son-of-man/prophecies.md) to support the deity of Christ. This perspective typically argues that Jesus pre-existed as the figure Daniel saw. It suggests that Jesus is both the "Son of Man" and the "Ancient of Days." Under this view, he is God. When Jesus refers to himself as the "Son of Man," he is specifically highlighting his human nature while remaining fully God.

## A Prophetic Perspective

However, an alternative view suggests that Daniel was seeing a vision of the future rather than a pre-existent being. Prophets often witnessed future events as if they were happening in the present. In this context, the "one like a son of man" was [distinct from](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) the "Ancient of Days." The vision depicts the Son of Man approaching God. This reinforces their separate identities.

The first-century Jewish audience was [expecting a human](../../son-of-man/human.md) Messiah. By using the title "Son of Man," Jesus identified himself as the prophesied [Christ](../../son-of-man.md) without necessarily claiming to be God. This conclusion avoids the [logical difficulties](../../nature.md#concerns) associated with a dual nature.

## The High Priest's Reaction

Critics often point to the trial of Jesus as evidence that his use of the "Son of Man" title was a claim to deity. When the High Priest asked if he was the Christ, Jesus confirmed his identity and added a reference to Daniel's vision.

> Jesus said to him, “You have said so. But I tell you, from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven.” — Matthew 26:64 (ESV)

The High Priest reacted by tearing his clothes and charging Jesus with blasphemy. This is often interpreted as a response to a claim of divinity. However, the blasphemy charge was likely directed at Jesus' claim to be the unique, exalted figure who would sit at God's right hand. To the religious leaders, a man claiming such an exalted status was considered offensive to God's unique majesty. This was true regardless of whether he claimed to be God.

## Conclusion

Jesus' use of the "Son of Man" title connects him to the [vision of Daniel](#the-vision-of-daniel) and the expectations of a [human Messiah](#a-prophetic-perspective). While [Trinitarian interpretations](#trinitarian-interpretations) see it as a claim to deity, the biblical context suggests a [distinct identity](#a-prophetic-perspective) for Jesus as the one granted authority by the Ancient of Days. Even the [High Priest's reaction](#the-high-priests-reaction) can be understood as a response to Jesus' claim of supreme exaltation.
