# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

Jesus often referred to himself by a mysterious title: "the Son of Man." This
phrase appears throughout the Gospels, yet its meaning is often debated. Does
it highlight his humanity, or does it point to something more? To understand
why Jesus chose this specific name, one must look back to a remarkable vision
recorded centuries earlier by the prophet Daniel.

## The Prophecy of Daniel

Daniel described a celestial scene where a human-like figure appears before
God:

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came
> one like a son of man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented
> before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

In this vision, the figure is granted authority and a kingdom that will never
be destroyed. The phrase "son of man" in this context suggests a human being
appearing in the presence of the Divine.

## Jesus as the Son of Man

Jesus used this title more than any other to describe himself. It appears in
numerous passages across the New Testament. Examples include:

Matthew 8:20, 9:6, 10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27,28, 17:9,12,22,
18:11 (KJV), 19:28, 20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13 (KJV),31, 26:2,24,
26:45 (KJV),64; Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62;
Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44, 9:56 (KJV), 9:58 (KJV), 11:30, 12:8,10,40,
17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7; John 1:51, 3:13,14,
5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31; Acts 7:56; Revelation 1:13, 14:14.

## Trinitarian Interpretation

Many [Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) interpret this [prophecy about
Jesus](../similarities.md) to mean that Jesus pre-existed in heaven. They argue
that if Jesus is the "Son of Man" who comes before the "Ancient of Days," he
must be God. According to this view, when Jesus calls himself the "Son of Man,"
he is specifically referring to his human nature while remaining fully divine.

## Prophetic Vision of the Future

A different perspective suggests that prophets often see future events as if
they are happening in the present. Daniel's vision may not be describing Jesus'
pre-existence, but rather a future event where the Messiah is presented before
God. Other prophecies in the Bible follow this pattern of describing future
realities through visionary experiences. Support for this can be found in
[commentaries on Daniel 7](https://revbible.com/comm/Daniel/7/13).

## Distinction in the Vision

In the text of Daniel, the "one like a son of man" is clearly distinct from the
"Ancient of Days." They are presented as two separate entities. The "Son of Man"
approaches the "Ancient of Days" to receive a kingdom. This distinction
suggests that they are [not the same being](../../index.md#what-does-son-of-god-mean).
If they were the same, the imagery of one approaching the other would be
redundant.

## Expectation of a Human Messiah

The Jewish people of the time were [expecting a
human](../../son-of-man/human.md) Messiah. The title "Son of Man" reinforced
this expectation. It identified the Messiah as a human being who would fulfill
God's purposes on earth. This aligns with the biblical presentation of Jesus
as the [Christ](../../son-of-man/prophecies.md) who was born of a woman and
lived as a man.

## Conclusion

The title "Son of Man" connects Jesus to the powerful [Prophecy of
Daniel](#the-prophecy-of-daniel). While some interpret this as proof of
pre-existence, the vision shows a clear [Distinction in the
Vision](#distinction-in-the-vision). This title affirms the expectation of a
[human Messiah](#expectation-of-a-human-messiah) who is granted authority by
God.
