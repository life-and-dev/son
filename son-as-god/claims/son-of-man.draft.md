# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

The title "Son of Man" appears frequently in the Gospels as Jesus's preferred
way to refer to himself. While it sounds like a simple description of humanity,
it carries deep prophetic weight from the Old Testament. Why did Jesus choose
this specific title over others? This article explores the origins of this
phrase and how different theological views interpret its meaning.

## Prophetic Vision

The prophet Daniel recorded a vision of a future heavenly scene. He saw
someone resembling a human being approaching God.

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came
> one like **a son of man**, and he came to [the Ancient of Days](../../son-of-man/distinct.md)
> and was presented before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

Jesus repeatedly referred to himself as "the Son of Man" throughout his
ministry. This occurs in numerous passages. For example, see Matthew 8:20, 9:6,
10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27-28, 17:9,12,22, 18:11 (KJV), 19:28,
20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13 (KJV),31, 26:2,24,45 (KJV),64. It is also
found in Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62. Luke
records it in Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44, 56 (KJV), 58 (KJV), 11:30,
12:8,10,40, 17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7. John
includes it in John 1:51, 3:13-14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31.
The title also appears in Acts 7:56 and Revelation 1:13, 14:14.

## Trinitarian View

[Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often argue that this
[prophecy about Jesus](../../son-of-man/prophecies.md) supports his divinity.
They suggest that Jesus pre-existed because Daniel saw him in the vision. Some
even equate "the Son of Man" with "the Ancient of Days" to claim they are the
same God. From this perspective, Jesus uses the title to refer to his human
nature while remaining fully God. They may also point to the "serving" of the
Son of Man in Daniel 7:14 as evidence of divine worship.

## Unitarian Defense

Unitarian scholars offer different explanations for these points. They note
that prophets often see future events as if they are happening in the present.
This does not necessarily imply the literal pre-existence of the person seen.
In Daniel's vision, "the Son of Man" is clearly distinct from "the Ancient of
Days." They are presented as two separate beings rather than
[the same God](../similarities.md).

Furthermore, Daniel 7:14 states that dominion and a kingdom were "given" to the
Son of Man. If he were God, he would already possess inherent authority. The
fact that he receives it from another indicates a subordinate position.
Regarding the term for "serve" (*pelach*) in the Aramaic text, it can refer to
the service rendered to a high-ranking official or king. It does not
exclusively denote the religious worship reserved for God alone.

The Jewish people were [expecting a human](../../son-of-man/human.md) Messiah or
[Christ](../../son-of-man.md). The title emphasizes his role as the ideal
representative of humanity. It highlights his connection to the human race
rather than an ontological claim to divinity.

## Conclusion

The title "Son of Man" connects [Old Testament prophecy](#prophetic-vision)
with the identity of the Messiah. Theological views differ on whether the
title implies [divinity or human messiahship](#trinitarian-view). The phrase
describes a [prophesied human figure](#prophetic-vision) who receives an
everlasting kingdom from God.
