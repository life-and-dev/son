# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

Among all the titles attributed to Jesus in the New Testament, one appears on
his lips more frequently than any other: "the Son of Man." While modern readers
might assume this simply refers to his humanity, its roots in ancient prophecy
suggest a much deeper and more complex significance. Why did Jesus favor this
specific designation, and what did it signal to his first-century audience? By
examining the origins of this title in the book of Daniel and its usage
throughout the Gospels, we can begin to uncover the layers of meaning behind
this enigmatic self-identification.

## Prophetic Vision in Daniel

The prophet Daniel recorded a remarkable vision that serves as the backdrop for
this title:

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came
> one like **a son of man**, and he came to [the Ancient of Days](../../son-of-man/distinct.md)
> and was presented before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

In this vision, the figure who is "like a son of man" is distinct from the
"Ancient of Days." He approaches the throne of the Almighty and is granted
authority and a kingdom.

## Jesus Using the Title

Jesus adopted this title and applied it to himself on numerous occasions. His
usage spanned from his earthly ministry to his future return in glory. Examples
include:

- Matthew 8:20, 9:6, 10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27,28, 17:9,12,22,
  18:11 (KJV), 19:28, 20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13 (KJV),31,
  26:2,24,45(KJV),64
- Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26, 14:21,41,62
- Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44,56(KJV),58(KJV), 11:30, 12:8,10,40,
  17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7
- John 1:51, 3:13,14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31
- Acts 7:56
- Revelation 1:13, 14:14

## Trinitarian Interpretations

[Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often view this
[prophecy about Jesus](../../son-of-man/prophecies.md) as evidence for several
theological points. They reason that it implies Jesus pre-existed in heaven
before his birth. They also suggest that because the Son of Man receives
dominion and glory, he must be divine. In this view, when Jesus refers to
himself as the Son of Man, he is specifically highlighting his human nature
while remaining fully God.

## Unitarian Perspectives

Other theological traditions, such as [Unitarians](../../nature.md#concerns),
offer different explanations for these passages. They point out that prophets
like Daniel often saw future events as if they were happening in the present.
This doesn't necessarily mean the figure pre-existed, but rather that the vision
was a preview of things to come.

Furthermore, in Daniel's vision, the "one like a son of man" is clearly
distinct from the "Ancient of Days." They are presented as two separate beings
rather than one God in different forms. This aligns with the historical Jewish
expectation of a human Messiah. The Jews were
[expecting a human](../../son-of-man/human.md) Messiah ([Christ](../../son-of-man.md))
who would be anointed by God to lead His people.

## Arguments Regarding Divine Service

A common argument for the divinity of the Son of Man is found in the following
verse:

> And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples,
> nations, and languages should **serve** him; his dominion is an everlasting
> dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be
> destroyed. — Daniel 7:14 (ESV)

Some argue that the word for "serve" here implies religious worship reserved
for God. However, others note that this term can also refer to the service or
obedience given to a king or ruler. In the Septuagint, different Greek words
are used to translate this concept. One version uses *latreuousin* (often
associated with divine service), while another uses *douleuousin* (general
service or obedience). This highlights that the nature of the service given to
the Son of Man can be interpreted in multiple ways depending on the linguistic
context and theological framework.

## Conclusion

The title "Son of Man" originates from
[Daniel's prophetic vision](#prophetic-vision-in-daniel) of a figure
approaching God's throne. Jesus [consistently used this title](#jesus-using-the-title)
to describe himself throughout his ministry. While
[Trinitarians interpret this](#trinitarian-interpretations) as evidence of
pre-existence and divinity, [Unitarians see it](#unitarian-perspectives) as a
fulfillment of the expectation of a human Messiah. The
[nature of the service](#arguments-regarding-divine-service) promised to this
figure remains a point of theological discussion.
