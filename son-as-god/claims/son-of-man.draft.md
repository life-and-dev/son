# Jesus Claims to Be the Son of Man

The title "Son of Man" was Jesus' most frequent self-designation during his
earthly ministry. While often understood as highlighting his humanity, the
title carries deep messianic significance rooted in Old Testament prophecy.
An examination of the origins and usage of this title provides understanding of
how Jesus viewed his own identity and mission.

## Daniel's Vision of the Son of Man

The most prominent background for this title is found in the book of Daniel.
In a night vision, Daniel saw a heavenly scene where a human-like figure was
presented before God.

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came
one like a son of man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented
before him. — Daniel 7:13 (ESV)

In this context, "a son of man" simply means a human being. This figure is
distinct from the [Ancient of Days](../../son-of-man/distinct.md), who
represents God.

## Jesus' Usage of the Title

Jesus referred to himself as "the Son of Man" on numerous occasions throughout
the Gospels. This title appears in contexts of his humble earthly life, his
impending suffering, and his future glory.

Examples include: Matthew 8:20, 9:6, 10:23, 11:19, 12:8,40, 13:41, 16:13,27-28,
17:9,12,22, 18:11 (KJV), 19:28, 20:18,28, 24:27,30,37,39,44, 25:13 (KJV),31,
26:2,24,45 (KJV),64; Mark 2:10,28, 8:31,38, 9:9,12,31, 10:33,45, 13:26,
14:21,41,62; Luke 5:24, 6:5, 7:34, 9:22,26,44,56 (KJV),58 (KJV), 11:30,
12:8,10,40, 17:22,24,30, 18:8,31, 19:10, 21:27,36, 22:22,48,69, 24:7;
John 1:51, 3:13-14, 5:27, 6:27,53,62, 8:28, 12:23,34, 13:31; Acts 7:56;
Revelation 1:13, 14:14.

## Interpretations of the Title

[Trinitarians](../../trinity.md) often interpret this
[prophecy about Jesus](../../son-of-man/prophecies.md) to mean that he
pre-existed in heaven as the figure Daniel saw. Some also argue that because the
Son of Man receives glory and dominion, he must be God himself. In this view,
when Jesus calls himself the Son of Man, he is specifically referring to his
human nature as part of a dual-natured being.

However, several points suggest a different conclusion:

1. Prophets like Daniel often saw future events as if they were happening in
their own time. This does not require the figure to have existed at the time of
the vision.
2. In the vision, the figure is clearly distinct from God (the Ancient of Days).
They are [not the same being](../../son-as-god/similarities.md).
3. The Jewish expectation for the Messiah was that he would be a
[human descendant](../../son-of-man/human.md) of David, not an ontological
deity. When Peter identified Jesus as "the Christ, the Son of the living God"
(Matthew 16:16), Jesus affirmed this as a revelation from God, yet he
continued to refer to himself as the Son of Man. This affirmed his role as the
[Christ](../../son-of-man.md) while remaining within this human framework.

## Divine Authority Given to the Son of Man

A critical point in Daniel's vision is that the Son of Man is *given* authority
and dominion by the Ancient of Days.

> And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples,
nations, and languages should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting
dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be
destroyed. — Daniel 7:14 (ESV)

Some argue that the word for "serve" used here (*pelach* in Aramaic) implies
divine worship. However, the Greek Septuagint translation uses *douleuousin* (to
serve as a subject) in the oldest manuscripts, which refers to political or
general service rather than the religious worship (*latreuo*) reserved for God
alone. The fact that this authority is given to him demonstrates that it is not
inherent to his being, but granted to him by God.

## Conclusion

The title "Son of Man" identifies Jesus as the promised human Messiah who
receives authority from God. It highlights his
[distinction from God](#daniels-vision-of-the-son-of-man) and his
[role as the human King](#interpretations-of-the-title) in God's kingdom.
Rather than claiming deity, Jesus used this title to affirm his
[given authority](#divine-authority-given-to-the-son-of-man) and his
identification with humanity.
