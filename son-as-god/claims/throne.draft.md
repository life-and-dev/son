# Jesus Claims the Throne

The concept of a throne often evokes images of ultimate authority and divine
status. In the New Testament, Jesus is frequently depicted as sitting on a
"glorious throne" or sharing the throne of God. Some interpret this as proof
of his divinity. However, a closer examination of the scriptures reveals a
more complex picture. The Bible provides several examples of others who are
granted similar positions. This article explores whether sitting on the
throne of God inherently signifies being God.

## The Throne of the Son of Man

> When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then
> he will sit on his glorious throne. — Matthew 25:31 (ESV)

Jesus speaks of a time when he will take his place on a throne of glory.
This position represents his role as King and Judge.

## Believers and Shared Thrones

Followers of Jesus are also promised a share in this royal authority.

> Jesus said to them, “Truly, I say to you, in the new world, when the Son
> of Man will sit on his glorious throne, you who have followed me will
> also sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.”
> — Matthew 19:28 (ESV)

Furthermore, Jesus extends this promise to all who prevail.

> The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I
> also conquered and sat down with my Father on his throne.
> — Revelation 3:21 (ESV)

This shared inheritance is also mentioned in other parts of the New
Testament. Believers are described as being made into a kingdom and priests
to God (Revelation 1:6). This indicates that sitting on a throne does not
necessarily make the occupant a deity.

## Celestial Beings on Thrones

The book of Revelation describes other beings who occupy thrones in the
presence of God.

> Around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and seated on the thrones
> were twenty-four elders, clothed in white garments, with golden crowns
> on their heads. — Revelation 4:4 (ESV)

These elders hold positions of honor and authority. Yet they are not
considered gods. Instead, they acknowledge the supreme authority of the one
who sits on the main throne.

> And whenever the living creatures give glory and honor and thanks to him
> who is seated on the throne, who lives forever and ever, the twenty-four
> elders fall down before him who is seated on the throne and worship him
> who lives forever and ever. They cast their crowns before the throne,
> saying, “Worthy are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor
> and power, for you created all things, and by your will they existed
> and were created.” — Revelation 4:9-11 (ESV)

The Lamb, who represents Jesus, is also seen among these elders
(Revelation 5:6).

## Historical Precedent in the Old Testament

The idea of a human sitting on the throne of God is not unique to the New
Testament. The Old Testament records a similar event involving King Solomon.

> Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king in place of David his
> father. And he prospered, and all Israel obeyed him.
> — 1 Chronicles 29:23 (ESV)

In this context, the "throne of the LORD" refers to the royal seat of
authority over God's people. Solomon sat on this throne as God's chosen
representative (see [1 Chronicles 29:23 commentary](https://www.revbible.com/1Chronicles/29/23)).
This historical example shows that occupying the throne of the LORD does not
imply that the king is God Himself.

## Conclusion

The biblical narrative demonstrates that sitting on a throne is a symbol of
granted authority rather than an indicator of divine nature. Jesus sits on a
[glorious throne](#the-throne-of-the-son-of-man) as the appointed King.
However, [believers are also promised a place on thrones](#believers-and-shared-thrones)
and [celestial elders](#celestial-beings-on-thrones) occupy them as well.
The [historical example of Solomon](#historical-precedent-in-the-old-testament)
confirms that sitting on the throne of the LORD is a role given to God's
chosen representatives.
