# Jesus Claims the Throne

The image of Jesus seated on a glorious throne is a powerful one. It is often
used to emphasize his supreme authority. Many see this as a clear claim to
divinity. They suggest that only God can occupy such a position. However, a
closer look at the scriptures reveals a more complex picture. The biblical
narrative shows how thrones and authority are distributed.

## The Promise of Shared Authority

Jesus explicitly spoke about his future role on a throne. He described a time
when he would exercise judgment and rule.

> When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then he
> will sit on his glorious throne. — Matthew 25:31 (ESV)

While this passage highlights his importance, it does not imply he is the only
one with a throne. Jesus promised his followers that they would also share in
this authority. In a conversation with his disciples, he described their
future reward.

> Jesus said to them, "Truly, I say to you, in the new world, when the Son of
> Man will sit on his glorious throne, you who have followed me will also sit
> on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel."
> — Matthew 19:28 (ESV)

This theme of shared authority continues in the book of Revelation. Jesus
offers a remarkable promise to those who remain faithful.

> The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I also
> conquered and sat down with my Father on his throne. — Revelation 3:21 (ESV)

In these verses, the throne is a symbol of authority that can be shared. Just
as Jesus sits on the Father's throne, believers are invited to sit on the
throne of Jesus. This concept is also mentioned in Revelation 1:6. It states
that believers have been made a kingdom and priests.

The idea of a human sitting on the throne of God is not unique to the New
Testament. The Old Testament provides a clear example with King Solomon.

> Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king in place of David his
> father. And he prospered, and all Israel obeyed him.
> — 1 Chronicles 29:23 (ESV)

Solomon occupied the throne of the LORD. Yet he was never considered to be God
himself. The throne represented the authority given to him by God to rule over
Israel. More information on the future kingdom and the shared rule can be
found in the [REV commentary on Matthew 19:28][rev-matthew].

## The Thrones of the Elders

The vision of the heavenly court in Revelation further illustrates this point.
It describes other beings who occupy thrones in the presence of God.

> Around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and seated on the thrones were
> twenty-four elders, clothed in white garments, with golden crowns on their
> heads. — Revelation 4:4 (ESV)

These twenty-four elders are given positions of great honor. They sit on
thrones and wear crowns. Despite their high status, they are distinct from the
one seated on the central throne. Their actions demonstrate their subordinate
position.

> And whenever the living creatures give glory and honor and thanks to him who
> is seated on the throne, who lives forever and ever, the twenty-four elders
> fall down before him who is seated on the throne and worship him who lives
> forever and ever. They cast their crowns before the throne, saying, “Worthy
> are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power...”
> — Revelation 4:9-11 (ESV)

The elders acknowledge that their authority comes from God. They worship the
one on the throne and cast their crowns before him. This shows that sitting on
a throne does not automatically make one God. It is a position of delegated
authority.

Even the Lamb is described in relation to these elders and the throne. He is
seen "between the throne and the four living creatures and among the elders"
(Revelation 5:6). This placement suggests a unique role within the heavenly
hierarchy.

## Conclusion

The biblical evidence suggests that sitting on a throne represents authority
rather than divinity. Jesus sits on a glorious throne, but he also promises
that his followers will [share in that authority][shared-authority].
The presence of [twenty-four elders on thrones][elder-thrones] shows that many
can occupy such positions while still being subordinate to God. Ultimately,
the throne is a place of rule that God grants to those whom He chooses.
