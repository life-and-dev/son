# Jesus Claims the Throne

Many point to Jesus sitting on a glorious throne as definitive proof of his
divinity. However, a closer look at biblical promises reveals that the
privilege of sitting on a throne is not unique to him. What does it truly
mean to share in this heavenly authority, and how does the Bible distinguish
between the throne of God and the thrones given to others?

## The Throne of Glory

Jesus spoke of a future time when he would occupy a position of great power.
This authority is often associated with his role as the Son of Man, a title
that emphasizes his humanity and his messianic mission.

> When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then he
> will sit on his glorious throne. — Matthew 25:31 (ESV)

## Thrones for the Disciples

While Jesus has a throne of glory, he also promised that his followers would
share in this judicial and regal authority. In the coming age, the apostles
are expected to occupy their own thrones.

> And Jesus said to them, "Truly I say to you, that you who have followed me, in
> the New Beginning, when the Son of Man sits on the throne of his glory, **you
> also will sit on twelve thrones**, judging the twelve tribes of Israel." —
> Matthew 19:28 (ESV)

## Sitting on the Father's Throne

Jesus makes a distinction between his own throne and the throne of the Father.
He invites overcomers to sit with him on his throne, just as he sat down with
his Father on the Father's throne. This suggests a hierarchy of authority
rather than an identity of being.

> The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I also
> conquered and sat down with my Father on his throne. — Revelation 3:21 (ESV)

The concept of a human sitting on the "throne of the LORD" is not new to the
New Testament. The Old Testament records that King Solomon sat on the very
throne of God as his earthly representative.

> Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king in place of David his
> father. He prospered, and all Israel obeyed him. — 1 Chronicles 29:23 (ESV)

Just as Solomon was not considered to be the LORD himself, Jesus sitting on the
Father's throne denotes his delegated authority as the Messiah.

## The Twenty-Four Elders

The book of Revelation describes other heavenly beings who occupy thrones. The
twenty-four elders are seen seated on thrones around the central throne of
God. Like believers, these elders are not considered divine beings equal to
God, despite their high positions of honor.

> Around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and seated on the thrones were
> twenty-four elders, clothed in white garments, with golden crowns on their
> heads. — Revelation 4:4 (ESV)

## Worship Before the Throne

A clear distinction is maintained between those on the surrounding thrones and
the One who sits on the central throne. The elders acknowledge the supreme
authority of God by casting their crowns before him and offering worship.

> And whenever the living creatures give glory and honor and thanks to Him who
> is seated on the throne, Who lives forever and ever, the twenty-four elders
> fall down before Him who is seated on the throne and worship Him who lives
> forever and ever. They cast their crowns before the throne, saying, “Worthy
> are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power...” —
> Revelation 4:9-11 (ESV)

The Lamb, who is also present in this heavenly scene, is distinguished from the
One on the throne. This separation highlights the unique status of the Father
as the ultimate object of worship.

## Addressing Potential Critiques

Some argue that Jesus sitting *on* the Father's throne is different from the
elders or disciples sitting on their own thrones. They suggest this indicates
Jesus is part of the Godhead. However, Revelation 3:21 clarifies that
believers will sit *with* Jesus on his throne. If sitting on God's throne
makes Jesus God, then by the same logic, believers sitting on that same throne
would also be God. Instead, the Bible consistently portrays this as a sharing
of authority granted by the Father to his faithful servants.

## Conclusion

The Bible portrays the act of sitting on a throne as a symbol of delegated
authority rather than an inherent claim to divinity. Jesus occupies a
[throne of glory](#the-throne-of-glory) as the Messiah, but he also promises
that his [disciples](#thrones-for-the-disciples) and other
[overcomers](#sitting-on-the-fathers-throne) will share in this regal authority.
The presence of [twenty-four elders](#the-twenty-four-elders) on thrones around
God's central seat further emphasizes that heavenly honor is granted to many.
Ultimately, all who sit on these thrones continue to
[worship the Father](#worship-before-the-throne) as the supreme source of all
power.
