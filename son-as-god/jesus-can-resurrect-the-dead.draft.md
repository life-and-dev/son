# Can Jesus Raise the Dead?

Jesus has a real, exalted role in the resurrection of the dead. The question is whether that role is inherent divine power or authority received from God.

> “The Father **has granted** the Son also to have life in himself, and he has **given** him authority to execute judgment.” — John 5:26-27 (ESV)

Trinitarians rightly observe that John 5:21 says the Son gives life to whom he will, and Philippians 3:20-21 says he will transform believers. The Unitarian reading gives these claims full force while reading them with God’s grant and with Paul’s final order, where the Son is subject to God (1 Corinthians 15:24-28). See [Jesus Was Resurrected](jesus-was-resurrected.draft.md#jesus-resurrection).

*Jesus’ life-giving authority supports his unique messianic office; the texts expressly present it as received from the Father.*

## Contextual Distinction

The strongest deity inference treats resurrection power as a property only God can possess. Scripture also assigns Jesus a real role in raising the dead, but explains it as a commission: the Father gives the Son life and authority (John 5:26-27). At the resurrection Jesus delivers the kingdom to God the Father and is himself subject to God (1 Corinthians 15:24-28). His life-giving work honours God’s appointed human Messiah; it does not make the recipient of authority the God who grants it.

# 1 Corinthians 15:22

> For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive. -- 1 Corinthians 15:22 (ESV)

Paul describes resurrection in relation to Christ:

> In fact **Christ has been raised from the dead**, the firstfruits **of those who have fallen asleep**. 
> 
> For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. 
> 
> For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive. 
> 
> -- 1 Corinthians 15:20-22 (ESV)

Christ had died, so will we die. Christ has been resurrected, so will we be made alive again.
