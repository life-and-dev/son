# Is Jesus Holy Because He Is God?

Jesus is holy, sinless, and uniquely consecrated by God. Scripture also calls God’s servants, people, and angels holy, so holiness alone is not an identity claim.

> “The angel answered her, ‘The Holy Spirit will come upon you ... therefore the child to be born will be called **holy**, the Son of God.’” — Luke 1:35 (ESV)

Trinitarians rightly note Jesus’ sinlessness and the worshipful confession “the Holy One of God” (Mark 1:24). The Unitarian case reads this with Jesus’ prayer that God sanctify him and with Peter’s description of “Jesus of Nazareth, whom God anointed” (John 17:19; Acts 10:38). God is holy in a unique, underived sense; Jesus is the holy Messiah whom God sanctified. See [The Son as God](the-son-as-god.draft.md).

*Jesus’ holiness supports his saving fitness and divine commission, not necessarily identity with the God who sanctifies him.*

## Holiness and God’s Commission

Jesus’ holiness is moral and vocational: he is without sin and set apart for God’s work. The New Testament can call believers holy without treating them as God, while Jesus’ holiness is unique in its saving role.

> “He committed **no sin**, neither was deceit found in his mouth.” — 1 Peter 2:22 (ESV)

This perfection makes Jesus a fitting sacrifice. It does not remove the distinction in which God sanctifies, sends, raises, and exalts him (John 10:36; Acts 3:13-15).
