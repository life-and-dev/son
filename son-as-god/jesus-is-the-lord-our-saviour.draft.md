# Is Jesus Our Great God and Saviour?

Titus calls Jesus Christ Saviour and contains one of the New Testament’s strongest grammatical cases for calling him God.

> “Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our **great God and Saviour Jesus Christ**.” — Titus 2:13 (ESV)

The single article before “God and Saviour” strongly favours a single referent, Jesus Christ. That is a serious Trinitarian argument, not a translation error. A Unitarian reading must weigh it with Titus’s repeated distinction between “God our Saviour” and “Jesus Christ our Saviour,” and Paul’s one-God, one-mediator confession (Titus 1:3-4; 3:4-6; 1 Timothy 2:5). See [The Word With God](the-word-with-god.draft.md#the-word-with-god).

*The grammar gives Titus 2:13 full evidential weight, while the letter’s wider God-and-Messiah pattern remains relevant to its interpretation.*

## Titus 2:13

The strongest Trinitarian reading takes “our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ” as one person. The single-article construction gives that reading substantial grammatical support and should receive full weight. It is not fair to dismiss it as translator bias or to divide the nouns merely because Paul elsewhere distinguishes God and Christ.

The wider letter still matters. Titus calls both God and Jesus “Saviour” and says that God pours out salvation through Jesus Christ (Titus 1:3-4; 3:4-6). Those repeated distinctions give a Unitarian interpreter a canonical reason to ask whether Titus 2:13 uses “God” in an exceptional Christological sense or presents God’s glory in association with Christ’s appearing.

*The grammar strongly favours Jesus as referent of both titles; the Unitarian case rests on the wider God-and-Messiah pattern, not on denying that grammar.*

### Contextual Distinction

Even if Titus 2:13 grammatically calls Jesus “God and Saviour,” one debated construction cannot overturn the letter’s explicit distinction. The Father is “God our Saviour,” and salvation is poured out through Jesus Christ our Saviour (Titus 3:4-6). “Saviour” describes both source and appointed means. This honours Jesus’ saving office while keeping the one God as the Father, as Titus and Paul elsewhere do (Titus 1:3-4; 1 Corinthians 8:6).
