# Jesus was baptized

The baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist is a pivotal event recorded in the Gospels. While the act itself is well-known, its theological significance has been a subject of deep discussion for centuries. Why would a sinless figure undergo a baptism meant for repentance, and what does this act reveal about his nature and mission?

## The Apparent Contradiction

The Old Testament states that God is not a "son of man" that he should repent.

> “**God is not a man**, that He should lie, **nor a son of man**, that He should **repent**. — Numbers 23:19 (NKJV)

John's baptism was explicitly a "baptism of repentance."

> Then Paul said, “**John indeed baptized with a baptism of repentance**, saying to the people that they should believe on him who would come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus.” — Acts 19:4 (NKJV)

Yet, Jesus, who many believe to be God, was baptized by John.

> It came to pass in those days that **Jesus** came from Nazareth of Galilee, and **was baptized by John** in the Jordan. — Mark 1:9 (NKJV)

This raises the question: If Jesus is God, and God does not repent, why was he baptized with a baptism of repentance?

## Theological Interpretations

The apparent contradiction has led to various theological interpretations.

### To Fulfill All Righteousness

In the Gospel of Matthew, John the Baptist himself expresses hesitation in baptizing Jesus.

> Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan to be baptized by John. But John tried to deter him, saying, “I need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?”
>
> Jesus replied, “Let it be so now; it is proper for us to do this to **fulfill all righteousness**.” Then John consented. — Matthew 3:13-15 (NIV)

Many theologians interpret "to fulfill all righteousness" as Jesus identifying with sinful humanity and setting an example of obedience to God's will. By undergoing baptism, Jesus was not repenting for personal sin, but rather identifying with the human condition and beginning his public ministry.

### To Identify with Sinners

Another common interpretation is that Jesus's baptism was an act of solidarity with sinners. By participating in a ritual meant for sinners, he was demonstrating his role as the one who would take upon himself the sins of the world. This is supported by John the Baptist's declaration the day after the baptism:

> The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, “Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world! — John 1:29 (NIV)

## Conclusion

The baptism of Jesus presents a theological puzzle. While the act appears to contradict the idea of a sinless divine being undergoing a baptism of repentance, theological interpretations offer various explanations. Whether seen as an act [to fulfill all righteousness](#to-fulfill-all-righteousness) or a profound act of [identification with sinners](#to-identify-with-sinners), the baptism of Jesus remains a significant event in Christian theology, marking the beginning of his public ministry and setting the stage for his ultimate sacrifice.
