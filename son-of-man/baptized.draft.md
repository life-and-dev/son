# Jesus was baptized

The baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist is a pivotal event in Christian theology, yet it presents a profound question: If John's baptism was one of repentance for sin, why did Jesus, the sinless Son of God, undergo it? This article delves into the scriptural accounts to understand the deeper significance of this sacred act.

## The Apparent Contradiction

The scriptures present a potential paradox. On one hand, the Old Testament states that God is not a man who needs to repent.

> **God is not a man**, that He should lie, **nor a son of man**, that He should **repent**. — Numbers 23:19 (NKJV)

John's baptism was explicitly for repentance.

> Then Paul said, “**John indeed baptized with a baptism of repentance**, saying to the people that they should believe on him who would come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus.” — Acts 19:4 (NKJV)

Yet, Jesus himself was baptized by John.

> It came to pass in those days that **Jesus** came from Nazareth of Galilee, and **was baptized by John** in the Jordan. — Mark 1:9 (NKJV)

This raises the question of why Jesus would need a baptism of repentance.

## Fulfilling All Righteousness

The answer lies in Jesus' own words. When John the Baptist hesitated to baptize Him, Jesus replied:

> Let it be so now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness. — Matthew 3:15 (ESV)

Jesus' baptism was not an admission of personal sin, but an act of identification with humanity and a fulfillment of God's righteous plan. By submitting to baptism, Jesus identified with sinners, not as a sinner, but as the one who would ultimately bear their sins. This act also served as a public anointing, marking the official beginning of His earthly ministry and receiving divine affirmation:

> And when Jesus was baptized, immediately he went up from the water, and behold, the heavens were opened to him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming to rest on him; and behold, a voice from heaven said, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased.” — Matthew 3:16-17 (ESV)

## Conclusion

The baptism of Jesus, while seemingly contradictory, holds deep theological meaning.

- It presents an [apparent contradiction](#the-apparent-contradiction) when considering the nature of God and the purpose of John's baptism.
- Jesus' own explanation reveals his baptism as an act of [fulfilling all righteousness](#fulfilling-all-righteousness), identifying with humanity, and inaugurating His public ministry, which was confirmed by a divine proclamation.
