# Did Jesus Deny Being God?

The Gospels portray Jesus in many ways, but one of the most debated aspects of his identity is his relationship with God. This article explores several passages in which Jesus' own words seem to create a clear distinction between himself and God, raising important questions for theological interpretation.

## Direct Denials

> So He said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good but **One, that is, God**. — Matthew 19:17 (NKJV); Mark 10:18; Luke 18:19

and

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing....
>
> ...How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from **the only God**?
>
> Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father.
>
> — John 5:19, 44-45 (ESV)

and

> Jesus answered, If I honour myself, my honour is nothing: it is my Father that honoureth me; of whom ye say, that **He is your God.** — John 8:54 (KJV)

and

> Jesus spoke these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said: “Father, the hour has come. Glorify Your Son, that Your Son also may glorify You, as You have given him authority over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as You have given him. And this is eternal life, that they may know You, **the only true God**, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent. — John 17:1-3 (NKJV)

## Implicit Denials

> But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son**, but **only the Father**. — Mark 13:32 (ESV)

[Jesus called himself "the Son"](../index.md), therefore he implied that he is not our Father God.

and

> Jesus answered, “I do not have a demon, but **I honor my Father**, and you dishonor me. Yet **I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and He is the judge**. — John 8:49-50 (ESV)

Jesus made a clear distinction between himself and the Father. This implies that Jesus is not the Father.

## Alternative Interpretations

It is important to acknowledge that these verses are interpreted in various ways. For instance, some theologians argue that when Jesus speaks of the Father being "the only God," he is speaking from his human nature, and not diminishing his divine nature. The statement in Mark 13:32, where Jesus claims not to know the hour of his return, is often explained as a voluntary limitation of his divine omniscience during his earthly ministry, a concept known as kenosis.

These interpretations seek to reconcile the passages in this article with other parts of the New Testament that appear to affirm Jesus' divinity, such as John 1:1 ("the Word was God") and John 20:28 ("My Lord and my God!"). The theological position of the Trinity, for example, holds that the Father and the Son are distinct persons, but co-equal in essence, which allows for the distinctions Jesus makes without denying his divinity.

## Conclusion

The passages reviewed in this article present a consistent theme: Jesus' words often distinguish him from God. The evidence presented can be summarized as follows:

-   Jesus directly deflects the title of "good" to God alone, as documented in the Gospels of [Matthew, Mark, and Luke](#direct-denials).
-   He emphasizes his subordination to the Father, stating he can do nothing of his own accord in the book of [John](#direct-denials).
-   Jesus' explicit statement that only the Father knows the day and hour of his return suggests a clear limitation of his own knowledge, as recorded in the book of [Mark](#implicit-denials).

These statements have led to a variety of theological interpretations, but a surface-level reading suggests a clear distinction between Jesus and God the Father.
