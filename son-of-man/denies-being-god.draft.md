# Jesus Denies Being God

The nature of Jesus's relationship with God has been a topic of theological debate for centuries. While many believe in the divinity of Christ, a closer look at the Gospels reveals several instances where Jesus's own words seem to suggest a different perspective. This article explores those specific passages, inviting readers to consider the nuances of his statements and what they might imply about his identity.

## Direct Denies

### God is the only good

> So He said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good but **One, that is, God**. — Matthew 19:17 (NKJV); Mark 10:18; Luke 18:19

### God is the only God

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing...
>
> How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from **the only God**? 
> 
> Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father.
>
> — John 5:19,44-45 (ESV)

### God is Jesus' God

> Jesus answered, If I honour myself, my honour is nothing: it is my Father that honoureth me; of whom ye say, that **He is your God.** — John 8:54 (KJV)

### God is the only true God

> Jesus spoke these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said: “Father, the hour has come. Glorify Your Son, that Your Son also may glorify You, as You have given him authority over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as You have given him. And this is eternal life, that they may know You, **the only true God**, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent. — John 17:1-3 (NKJV)

## Implicit Denies

### Jesus is not all-knowing

> But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son**, but **only the Father**. — Mark 13:32 (ESV)

[Jesus called himself "the Son"](../index.md), therefore he implied that he is not our Father God.

### Jesus does not seek his own glory

> Jesus answered, “I do not have a demon, but **I honor my Father**, and you dishonor me. Yet **I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and He is the judge**. — John 8:49-50 (ESV)

Jesus made a clear distinction between himself and the Father. This implies that Jesus is not the Father.

## Critiques

Some may argue that when Jesus speaks of God as a separate being, he is simply operating within the limitations of his human nature. From this perspective, Jesus’s statements are not denials of his divinity, but rather acknowledgements of his role as the Son in the Trinitarian framework.

However, this interpretation requires reading a complex theological concept back into the text. The straightforward reading of these passages suggests a clear distinction between Jesus and God.

## Conclusion

The Gospels present a series of statements from Jesus that, on a plain reading, suggest a clear distinction between himself and God. [He deflects the term "good" to God alone](#god-is-the-only-good), speaks of [God as a separate being to whom he is subordinate](#god-is-jesus-god), and explicitly denies having the [same knowledge as the Father](#jesus-is-not-all-knowing). While theological interpretations can reconcile these statements with the doctrine of the Trinity, the texts themselves present a more straightforward picture of Jesus as the Son of God, distinct from the Father.
