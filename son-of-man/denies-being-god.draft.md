# Jesus Denies Being God

Many Christians believe that Jesus is God. However, a closer look at the Gospels reveals several statements from Jesus himself that seem to challenge this core belief. This article explores those statements and their implications.

## Direct Denies

> So He said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good but **One, that is, God**. — Matthew 19:17 (NKJV); Mark 10:18; Luke 18:19

and

> So Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing...
>
> How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from **the only God**? 
> 
> Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father.
>
> — John 5:19,44-45 (ESV)

and

> Jesus answered, If I honour myself, my honour is nothing: it is my Father that honoureth me; of whom ye say, that **He is your God.** — John 8:54 (KJV)

and

> Jesus spoke these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said: “Father, the hour has come. Glorify Your Son, that Your Son also may glorify You, as You have given him authority over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as You have given him. And this is eternal life, that they may know You, **the only true God**, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent. — John 17:1-3 (NKJV)

## Implicit Denies

> But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son**, but **only the Father**. — Mark 13:32 (ESV)

[Jesus called himself "the Son"](../index.md), therefore he implied that he is not [our Father God](https://ofgod.info).

and

> Jesus answered, “I do not have a demon, but **I honor my Father**, and you dishonor me. Yet **I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and He is the judge**. — John 8:49-50 (ESV)

Jesus made a clear distinction between himself and the Father. This implies that Jesus is not the Father.

## Counterarguments

Trinitarian theology offers interpretations of these verses that affirm Jesus's divinity.

### "Why do you call me good?"

Trinitarians argue that Jesus was not denying His own goodness, but rather challenging the man's understanding of "good." By saying, "No one is good but God alone," Jesus was prompting the man to recognize that if he called Jesus "good," he must also recognize His divine nature. The question, in this view, is a rhetorical device to lead the man to a deeper understanding of who Jesus is.

### "Nor the Son"

Regarding Mark 13:32, where Jesus states that the Son does not know the hour of His return, Trinitarians often explain this by referring to the doctrine of the hypostatic union—the belief that Jesus is both fully God and fully man. In this view, Jesus voluntarily limited His divine knowledge in His human nature. Therefore, His statement reflects the limitations of His humanity, not a denial of His divinity. The distinction is made between what Jesus knew in His human consciousness and what He knew as the divine Son of God.

## Conclusion

The question of whether Jesus denied being God is a complex one, with scripture offering passages that are interpreted in different ways.

- The Gospels record several instances where Jesus makes statements that, on the surface, seem to [directly](#direct-denies) or [implicitly](#implicit-denies) deny his divinity.
- These passages are central to the arguments of those who do not believe in the Trinity.
- However, [Trinitarian theology offers counterarguments](#counterarguments) that interpret these same passages in a way that is consistent with Jesus's divinity.

Ultimately, a reader's conclusion on this matter will depend on their interpretation of these key passages and their broader theological framework.
