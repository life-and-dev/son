# The Distinction Between Jesus and God

The relationship between Jesus and God has been a subject of theological debate for centuries. While many believe them to be one and the same, the Bible records numerous interactions between them that suggest a more distinct relationship. This article will explore these interactions, examining prophecies and testimonies that shed light on their unique connection.

## Old Testament Prophecies

### Isaiah's Prophecy of the Anointed One

In the book of Luke, Jesus reads from the prophet Isaiah to announce the fulfillment of a prophecy.

> He was handed the book of the prophet Isaiah. And when he had opened the book, he found the place where it was written: “The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me to preach good tidings to the poor; He has sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound; To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord.” ... Then he closed the book, and gave it back to the attendant and sat down. And the eyes of all who were in the synagogue were fixed on Him. And He began to say to them, “Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.”
> — Luke 4:17-21 (NKJV)

This passage from Isaiah 61:1-2, which Jesus quotes, describes one who is anointed by "the Lord God," or "YHVH." This distinction is significant. If Jesus and God were the same being, this passage would describe God anointing Himself.

### Daniel's Vision of the Son of Man

The prophet Daniel also foresaw a distinction between Jesus and God. In his vision, he saw "the son of man" brought before "the Ancient of Days," indicating two separate individuals.

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before Him. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed.
> — Daniel 7:13-14 (ESV)

Many other [prophecies also identify Jesus as distinct from God](prophecies.md#jesus-is-distinct-from-god).

## Jesus's Earthly Life

### Jesus Grew in Favor with God

Luke records that Jesus matured in his relationship with both God and humanity.

> And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature, and in favor with God and man.
> — Luke 2:52 (NIV)

This growth suggests a relationship between two distinct beings, rather than one being growing in favor with himself.

### Jesus as a Separate Witness from God

Jesus declared that his testimony was valid because it was confirmed by a second witness, his Father, in accordance with the law.

> In your Law it is written that the testimony of two people is true. I am the one who bears witness about myself, and the Father who sent me bears witness about me.
> — John 8:17-18 (ESV)

This distinction is essential for his testimony to be considered valid under the law he cited.

### Jesus Prayed to God

Throughout his ministry, Jesus consistently prayed to God, whom he referred to as his Father in heaven.

> Pray then like this: "Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name..."
> — Matthew 6:9 (ESV)
>
> Or do you think that I cannot now pray to my Father, and He will provide Me with more than twelve legions of angels?
> — Matthew 26:53 (NKJV)

Jesus's reliance on prayer suggests a relationship of dependence on the Father, which is consistent with two distinct beings.

### Jesus's Cry on the Cross

In one of his final moments, Jesus cried out to God, feeling forsaken.

> And about the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, saying, “My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?”
> — Matthew 27:46 (NKJV); Mark 15:34

This cry from the cross highlights a moment of profound separation between Jesus and God.

## Apostolic Testimonies

### Stephen's Vision of Jesus and God

As Stephen was being martyred, he saw a vision of Jesus and God as two separate beings.

> But he, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God. And he said, “Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.”
> — Acts 7:55-56 (ESV)

### Paul's Teaching on the Mediator

Paul identified Jesus as the mediator who stands between God and humanity.

> For there is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.
> — 1 Timothy 2:5 (NKJV)

The role of a mediator implies three parties: God, humanity, and Jesus who bridges the gap between them.

### John's Vision of the Lamb and the Throne

In the book of Revelation, John describes a vision where the Lamb (Jesus) approaches the one seated on the throne (God) to take a scroll. This imagery clearly depicts two distinct individuals.

> And he went and took the scroll from the right hand of Him who was seated on the throne. And when he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb...
> — Revelation 5:7-8 (ESV)

## Critiques and Counterarguments

It is often argued that the concept of the Trinity—one God existing in three persons—explains the interactions between Jesus and the Father. From this perspective, Jesus and the Father are distinct persons, but they are both fully God. While this view is widely held, the scriptures cited in this article present a challenge to it by portraying a clear hierarchy and distinction that is not easily reconciled with the idea of co-equal persons of a Godhead.

Another common argument is that when Jesus interacted with God, he was merely setting an example for his followers. If these interactions were not genuine, however, it would imply that Jesus was pretending to pray to a separate being, which raises questions about the authenticity of his teachings and the apostles' testimonies.

Similarly, some suggest that these interactions were symbolic prophecies. But prophets in the Bible always clarified when they were delivering a message from God. The Gospel writers, in contrast, present these events as straightforward accounts of Jesus's life, without any indication that they should be interpreted symbolically.

## Conclusion

The biblical record provides numerous examples of Jesus and God interacting as distinct beings. From [Old Testament prophecies](#old-testament-prophecies) to [Jesus's own words and actions](#jesuss-earthly-life) and the [testimonies of his apostles](#apostolic-testimonies), the scriptures consistently portray a relationship between a Father and a Son. While various interpretations exist to explain these interactions, the most straightforward reading of these passages suggests that Jesus and God are not one and the same, but rather two distinct and separate individuals.
