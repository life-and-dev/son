# The Distinction Between Jesus and God

The Bible presents a nuanced relationship between Jesus Christ and God the Father. While they share a divine connection, numerous passages highlight their distinct identities. This article explores the biblical evidence for this distinction, drawing from prophecies, Jesus's own words, their interactions, and the testimonies of the apostles.

## Prophetic Portrayals

Old Testament prophecies foretold a Messiah who would be distinct from God.

### Isaiah's Prophecy

In the synagogue, Jesus read from the prophet Isaiah:

> “The Spirit of **the Lord God** is upon me, because the **LORD** (YHVH) has anointed me to preach good tidings to the poor; He has sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound; To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord.” — Isaiah 61:1-2 (quoted in Luke 4:18-19)

Jesus then declared, "Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing" (Luke 4:21). This prophecy distinguishes between the one who anoints (the LORD) and the one who is anointed (Jesus). If Jesus were the same being as the "Lord God," it would be illogical for him to anoint himself.

### Daniel's Vision

Daniel's vision also presents two separate beings:

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man, and **he came to** the Ancient of Days and was **presented before Him**. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom... — Daniel 7:13-14 (ESV)

Here, the "son of man" (a title Jesus used for himself) approaches the "Ancient of Days" (God the Father), clearly indicating two distinct individuals.

## Jesus's Own Testimony

Jesus's words often emphasized his distinctness from the Father.

### Jesus as a Witness

In a legal context, Jesus invoked the requirement of two witnesses:

> In your Law it is written that the testimony of **two** people is true. I am the one who bears witness about myself, and **the Father** who sent me bears witness about me. — John 8:17-18 (ESV)

If Jesus and the Father were the same person, his testimony would be invalid as there would only be one witness.

### Jesus's Prayers

Jesus consistently prayed to the Father as a separate being. He taught his disciples to pray to "Our Father in heaven" (Matthew 6:9) and referred to God as "your Father who is in secret" (Matthew 6:6). In a moment of personal need, he said:

> Or do you think that I cannot now **pray to my Father**, and He will provide Me with more than twelve legions of angels? — Matthew 26:53 (NKJV)

This prayer would be unnecessary if Jesus had the authority to command angels himself, without appealing to a higher power.

### Jesus's View on 'Father'

Jesus made a clear distinction between earthly fathers and the heavenly Father:

> Do not call **anyone on earth** your father; for One is **your Father, He who is in heaven**. — Matthew 23:9 (ESV)

By his own words, Jesus, being on earth, could not be "the Father."

## Interactions and Relationships

The Gospels describe a clear relationship between Jesus and God, one of interaction and distinction.

### Jesus Grew in Favor with God

Luke records Jesus's development:

> And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature, and in **favor with God** and man. — Luke 2:52 (NIV)

This verse suggests a relationship between two separate beings, where one grows in favor with the other.

### The Samaritan Woman's Understanding

The Samaritan woman at the well expected a Messiah distinct from God, and Jesus confirmed her belief:

> The woman said to him, “I know that Messiah is coming (he who is called Christ). When he comes, he will tell us all things.” Jesus said to her, “I who speak to you am he.” — John 4:25-26 (ESV)

Jesus did not correct her understanding of a distinct Messiah.

### God's Public Glorification of Jesus

On one occasion, Jesus prayed:

> "Father, glorify your name.” Then a voice came from heaven: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.” — John 12:28 (ESV)

This is a clear interaction between two distinct beings, one making a request and the other responding.

### Belief in God and Jesus

Jesus encouraged belief in both God and himself as separate objects of faith:

> "Let not your hearts be troubled. Believe in God; believe **also** in me." — John 14:1 (ESV)

The word "also" indicates a second object of belief, distinct from the first.

### The Separation on the Cross

On the cross, Jesus cried out:

> “My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?” — Matthew 27:46 (NKJV)

This cry of dereliction is one of the most powerful expressions of the distinction between Jesus and God. It makes little sense if Jesus were God himself.

## The Apostles' Witness

The apostles consistently testified to Jesus and God as distinct beings.

### Stephen's Vision

As he was being stoned, Stephen saw a vision of heaven:

> "Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.” — Acts 7:56 (ESV)

Stephen saw two distinct beings: the Son of Man and God.

### John's Testimony

John the Baptist, who was also an apostle, preached:

> how **God anointed Jesus** of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power... — Acts 10:38 (NKJV)

The act of anointing implies two distinct parties: the one who anoints (God) and the one who is anointed (Jesus).

### Paul's Understanding

Paul described Jesus as a mediator between God and humanity:

> For there is one God and one Mediator **between** God and men, the **man** Christ Jesus — 1 Timothy 2:5 (NKJV)

A mediator, by definition, is a go-between who is distinct from the parties being reconciled.

### The Author of Hebrews' View

The book of Hebrews portrays Jesus as a high priest who is faithful to the one who appointed him:

> ...consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, Christ Jesus, **who was faithful to Him Who appointed him**... — Hebrews 3:1-2 (NKJV)

This again shows a relationship of appointment and faithfulness between two distinct beings.

### John of Patmos' Vision in Revelation

In the book of Revelation, John saw a vision of the heavenly throne room:

> And I saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain... And he went and took the scroll from the right hand of Him who was seated on the throne. — Revelation 5:6-7 (ESV)

The Lamb (Jesus) is clearly distinct from "Him who was seated on the throne" (God).

## Post-Resurrection Appearances

After his resurrection, Jesus's distinction from God continued to be evident.

### Mark's Account

Mark's Gospel concludes with Jesus's ascension:

> So then, after the Lord had spoken to them, He was received up into heaven, and sat down **at the right hand** of God. — Mark 16:19 (NKJV)

This fulfills the prophecy of Psalm 110:1 and shows Jesus in a position of honor next to God, not as God himself.

## Addressing Counterarguments

Some may argue that these interactions are symbolic or that Jesus was merely setting an example. However, the biblical authors present these events as real interactions between distinct beings. If these were merely symbolic, one would expect the authors to have made that clear. The consistent testimony of Scripture points to a clear distinction between Jesus and God the Father.

## Conclusion

The Bible provides a wealth of evidence that Jesus and God are distinct beings. From Old Testament prophecies to Jesus's own words, his interactions with the Father, and the consistent witness of the apostles, the picture that emerges is one of two separate and distinct individuals in a unique and divine relationship.
