# Jesus and God are Distinct

The relationship between Jesus and God has been a subject of theological debate for centuries. While some Christian doctrines view them as one and the same being, the biblical texts contain numerous passages where Jesus and God appear to interact as distinct individuals. This article will explore various scriptural accounts, from Old Testament prophecies to New Testament testimonies, that depict a clear distinction and interaction between the Son and the Father.

## Prophecies

### Isaiah Prophesied that Jesus Would Be Distinct

In Luke 4:17 (NKJV), Jesus reads from the prophet Isaiah:

> “[The Spirit of the Lord GOD](/god/spirit) is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me to preach good tidings to the poor; He has sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound; To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD.”

After reading this passage from Isaiah 61:1-2, Jesus declared, “Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing” (Luke 4:21). By applying this prophecy to himself, Jesus confirmed that Isaiah was speaking about him.

This prophecy raises a logical inconsistency for the belief that Jesus is the same being as the "Lord God." If they were one and the same, it would mean Jesus sent his own spirit to himself and anointed himself for his mission.

### Daniel Prophesied that Jesus Would Be Distinct

The prophet Daniel also described seeing two separate beings. He testifies that he saw "[the son of man](human.md)" brought before "[the Ancient of Days](/god/father/ancient-of-days)":

> I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man, and **he came to** the Ancient of Days and was **presented before Him**. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed. — Daniel 7:13-14 (ESV)

There are many more [prophecies that identify Jesus as distinct from God](prophecies.md#jesus-is-distinct-from-god).

## Jesus Grew in Favour

Luke records that Jesus was not stagnant but developed as a person:

> And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature, and in **favor with God** and man. — Luke 2:52 (NIV)

The fact that Jesus could grow in favor with God suggests a relationship between two distinct individuals, as it implies one being observed and approved of the other's development.

## Jesus Witnessed with God

If Jesus were the same person as God the Father, his own testimony would have been singular. However, Jesus presented himself and the Father as two separate witnesses:

> In your Law it is written that the testimony of **two** people is true.
>
> 1. **I** am the one who bears witness about myself, and
> 2. **the Father** who sent me bears witness about me.
>
> -- John 8:17-18 (ESV)

## Jesus Prays to a Distinct God

While on earth, Jesus consistently prayed to and spoke of the Father as a separate being in heaven.

> “And when you pray, do not heap up empty phrases as the Gentiles do, for they think that they will be heard for their many words. Do not be like them, for **your Father** knows what you need before you ask him. Pray then like this:
>
> "**Our Father in heaven**, hallowed be your name..."
>
> -- Matthew 6:8-9 (ESV)

Jesus's consistent references to a Father "in heaven" imply a distinction in person and location, which is difficult to reconcile with the idea that he himself is the Father.

Some might argue that Jesus was only setting an example for his disciples. However, in a moment of personal crisis, he again distinguished himself from the Father:

> Or do you think that I cannot now **pray to my Father**, and He will provide Me with more than twelve legions of angels? — Matthew 26:53 (NKJV)

This was not merely an example for his disciples, as the specific request for legions of angels was unique to his situation. Jesus had to pray to get this request granted. If he were God Himself, he would have no need to pray to someone else to send angels.

## The Samaritans Believed God and Jesus Were Distinct

When Jesus spoke with a Samaritan woman, he referred to God as a distinct spirit who should be worshipped.

> Jesus said to her, “Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you worship [the Father](/god/father)... the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father is seeking such people to worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.”
>
> The woman said to him, “**I know that [Messiah](/god/son/christ) is coming**... **When he comes, [he will tell us all things](/god/son/purpose).**”
>
> Jesus said to her, “**I who speak to you am he.**” — John 4:21-26 (ESV)

The Samaritan woman believed that a distinct person, the Messiah, would be sent by God. Jesus did not correct her but confirmed that he was the Messiah.

## God Publicly Glorified Jesus

On one occasion, a voice from heaven affirmed Jesus, demonstrating an interaction between two distinct beings:

> “Now is my soul troubled. And what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour? But for this purpose I have come to this hour. Father, glorify your name.”
> Then a voice came from heaven: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”
>
> -- John 12:27-28 (ESV)

## It Is Possible to Believe in God and Jesus Separately

Jesus himself made a distinction between believing in God and believing in him:

> Let not your hearts be troubled.
>
> 1. Believe in God;
> 2. believe **also** in me.
>
> In my Father’s house are many rooms.
>
> -- John 14:1-2 (ASV)

This statement would be confusing if Jesus is the same person as God. Jesus acknowledged that it was possible for people to believe in God but not in him, which was the case with many religious leaders of his time.

## Jesus and God Were Separated

On the cross, Jesus cried out:

> *“Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?”* that is, “***My God, my God***, why have You forsaken me?”
>
> -- Matthew 27:46 (NKJV); Mark 15:34

This cry raises several critical questions for the view that Jesus is God. It is logically challenging to understand why he would ask a question of himself, how one could forsake oneself, and why he would address another being as "My God" if he himself were that God.

Some Trinitarians explain this by reasoning that "forsaken" refers to Jesus taking on the sin of the world. However, this reasoning does not fully address why he would cry out "with a loud voice" to himself or address himself as "my God," especially when there are no other examples of him praying to or worshipping himself. The crowd's reaction further supports the idea of two distinct beings:

> They were filled with awe and said, “Truly this was the Son of God!” — Matthew 27:54 (ESV)

The onlookers realized they had killed a person distinct from God, whom God dearly loved.

## The Apostles' Witnesses

### Mark Saw Jesus Distinct from God

Mark testifies that Jesus sat separately from God in heaven:

> So then, after the Lord had spoken to them, He was received up into heaven, and sat down **at the right hand** of God. — Mark 16:19 (NKJV)

This fulfills the prophecy in Psalm 110:1.

### Stephen's Vision

Stephen saw Jesus standing next to God as a separate person.

> But he, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God, and **Jesus standing at the right hand of God**.<br/>
> And he said, “Behold, I see the heavens opened, and ***the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God***.”
>
> -- Acts 7:55-56 (ESV)

### Peter's Testimony

Peter testified that God worked through Jesus, highlighting their distinct roles:

> ...how **God anointed Jesus** of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power... — Acts 10:38 (NKJV)

### Paul's Explanation

Paul confirmed that Jesus is the mediator **between** God and humans, which requires him to be distinct from God:

> For there is one God and one Mediator **between** God and men, the **man** Christ Jesus — 1 Timothy 2:5 (NKJV)

### John of Patmos's Vision

In the book of Revelation, John saw "a Lamb" (representing Jesus) approach the "One seated on the throne" (God) to take a scroll. This vision clearly depicts two separate individuals:

> And **he went and took the scroll from the right hand of Him who was seated on the throne**. And when he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty - four elders fell down before the Lamb...
>
> -- Revelation 5:7-8 (ESV)

## Critiques

One common counter-argument is that when Jesus talked, prayed, or interacted with God, he was merely setting an example for his disciples. However, if he were simulating an interaction with an imaginary being to demonstrate a concept, the Gospel writers likely would have explained this to avoid misleading their readers.

Another argument is that these interactions are symbolic prophetic messages. This view is also unlikely, as prophets in the Bible typically made it clear when they were delivering a message from God versus speaking their own words. The Gospel authors, however, omitted any such explanation for the "prophetic interactions" Jesus had with the Father.

## Conclusion

The scriptures present a consistent picture of Jesus and God as distinct individuals who interacted with each other. This is supported by multiple lines of evidence throughout the Bible:

-   **Old Testament Prophecies**: Prophets like [Isaiah and Daniel](#prophecies) foretold a Messiah who would be separate from God.
-   **Jesus's Own Words and Actions**: Jesus consistently spoke of God as a separate being, as seen when he [prayed to the Father](#jesus-prays-to-a-distinct-god), claimed to be a [co-witness with the Father](#jesus-witnessed-with-god), and acknowledged [growing in favor with God](#jesus-grew-in-favour).
-   **Public Interactions**: There were moments when God [publicly glorified Jesus](#god-publicly-glorified-jesus) with a voice from heaven.
-   **The Crucifixion**: Jesus's cry on the cross, asking why God had [forsaken him](#jesus-and-god-were-separated), strongly implies a separation between them.
-   **Apostolic Testimony**: The apostles, including [Mark, Stephen, Paul, and John](#the-apostles-witnesses), consistently described Jesus as being with or at the right hand of God, acting as a mediator or high priest.

These accounts, taken together, provide a compelling case for understanding Jesus and God as two distinct and separate beings.
