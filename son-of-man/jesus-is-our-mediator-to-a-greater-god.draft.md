## One God and One Mediator

First Timothy 2:5 states: “There is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the **man** Christ Jesus.” Paul identifies the one God and identifies the mediator as “the man Christ Jesus.” The next verse grounds this mediation in his self-giving ransom (1 Timothy 2:6). This is positive evidence that Jesus represents humankind before God, not merely that he appeared human.

### The Trinitarian Reading

Trinitarians hold that the one mediator is fully God and fully human. “Man” can name the incarnate Son’s representative office without exhaustively defining his identity. Hebrews connects his shared flesh and blood with priestly help for his people (Hebrews 2:14-18), and the Word becoming flesh is read as the basis for this account (John 1:1,14). This is a coherent explanation of why a mediator can truly belong to both parties; humanity, service, or prayer do not by themselves disprove incarnation or two natures.

The argument that only a divine mediator could reconcile humanity to God, however, is a theological inference. First Timothy directly gives Christ’s ransom and humanity, while naming God as the one God (1 Timothy 2:5-6). It does not state that mediation requires the mediator to be God.

### The Unitarian Reply

Paul’s wording makes Jesus’ humanity relevant to mediation. A mediator is distinguished from the God before whom he represents people. God appointed, raised, and exalted Jesus for this saving work (Acts 2:32-36; Philippians 2:8-11). Unique office and delegated authority can establish Jesus’ saving agency without identifying him with the one God.

After his resurrection Jesus still says “my God” (John 20:17; Revelation 3:12). Paul names “one God, the Father” and “one Lord, Jesus Christ” (1 Corinthians 8:6). A Trinitarian may read this as distinction within the Godhead, especially as a reworked Shema; a Unitarian reads its differentiated titles and source-through-agency pattern as identifying the Father alone as God. Neither reading should erase the other’s textual data.

*The warranted Unitarian conclusion is that 1 Timothy presents God and the risen human mediator as distinct, while it does not by itself settle every Trinitarian claim about pre-existence.* See [Jesus’ humanity](human.md#jesus-is-human) and [Jesus having a God](has-a-god.md#jesus-has-a-god).
