# Jesus Serves God

This article shows that Jesus serves, obeys, and is subject to God.

> That they may know that You [alone](../shema.md) — Your name is [Yahweh](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/name) — Are **the Most High** over all the earth. — Psalm 83:18 (LSB)

Psalm 83:18 identifies Yahweh as Most High. The following passages are presented as evidence that the Father has authority over Jesus during Jesus' mission.

Jesus said that God had sent, or commissioned, him:

> **Jesus spoke** these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said:
>
> “Father, the hour has come. Glorify Your Son, that Your Son also may glorify You, as You have given him authority over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as You have given him. And this is eternal life, **that they may know You, the only true [God](/god)**, and Jesus Christ **[whom You have sent](https://biblehub.com/greek/649.htm)** *(commission/sent on a mission)*." — John 17:1-3 (NKJV)

## Jesus Submits to God

> And you are Christ’s, and **Christ is God’s**. — 1 Corinthians 3:23 (NKJV)

> But I want you to understand that the head of every man is Christ, the head of a wife is her husband, and **the head of Christ is God**. — 1 Corinthians 11:3 (ESV)

> Now when all things are made subject to him, then **the Son himself will also be subject to Him** who put all things under him, that God may be all in all. — 1 Corinthians 15:28 (NKJV)

These texts describe Christ as belonging to God, having God as his head, and being subject to God. They support the view that [the Father](https://ofgod.info) holds higher authority than Jesus. Other Christians understand this authority as compatible with equality of divine nature, rather than as a denial of Jesus' divinity.

The argument also draws on the following passages:

* God, identified as [Yahweh](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/name), has supreme authority (Psalm 83:18; 103:19-22).
* God calls Jesus “My Servant” (Isaiah 42:1; 52:13-53:12).
* Jesus directs worship to God alone (Matthew 4:10; Luke 4:8).
* Jesus was sent by the Father (John 3:16-18; 4:34; 6:57; 8:29; 14:24; 17:1-3; 1 John 4:14).
* Jesus obeys the Father (John 4:34; 5:19; 6:38; 8:55; 10:37; 14:31).
* Jesus calls himself the vine and his Father the vinedresser (John 15:1-9). This metaphor portrays the Father as the one who tends the vine.
* Jesus entrusted himself to God (1 Peter 2:23).

## Jesus Is Called God's Servant

[The prophets expected Jesus to be a servant of God.](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/messianic)

Peter taught:

> The God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, glorified **His servant** Jesus... — Acts 3:13 (ESV)

Peter also prayed:

> "Truly in this city there were gathered together against your holy **servant** Jesus, whom you anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, along with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place. And now, Lord, look upon their threats and grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness, while You stretch out Your hand to heal, and signs and wonders are performed through the name of Your holy **servant** Jesus." — Acts 4:27-30 (ESV)

Some readers limit these descriptions to Jesus' human mission. However, Paul writes that the risen Jesus “lives to God”:

> For the death that he died, he died to sin once for all; but **the life that he lives, he lives to God**. — Romans 6:10 (NKJV)

## Jesus Teaches Service to God

> And Jesus answered and said to him, “Get behind Me, Satan! For it is written,
>
>> ‘You shall worship the LORD your God, and Him only you shall serve.’” — Deuteronomy 6:13
>
> — Matthew 4:10 (NKJV)

A similar instruction appears in 1 Samuel:

> Samuel said to all the house of Israel, “If you are returning to the LORD with all your heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you and direct your heart to the LORD [***(Yahweh)***](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/1_samuel/7-3.htm)* and **serve Him only**, and He will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines.”
>
> So the people of Israel put away the Baals and the Ashtaroth, and they served the LORD [***(Yahweh)***](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/1_samuel/7-4.htm)* **only**. — 1 Samuel 7:3-4

In this context, LORD translates the Hebrew divine name YHWH. The command is to serve him alone. Christians who identify Jesus with YHWH interpret these passages differently; the text itself does not explicitly identify Jesus in this passage.

Paul similarly said:

> But this I confess to you, that according to the Way, which they call a sect, **I worship the God of our fathers**, believing everything laid down by the Law and written in the Prophets. — Acts 24:14 (ESV)

The “fathers” are Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. They lived long before Jesus' earthly birth. This supports the claim that Paul worshipped the God known to Israel. It does not by itself settle whether Jesus existed before his birth or whether he shares God's identity, as many Christians affirm.

## Jesus Fasted

> Then **Jesus was led up by the Spirit** into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil. And after **fasting** forty days and forty nights, he was hungry. — Matthew 4:1-2 (ESV)

This account is used to raise three theological questions:

1. Jesus was led by the Spirit, which implies he was subjectives to God's direction.
2. Jesus fasted, an act commonly associated with devotion to God and [worship](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/worship).
3. Jesus faced temptation, while James 1:13 states that God cannot be tempted by evil.

These observations support the view that Jesus served God in his earthly life. Trinitarian Christians commonly answer that Jesus could experience hunger, obedience, and temptation in his human nature while remaining divine.

## Conclusion

The passages considered here portray Jesus as [subject to God](#jesus-submits-to-god), [called God's servant](#jesus-is-called-gods-servant), and teaching exclusive [service to God](#jesus-teaches-service-to-god). His [fasting and temptation](#jesus-fasted) are also central to the discussion.
