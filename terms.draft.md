## Reading Rules

**Evidence order.** Use an explicit biblical definition first; then a repeated biblical pattern of at least three uses; then local context; then a dictionary; then a formal creed or church publication. References without quotations mark repeated patterns. A link marks contextual or project treatment.

**Labels.** **Lexical fact** states ordinary word meaning. **Contextual inference** states what a passage means in context. **Project conclusion** states this site’s reading: God is normally the Father; Jesus is God’s human Messiah and agent, not God; the Trinity is false. A word’s history or etymology does not by itself prove theology.

**Scope.** This glossary covers requested debate terms and Gospel idioms relevant to identity, agency, and Christology. It does not inventory every Gospel-language idiom.

**Scripture text.**

> “And the man called his wife’s name Eve, because she was the mother of all living.” — Genesis 3:20 (ESV)

This is an explicit example of a name explaining role, not an identity claim. See [the name of Jesus](name.md#the-meaning-of-yeshua).

## Biblical Titles and Agency

**Scope.** This table exhausts this glossary’s Jesus-title and agency inventory: Son of Man, Son of God, Man of God, Messiah/Christ, Saviour, Mediator, Shepherd, Angel/Angel of the LORD, God’s agency, beget/begotten, genesis, firstborn, and baptism. It does not catalogue every Scripture title, office, or figure.

> “For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.” — 1 Timothy 2:5 (ESV)

| Term | Short definition and evidence |
|---|---|
| **Son of Man** | **Lexical fact:** a human being, or a human representative. Repeated pattern: Ezek 2:1; Ps 8:4; Dan 7:13. **Project conclusion:** Jesus’ preferred title identifies the human Messiah, distinct from God. See [Son of Man](son-of-man.md#common-meaning). |
| **Son of God** | A relational title for angels, Adam, Israel, David, believers, and pre-eminently Jesus; it does not lexically mean “God the Son.” Repeated pattern: Gen 6:2; Luke 3:38; Exod 4:22; Rom 8:14; Ps 2:7. See [sons of God](sons-of-god.md#the-sons-of-god). |
| **Man of God** | A human servant or messenger of God, especially a prophet. Repeated pattern: Deut 33:1; 1 Sam 2:27; 1 Kgs 17:18. It does not make the man God. |
| **Messiah / Christ** | Hebrew *māšîaḥ* and Greek *christos*: anointed one. **Contextual inference:** Jesus is Israel’s promised, anointed human king. John 1:41; 4:25; Acts 2:36. See [human Christ prophecy](son-of-man/prophecies.md#a-human-christ-was-prophesied). |
| **Saviour** | One who delivers or rescues. God is called Saviour, and he saves through appointed human agents. Isa 43:11; Luke 2:11; Acts 5:31. **Project conclusion:** Jesus is Saviour by God’s appointment, not because he is God. |
| **Mediator** | One who stands between parties; explicit definition quoted above. |
| **Shepherd** | Literal flock keeper and figurative leader or carer. Human rulers receive the image. Num 27:17; 2 Sam 5:2; John 10:11. Jesus’ “good shepherd” claim is messianic leadership, not an identity claim. See [shepherd](son-as-god/claims/shepherd.md#the-good-shepherd). |
| **Angel / Angel of the LORD** | Hebrew *mal’akh* and Greek *angelos*: messenger. “Angel of the LORD” means the LORD’s messenger; agency can make a messenger speak for sender. Exod 3:2,4; Judg 6:11-14; Luke 1:11-19. **Project conclusion:** passages need context; title alone neither proves nor requires that Jesus is the angel. See [Angel of the LORD](son-as-angel.md#understanding-the-hebrew-term). |
| **God’s agency** | An authorised representative may bear sender’s words, authority, or name without being sender. Exod 23:20-21; John 5:43; 13:20. **Project conclusion:** receiving Jesus can mean receiving God who sent him. See [representation](son-as-god/claims/receive-god.md#the-logic-of-representation). |
| **Beget / begotten** | To father or bring forth; biblical use varies by context, including royal appointment. Ps 2:7; Acts 13:33; Heb 1:5. It is not a biblical definition of eternal generation. |
| **Genesis** | Origin, birth, or beginning; Greek *genesis*. Matt 1:18; Jas 1:23. It does not itself decide whether a subject is created, eternally generated, or metaphorically appointed. |
| **Firstborn** | First to be born, or the heir with highest rank. Exod 4:22; Ps 89:27; Col 1:15. **Project conclusion:** Colossians 1 must be read in its context, not as a standalone claim that Jesus is God. See [firstborn](son-as-angel/firstborn.md#what-does-firstborn-mean). |
| **Baptism** | Immersion or washing marking repentance, discipleship, or incorporation. Matt 3:6; Acts 2:38; 8:16. Matthew 28:19 names Father, Son, and Holy Spirit but does not define a Trinity. See [baptism formula](trinity/baptism-formula.md#missing-trinity-definition). |

## God, Divine Language, and Attributes

**Scope.** This table exhausts this glossary’s divine-language and attribute inventory: God, YHWH, divine, godhead, one, monotheism, omniscience, omnipresence, immortal, heaven, hell/Gehenna, Word, and Wisdom/Light/Life/Way/Truth. It does not catalogue every Scripture attribute, philosophical attribute, or divine title.

> “To the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen.” — 1 Timothy 1:17 (ESV)

| Term | Short definition and evidence |
|---|---|
| **God** | **Lexical fact:** Hebrew *el* is “god/mighty one”; *elohim* is grammatically plural and can denote the one God, gods, or human judges by context. Gen 1:1; Exod 7:1; Ps 82:1,6. **Project conclusion:** “God” normally identifies the Father in the New Testament. John 17:1,3; 1 Cor 8:6. See [one God](unitarianism.md#one-god). |
| **YHWH** | God’s covenant name in the Hebrew Bible, conventionally rendered “the LORD.” Exod 3:15; 6:2-3; Deut 6:4. A name or quotation overlap requires contextual identity and agency analysis; it does not automatically identify Jesus as YHWH. See [God’s name](son-as-god/claims/i-am.md#gods-name). |
| **Divine** | Of, from, or pertaining to God; Greek *theios* is an adjective, not a personal name. Acts 17:29; 2 Pet 1:3-4. Calling something divine does not by itself establish it as God. See [*theios*](godhead.md#theios). |
| **Godhead** | Older English translation label for Greek *theiotēs* (divine quality, Rom 1:20) and *theotēs* (deity, Col 2:9), not a Bible-defined three-person God. See [translations](godhead.md#translations). |
| **One** | A numerical one; Hebrew *echad* and Greek *heis/mia/hen* agree with grammatical gender and receive context-specific uses. Deut 6:4; Mark 12:29; Gal 3:28. **Project conclusion:** the Shema and Jesus’ citation confess one God, the Father. See [Shema](shema.md#what-the-shema-teaches). |
| **Monotheism** | Belief that one God exists and is worshipped. Repeated biblical pattern: Deut 6:4; Isa 45:5; 1 Cor 8:4-6. **Project conclusion:** biblical monotheism is compatible with God’s Messiah and agents, not a triune God. See [Trinity history](trinity.md#monotheism). |
| **Omniscience** | Knowing all things. Scripture predicates exhaustive knowledge of God. Ps 139:1-6; 1 John 3:20. **Project conclusion:** Jesus’ stated limits show he is not omniscient. Mark 13:32; Luke 2:52. See [limitations](son-of-man/limitations.md#jesus-does-not-know-everything). |
| **Omnipresence** | Presence everywhere, an attribute ascribed to God. Ps 139:7-10; Jer 23:23-24. “I am with you” may describe support or presence through God’s Spirit; it is not automatically spatial omnipresence. See [spiritual presence](son-as-god/claims/omnipresent.md#spiritually-present). |
| **Immortal** | Not subject to death or deathlessness; explicit definition quoted above. Jesus died; God raised him. Acts 2:24; Rom 6:9. |
| **Heaven** | Sky, celestial realm, or God’s dwelling by context. Gen 1:8; Ps 115:3; Matt 6:9. It is not a lexical proof of a person’s pre-human existence. |
| **Hell / Gehenna** | English “hell” covers several terms. *Gehenna* is the valley image Jesus uses for final judgement; *Hades* is the realm of dead; neither should be silently substituted for the other. Matt 10:28; Luke 16:23; Rev 20:14. |
| **Word** | Greek *logos*: word, message, account, reason, or plan by context. **Project conclusion:** John’s Word is God’s self-expression and plan embodied in Jesus, not a second divine person. John 1:1-14; Acts 13:26; 1 Pet 1:23. See [Word](word.md#the-meaning-of-the-word). |
| **Wisdom, Light, Life, Way, Truth, and related abstractions** | Scripture personifies qualities and speaks of them concretely; personification alone does not create a second person. Prov 8:1-4; 9:1-3; John 8:12; 14:6. **Project conclusion:** Jesus embodies and reveals God’s wisdom, light, life, way, and truth as God’s Messiah. See [light](son-as-god/claims/light.md#the-light-of-the-world) and [life](son-as-god/claims/life.md#the-means-to-eternal-life). |

## Life, Death, and Worship

> “then the LORD God formed the man of dust from the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living creature.” — Genesis 2:7 (ESV)

| Term | Short definition and evidence |
|---|---|
| **Death** | The cessation of life; biblical usage can also describe alienation or a condition needing resurrection. Gen 3:19; Ezek 18:4; John 5:24. Do not impose an afterlife system on every occurrence. |
| **Soul** | Hebrew *nephesh* and Greek *psychē*: person, self, living creature, life, appetite, or inner life by context. Genesis 2:7 explicitly calls the formed man a living creature; *living creature* translates *nephesh*. See [BibleHub, *nephesh*](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/5315.htm). |
| **Spirit** | Hebrew *ruach* and Greek *pneuma*: wind, breath, life, disposition, or God’s empowering presence by context. Gen 1:2; Num 11:25; John 3:8; Acts 2:17. **Project conclusion:** Holy Spirit is God’s Spirit and power, not a separate coequal person. See [Holy Spirit](trinity/holy-spirit.md#there-can-only-be-one-holy-spirit). |
| **Afterlife** | Modern umbrella term for life after death. Biblical texts distinguish death, Sheol/Hades, resurrection, judgement, and eternal life; define claim from the passage, not the umbrella. Dan 12:2; John 5:28-29; 1 Cor 15:20-23. |
| **Worship** | English umbrella. Greek *proskyneō* denotes bowing, homage, or worship according to recipient and setting; *latreuō* denotes sacred service offered to God. Matt 2:11; 18:26; Luke 4:8; Rom 12:1. **Project conclusion:** homage to Jesus need not make him God; sacred service belongs to God. See [worship terms](son-as-god/worship.md#two-biblical-concepts-hidden-by-worship). |
| **Pray / prayer** | To ask, entreat, or address God; prayer may include praise, confession, petition, and thanksgiving. Matt 6:9; Phil 4:6. **Project conclusion:** Jesus prays to God and teaches prayer to the Father, marking distinction. See [Jesus’ prayers](son-of-man/has-a-god.md#jesus-prayers). |
| **Serve, ordinary / sacred** | Ordinary service is work for a master or neighbour; Greek *latreuō* is religious service, while *diakoneō* commonly serves or ministers. Matt 20:26-28; Luke 4:8; Rom 1:9. Do not merge terms merely because English renders both “serve.” |
| **Essence** | What something is, or its essential nature. Bible uses ordinary identity language but does not define a divine essence shared by three persons. **Project conclusion:** later metaphysical usage must not control biblical titles. See [nature](nature.md#history-of-christianity). |
| **Person** | In modern English, an individual self. Biblical Hebrew and Greek use several context-bound terms, not a technical three-person definition of God. Use “person” only after defining its sense. See [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Hypostasis** | Greek *hypostasis*: standing under, confidence, reality, or underlying reality by context. Heb 1:3; 3:14; 11:1. It is not a biblical definition of three divine persons. See [Nicene development](trinity.md#nicene-creed). |

## Language, Names, and Gospel Idioms

**Scope.** This section is a complete closed practical inventory under its two printed tables. The retained-form table follows its stated rule. The construction table contains only its printed reading controls for identity, agency, preexistence, worship, knowledge, death, or Trinity. Ordinary Greek is narrative, not Semitic; ordinary names, unmarked naturalised loans, unrelated rhetoric, and textual variants are excluded.

| Term | Short definition and evidence |
|---|---|
| **Name as authority** | A name may identify, represent character, or carry delegated authority. Exod 23:20-21; John 5:43; Acts 4:12. “In the name of” often means by authorised allegiance or authority; it does not itself state shared essence. See [name and authority](trinity/baptism-formula.md#confusing-identity-with-authority). |
| **Greek *en*** | “In,” with a contextual range including location, sphere, means, association, and instrument. John 14:10-11; 17:21-23. It does not by itself prove metaphysical mutual indwelling. See [Greek *en*](godhead/jesus-version.md#the-greek-preposition-en). |
| **Ego eimi / “I am”** | Greek “I am,” sometimes with an implied predicate, a self-identification, or an absolute form. John 9:9; 8:24,58; 18:5-6. **Project conclusion:** grammar alone does not identify Jesus as YHWH. See [linguistic usage](son-as-god/claims/i-am.md#linguistic-usage-of-i-am). |
| **See / know in Gospels** | “See” and “know” can mean physical perception, recognition, experience, or relational knowledge. John 1:18; 14:7-9; 17:3. Determine sense from speaker, object, and context; neither verb automatically proves identity. |

### Semitic Constructions and Narrative Scope

This table is complete only for the printed reading controls used here for identity, agency, preexistence, worship, knowledge, death, or Trinity. A construction is labelled Semitic only where Hebrew, Aramaic, or Septuagintal influence is well supported. Ordinary Greek is narrative, not Semitic.

| Construction | Reading control | References |
|---|---|---|
| **Son of** | Descent, membership, character, or appointment, not automatic ontology. | Matt 5:9; Luke 3:38; John 10:36 |
| **Son of Man** | Semitic human-representative construction; at Jesus’ trial it evokes Daniel 7’s enthroned human figure without identifying him as God. | Dan 7:13-14; Matt 26:64; Mark 14:62; Luke 22:69 |
| **Name** | Identity, reputation, or authority. | Exod 23:21; John 17:6,11 |
| **Coming from / from heaven** | Origin, commission, or heavenly source can be in view; context decides. | John 3:13; 6:38; 16:28 |
| **Before** | Temporal priority, rank, or prior purpose; grammar and context decide. | John 8:58; 15:18; 17:5 |
| **Seeing / knowing** | Perception, recognition, or relationship. | John 6:36; 14:7-9; 17:3 |
| **Hate / love preference** | “Hate” may express lesser loyalty by comparison. | Gen 29:30-31; Luke 14:26 |
| **Bind / loose** | Authorise or forbid within community discipline. | Matt 16:19; 18:18 |
| **Flesh and blood** | Human limitation or human source. | Matt 16:17; Gal 1:16 |
| **Kingdom of heaven** | Jewish reverential circumlocution for God’s kingdom. | Matt 4:17; 5:3; Mark 1:15 |
| **The Power** | Jewish reverential circumlocution for God or the Almighty in the trial sayings. | Matt 26:64; Mark 14:62; Luke 22:69 |
| **Amen, I say** | Solemn introductory affirmation. | Matt 5:18; John 3:3 |
| **Three days / nights** | Inclusive Jewish time reckoning may count portions of days. | Esth 4:16; Matt 12:40; 16:21 |
| **This generation** | Usually Jesus’ contemporaries; context can specify a moral kind. | Matt 11:16; 23:36; 24:34 |
| **Evil / good eye** | Idiom for stinginess/envy or generosity. | Deut 15:9; Matt 6:22-23; 20:15 |
| **Move mountains** | Hyperbole for overcoming the impossible. | Matt 17:20; 21:21 |
| **Fullness / all / none** | Ordinary-Greek narrative scope: totalising terms can be distributive, representative, or absolute; immediate scope decides. | Col 1:19; 2:9; Luke 14:33 |

## Trinity and Creed Vocabulary

**Scope.** This table is exhaustive only for its printed formal vocabulary. Its `-theism` inventory is tritheism, unitarianism, modalism, subordinationism, Arianism, and binitarianism. It does not catalogue every creed, philosophy, or historical theological label.

**Historical boundary.** “Trinity” and its technical system are not biblical words or explicit biblical definitions. These terms arose in post-biblical debates. Their historical meanings should be stated before they are used as conclusions. Project critique: [Trinity](trinity.md#the-development-of-the-trinity-theology).

| Term | Short definition and source |
|---|---|
| **Trinity** | One God in three distinct persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. This is a post-biblical doctrinal formula, not a Bible definition. [Vatican Catechism §§253–254](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM) states this formal doctrine. **Project conclusion:** false; Scripture identifies one God, the Father, and Jesus as his Messiah. See [one God](unitarianism.md#one-god). |
| **Trinitas** | Latin for “triad” or “trinity”; early Latin theological label, not biblical vocabulary. [Tertullian, *Against Praxeas*](https://www.tertullian.org/latin/adversus_praxean.htm) documents its early use. |
| **Ousia** | Greek: being, substance, or essence. In Nicene use, the one divine essence. It is not a biblical three-person definition. See [Merriam-Webster: *ousia*](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/ousia) and [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Hypostasis** | Greek: underlying reality or subsistence; later pro-Nicene usage distinguishes three hypostases. New Testament occurrences are context-sensitive and nontechnical. Heb 1:3; 3:14; 11:1. See [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Homoousios** | Greek: “of the same essence/substance”; Nicene description of Son’s relation to Father. Post-biblical and disputed in fourth-century debate. See [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Homoiousios** | Greek: “of similar essence/substance”; fourth-century alternative to *homoousios*. [Britannica: Arianism](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Arianism) documents the controversy. |
| **Substantia / essentia** | Latin: substance / essence; Latin renderings used for what God is. They are later metaphysical terms, not Bible definitions. [Augustine, *De Trinitate*, Latin text](https://www.augustinus.it/latino/trinita/trinita_05_libro.htm) uses this Latin vocabulary. |
| **Persona** | Latin: legal mask, role, or person in its historical range; later Western Trinity vocabulary. It should not be projected unchanged onto biblical language. See [New Advent: Person](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11726a.htm) and [Lewis & Short: *persona*](https://alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=persona). |
| **Consubstantial** | Sharing one substance or essence; common translation of *homoousios*. Creed vocabulary, not biblical wording. See [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Coequal / coeternal** | Equal in status / eternal together without beginning. These are later doctrinal conclusions, not explicit biblical definitions. [Athanasian Creed, Christian Reformed Church](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/creeds/athanasian-creed) states this creed language. **Project conclusion:** Jesus receives authority from God and has a God. Matt 28:18; John 20:17. |
| **Perichoresis / circumincession** | Trinitarian *perichoresis*, Latin *circumincessio*, means mutual indwelling of three divine persons. [Merriam-Webster: circumincession](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/circumincession) identifies the Latin as a translation of Greek *perichoresis*; see also [Merriam-Webster: perichoresis](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/perichoresis). It is later technical vocabulary, not a Bible definition. |
| **Filioque** | Latin “and from the Son”; Western addition about Spirit’s procession to the creed. Historical doctrinal clause, not biblical vocabulary. See [Vatican Catechism, Trinity](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM). |
| **Eternal generation** | Doctrine that Son is eternally begotten by Father, without temporal beginning. It is later dogmatic language, not a biblical definition of “begotten.” See [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trinity/). |
| **Procession** | In Trinitarian doctrine, eternal origin of Spirit from Father, or Father and Son in Western usage. It is a creed-system term, not a lexical gloss for biblical “send.” See [Vatican Catechism, Trinity](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM). |
| **Relations of origin** | Father begets, Son is begotten, Spirit proceeds. Formal Trinitarian scheme; not a biblical list or definition. [Vatican Catechism §§251–255](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM) states this scheme. |
| **Economic / immanent Trinity** | Modern labels summarising the official categories “divine economy” and God’s “inmost life”; they are not Bible terms. [Vatican Catechism §§236–237](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM) uses those categories. |
| **Communicatio idiomatum** | Latin “communication of properties”; Christological rule that predicates of either nature may be said of Christ’s one person. Chalcedonian vocabulary, not a biblical definition. See [Book of Concord, Solid Declaration VIII](https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/person-of-christ/). |
| **Triad** | A group or set of three, usually related. [Britannica Dictionary: triad](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/triad). A triadic passage is not thereby a Trinity definition. Matt 28:19; 2 Cor 13:14. |
| **Tritheism** | Belief that Father, Son, and Spirit are three gods rather than one God. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Trinity](https://iep.utm.edu/trinity/). |
| **Unitarianism** | Here, biblical unitarianism: one God, the Father; Jesus is God’s human Messiah; Holy Spirit is God’s Spirit. See [Unitarian views](unitarianism.md#unitarian-views). |
| **Modalism** | One God appearing in successive or different modes rather than distinct Father, Son, and Spirit. Historical model; project rejects it because Father and Son are distinct. See [modalism](trinity.md#modalism). |
| **Subordinationism** | In this project’s history, a Trinity model with Father, Son, and Spirit as distinct parts ordered in a hierarchy: Father highest, Son lesser, Spirit least. Historical varieties differ; this entry follows [the project’s subordinationism definition](trinity.md#subordinationism). It is not a label for biblical unitarianism. |
| **Arianism** | Fourth-century family of views treating Son as created or unlike the Father in a disputed sense. It is not a catch-all label for biblical unitarianism. See [Arianism](trinity.md#arianism). |
| **Binitarianism** | Belief in two persons within one Godhead, typically Father and Son. [Scholarly encyclopedia article (PDF)](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstreams/b8b5cc86-7f00-4ce5-a06b-776e42b810eb/download). Not the project position. |
