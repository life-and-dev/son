# Tritheism

This draft expands the [development overview](../development.md). Tritheism should not be used as a label for every Trinitarian confession. It names a specific failure to preserve numerical monotheism.

## Definition

Tritheism is the belief that Father, Son, and Spirit are three separate divine beings or gods. Classical Trinitarians ordinarily reject it, claiming one divine essence rather than three gods. The question is not whether three terms occur in Scripture, but what makes three distinct agents one God rather than three beings sharing a kind or purpose.

## Biblical Tests

### Old Testament

Isaiah 43:10 says, “before me no **god** was formed, nor shall there be any after me.” Isaiah 44:6 declares, “besides me there is **no god**,” and Isaiah 45:5 says, “there is **no other**.” These texts directly oppose a pantheon of separate true gods. Deuteronomy 6:4 supplies the same monotheistic foundation, explained in [the Shema](../../shema.md).

### New Testament

First Corinthians 8:6 says that for Christians there is “one **God**, the Father” and “one **Lord**, Jesus Christ.” It distinguishes Father and Jesus without presenting them as competing true gods. John 17:3 similarly identifies the Father as “the only true **God**” and Jesus as the sent Christ. The defended reading keeps this grammar: Father alone is Almighty God, Jesus is His human Son, and the Spirit is God's active interaction.

Scripture can use *god* in honourific or representative senses. Such titles do not necessarily identify their bearer as a rival true God. Therefore a high title for Jesus is not automatically tritheism. The decisive issue is whether the title is made into a claim that he is a separate almighty being alongside the Father.

## Premises and Inferences

Tritheism follows if three persons are understood as three independent centres of divine being, each possessing deity in the same individual sense. A claim that they share a common species does not solve this: three humans share humanity but are three humans, not one human. Common purpose likewise establishes unity of action, not numerical identity.

Classical Trinitarianism avoids the conclusion by positing one undivided essence. Critics may question whether that account succeeds, but they should not assume that “three persons” simply means three gods. Conversely, asserting one essence cannot merely be a verbal rule. It must explain how real personal distinctions do not yield three divine beings.

Independence matters more than the number of names. Scripture can name God, His word, His wisdom, His spirit, His angels, and His Messiah without creating a pantheon. Tritheism appears only when Father, Son, and Spirit are each treated as a separate ultimate divine being. Perfect agreement among three such beings would be harmony, not numerical identity. A common divine kind would likewise describe what each is, not why the three are one individual God.

## Strongest Defence and Reply

The strongest defence says divine essence is not a species or a divisible material. Father, Son, and Spirit are inseparable in operation and possess the one identical divine being. The charge of tritheism thus misconstrues the intended doctrine.

The reply concedes that this is the intended distinction. It then asks for the biblical warrant for the metaphysical claim. Isaiah's exclusive LORD language and Paul's Father-focused “one God” wording do not describe three persons sharing an essence. The Father-alone account preserves numerical monotheism without that extra proposal and maintains Jesus' real distinction, as [the Son's distinctness](../../son-of-man/distinct.md) shows.

This reply should not claim that every essence account is formally contradictory. “Person” and “being” may be technical terms used differently, so simple arithmetic does not decide the issue. The narrower test asks whether the proposed relation is defined clearly and grounded in Scripture. Where it is not, the tritheism concern remains a diagnostic question rather than an automatic verdict.

## Conclusion

Tritheism means [three separate divine beings](#definition), not simply Trinitarian language. The [Old Testament exclusivity texts](#old-testament) and Paul's Father-focused confession challenge every model to explain numerical oneness without turning honourific titles into rival gods.
