# Shared Biblical Unitarian and Christadelphian Views

Scope note: the sources used here represent overlap from [Spirit & Truth Fellowship International / BiblicalUnitarian.com](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/) and [Christadelphia.org / TheChristadelphian.com](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php). This is not an exhaustive survey of all Unitarian groups.

## One God

- STFI says ["Yahweh alone ... one true God"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/) and calls Him a ["unitary being. The heavenly Father alone"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/).
- Christadelphian sources likewise say ["the only true God ... the ONE FATHER"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php) and, more simply, ["There is only one God"](https://thechristadelphian.com/about-us/christadelphian-beliefs/).
- Assumption/Conclusion: the shared wording points to one God, identified as the Father.

## Holy Spirit

- STFI says the ["Holy Spirit" refers to God our heavenly Father](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/) and is ["not a separate person"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/).
- Christadelphian sources say God is ["everywhere present by His Spirit"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php), that the Holy Spirit is ["God's personal power"](https://thechristadelphian.com/about-us/christadelphian-beliefs/), and ["not a separate person but God's own radiant power"](https://thechristadelphian.com/read-booklets-online/the-holy-spirit/).
- Assumption/Conclusion: both traditions describe the Spirit as God's own power or presence, not another divine person.

## What Was and Is Jesus?

- STFI and BiblicalUnitarian.com describe Jesus as the ["Son of God and Messiah"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/) and the ["fully human Son of God, not God the Son"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/why-biblical-unitarianism).
- Christadelphian sources say Jesus is ["a man, not God"](https://christadelphia.org/belief.php), was ["born of woman at a specific point in history"](https://christadelphia.org/christ.php), and is the ["Son of God"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php).
- STFI also says Jesus is now ["exalted to God's right hand as Lord over all, regent king and vicegerent"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/).
- Assumption/Conclusion: both traditions present Jesus as the human Son of God, not God Himself.

## Pre-existence

- STFI says Jesus ["did not personally exist prior to birth or conception"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/).
- BiblicalUnitarian.com distinguishes ["ideal or notional pre-existence"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-pre-existence-literal-or-ideal) from ["actual pre-existence"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-pre-existence-literal-or-ideal).
- Christadelphian material says Jesus did not exist ["as a person before Eve, Abraham, or David"](https://christadelphia.org/belief.php).
- Assumption/Conclusion: both traditions reject Jesus' personal pre-human existence. The "ideal or notional" explanation was not clearly sourced here from Christadelphian material.

## How Is Jesus Son of God While Fully Human?

- STFI says Jesus was ["miraculously conceived"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/) and that his life ["began when he was conceived"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/).
- BiblicalUnitarian.com says Jesus is the ["fully human Son of God, not God the Son"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/why-biblical-unitarianism) and argues that ["Son of God" does not mean "God"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-is-the-son-of-god-not-god-the-son).
- Christadelphian sources say Jesus was ["begotten of the Virgin Mary by the Holy Spirit"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php), the ["only begotten Son of God"](https://thechristadelphian.com/about-us/christadelphian-beliefs/), ["shared every aspect of our nature"](https://thechristadelphian.com/about-us/christadelphian-beliefs/), and is ["The Son of God, but certainly not God Himself"](https://christadelphia.org/belief.php).
- Assumption/Conclusion: the shared sourced view is that Jesus is Son of God because God directly begot or conceived him, while he remains fully human because he truly came from Mary.

## Why Do Jesus' Blood and Sacrifice Save?

- STFI says Jesus ["paid for sin by sacrificing himself"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/), was the ["atoning sacrifice for the whole world"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/), and that remission of sins comes through the ["offering of the body of Jesus once for all"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php).
- BiblicalUnitarian.com argues that not just any human death could save. Jesus alone was fit because of his ["original genetic purity"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/1/christianity-101-two-adams) and ["behavioral purity"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/1/christianity-101-two-adams), making him the ["perfect sacrifice"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/1/christianity-101-two-adams).
- BiblicalUnitarian.com also says the ["payment for sin came by man"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/how-can-a-man-atone-for-the-sins-of-mankind) because the representative had to belong to the race that sinned, while God showed the cost of salvation by giving ["his Son"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/did-god-give-himself-or-his-own-son).
- Christadelphian sources likewise say Jesus' ["perfect life, sacrificial death and resurrection to immortality guarantees"](https://thechristadelphian.com/about-us/christadelphian-beliefs/) victory, and that he was the final sacrifice, ["one sacrifice for sins forever"](https://thechristadelphian.com/read-booklets-online/the-crisis-of-the-cross/).
- Christadelphian wording is slightly different at one key point. It says, ["Jesus did not die instead of us but he died on our behalf"](https://thechristadelphian.com/read-booklets-online/the-crisis-of-the-cross/), and that God ["condemned sin in the flesh"](https://thechristadelphian.com/read-booklets-online/the-crisis-of-the-cross/).
- Assumption/Conclusion: both traditions reject the idea that any random human death could save. They present Jesus as uniquely qualified because he was the sinless human Son God gave, the fitting representative of the race, and the final sacrifice. Christadelphians express this with more emphasis on Jesus dying "on our behalf" rather than simply "instead of us."

## "Spirit of Jesus" or "Spirit of Christ"

- STFI says that after the resurrection Jesus is [occasionally referred to as "the Spirit"](https://stfi.org/articles/usages-of-spirit-in-the-new-testament/) and discusses the phrase ["spirit of Christ"](https://stfi.org/articles/usages-of-spirit-in-the-new-testament/).
- BiblicalUnitarian.com says the ["spirit of Christ"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/verses/1-peter-1-11) is called that because it is associated with Christ and foretold of Christ, not because there are different spirits.
- Christadelphian sources now give a more direct explanation. On [Romans](https://christadelphia.org/books/spirit/Romans.php), "Spirit of God", "Spirit of Christ", and "Christ in you" are treated as similar in meaning. It says, "if any man have not a Christ-like spirit he is none of his" and believers should "share Christ's thinking".
- On [Galatians](https://christadelphia.org/books/spirit/Galatians.php), "God sent forth the spirit of His Son into our hearts" means the "spirit (mind) of Christ is fashioned in men", and "The Spirit of His Son was that tender, respectful, obedient love...".
- On [Acts](https://christadelphia.org/books/spirit/Acts.php), "Spirit of Jesus" is treated as equal to "the Holy Spirit" in that context. On [Philippians](https://christadelphia.org/books/spirit/Philippians.php), revelations guided Paul and Christ was closely involved in his activities.
- Assumption/Conclusion: both traditions can speak of "spirit of Christ" in ways tied to Christ's mind, character, or activity, without requiring a separate spirit from God's Spirit.

## Jesus' Role Today

- STFI says Jesus ["currently reigns as God's regent king and vicegerent"](https://stfi.org/core-beliefs-values/). REV/STFI also says he is the one ["between"](https://www.revisedenglishversion.com/comm/1-Timothy/chapter2/5) God and people, serving a priestly role by [interceding for us](https://www.revisedenglishversion.com/comm/1-John/chapter2/1) so that through him believers can [approach God](https://www.revisedenglishversion.com/comm/Hebrews/chapter4/16) openly.
- Christadelphian sources say Jesus was ["exalted to the heavens as priestly mediator between God and man"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php) and is ["mediator and high priest"](https://christadelphia.org/belief.php), who [opened the way to God](https://christadelphia.org/christ.php) and ["makes intercession for erring brethren"](https://christadelphia.org/basf.php).
- Assumption/Conclusion: in plain terms, both traditions present the risen Jesus as reigning under God and now helping believers come to God by standing between God and man as high priest and intercessor. In these sourced descriptions, the problem is human sin, repentance, and access to God through Christ, not an unwilling God who must be persuaded to forgive.

## How Do Believers Interact With Jesus Today?

- Shared overlap: both sets of sources say [God alone is God](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/what-do-biblical-unitarians-believe), Jesus is the ["one mediator"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/1-timothy-2-5-one-mediator-a-man) between God and believers, and Christians obey, follow, remember, and serve Jesus ([BiblicalUnitarian.com](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/what-do-biblical-unitarians-believe); [Christadelphia belief](https://www.christadelphia.org/belief.php); [BASF](https://www.christadelphia.org/basf.php); [Commandments of Christ](https://www.christadelphia.org/command.php)).
- Biblical Unitarian sources add that one ["can pray to Jesus, but does not have to"](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-christ/can-we-pray-to-jesus-christ). They still present the Father as the chief focus, while Jesus now serves in an active mediatorial role ([What Do Biblical Unitarians Believe?](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/what-do-biblical-unitarians-believe); [What Is Jesus Christ Doing Now?](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-christ/what-is-jesus-christ-doing-now)).
- Christadelphian sources emphasise approach to God ["through Christ"](https://www.christadelphia.org/basf.php). Their prayer and praise language is centred on God, while Jesus is followed, obeyed, memorialised, and served ([belief](https://www.christadelphia.org/belief.php); [BASF](https://www.christadelphia.org/basf.php); [command.php](https://www.christadelphia.org/command.php)).
- Assumption/Conclusion: the shared sourced pattern is God-centred worship with Jesus as mediator and Lord to be followed and obeyed. The mediation language here describes believers approaching God through Christ, especially where sin and confession are in view, rather than Jesus persuading an unwilling God. The clearest sourced difference in the links provided is that BiblicalUnitarian.com explicitly allows prayer to Jesus, while the Christadelphian material here stresses prayer to God through Christ rather than directly addressing Jesus.

## What Is Clearly Shared vs. What Was Not Clearly Sourced

- Clearly shared in the provided sources: [one God who is the Father](#one-god), the [Holy Spirit as God's own power or presence rather than a separate person](#holy-spirit), [Jesus as the human Son of God rather than God Himself](#what-was-and-is-jesus), [no personal pre-human existence of Jesus](#pre-existence), [Jesus' miraculous conception and real humanity](#how-is-jesus-son-of-god-while-fully-human), [his sinlessness and saving sacrificial death](#why-do-jesus-blood-and-sacrifice-save), [his present exalted role](#jesus-role-today), and [believers' allegiance and obedience to him](#how-do-believers-interact-with-jesus-today).
- Not clearly sourced as jointly supported in the material provided: the BiblicalUnitarian idea of [ideal or notional pre-existence](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-pre-existence-literal-or-ideal) as a shared Christadelphian explanation, and a clearly shared practice of [praying directly to Jesus](https://www.biblicalunitarian.com/articles/jesus-christ/can-we-pray-to-jesus-christ).
