# Jesus (Yeshua)

## The Real Name

The English form "Jesus" reflects a chain of translations. In [Hebrew and Aramaic, the languages spoken in first-century Judea, the name was **Yeshua** (יֵשׁוּעַ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshua). The Greek New Testament rendered it **Iēsous** (Ἰησοῦς); the Latin Bible spelled it **Iesus**. The form ["Jesus" appeared only after the name reached English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_(name)), as documented on Jesus and [Etymonline: Jesus](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Jesus).

A primary reason is that the letter J did not exist in the ancient world; Hebrew, Greek, and Latin had no J sound, which entered English only in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. The famous [1611 King James Bible still spelled the name **"Iesus"**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J). The Italian scholar Gian Giorgio Trissino is generally credited with distinguishing I and J in 1524. A fuller linguistic history is given by the [Ancient Hebrew Research Center](https://ancient-hebrew.org/hebrew-names/from-where-did-the-name-jesus-come.htm).

The name **Yeshua** is a short sentence. From the Hebrew root *y-sh-ʿ*, [it means **"Yahweh saves"** or **"Yahweh is salvation"**](https://biblehub.com/q/what_does_the_name_jesus_mean.htm) with "Yahweh" being the covenant name of the God of Israel.

Throughout this article the form **Yeshua** is used as the closer equivalent of the original, however the rest of this website uses "Jesus" because it is more familiar to English speakers.

## Historic Facts

Yeshua was a Jewish man born around 4 BCE in the Roman province of Judea (Matthew 2:1-6; Luke 2:4-7), who grew up in Nazareth (Luke 2:39-40; John 1:46), was raised in a Jewish family (Luke 2:41-52; Matthew 13:55), and worked as a craftsman (Mark 6:3; Matthew 13:55).

As an adult he was baptized by John the Baptist (Matthew 3:13-17; Mark 1:9), gathered followers (disciples), and taught throughout Galilee and Judea (Matthew 4:18-25; Luke 4:14-15), drawing crowds and opposition from religious and political authorities (Mark 3:7-8; John 7:1).

Around 30 to 33 CE, in Jerusalem during Passover, he was arrested, tried, and executed by Roman crucifixion under a Roman governor (Matthew 27:1-2, 11-26; John 18:28-40; 19:16-18).

A small group of his closest followers then reported that God raised him from the dead three days later (Luke 24:1-12; John 20:1-18; 1 Corinthians 15:3-8). They spread his teachings across the Roman world (Acts 1:8; Romans 1:8; 1 Thessalonians 1:8), and within a few centuries the movement became a major religion known today as Christianity. Today more than two billion people identify as Christians and regard Yeshua as [the Christ](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ) which is the central figure of their faith and [salvation](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/life).

## What Makes Yeshua Unique

### The only man who was ever born of a virgin

Two gospel accounts (Matthew 1:18; Luke 1:35) state that Mary, pledged to Joseph, became pregnant through the direct action of God's Spirit, with no human father. The New Testament applies the title **"only-begotten [Son of God](index.md)"** to Yeshua (John 3:16), highlighting his unique relationship with the Father as God's appointed and worthy heir rather than a generic one‑of‑a‑kind label. He is also called **"[the Son of Man](son-of-man.md)"** (John 5:27), emphasizing his full humanity.

### The only man called "the Last Adam"

Paul gives Yeshua the title **"the last Adam"** (1 Corinthians 15:45).

The genealogy in Luke 3:38 ends with "Adam, the son of God," since the first man was created directly by God, without human parents. Adam was the first head of the human family.

Paul presents Yeshua as the second and final head. Adam's rebellion introduced **sin** (rebellion and resulting brokenness) into the world, affecting all humanity. Romans 5:12-21 implies that only a being directly created by God, like Adam, could undo that damage, making Yeshua uniquely qualified as **Savior** of the human race.

### The only man who had perfect faith in God and lived a sinless life

The New Testament states that although fully human, Yeshua never sinned (1 Peter 2:22; 1 John 3:5). He says he always does what pleases his Father (John 8:29). Hebrews 4:15 notes that he was **"tempted in every way, just as we are,"** and the texts depict him as genuinely tested, hungry, afraid, and in pain.

Adam, created without an innate pull toward sin (a "sin nature"), chose disobedience anyway.

Yeshua never yielded, which the texts present as the basis for his unique qualification to represent humanity before God.

### The only man who died as the perfect sacrifice for our sins

The Bible frames human wrongdoing as creating a debt before God, addressed in the Israelite temple system by offering spotless animals in place of the sinner.

Hebrews 9:12-14 and 10:10-12 state that unlike those repeated sacrifices, Yeshua offered himself **once for all** as a perfect sacrifice. 1 John 2:2 and 1 John 4:10 state that God sent his Son to be the payment for sins. The technical term is **propitiation** (Hebrews 2:17), the gift that turns away deserved anger. In Christian teaching, Yeshua's death on the cross is the provision by which the broken relationship ([righteousness](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/life/righteousness)) between humanity and God is repaired.

### The only man God ever raised from the dead to everlasting life

The resurrection is the central event of Christian proclamation. Acts 17:31 states that God has given proof to all by raising Yeshua from the dead. Romans 1:4 says he was **"declared to be the Son of God"** by that resurrection. Acts 2:32 and 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 record the witnesses to his resurrection.

### The only man God has highly exalted as Lord, Head of the Church, and ruler of all

Several New Testament texts (Philippians 2:9; Acts 2:36; Ephesians 1:22; Colossians 1:18; Matthew 28:18) state that after the resurrection God the Father exalted Yeshua and gave him **"all authority in heaven and on earth."** He is called **"Lord"** (Greek *Kyrios*, the term the Greek Old Testament uses for Yahweh) and **"Head of the Church,"** the worldwide community of his followers. Daniel 7:13-14 pictures a human-like figure coming **"with the clouds of heaven"** to receive an everlasting kingdom, and Joseph's elevation to second-in-command over Egypt (Genesis 41:37-46) parallels Yeshua as the once-rejected man now at God's right hand (Ephesians 1:20).

### The only man who is now the Mediator between God and mankind

1 Timothy 2:5 states that there is one God and one mediator between God and humanity, [the Christ](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ) (meaning "the anointed one"). A **mediator** is a go-between who helps two estranged parties communicate. In the Old Testament the high priest entered the temple's innermost room once a year to offer blood on behalf of the people. Romans 8:34, Hebrews 7:25, 8:34 present him as the one who "intercedes" which means he to speaks to God on our behalf.

### The only man who will gather all Christians to meet him "in the air"

1 Thessalonians 4:17 describes a future event in which Yeshua will return and his followers will be **"caught up … to meet the Lord in the air,"** often called "the rapture" by Christians. The wording is vivid, and the biblical context connects it to the Lord's visible return in glory, as seen in passages such as Daniel 7:13-14, Matthew 24:30, Acts 1:9-11, and Revelation 1:7. In that setting, **"the air"** and **"the clouds"** are not meant to suggest a literal scene of people drifting around in the sky; rather, they describe the moment when believers are gathered into the Lord's presence at his appearing. In the wider biblical story, this belongs to the sequence of resurrection, transformation, and final judgment, and the fuller picture of judgment and kingdom comes later in passages such as Matthew 25, Acts 17:31, and Revelation 20.

### The only man who will return, destroy evil, and rule the earth for a thousand years

Revelation 19:11-16 and 20:1-6 contain a vision in which Yeshua returns as a warrior-king on a white horse, defeats his enemies, imprisons the devil ("Satan") and evil spirits, and rules from Jerusalem for a thousand years, the **Millennium** (Latin for "a thousand years"). John the Baptist identifies Yeshua as **"the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world"** (John 1:29), the sacrificial figure. The crowds entering Jerusalem cite Genesis 49:9, calling him **"the Lion of Judah,"** the fierce king. Christian theology holds that Yeshua is both.

### The only man who will judge all men and women of all time

John 5:22, 27 states that the Father has handed **all judgment to the Son.** Acts 17:31 says God has appointed a day on which he will judge the world, with Yeshua's resurrection as the proof. 2 Corinthians 5:10 and Acts 10:42 affirm that Christ will judge the living and the dead. John 5:28-29 distinguishes two outcomes: some will rise **"to the resurrection of life,"** and some **"to the resurrection of judgment."**.

### The only man who will restore the Paradise the first Adam lost

Genesis opens with Eden, a garden of peace and unbroken fellowship between God and humanity. Adam and Eve's rebellion ended that state, and the creation has since **"groaned"** under the loss (Romans 8:22). In 1 Corinthians 15:24-28, Paul describes the end, when Yeshua delivers the completed kingdom to his Father, having **"put all his enemies under his feet,"** with death as the last enemy destroyed. Revelation 21:1-4 depicts a renewed creation in which God dwells with his people, and crying, pain, and death are no more.

## Yeshua's Titles

Christian tradition applies many titles to Yeshua, each highlighting a different aspect of his role.

- **Savior**: he rescues from the consequences of sin and death (Romans 5:10; Titus 2:13).
- **Redeemer**: he paid the price to set free (Galatians 3:13; Titus 2:14).
- **Mediator**: he stands between humanity and God and keeps the relationship open (1 Timothy 2:5).
- **Lord**: he is the one obeyed, holding final authority (Romans 10:9; Philippians 2:11).
- **Best Friend**: in John 15:14-15, he calls his followers friends, not servants (John 15:14-15).
- **Big Brother**: he is the firstborn of a new human family; those who trust in him become his younger siblings (Hebrews 2:11).
- **Light of our lives**: he shows the way when everything is dark (John 8:12).
- **Peace**: he calms the guilty conscience before God (Philippians 4:7; John 14:27).
- **Joy**: he is the reason his followers can be glad in hardship (Romans 14:17; John 15:11).
- **Mentor in the art of faith**: he is watched, listened to, and learned from, so believers may grow to be like him (John 13:13; 1 Peter 2:21).
