# Jesus (Yeshua)

## The Real Name

The English form "Jesus" reflects a chain of translations. In [Hebrew and Aramaic, the languages spoken in first-century Judea, the name was **Yeshua** (יֵשׁוּעַ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshua). The Greek New Testament rendered it **Iēsous** (Ἰησοῦς); the Latin Bible spelled it **Iesus**. The form ["Jesus" appeared only after the name reached English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_(name)), as documented on Jesus and [Etymonline: Jesus](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Jesus).

A primary reason is that the letter J did not exist in the ancient world; Hebrew, Greek, and Latin had no J sound, which entered English only in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. The famous [1611 King James Bible still spelled the name **"Iesus"**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J). The Italian scholar Gian Giorgio Trissino is generally credited with distinguishing I and J in 1524. A fuller linguistic history is given by the [Ancient Hebrew Research Center](https://ancient-hebrew.org/hebrew-names/from-where-did-the-name-jesus-come.htm).

The name **Yeshua** is a short sentence. From the Hebrew root *y-sh-ʿ*, [it means **"Yahweh saves"** or **"Yahweh is salvation"**](https://biblehub.com/q/what_does_the_name_jesus_mean.htm) with "Yahweh" being the covenant name of the God of Israel.

Throughout this article the form **Yeshua** is used as the closer equivalent of the original, however the rest of this website uses "Jesus" because it is more familiar to English speakers.

## A Quick Orientation

Yeshua was a Jewish man born around 4 BCE in the Roman province of Judea (Matthew 2:1-6; Luke 2:4-7), who grew up in Nazareth (Luke 2:39-40; John 1:46), was raised in a Jewish family (Luke 2:41-52; Matthew 13:55), and worked as a craftsman (Mark 6:3; Matthew 13:55). As an adult he was baptized by John the Baptist (Matthew 3:13-17; Mark 1:9), gathered followers, and taught throughout Galilee and Judea (Matthew 4:18-25; Luke 4:14-15), drawing crowds and opposition from religious and political authorities (Mark 3:7-8; John 7:1). Around 30 to 33 CE, in Jerusalem during Passover, he was arrested, tried, and executed by Roman crucifixion under a Roman governor (Matthew 27:1-2, 11-26; John 18:28-40; 19:16-18). A small group of his closest followers then reported that God raised him from the dead three days later (Luke 24:1-12; John 20:1-18; 1 Corinthians 15:3-8). They spread his teachings across the Roman world (Acts 1:8; Romans 1:8; 1 Thessalonians 1:8), and within a few centuries the movement became a major religion. Today more than two billion people identify as Christians and regard Yeshua as the central figure of their faith.

## Part 3: Fourteen Things That Make Yeshua Unique

The following are fourteen claims made about Yeshua in the New Testament, presented in plain language. References such as "(Matthew 1:18)" cite the passage location in the Bible. Verses are paraphrased, not quoted.

### 1. The only man who was ever born of a virgin

Two gospel accounts (Matthew 1:18; Luke 1:35) state that Mary, pledged to Joseph, became pregnant through the direct action of God's Spirit, with no human father. The New Testament applies the title **"only-begotten Son of God"** to Yeshua (John 3:16), meaning he is one of a kind. he is also called **"the Son of Man"** (John 5:27), emphasizing his full humanity. In Christian teaching the virgin birth is read as indicating that Yeshua originated from God and entered creation as a person.

### 2. The only man called "the Last Adam"

Paul gives Yeshua the title **"the last Adam"** (1 Corinthians 15:45). The genealogy in Luke 3:38 ends with "Adam, the son of God," since the first man was created directly by God, without human parents. Adam was the first head of the human family; Paul presents Yeshua as the second and final head. Adam's rebellion introduced **sin** (rebellion and resulting brokenness) into the world, affecting all humanity. Romans 5:12-21 argues that only a being directly created by God, like Adam, could undo that damage, making Yeshua uniquely qualified as **Savior** of the human race.

### 3. The only man who had perfect faith in God and lived a sinless life

The New Testament states that although fully human, Yeshua never sinned (1 Peter 2:22; 1 John 3:5). He says he always does what pleases his Father (John 8:29). Hebrews 4:15 notes that he was **"tempted in every way, just as we are,"** and the texts depict him as genuinely tested, hungry, afraid, and in pain. Adam, created without an innate pull toward sin (a "sin nature"), chose disobedience anyway. Yeshua never yielded, which the texts present as the basis for his unique qualification to represent humanity before God.

### 4. The only man who died as the perfect sacrifice for our sins

The Bible frames human wrongdoing as creating a debt before God, addressed in the Israelite temple system by offering spotless animals in place of the sinner. Hebrews 9:12-14 and 10:10-12 state that unlike those repeated sacrifices, Yeshua offered himself **once for all** as a perfect sacrifice. 1 John 2:2 and 1 John 4:10 state that God sent his Son to be the payment for sins. The technical term is **propitiation** (Hebrews 2:17), the gift that turns away deserved anger. In Christian teaching, Yeshua's death on the cross is the provision by which the broken relationship between humanity and God is repaired.

### 5. The only man God ever raised from the dead to everlasting life

The resurrection is the central event of Christian proclamation. Acts 17:31 states that God has given proof to all by raising Yeshua from the dead. Romans 1:4 says he was **"declared to be the Son of God in power"** by that resurrection, understood as the vindication of his claims. Acts 2:32 and 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 record the witnesses to his resurrection. He says of himself, **"I am the way, and the truth, and the life"** (John 14:6), a claim Christian theology grounds in his having already passed through death into permanent life.

### 6. The only man God has highly exalted as Lord, Head of the Church, and ruler of all

Several New Testament texts (Philippians 2:9; Acts 2:36; Ephesians 1:22; Colossians 1:18; Matthew 28:18) state that after the resurrection God the Father exalted Yeshua and gave him **"all authority in heaven and on earth."** He is called **"Lord"** (Greek *Kyrios*, the term the Greek Old Testament uses for Yahweh) and **"Head of the Church,"** the worldwide community of his followers. Daniel 7:13-14 pictures a human-like figure coming **"with the clouds of heaven"** to receive an everlasting kingdom, and Joseph's elevation to second-in-command over Egypt (Genesis 41:37-46) parallels Yeshua as the once-rejected man now at God's right hand (Ephesians 1:20).

### 7. The only man who is now the Mediator between God and mankind

1 Timothy 2:5 states that there is one God and one mediator between God and humanity, the man Messiah Christ (both "Messiah" and "Christ" mean "the anointed one"). A **mediator** is a go-between who helps two estranged parties communicate. In the Old Testament the high priest entered the temple's innermost room once a year to offer blood on behalf of the people. Christian teaching holds that Yeshua now fulfills that role permanently. Hebrews 7:25 says he is able to save completely everyone who comes to God through him, **because he always lives to intercede for them.** Romans 8:34 and Hebrews 9:15 also present him as the one who pleads for the redeemed. "Intercede" means to speak on someone's behalf.

### 8. The only man who will gather all Christians to meet him "in the air"

1 Thessalonians 4:17 describes a future event in which Yeshua will return and his followers will be **"caught up … to meet the Lord in the air,"** sometimes called the Rapture. At that moment believers will receive **new bodies like his own** (Philippians 3:21), modeled on his resurrection body, no longer subject to sickness, aging, or death. 1 Corinthians 15:51-52 says the dead will be raised in a flash at that time. The event also connects to Genesis 3:15, where after the rebellion, God says the **"seed"** of the woman will crush the serpent's head, which Christian tradition reads as pointing to Yeshua.

### 9. The only man who will return, destroy evil, and rule the earth for a thousand years

Revelation 19:11-16 and 20:1-6 contain a vision in which Yeshua returns as a warrior-king on a white horse, defeats his enemies, imprisons the devil ("Satan") and evil spirits, and rules from Jerusalem for a thousand years, the **Millennium** (Latin for "a thousand years"). John the Baptist identifies Yeshua as **"the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world"** (John 1:29), the sacrificial figure. The crowds entering Jerusalem cite Genesis 49:9, calling him **"the Lion of Judah,"** the fierce king. Christian theology holds that Yeshua is both.

### 10. The only man who will raise from the dead every human being who has ever lived

In John 5:21, 25, Yeshua states that as the Father raises the dead and gives life, so the Son also gives life to whom he wills, and that an hour is coming when **"the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God."** John 6:40 and 11:25-26 also speak of everyone who believes having eternal life and being raised on the last day. The texts present this as a universal resurrection: every person who has ever died will come forth. The Son is described as having **"life in himself,"** the same self-existing life the Father has, and is therefore the agent through whom the dead will be raised to face judgment.

### 11. The only man who will judge all men and women of all time

John 5:22, 27 states that the Father has handed **all judgment to the Son.** Acts 17:31 says God has appointed a day on which he will judge the world, with Yeshua's resurrection as the proof. 2 Corinthians 5:10 and Acts 10:42 affirm that Christ will judge the living and the dead. John 5:28-29 distinguishes two outcomes: some will rise **"to the resurrection of life,"** and some **"to the resurrection of judgment."** "Judgment" here does not necessarily mean annihilation; many early Christian writers taught that the wicked are removed from God's presence permanently. The texts assign to Yeshua alone the authority to determine the final disposition of every human being.

### 12. The only man who will restore the Paradise the first Adam lost

Genesis opens with Eden, a garden of peace and unbroken fellowship between God and humanity. Adam and Eve's rebellion ended that state, and the creation has since **"groaned"** under the loss (Romans 8:22). In 1 Corinthians 15:24-28, Paul describes the end, when Yeshua delivers the completed kingdom to his Father, having **"put all his enemies under his feet,"** with death as the last enemy destroyed. Revelation 21:1-4 depicts a renewed creation in which God dwells with his people, and crying, pain, and death are no more.

### 13. The only man who is our Savior, Redeemer, Mediator, Lord, Companion, Best Friend, Big Brother, Light, Peace, Joy, and Mentor in the art of faith

Christian tradition applies many titles to Yeshua, each highlighting a different aspect of his role.

- **Savior**: he rescues from the consequences of sin and death (Romans 5:10; Titus 2:13).
- **Redeemer**: from the slave market, he paid the price to set free (Galatians 3:13; Titus 2:14).
- **Mediator**: he stands between humanity and God and keeps the relationship open (1 Timothy 2:5).
- **Lord**: he is the one obeyed, holding final authority (Romans 10:9; Philippians 2:11).
- **Constant Companion**: he has promised never to leave his followers (Matthew 28:20; Hebrews 13:5).
- **Best Friend**: in John 15:14-15, he calls his followers friends, not servants (John 15:14-15).
- **Big Brother**: he is the firstborn of a new human family; those who trust in him become his younger siblings (Hebrews 2:11).
- **Light of our lives**: he shows the way when everything is dark (John 8:12).
- **Peace**: he calms the guilty conscience before God (Philippians 4:7; John 14:27).
- **Joy**: he is the reason his followers can be glad in hardship (Romans 14:17; John 15:11).
- **Mentor in the art of faith**: he is watched, listened to, and learned from, so believers may grow to be like him (John 13:13; 1 Peter 2:21).

The titles point to a single figure: one Lord seen from multiple angles.

### 14. The Lover of our souls, and that is why we love him and confess him as Lord

Romans 10:9 states that openly declaring "Yeshua is Lord" and believing in the heart that God raised him from the dead is the confession on which Christian salvation is grounded. 1 John 4:10 and 1 John 4:19 say that God loved us first and that we love because he first loved us. Christian tradition holds that behind every title stands a person who loves those he has come to save. The texts present Yeshua as neither a distant judge nor an abstract doctrine, but as one who, in the New Testament's portrayal, laid down his life for others. Christian devotion describes this role as **"the Lover of our souls,"** a phrase from Christian worship understood as the personal ground for the response of love and confession in return.
